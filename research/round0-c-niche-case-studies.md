## Summary

- 30+ real companies/products looked at (24 subsections) across 12 niches: food-safety and cold-chain monitoring (Checkit, TempAlert/SmartSense by Digi, ComplianceMate, Sonicu), sensor hardware (Disruptive Technologies), insurer-backed leak and fire detection (LeakBot/Ondo, Ting/Whisker Labs, Roost, Shayp), damp and mould in social housing (Switchee, Aico HomeLINK), concrete maturity (Giatec, Converge), livestock water (Farmbot/Ranchbot), feed and grain bins (BinSentry, TeleSense, grain-cable vendors), tank levels (Otodata, Tank Utility, Kingspan Watchman and UK oil distributors), waste fill level (Enevo, Compology, Sensoneo), beehives (BeeHero, The Bee Corp, Arnia, ApisProtect), pest control (Rentokil PestConnect), hire-fleet tracking (Trackunit/Satrak) and legionella temperature monitoring (Invisible Systems, PTSG).
- **The usual outcome is a business of £/$4–20m revenue or a trade sale, not a unicorn.** Checkit's ARR is about £14m [1]. Ondo/LeakBot revenue is about £3.8m, with operating losses [12]. Disruptive Technologies had $15m revenue and $4m ARR when Digi agreed to buy it for $130m [7][8]. TempAlert sold to Digi for $45m at more than 20,000 customer sites [4][5].
- **The winners take recurring revenue and sell through a party that already pays for the loss or already visits the site.** Examples: fuel distributors (Otodata, more than 3.5m tanks [44]), Rentokil technicians (319k connected devices on 18k sites [70]), Sika's admixture sales network (Giatec [33]), insurers paying 100% of the device (Ting [19][20]), and landlords using public retrofit funds (Switchee [24][25]).
- **The ones that struggled** relied on slow-moving payers or on rare-event value only. Enevo (municipal waste) went bankrupt [55]. The Bee Corp dissolved [64]. Roost was sold and its product discontinued [23]. LeakBot remained loss-making about 9 years after its first insurer deal [9][12].

## Cross-cutting patterns (with evidence)

1. **The money is in recurring revenue, and hardware is bundled or subsidised.**
   - Checkit: 96% of revenue is recurring [1].
   - BinSentry: a monthly subscription that includes hardware, cellular and guarantee, and the sensor cannot be bought outright [37][38].
   - Sonicu: warranty is tied to an active subscription [16].
   - Otodata: the app is free to the end customer [45].
   - Disruptive Technologies: ARR was only $4m of $15m revenue, worth about 32.5x ARR to a platform buyer [7][8]. Hardware-heavy revenue is valued as a feature, not a business.
2. **Observed price points for a niche B2B sensor subscription:**
   - Oil tank: £25/yr [49]; £4.99/month on £75 hardware [51]; from $1.50/month at distributor scale [46].
   - Livestock water: A$342/yr data subscription on A$1,290 hardware [31].
   - Grain bin: $200 per bin per year [80].
   - Restaurant: $69–$159 per site per month for 1–2 sensors (listing) [13].
   - Vaccine fridge: $672 for a kit including 1 year [15].
   - Hive: about $160/yr (forum) [67].
   - Typical range: **£2–£15 per sensor per month, or £100–£2,000 per site per year**, with hardware at £50–£1,300.
3. **The payer is whoever owns the loss or the logistics, often not the site user.**
   - Insurers: Ting [19][21], LeakBot [9].
   - Growers: BeeHero [60].
   - Feed mills: BinSentry [39].
   - Fuel distributors: Otodata, NWF Fuels [44][52].
   - Landlords, using public grants: Switchee, Aico [24][25][27].
4. **The winning channel already visits the site or already sells to the buyer.**
   - Rentokil technicians: 319k devices [70].
   - Sika's network in more than 100 countries [33].
   - Aico smoke-alarm installers and landlords [28].
   - Fuel distributors [44].
   - HomeServe engineers [9][11].
   - Generac dealers [48].
   - Compliance contractors such as PTSG [79].
5. **A named compliance rule gives the most durable demand, especially when it forces records or recalibration.**
   - CDC VFC: a data logger plus calibration certificate, recalibrated every 24 months [14][17].
   - HACCP: ComplianceMate, Checkit [3][13].
   - ACoP L8/HSG274: monthly temperature checks [78].
   - Awaab's Law: Switchee Mould iQ [26].
   - Recalibration and certificate expiry create a built-in replacement cycle (Sonicu [18]).
6. **Insurer-only distribution is slow unless the insurer pays everything.**
   - LeakBot: from its first deal in 2016 [9] it reached only about £3.8m revenue, with a £5.2m operating loss, in FY25 [12].
   - Roost: "awaits insurance industry transformation", then was sold and its product discontinued [22][23].
   - Ting scaled to more than 1m sensors with State Farm paying 100% [20][21].
7. **Labour saving is a strong second hook.**
   - Bin climbing avoided [39].
   - Manual temperature checks replaced [3].
   - Remote water points checked without driving [31][34].
   - Concrete strength known without waiting for break tests (Giatec "eliminate concrete testing delays" [29]; Converge [35]).
8. **Hardware is usually off-the-shelf radio plus a simple sensor; the differentiator is software, service and channel.**
   - LPWAN radios: Sigfox (LeakBot, Shayp [11][53]); LoRaWAN (Invisible Systems, Sensoneo [77][58]).
   - Cellular or LTE: Tank Utility, Farmbot, BinSentry [48][31][40].
   - Third-party sensors: Checkit integrates Disruptive Technologies hardware [6].
   - 3D-printed enclosures: BeeHero [65].
9. **Exits are mostly trade sales in the tens of millions:**
   - TempAlert: $45m [4].
   - Disruptive Technologies: $130m [7].
   - Tank Utility: undisclosed [47].
   - Compology: undisclosed [56].
   - Satrak: undisclosed [74].
   - Otodata: majority stake to private equity [44].
10. **Failures and stalls:**
    - Enevo: municipal and hauler buyers; bankrupt in 2020 [55].
    - The Bee Corp: beekeepers and growers; dissolved in 2024 [64].
    - Roost: insurers [23].
    - TeleSense and ApisProtect: no news found after their last rounds (see below).
    - The sources do not state the causes. The common feature visible here is a payer with a weak or slow budget, or value tied to rare events only (researcher observation).

## Implications for the scoring rubric
- **Cost of incident:** 10 = sourced loss ≥ ~£5k/incident or named stock/animal loss (escape-of-water avg £8,500 [82]; livestock death [31]); 5 = £500–5k; 0 = inconvenience only.
- **Frequency:** 10 = sensor replaces a routine daily/weekly task (fridge logs, feed/fuel deliveries, water runs) [3][39][31]; 5 = incidents a few times/yr/site; 0 = rare event only — rare-event insurer plays were slowest [12][22].
- **Workaround:** 10 = named manual task with labour (bin climbing [39], manual temperature checks [3]); 5 = occasional checks; 0 = none.
- **Compliance/insurance:** 10 = named rule forcing continuous records or recalibration (CDC VFC [14][17], HACCP [13], ACoP L8 [78], Awaab's Law [26]) or insurer funding 100% [19]; 5 = guidance, or insurer interest without funding [22]; 0 = none.
- **Buyer with budget:** 10 = payer owns the loss/logistics (feed mill, fuel distributor, landlord, grower) [39][44][27][60]; 5 = budget but slow procurement (councils, insurers) [55][12]; 0 = hobbyist/consumer [67].
- **Distribution:** 10 = route already on site (pest technicians [70], fuel distributors [44], materials reps [33], alarm installers [28]); 5 = resellers/wholesalers; 0 = cold direct-to-SME.
- **Market gap:** 10 = incumbents enterprise-only or absent for the niche; 5 = priced but ill-fitting competitors; 0 = commoditised (oil-tank monitoring £25/yr or free [49][52]).
- **Buildability:** 10 = comparable off-the-shelf LPWAN/cellular sensor retails ~€50 [81], so BOM < £50 plausible; 5 = custom probe/enclosure or calibration certificate [14]; 0 = embedded/consumable, ATEX or medical-device class.
- **Recurring revenue:** 10 = ≥ ~£100/site/yr or ≥ £5/sensor/month with a structural reason (records, recalibration [18]); benchmarks $200/bin/yr [80], A$342/yr [31], $69–159/site/month [13]; 5 = £1–3/month [46][49]; 0 = hardware-only (Disruptive's ARR ~27% of revenue [7]).
- **Public evidence:** 10 = regulator/insurer data plus trade press; 5 = trade press only; 0 = vendor claims only.
