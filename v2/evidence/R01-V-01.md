## Problem (neutral)
- Statement: Holiday parks and glamping sites rent lodges with private hot tubs to successive guests. HSE guidance HSG282 applies to hot tubs used in a business, including holiday lets. Staff test disinfectant and pH by hand (typically twice daily in use and at each changeover) and keep paper logs that can be incomplete. Hot-tub-linked illness leads to council action, prosecutions and compensation claims.
- Buyer job title: Holiday Park Operations Manager / Maintenance Manager at multi-lodge operators (who employ "hot tub technicians"); secondary buyer: hot-tub service contractors that maintain holiday-let tubs for owners.
- Organisation types: multi-park holiday groups (e.g. Haulfryn, Waterside Holiday Group, Parkdean, Hoburne, Away Resorts, Blair's), single lodge parks, glamping sites, holiday-cottage agencies.
- Geography: UK (England, Wales, Scotland). One US comparator (CDC) noted.

## Proposed solution form, wedge, price
- Form: hybrid — software-first (mobile HSG282 log + escalation) delivered with an optional AI-assisted review service, adding sensor pods only for tubs where probes prove reliable.
- Wedge: "Changeover-proof hot tub log" for a single park: per-tub QR code, photo-captured photometer readings with timestamps, changeover checklist, out-of-range alerts to the duty manager, and a monthly council-ready PDF per tub.
- Price: ~£8–£12 per tub per month (a 40-tub park ≈ £4–6k a year, below one seasonal technician at ~£25k); optional sensor pod +£15/tub/month.
- Why this form beats the others: in-water probes are unreliable (S2 complaints, probe-life data), so a sensor-only product risks false comfort and opex; staff already test with photometers, so capturing that work digitally gives proof without hardware risk. Liability stays with the operator, as HSG282 intends. Parks buy locally from suppliers, so a low-touch app sold through hot-tub suppliers fits how they buy.
