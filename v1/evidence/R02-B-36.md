## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Buildings with reinforced autoclaved aerated concrete (RAAC) roof planks that are kept in place, rather than removed, are managed by periodic engineer inspection. Deflection relative to span is a key indicator in published guidance. Between visits, a plank can deteriorate unobserved, for example after a roof leak.
- Who suffers / who pays: Occupants (pupils, staff, patients) and estates teams; the building owner (academy trust, local authority, diocese, NHS body) pays for inspection, mitigation and temporary accommodation.
- Sensor concept: A ceiling-mounted time-of-flight distance sensor or MEMS tilt node on each critical plank, plus a moisture spot sensor in the roof void. LoRaWAN or NB-IoT with multi-year battery. Alerts on a deflection threshold or rate, and sends a monthly report to the engineer.

The summary did not say which page carried this; it is likely the Building or Schools Week coverage (search extract) — https://building.co.uk/news/temporary-classrooms-supplied-after-raac-crisis-cost-dfe-35m/5126907.article
- England: the Guardian (via Hargreaves Lansdown) put the cost of fixing the school concrete crisis "approaching £150m", based partly on Rapleys' rough estimate of £1,000 per m² to replace a RAAC roof (search extract) — https://hl.co.uk/news/the-guardian-price-of-fixing-school-concrete-crisis-in-england-approaching-150m

- These costs come mainly from closure and removal. None of them is a cost that monitoring would have avoided, because DfE closed buildings on identification rather than monitoring them.

## 3. Current workarounds
- DfE-linked guidance calls for regular checks for visual signs of cracks, water penetration, deflection of soffits and ponding (search extract) — https://localgovernmentlawyer.co.uk/education-law/394-education-news/54939-schools-and-concrete-safety
- NHS Property Services' RAAC risk report (Oct 2021) bands deflection by span ratio, e.g. "span/250 or less – 15mm or less", with bands for span/100–span/200 (search extract) — https://property.nhs.uk/media/4226/nhsps-risk-report-on-raac-attachment-1.pdf
- A council engineers' report says panel deflection "should be recorded, and the data used to classify the deflection of each panel", and that "differential deflections exceeding 20mm between adjacent panels is considered significant". This is manual measurement on site. The summary did not say which council report carried this; the Aberdeen appraisal or the Stroud Library report are the likely pages (search extract) — https://gloucestershire.gov.uk/media/g42pipsy/stroudlibrary-report-on-raac-at-stroud-library.pdf ; https://committees.aberdeencity.gov.uk/documents/s160463/A%20-%20A1%20Engineers%20Appraisal%20Report.pdf
- Airedale General Hospital went live with an Esri ArcGIS geospatial system (built by BIS Consult) to track defects and plank deterioration across its RAAC monitoring and repair programme. Software for manual inspection data, not sensors (search extract) — https://htn.co.uk/2024/05/08/airedale-general-hospital-goes-live-with-geospatial-system-for-raac-management/ ; https://www.twinfm.com/article/nhs-hospital-manages-raac-with-new-geospatial-system

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- Deflection threshold: the government-published report "Failure of Reinforced Autoclaved Aerated Concrete (RAAC) Planks" says roof-panel deflection exceeding span/100 could indicate panels are close to capacity, and failed panels showed span-to-deflection ratios of about 100 (search extract) — https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/645502b4479612000cc2914a/Failure_of_Reinforced_Autoclaved_Aerated_Concrete__RAAC__Planks.pdf
- IStructE: its RAAC guidance uses a red/amber/green risk matrix; after the September 2023 failures its study group concluded that "the current guidance therefore remains valid" (search extract) — https://www.istructe.org/resources/news/raac-and-the-management-of-school-estates/ ; https://www.buildingengineer.org.uk/news/istructe-issues-guidance-raac
- **Counter-evidence (critical):** the Scottish cross-sector RAAC working group minutes (5 Sept 2023) record that DfE "were not now adopting monitoring as a mitigation measure", which is more precautionary than the IStructE guidance (search extract) — https://gov.scot/publications/reinforced-autoclaved-aerated-concrete-raac-cross-sector-working-group-minutes-5-september-2023
- **Counter-evidence:** in October 2025 Education Secretary Bridget Phillipson set a 2029 deadline for removing RAAC from all schools and colleges (search extract) — https://schoolsweek.co.uk/phillipson-sets-2029-deadline-for-raac-removal/ ; https://www.concreteconnect.co.uk/news/government-pledges-to-remove-raac-from-schools-by-2029
- DfE capital funding page: DfE funds refurbishment or rebuilding "to permanently remove RAAC" for schools and colleges with confirmed RAAC, on top of SCA/CIF funding for 2026–27. Attribution to this page is probable but not confirmed (search extract) — https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/academies-devolved-formula-capital-overview
- NHS Scotland: SHTN 00-05 "RAAC in NHS estate – management and monitoring" (v1, July 2025) tells NHS boards how to live with RAAC, with a section on monitoring and inspection and on deflection measurements (search extract) — https://www.nss.nhs.scot/publications/raac-in-nhs-estate-management-and-monitoring-shtn-00-05/ ; https://www.nss.nhs.scot/media/6060/shtn-00-05-v1-july-25.pdf
- NHS Wales has an NHS RAAC guidance note from NWSSP Specialist Estates Services (search extract) — https://nwssp.nhs.wales/ourservices/specialist-estates-services/specialist-estates-services-documents/general-documents/nhs-raac-guidance-note/
- Reading: for English schools the policy is removal by 2029, not long-term monitoring, so the regulatory pull for sensor monitoring in schools is weak and shrinking. The pull is stronger for NHS estates (Scotland's SHTN 00-05 is explicitly about living with and monitoring RAAC) and for other public estates.

- **Gap (explicit):** I found no product marketed specifically as low-cost per-plank RAAC deflection monitoring, and no published price for the enterprise structural-monitoring systems (Worldsensing, Resensys, Senceive). So it is unknown whether a cheap incumbent exists; the structural-monitoring firms clearly have the technology. The bigger problem is demand: English schools are removing RAAC by 2029, and DfE did not adopt monitoring as a mitigation. Any gap is in NHS and other public estates that must live with RAAC for longer (NHS target 2035, per a search summary I could not tie to a specific page).

- Engineering caution (my inference, not sourced): low-cost ToF LiDAR modules are typically specified at centimetre-level accuracy. That may be too coarse to track early deflection trends (for example the 15 mm "span/250" band in the NHSPS report) as opposed to the ~40 mm span/100 alarm. A MEMS tilt sensor or a differential laser over a short gauge may be needed, which raises cost. The accuracy figure was not checked in this round.
- No certification barrier was found (it is not a safety device). Liability is a risk if a sensor misses a failure, so the product would likely be positioned as an aid to the engineer, not a replacement.

## Researcher observations
- The school framing is weak. DfE chose removal (2029 deadline) and closure over monitoring, so a school-focused product sells into a market that is being designed away. c1 costs (temporary classrooms) are real, but monitoring would not have avoided them.
- A better niche for the same problem is NHS estates. NHS Scotland's SHTN 00-05 (July 2025) is explicitly about "living with" and monitoring RAAC. Health boards (Hywel Dda, Forth Valley, Lanarkshire) run annual RAAC re-inspection programmes, and the NHS removal horizon is later (2035 per one search summary). Councils (Aberdeen housing, libraries) are a second niche. Channel: the engineering firms already writing those reports (WSP, Clancy, Harley Haddow).
- One search summary quoted NHS England's chief executive as calling RAAC monitoring "really quite burdensome" for staff. That is strong pain evidence, but the summary did not say which page carried it, so I have not cited it as a sourced bullet.
- Technical risk: cheap ToF sensors may not resolve the millimetre-level trends engineers care about. A tilt-plus-laser combination (as in Worldsensing LaserTilt90) is the proven design, but at enterprise prices.
