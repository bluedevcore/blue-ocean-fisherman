## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Local exhaust ventilation (LEV) for welding fume or wood dust can lose capture between its periodic thorough examination and test (TExT) — e.g. a blinded filter, slipping fan belt or closed damper. Routine checks between tests are manual and may be skipped, so workers can be exposed for long periods. HSE inspections find inadequate LEV and issue notices; occupational asthma claims follow.
- Who suffers / who pays: Welders, joiners and grinders; the employer (H&S lead / works manager) carries enforcement and civil-claim risk.
- Sensor concept: A differential-pressure sensor tapped into the hood plenum or duct plus a current clamp on the fan motor, on LoRaWAN or NB-IoT battery power. It alerts when static pressure leaves the commissioned band and auto-generates the routine check record/LEV logbook for the TExT contractor and inspector.

## 2. Frequency
- **Statutory test interval:** under COSHH Regulation 9, LEV must be thoroughly examined and tested by a competent person at least once every 14 months (UK; secondary guidance) — https://blog.hobarts.com/lev-testing-staying-fully-compliant-with-hse-guidelines (search extract); https://www.hseblog.com/lev-testing-maintenance/ (search extract); shorter intervals apply to some processes, e.g. grinding every six months and shot blasting monthly (COSHH Schedule 4, per secondary source; not verified against statute) — https://www.hseblog.com/lev-testing-maintenance/ (search extract)
- **Routine checks between tests:** a Knowsley Council LEV document states "Regulation 9 of COSHH states that all control systems should be: checked visually on a weekly basis and records kept of these checks" (secondary; the same document cites "COSHH 2004", so treat with caution) — https://www.knowsley.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2026-03/C2%20Emissions%20EPN7842%20LEV%20Huyton.pdf (search extract). HSE's LEV glossary describes routine performance checking that "may involve the use of airflow indicators" — https://www.hse.gov.uk/lev/glossary.htm (search extract)
- **Enforcement hit-rate in fabrication:** in a week-long South Yorkshire HSE operation covering metal fabrication, engineering, manufacturing and waste, 65% of firms breached health and safety law; inspectors served 3 prohibition and 31 improvement notices and 46 companies needed improvements; poor welding fume controls were among the breaches (The Star; UK) — https://www.thestar.co.uk/business/sixty-five-per-cent-of-south-yorkshire-firms-breach-health-and-safety-laws-in-week-long-operation-3534445 (search extract); same campaign — https://www.rothbiz.co.uk/2022/01/news-7942-hse-inspection-campaign-sheds.html (search extract)
- **Jersey fabricated-metal initiative (Dec 2024–Apr 2025):** 11 businesses inspected; failures to adequately control exposure to welding fume, and "in a minority of cases there were no exposure controls at all" (Jersey HSI) — https://www.gov.je/Government/Departments/JusticeHomeAffairs/HealthSafetyInspectorate/Updates/Pages/FabricatedMetalInitiativeOutcome.aspx (search extract)
- **Gap:** no data on how often LEV performance actually degrades between tests (e.g. % of systems failing TExT), which is the core claim of the idea.

## Researcher observations
- c4 and c10 look strong: COSHH reg 9 plus repeated HSE notices and prosecutions for LEV/welding-fume failures, targeted inspection campaigns, and HSE's own case narrative of unmaintained extraction causing asthma.
- Buildability looks good: LoRaWAN DP sensors are ~€150 retail, and an Adeunis DP + current-transformer combo already exists — which also means the hardware is not differentiating; value must come from the compliance record and channel.
- Most promising channel: **LEV testing contractors** and **engineering-inspection insurers (e.g. HSB)** who already visit every ≤14 months; extraction OEMs (Nederman, Kemper) already sell connected monitoring but only for their own filter units, leaving mixed-brand SME systems as the likely gap.
- Missing: per-system TExT cost and any buyer/budget evidence, both needed for c3/c5.
