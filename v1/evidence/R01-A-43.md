# R01-A-43 — Farm irrigation reservoirs under the Reservoirs Act need level and seepage records that are still taken by hand

> Research note: WebFetch was blocked in this environment (ENOTFOUND for gov.uk). Every bullet
> below comes from WebSearch result titles and extracts, so all are marked "(search extract)".
> No page was opened in full. The shared web-search budget ran out after 13 successful queries
> on this idea (2 verification queries were refused). Geography is England unless stated.

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Under the Reservoirs Act 1975, the undertaker of a large raised reservoir (above
  25,000 m³ above ground in England) must keep a prescribed record of water levels, leakage,
  settlement and repairs, and must employ a supervising engineer. On farms these readings are
  usually taken by hand. The idea assumes records are often incomplete and that the
  Environment Agency (EA) enforces when they are.
- Who suffers / who pays: The farm business as undertaker pays for the engineer, records and
  any safety works. Downstream residents and the EA carry the risk if a dam fails.
- Sensor concept: Pressure or ultrasonic level sensor in the reservoir, plus a level sensor on a
  V-notch weir at the toe drain to measure seepage. LTE-M or LoRaWAN radio. Automatic
  prescribed-form record, alerts, and a report for the supervising engineer.

  https://watermagazine.co.uk/2024/11/06/company-prosecuted-for-failing-to-complete-reservoir-safety-works

- Worst case (not a farm): the Toddbrook (Whaley Bridge) permanent repair plan was estimated at
  "£12-£16 million" in 2021 (England) (search extract) —
  https://marineindustrynews.co.uk/12m-restoration-plan-for-toddbrook-reservoir/

## 2. Frequency
- Readings: the Act sets no default reading frequency. The record must be kept "at such
  intervals and in such manner as may from time to time be required by any directions of the
  construction engineer or inspecting engineer" (England & Wales) (search extract) —
  https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1975/23/crossheading/monitoring-and-supervision/2016-04-01/data.html
- Engineer visits: the supervising engineer must give the undertaker a written statement "not
  less often than once a year" (England & Wales) (search extract) —
  https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1975/23/crossheading/monitoring-and-supervision/2016-04-01/data.html
- An NI committee report says a supervising engineer's annual visit "will typically be for half
  a day" (Northern Ireland) (search extract) —
  https://www.niassembly.gov.uk/globalassets/documents/official-reports/agriculture/2013-2014/140617_reservoirsbillcommitteereport.pdf
- New farm reservoirs: a parliamentary answer says "Approximately 34 newly built Large Raised
  Reservoirs ... have been built on farms in England in the last five years" (England) (search
  extract) — https://westminsterbrief.co.uk/archive/pq/HL16697
- Incidents are rare. In the 2023 annual report the EA received final information on "8
  incidents", spread across 2018, 2022 and 2023 (England) (search extract) —
  https://gov.uk/government/publications/reservoir-safety-post-incident-annual-reports/post-incident-reporting-for-reservoirs-annual-report-2023
- In 2023–2024 there were "no significant incidents resulting in loss of life and no total
  failures of a dam or reservoir" (England) (search extract) —
  https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/reservoir-safety-biennial-report/biennial-report-on-reservoir-safety-1-january-2023-to-31-december-2024
- HR Wallingford (2019): "the last fatal incident from failure of a reservoir in the UK was 94
  years ago in 1925" (UK) (search extract) — https://www.hrwallingford.com/news/are-our-reservoirs-safe

## 3. Current workarounds
- Undertakers record by hand. The record covers "(a) water levels and depth of water, including
  the flow of water over the waste weir or overflow; (b) leakages, settlements of walls or other
  works, and repairs" (England & Wales) (search extract) —
  https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1975/23/crossheading/monitoring-and-supervision/2016-04-01/data.html
- The record is commonly kept in ICE's "Prescribed Form of Record for a Large Raised Reservoir"
  book, which covers water levels and depth, volume, dam crest height, and leakages and repairs
  (UK) (search extract) —
  https://wildy.com/isbn/9780727757715/reservoirs-act-1975-prescribed-form-of-record-for-a-large-raised-reservoir-2nd-ed-paperback-ice-publishing
- Visual inspections: "The supervising engineer may direct the undertaker to carry out a visual
  inspection of the reservoir at specified intervals", and the undertaker "must notify the
  supervising engineer of each visual inspection that is carried out, and anything noticed in
  the course of it" (England & Wales) (search extract) —
  https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1975/23/crossheading/monitoring-and-supervision/2016-04-01/data.html
- GOV.UK guidance says owners should keep an inspection information pack that includes visual
  inspection survey records, condition surveys and photographs (England) (search extract) —
  https://gov.uk/guidance/reservoir-owner-and-operator-guidance-inspection-information-pack
- After the Ward's Reservoir owner failed to act, the EA "carried out its own weekly safety
  checks", which shows that weekly manual checking is the regulator's own fallback (England)
  (search extract) —
  https://watermagazine.co.uk/2024/11/06/company-prosecuted-for-failing-to-complete-reservoir-safety-works
- I found no labour-cost figure for an undertaker's routine readings.

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- Reservoirs Act 1975 s.11: "For every high-risk reservoir the undertakers shall keep a record
  in the prescribed form" of water levels, leakages, settlements and repairs. s.12: a qualified
  civil engineer (the supervising engineer) "shall be employed" (England & Wales) (search
  extract) —
  https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1975/23/crossheading/monitoring-and-supervision/2016-04-01/data.html
- Failing to carry out a directed visual inspection without reasonable excuse is an offence
  (England & Wales) (search extract) —
  https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1975/23/crossheading/monitoring-and-supervision/2016-04-01/data.html
- The Reservoirs Act 1975 (Capacity, Registration, Prescribed Forms, etc.) (England) Regulations
  2013 contain the prescribed form of record. They also require "the undertaker must notify the
  Agency within 28 days of the date on which an engineer ceases to be the supervising engineer"
  (England) (search extract) — https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/1677/made
- The EA's offence-response options list failing to appoint a supervising engineer (s.12(1)),
  with "warning, formal caution, prosecution" as possible responses (England) (search extract)
  — https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/offence-response-options-environment-agency/reservoir-offences
- Registration threshold: reservoirs over 25,000 m³ "must be regularly inspected by a qualified
  civil engineer – known as a panel engineer" (England) (search extract) —
  https://www.farminguk.com/news/farmers-urged-to-regularly-check-on-farm-reservoirs_53882.html
- The NFU says reservoirs under 25,000 m³ do not have to be registered, though health and safety
  rules and building regulations still apply (England) (search extract) —
  https://www.nfuonline.com/news/water-reservoirs-advice-and-support/
- Regulatory direction: the EA introduced "compliance action plans" with undertakers in 2024
  and a "risk mitigation matrix" to guide enforcement. Responses "may range from advice and
  guidance, formal warning or prosecution" (England) (search extract) —
  https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/reservoir-safety-biennial-report/biennial-report-on-reservoir-safety-1-january-2023-to-31-december-2024
- Toddbrook review (Balmforth, 2020): the cause was "a combination of poor design and
  intermittent maintenance" (UK) (search extract) —
  https://acenet.co.uk/news/industry/government-to-strengthen-reservoir-safety-following-toddbrook-reservoir-report
- The review made 22 recommendations, all accepted by government (England) (search extract) —
  https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/872769/toddbrook-reservoir-independent-review-reporta.pdf
- A post-Toddbrook advice note asks owners to keep long-term monitoring equipment maintained
  under the panel engineer's guidance (SEPA-hosted, Scotland) (search extract) —
  https://sepa.gov.uk/media/507896/advice-note-following-the-toddbrook-reservoir-incident.pdf
- Reform in progress. The Defra/EA reservoir safety reform programme covers "hazard
  management, ... supply and capacity of reservoir safety engineers, and ... regulation and
  enforcement". A new classification "will replace the current 'high risk' or 'not high-risk'
  classification" (England & Wales) (search extract) —
  https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/reservoir-safety-reform-programme
- The programme update says officials are reviewing proposals to "avoid disproportionate
  increases in costs to undertakers" (England & Wales) (search extract) —
  https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/defra-and-the-environment-agency-reservoir-safety-reform-programme-update/reservoir-safety-reform-programme-update
- In March 2026 trade press described a "shift away from the current one-size-fits-all
  regulatory model towards a more proportionate, risk-based framework" (England) (search
  extract) —
  https://www.watermagazine.co.uk/2026/03/26/reservoir-safety-reforms-gather-pace-as-government-sets-out-modernised-framework/
- A LexisNexis summary (9 April 2025) lists "regulation of smaller reservoirs, strengthened
  enforcement powers" and a move to the Environmental Permitting regime. I could not confirm
  this against a government source (England) (search extract) —
  https://www.lexisnexis.co.uk/legal/environmental-law/energy-renewables/renewable-energy
- Scotland and Northern Ireland have equivalent duties. Engineers direct the intervals for
  updating the record under Reservoirs (Scotland) Act 2011 s.51 (search extract) —
  https://www.legislation.gov.uk/asp/2011/9/section/51/2015-10-01/data.htm
- In NI, the explanatory notes to s.37 ("Recording of water levels etc. and record keeping")
  say engineers can direct how often records are updated (search extract) —
  https://www.legislation.gov.uk/nia/2015/8/notes/division/4/2/1/13
- I found no insurer requirement for reservoir monitoring.

## 5. Buyer & budget
- The legal duty sits with "the undertakers", meaning the owner or operator, so on a farm the
  budget holder is the farm business. A Strutt & Parker adviser notes that the inspecting
  engineer "will identify any safety measures that need to be carried out, and set a deadline"
  (England) (search extract) —
  https://rural.struttandparker.com/article/regular-checks-required-for-on-farm-reservoirs
- Engineer costs are small (NI in-house figure £163.72/yr; £2,255 per 10-year panel
  inspection), which suggests a low budget ceiling for any monitoring add-on (Northern Ireland,
  dated) (search extract) —
  https://www.niassembly.gov.uk/globalassets/documents/agriculture-and-rural-development/reservoirs/dard-re-18-february-meeting-reservoirs-bill.pdf
- Market size: about 34 new farm large raised reservoirs (LRRs) were built in a five-year
  period (England) (search extract) — https://westminsterbrief.co.uk/archive/pq/HL16697
- An EA-commissioned scoping report assumed "around 55% of Large Raised Reservoirs would be
  partially deregulated" under a risk-based regime. If that happens, part of the addressable
  base could lose its duties (England) (search extract) —
  https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6037784ee90e07055b44b735/14755_FD2701-SummaryReport.pdf

  https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1975/23/crossheading/monitoring-and-supervision/2016-04-01/data.html

  https://britishdams.org/assets/BDS%20Conference%202024%20-%20The%20Changing%20Role%20of%20the%20Supervising%20Engineer.pdf

## 7. Competitors & market gap
- Encardio-rite ESM-12S "Digital Seepage Monitoring System": drain water is led to a chamber
  where "the discharge is accurately measured with a 'V' notch and a water level measurement
  system" (low-range pressure sensor). No price found (global) (search extract) —
  https://www.encardio.com/uploads/manual/ESM-12S_Seepage_monitoring_system.pdf
- Encardio ESM-11V uses a vibrating-wire submersible sensor at the V-notch (global) (search
  extract) — https://www.encardio.com/uploads/manual/ESM-11V%20Seepage%20monitoring%20system.pdf
- Senceive (now with Sisgeo in Eddyfi Technologies) wireless geotechnical nodes are described as
  running "for over a decade on a single battery" (vendor claim), with InfraGuard movement
  alerts for dams (global) (search extract) —
  https://sisgeo.com/news/5-things-you-didnt-know-about-wireless-condition-monitoring/
- Generic farm level sensors are cheap. The Milesight EM500-SWL LoRaWAN submersible level sensor
  is listed at "$539.00" (Qantec) and "$615.00" (Field Solutions Group) on the NSW DPI AgTech
  directory (Australia, AUD) (search extract) —
  https://www.agtech.dpi.nsw.gov.au/products/milesight-submersible-sensor
- An AGTech360 LoRa tank/trough level sensor is listed at "$315.47" (Australia, AUD) (search
  extract) — https://www.agtech.dpi.nsw.gov.au/products/lora-water-sensor
- Lobaro LoRaWAN level sensor: "648.43 €" (Germany) (search extract) —
  https://iot-shop.de/en/shop/lob-3000701-lobaro-800089-lorawan-level-sensor-4981
- Gap: I found no UK product that combines farm-priced level and seepage sensing with automatic
  output in the prescribed-form record or a supervising-engineer report. Dam-instrument vendors
  (Encardio, Sisgeo/Senceive) target large dams. Ag sensors (Milesight, AGTech360) do not
  produce a compliance record.

  transmitted at hourly intervals, via satellite communications" replaced boat-based manual

  https://www.industrysearch.com.au/improving-safety-procedures-for-flow-measurement-of-v-notch-weirs/f/20404

## Researcher observations
- Strong: c4 has statutory record duties (s.11), supervising-engineer duties (s.12), visual
  inspection offences and an EA enforcement ladder. Supervising engineers make a ready-made
  specifier channel.
- Weak: the market is small (dozens of new farm LRRs per five years). Compliance spend is tiny
  (£100s per year), enforcement fines are small (£5,445 in the one case found), and fatal
  failures are extremely rare. Reform may deregulate around 55% of LRRs. Expect c1 and c2 to
  score low.
- Better buyer or niche for the same problem: sell to **supervising-engineer and panel-engineer
  consultancies** as a fleet tool that cuts visits across their reservoir portfolio, since
  engineer capacity is a named reform problem. Or target **non-farm small undertakers**
  (heritage lakes, angling clubs, estates, councils like Poynton TC). They appear in
  enforcement cases more often than farms.
