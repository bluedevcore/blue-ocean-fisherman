# R02-B-13 — Silage effluent tanks overflow after heavy rain and fresh clamping, and the effluent kills fish downstream

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Silage effluent has a very high biochemical oxygen demand. Most of it is produced in the first days after clamping, and rain getting into the clamp or tank adds volume. If the effluent tank is not emptied in time it can overflow to drains and watercourses and kill fish, which leads to regulator prosecution and clean-up costs.
- Who suffers / who pays: Dairy and beef farmers who make clamp silage (fines, costs, reputational damage) and downstream fisheries and anglers.
- Sensor concept: An ultrasonic level sensor or float switch in the effluent tank with a cellular uplink. Alerts at a high-level threshold, and logs level and emptying events as a compliance record.

## 1. Cost of incident
- England (Leicestershire): a farmer whose silage effluent discharge killed around 80 fish in a brook was fined £5,608 and ordered to pay £9,787.50 costs plus a £190 victim surcharge, over £15,000 in total. He also admitted failing to build an adequate silo (search extract) — https://gov.uk/government/news/leicestershire-farmer-pays-over-15-000-for-illegally-discharging-silage-effluent ; https://www.agriland.co.uk/farming-news/silage-effluent-farmer-fined-15k-for-causing-fish-kill/ ; https://www.thescottishfarmer.co.uk/news/20288317.farmer-fined-15-000-killing-80-fish-leicestershire/
- England (Dorset, 2021): a leaky silage clamp killed more than 400 fish in the River Divelish. The farmer was fined £1,300 and ordered to pay £6,079 costs (search extract) — https://www.gov.uk/government/news/dorset-farmer-s-leaky-silage-clamp-kills-fish-in-river ; https://www.southwestfarmer.co.uk/news/19426384.farmer-fined-silage-clamp-leak-kills-hundreds-fish/
- England (Nottinghamshire): two farming companies were fined a total of £28,800 after maize silage effluent reached a pond and caused a fish kill (search extract) — https://cedrec.com/r/news/28000-penalty-for-nottinghamshire-farming-companies-who-caused-serious-pollution-1558098519
- England (Devon, 2026, slurry not silage): a farmer was ordered to pay £4,778 after spills killed hundreds of fish (search extract) — https://www.gov.uk/government/news/devon-farmer-convicted-after-slurry-spill-kills-hundreds-of-fish
- Ireland and Northern Ireland: silage-effluent prosecutions give fines of about £750 to €2,000, plus costs, for example €3,500 total in one Irish fish-kill case (search extract) — https://www.agriland.ie/farming-news/galway-farmer-convicted-and-fined-for-polluting-river-with-silage-effluent ; https://www.agriland.ie/farming-news/fish-kill-farmer-convicted-and-ordered-to-pay-e3500/ ; https://www.agriland.ie/farming-news/court-ni-farmer-fined-for-water-pollution-offence/

## 3. Current workarounds
- Scotland: guidance says tank levels should be monitored "at all times but with greater frequency during the first 10 days following ensiling, and during wet weather", and suggests emptying when about two-thirds full (search extract) — https://www.gov.scot/Publications/2005/03/20613/51373
- Ireland: Teagasc advises checking tank levels daily while effluent is being produced, and says tanks must be monitored right through autumn and winter (search extract) — https://teagasc.ie/news--events/daily/collection-and-storage-of-silage-effluent/ ; https://teagasc.ie/wp-content/uploads/media/website/publications/2024/A-guide-to-storing-silage-and-managing-effluent-collection.pdf
- Practice advice: extend the clamp sheet beyond the effluent channels to keep rain out, keep channels clear, keep spare tank capacity, and use separate sealed effluent-only tanks (search extract) — https://www.agriland.ie/farming-news/advice-on-collection-and-storage-of-silage-effluent/ ; https://www.fas.scot/news/dealing-with-silage-effluent-and-its-impacts/
- UK trade press: Farmers Weekly items "Check wet clamps" and "Effluent warning" show manual checking has been the standard advice for decades. Both are old items (search extract) — https://www.fwi.co.uk/news/check-wet-clamps ; https://fwi.co.uk/news/effluent-warning
- I found no source pricing the labour or tanker time for manual checks and emptying.

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- England (GOV.UK, "Storing silage, slurry and agricultural fuel oil"): effluent tank minimum capacity should give at least 2 days' storage at peak flow, but "it's up to you to make sure you've got enough capacity to avoid the risk of pollution". Farms in above-average rainfall areas need more, and the EA must be notified at least 14 days before new or substantially altered storage is built (search extract) — https://www.gov.uk/storing-silage-slurry-and-agricultural-fuel-oil
- England (NFU explainer on the SSAFO rules) (search extract) — https://www.nfuonline.com/news/silage-slurry-and-agricultural-fuel-oil-storage-what-are-the-rules/
- Wales: capacity is set per cubic metre of silo, at 20 litres per m³ up to 1,500 m³ plus 6.7 litres per m³ above that, under the Water Resources (Control of Pollution) (Silage and Slurry) (Wales) Regulations 2010 (search extract) — https://www.gov.wales/node/62813 ; https://businesswales.gov.wales/farmingconnect/sites/farmingconnect/files/documents/cff_ssafo_1.pdf
- Northern Ireland: tanks built or substantially enlarged after 1 Dec 2003 must meet the rules; for silos of 1,500 m³ or more the minimum is 30 m³ plus 1 m³ per 150 m³ above 1,500 m³ (search extract) — https://netregs.org.uk/environmental-topics/materials-fuels-and-equipment/materials-and-equipment-for-agriculture-animal-care/silage-regulations
- England inspections: Farmers Weekly reports that SSAFO breaches made up 52% of notices issued as EA farm inspections intensify (a related summary frames 52% as a share of inspections, so the denominator is unclear) (search extract) — https://www.fwi.co.uk/news/environment/slurry-breaches-increase-as-farm-inspections-intensify
- England inspections: 51% of farm inspections in 2024/25 found some breach, across more than 4,500 visits to non-permitted farms (search extract) — https://www.farminguk.com/news/half-of-farms-fail-environment-agency-inspections-as-checks-ramp-up_68065.html
- England clamps: clamps built before 1991 are exempt from SSAFO, but 40% of post-1991 clamps inspected were non-compliant in 2023/24. The summary did not say which page carried this; it is likely the Vickers & Barrass summary (search extract) — https://www.vickersandbarrass.co.uk/environment-agency-report-for-2023-2024/
- EA blog: non-compliance is found in around 50% of farm inspections (2023) (search extract) — https://environmentagency.blog.gov.uk/2023/12/28/working-with-farmers-to-protect-our-future-land ; https://environmentagency.blog.gov.uk/2023/07/20/improving-environmental-performance-through-our-farming-inspections/
- Gap in the mandate: none of these rules requires level monitoring or a level log. The legal duty is to provide capacity and prevent pollution, so a log would be voluntary evidence of diligence, not a required record.

- A US patent describes a cloud-connected radar level node mounted above the maximum effluent level, with foam detection and two or more level targets (search extract) — https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11963487
- **Gap (explicit):** Generic farm tank monitors (iLevel at about £104 per tank per year; Titan; Pepperl+Fuchs WILSEN) already do level-plus-threshold alerts and could be put in a silage effluent tank today. I found no product marketed specifically for silage effluent tanks, or one that produces an SSAFO-oriented compliance log. The gap is packaging and positioning, not technology, and a cheap incumbent (iLevel) exists at the general farm-tank level. Critics should treat c7 as contested.

## 9. Recurring revenue
- Effluent is produced mainly at each cut (two to four cuts a year on many dairy farms) and must be watched through autumn and winter according to Teagasc. That supports an all-year alert service, but the volume of alerts is low between cuts (search extract) — https://teagasc.ie/wp-content/uploads/media/website/publications/2024/A-guide-to-storing-silage-and-managing-effluent-collection.pdf

## Researcher observations
- c4/c10 are well supported: SSAFO rules, about 50% EA inspection non-compliance, frequent fish-kill prosecutions, and the "most common cause of agricultural pollution in Scotland" line. c1 is moderate: fines plus costs of roughly £5k–£30k, which is low per farm, though the event is reputationally serious.
- c7 is the weak point. iLevel, Titan and Pepperl+Fuchs WILSEN already sell farm tank level monitoring with alerts. A new entrant would differentiate only through effluent-specific positioning and the compliance log.
- c2 is low: the risk window is a few weeks after each cut and in heavy rain, so most farms would see few real alerts a year.
- Better angles for the breeder: (a) sell through the Welsh NMIS or Farming Connect route, since Wales already ran an IoT slurry pilot and funds slurry/silage capital items; (b) bundle effluent, slurry-store level and fuel-tank alerts per farm; (c) target Ireland and NI, where Teagasc and DAERA push daily checks and fish kills are prosecuted often.
