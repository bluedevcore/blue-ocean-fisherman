# R02-D-31 — Hourly metal-detector tests at small food producers are back-filled on paper, and a detector that failed its test keeps running

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: HACCP plans and BRCGS/SALSA require metal detectors at a CCP to be challenged with ferrous, non-ferrous and stainless test pieces at set intervals, with rejects confirmed into a secure bin. In small factories tests can be missed and recorded later, and a detector that fails its test may keep running until someone notices.
- Who suffers / who pays: Line operators and technical managers (audit findings); the business pays for recalls, quarantined product and any delisting.
- Sensor concept: Retrofit box on the detector's dry-contact outputs (detect/reject/fault), NFC-tagged test wands read at the aperture, an IR beam in the reject bin and a reed switch on the bin lock. LoRaWAN/NB-IoT. Missed-test alerts and an audit-ready CCP record.

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- BRCGS clause 4.10.3.4 (quoted in a SafetyCulture template): tests use "separate test pieces containing ferrous metal, stainless steel and typically non-ferrous metal, unless the product is within a foil container where a ferrous-only test may be applicable" (search extract) — https://public-library.safetyculture.io/products/brcgs-v9-4-10-foreign-body-detection-and-removal-equipment-0p3gv2nqpgurso6q
- Secure storage: an IFSQN thread describes a BRCGS audit finding where test packs were left out between checks. The poster quotes clause 6.4.1 (CCP test equipment stored securely), and a reply suggests locking them in the reject box (forum, second-hand clause text) (search extract) — https://ifsqn.com/forum/index.php/topic/48207-does-the-brcgs-standard-require-metal-detection-test-packs-to-be-locked-up-when-not-in-use/?ampmode=1

## 7. Competitors & market gap
- Fortress Halo Automatic Testing System: generates a test signal without a physical test piece, checks the reject system, and logs every test (USB export to Contact Reporter). Programmable schedules per product. Fortress says it "can also be retrofitted to many existing systems, though it is typically installed at the factory". Payback for a new detector with Halo is "typically less than 12 months" (vendor claim). No price found (search extract) — https://fortresstechnology.com/products/halo-automatic-testing-system/ ; https://provisioneronline.com/articles/107137-fortress-places-halo-around-food-metal-detectors

- Mettler-Toledo M30 R-Series SENSE update adds guided performance tests, test-due notifications and scheduling, integrated with ProdX. Existing M30 R-Series units can be updated (vendor/trade press) (search extract) — https://www.foodbusinessmea.com/mettler-toledo-upgrades-m30-r-series-metal-detection-systems-to-further-automate-process/ ; https://packagingscotland.com/2023/04/mettler-toledo-upgrades-software-for-metal-detection-systems

- Software-only: SafetyCore (£150 a month per site), FoodDocs, FoodReady CCP monitoring, iFactory/Oxmaint. These digitise the checklist and send reminders but cannot prove a test piece passed through (search extract) — https://safetycore-marketing.pages.dev/pricing ; https://foodready.ai/app/ccp-monitoring-software/ ; https://ifactoryapp.com/article/food-plant-CCP-monitoring-digital
- GAP (explicit): the idea's claim that "digital self-test systems come only with new premium detectors" is partly contradicted. Fortress Halo is retrofittable to many existing (presumably Fortress) systems, and Mettler-Toledo offers missed-test SMS alerts on Profile-era units and a SENSE update for M30 R-Series. What remains is a brand-agnostic retrofit for older or mixed-brand detectors (Loma, older Safeline, Cintex, Ceia etc.) that proves the test happened and the reject reached the bin, at SME price points. Checklist apps at about £150 a month per site are the cheap incumbents for the record-keeping part. No vendor offering a cross-brand, sensor-verified retrofit was found.

## Researcher observations
- Strong compliance pull (BRCGS 4.10.3.4, CCP records, annual audits) and real recall-cost data (Lockton £38k SME estimate; 2026 survey about £550k average).
- c7 risk: OEMs already offer automatic testing and missed-test alerts, retrofittable within their own brands (Fortress Halo, Mettler-Toledo SENSE/Profile alerts). Cheap checklist apps cover reminders. The defensible gap is a cross-brand retrofit at SME price points.
- A better channel may be independent metal-detector service and validation companies. They visit every detector annually regardless of brand, and could sell the retrofit alongside validation.
- Variant to consider: the same retrofit on checkweighers and X-ray units, which are also CCPs with periodic challenge tests.
