# R02-C-23 — Infrared and red aviation obstruction lights on mid-size wind turbines and met masts fail unnoticed, breaching planning and CAA/MOD lighting conditions

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Many onshore turbines and met masts must carry aviation obstruction lighting. Tall turbines need CAA red lights, and planning conditions often require MOD infrared (IR) lights on turbines of 50 m or more. The operator must repair a failed light promptly, and in some regimes must have a NOTAM issued. At small or single-turbine sites the lights may not be remotely monitored, so a failure can go unnoticed for days.
- Who suffers / who pays: The owner or operator of the turbine or mast carries the planning-condition and aviation-rule liability. Low-flying military, air-ambulance and general-aviation pilots carry the safety risk.
- Sensor concept: A photodiode sensitive to visible and IR light, aimed at each fixture, plus a current clamp on the lighting circuit. It would send an LTE-M or LoRaWAN "light out" alert at night and keep an availability log as evidence of compliance.

- No UK fine, enforcement notice or penalty for a failed aviation light on a turbine was found (see "Searched for but not found"). The money at stake is indirect: breach of a planning condition, or loss of consent to operate.

In Perth, a council found that an Aviva-owned turbine had breached a planning condition over shadow flicker after a resident complained, which led to shutdowns. ## 3. Current workarounds
- Built-in redundancy: the search summary of CAP 764 material says standard onshore lighting has a primary 2,000 cd red light on the nacelle plus a second light in case the operating light fails. The summary did not tie this to one URL; candidates are the CAA CAP 764 PDF and a wind-turbine.com summary (UK) (search extract) — https://caa.co.uk/publication/download/14561 ; https://en.wind-turbine.com/magazine/news-from-the-market/250606/cap-764-and-dap-policy-124-regulatory-framework-for-wind-turbine-lighting-in-the-uk.html
- OEM monitoring where specified: Avlite says its obstruction lights are available with GSM cell-phone monitoring. This tracks alarm status, LED status, intensity, flash code and source voltage, and can send SMS or e-mail alerts on low voltage or light failure. On some models it is optional and only for the VDC variant (vendor claim) (search extract) — https://www.avlite.com/?p=562 ; https://www.avlite.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/08/AVLITE_CASA_LIOL_AV-OL-CL_Spec_E1_V1_HR.pdf
- O&M contractor remote monitoring: Full Circle (formerly EWT's service arm) has taken on single-turbine full-scope maintenance, for example a single 2.05 MW Senvion MM92 in Lancashire. Its scope includes "24/7/365 remote monitoring". The summary did not tie this to one article; candidates are below (UK) (search extract) — https://www.energyglobal.com/wind/05122025/full-circle-secures-two-operations-and-maintenance-contracts-in-uk/amp/ ; https://www.energyglobal.com/wind/19122022/full-circle-signs-long-term-service-agreement-for-enercon-turbines-in-the-uk/
- No source described or costed manual night-time light checks at small sites. MOD lights are infrared, so a person checking by eye at night cannot easily confirm an IR light is working. This is researcher inference, not sourced.

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- CAA (UK, 2017 policy statement): onshore turbines with a blade-tip height of 150 m AGL or more must carry a medium-intensity red light. "The person in charge" must repair or replace a failed light "as soon as practicable". Turbines under 150 m are not routinely lit for civil aviation unless stakeholders ask or a planning condition requires it (search extract) — https://caa.co.uk/publication/download/16178
- CAP 764 (CAA policy and guidelines on wind turbines): the search summary says Edition 7 is dated 17 December 2025, adds aircraft-detection lighting and aligns with IEC TS 61400-29:2023. It says "wind farm operators are expected to monitor the serviceability of obstacle lighting". It also cites a NOTAM where an outage is expected to exceed 12 hours ("one source"). The summary did not tie each statement to one URL (UK) (search extract) — https://caa.co.uk/publication/download/14561 ; https://consultations.caa.co.uk/policy-development/proposed-revision-to-cap-764-caa-policy-and-guidel/supporting_documents/Draft%20CAP764%20Ed7%20Red%20Underline.pdf ; https://en.wind-turbine.com/magazine/news-from-the-market/250606/cap-764-and-dap-policy-124-regulatory-framework-for-wind-turbine-lighting-in-the-uk.html
- MOD planning conditions (UK, mid-size turbines): MOD consultation responses to Copeland (Cumbria) ask for at least 25 cd visible or IR lighting, or IR as a minimum. The suggested condition requires an approved aviation lighting scheme, with the lights to "remain operational for the lifetime of the development". One 2024 letter says a 76 m tip-height turbine is above the 50 m threshold that requires a lighting scheme. A 2026 discharge letter accepts an MB17UK-IR light. The summary did not tie each point to one letter; all are Copeland MOD letters (search extract) — https://www.copeland.gov.uk/sites/default/files/planning-applications/20240404_mod_response_letter.pdf ; https://www.copeland.gov.uk/sites/default/files/planning-applications/20241108_mod_response_letter.pdf ; https://www.copeland.gov.uk/sites/default/files/planning-applications/20260427_mod_response_0.pdf ; https://www.copeland.gov.uk/sites/default/files/planning-applications/condition_11_and_12_mod_letter.pdf
- The same MOD pattern appears in Scottish planning files: Comhairle nan Eilean Siar, Dumfries & Galloway (MoD comments of 25.11.2024 and 01.07.2025) and Scottish Borders (search extract) — https://www.cne-siar.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2024-10/MOD.pdf ; https://dumfriesgalloway.moderngov.co.uk/documents/s71582/15.%20242164ful%20MoD%2025.11.2024.pdf ; https://scottishborders.moderngov.co.uk/documents/s4684/5e%20Additional%20representation%20from%20MOD.pdf
- US (FAA): FAA determinations of no hazard say a failure or malfunction lasting more than 30 minutes that affects a top or flashing obstruction light should be reported immediately so a NOTAM can be issued (search extract) — https://puc.sd.gov/commission/dockets/electric/2019/el19-007/FAADONH.pdf
- FAA NOTAM guidance treats any failure of a top or flashing light as a NOTAM condition, and wind-farm NOTAMs use "LGT U/S" (US) (search extract) — https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/atpubs/notam_html/chap5_section_2.html
- Canada: per the Kingstonist report, the operator must report an outage to Transport Canada, which notifies NAV CANADA, and a NOTAM must be issued as soon as possible (search extract) — https://www.kingstonist.com/?p=124218
- Insurance: no insurer requirement for aviation-light monitoring was found.

## 5. Buyer & budget
- The CAA puts the duty to repair on "the person in charge" of the turbine. The MOD condition binds the developer or operator for the life of the development. Liability therefore sits with the owner or asset manager (UK) (search extract) — https://caa.co.uk/publication/download/16178 ; https://www.copeland.gov.uk/sites/default/files/planning-applications/condition_11_and_12_mod_letter.pdf

## Researcher observations
- Strong: the obligation is real and clearly documented. CAA says to repair as soon as practicable, CAP 764 expects operators to monitor serviceability, and MOD conditions require lights to stay operational "for the lifetime of the development". The 50 m MOD trigger means mid-size single turbines (e.g. 76 m tip) are caught, not just large wind farms.
- Weak: there is no money evidence (no fines, no claims) and no frequency data. The incumbents are light OEMs with GSM monitoring and full-scope O&M contractors with 24/7 remote monitoring. A ~£90 LoRaWAN current clamp is a cheap DIY substitute.
- A better niche for the same problem may be non-turbine structures under the same MOD 50 m rule: met masts, telecom masts and cranes. These are less likely to have SCADA. The breeder could also look at the ADLS (aircraft-detection lighting) fail-safe monitoring requirement in CAP 764 Ed. 7. Another option is selling through O&M firms like Full Circle as an independent compliance log, not to owners directly.
