## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Hay (and to a lesser extent straw/haylage) baled above a safe moisture level can self-heat in storage over the first weeks and ignite. Fire services and insurers advise regular temperature probing of stacks, which is manual. Barn fires destroy buildings, fodder and machinery.
- Who suffers / who pays: Livestock and arable farmers, forage merchants and contractors; insurers pay claims; fire services attend.
- Sensor concept: Long thermistor probe spikes left in the stack, reporting over battery LoRaWAN or LTE-M; alerts on rising temperature; keeps a post-harvest temperature record.

## 2. Frequency
- Required checking frequency during the risk window is daily or more: Dorset & Wiltshire FRS schedule — from 65°C "the beginning of the danger zone… check temperature daily", readings every four hours from 70°C, call 999 at 100°C (UK) — https://www.dwfire.org.uk/?p=43324 (search extract)
- Victoria CFA: below 50°C check daily; above 70°C check every 2–4 hours (Australia) — https://www.cfa.vic.gov.au/hay (search extract)
- Alberta Agriculture: at 150°F (65.5°C) "check the temperature everyday"; above 160°F "Check temperature every few hours. Notify the fire department" (Canada) — https://www1.agric.gov.ab.ca/$department/deptdocs.nsf/all/faq9923 (search extract)
- University of Kentucky extension: at 150°F move bales and "check the temperature at least two to three times per day" (US; page attribution among several UKY results inferred) — https://news.ca.uky.edu/article/temperature-checking-hay-can-keep-fires-bay (search extract)
- Risk window: hay fires generally occur within about six weeks of baling (fire-service/extension guidance; Agriland piece "Hay fires: Farmers urged to 'keep an eye' on freshly baled hay") — https://www.agriland.co.uk/farming-news/hay-fires-farmers-urged-to-keep-an-eye-on-freshly-baled-hay/ (search extract; exact page for the six-week figure inferred)
- Seasonal incidence: Mid and West Wales FRS linked a rise in barn fires to early warm, dry weather in May–June giving earlier haymaking, and cited a UK annual average of around 1,600 farm buildings destroyed by fire (via FarmingUK "Farmers warned to lower risk of barn fires after uptick in incidents"; attribution inferred) — https://www.farminguk.com/news/farmers-warned-to-lower-risk-of-barn-fires-after-uptick-in-incidents_62895.html (search extract); Harrogate crews describe hay spontaneous combustion as a common cause of summer barn fires (The Stray Ferret) — https://thestrayferret.co.uk/tag/straw/ (search extract)
- Fire-service incident record: Devon & Somerset FRS attributed a July 2021 agricultural property fire (TA18 8) to self-heating bales, causing 20% structural damage to the barn — https://dsfire.gov.uk/incidents/property-fire-45 (search extract)
- **Gap:** no national count of self-heating hay fires per year; per-farm incidence is low (a fire is rare per farm), so the recurring pain is the *probing task*, which is daily for a few weeks a year — seasonal, not year-round.

## 3. Current workarounds
- Manual probing: build a probe from steel pipe or conduit (or buy one), lower a thermometer on a string and leave it about 10 minutes, at several locations and depths, wettest hay first (Dorset & Wiltshire FRS "Safe storage of hay" and extension guidance) — https://www.dwfire.org.uk/business-fire-safety/fire-safety-and-the-law/fire-risks-on-farms/safe-storage-of-hay/ (search extract)
- NFU Mutual/North Devon Gazette: the best way to check is to insert an extendable temperature probe into the middle of the bales — https://www.northdevongazette.co.uk/news/warning-to-help-farmers-avoid-barn-fires-7442803 (search extract)
- Fire services issue seasonal reminders: "Fire service urges farmers to check hay bales following fires in county" (Cumbria FRS press release) — https://www.cumbriafire.gov.uk/news/fire-service-urges-farmers-check-hay-bales-following-fires-county-0 (search extract); Shropshire FRS farm fire prevention advice — https://shropshirefire.gov.uk/node/63437 (search extract)
- Moisture control at baling: hay preservative applicators driven by on-baler moisture sensors (Harvest Tec 700 Series reads crop moisture across the bale and applies preservative accordingly) — https://harvesttec.com/harvest-tec-uk-eu (search extract); handheld moisture testers with long probes are sold through Sparex — https://euro4gb-m2.sparex.com/hay-moisture-tester-probe-only-28719.html (search extract)
- **Gap:** no source quantified the labour time for daily probing (minutes per stack per day).

## Researcher observations
- Strongest evidence: insurers are already engaged — a UK mutual (Cornish Mutual) is running a hay-fire prevention trial with members, and Nationwide (US) bundles Haytech at $99/month. That is a ready channel and payer, but also shows Haytech is the incumbent.
- Weaknesses: per-farm incidents are rare (c2 relies on a seasonal daily probing task for ~2–6 weeks/year), no £-per-claim figure was found for self-heating fires specifically, and there is no compliance requirement.
- Possible differentiation for the breeder: Haytech's GPRS kit uses 2G; an LTE-M/LoRaWAN probe sold via mutual insurers (NFU Mutual, Cornish Mutual) or via baler-sensor dealers (Harvest Tec network, Carr's Billington) could target large hay merchants and horse-hay producers, who store larger volumes over longer periods.
