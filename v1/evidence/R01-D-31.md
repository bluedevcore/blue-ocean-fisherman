## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Food sites audited to BRCGS and retailer codes are said to increasingly expect continuous electronic rodent monitoring. Large pest-control firms offer their own connected systems; independent contractors may have no equivalent and may lose food-site contracts. Routine visits still involve opening every station.
- Who suffers / who pays: Independent pest-control firms (the buyer and reseller); food-site technical managers, who pay for the contract.
- Sensor concept: Retrofit activity and lid/trigger sensor for standard bait/monitoring stations and snap traps, LoRaWAN gateway with LTE-M backhaul (no client IT), white-label portal for independents to resell, audit-ready trend reports.

## 3. Current workarounds
- Routine technician visits (8–24 a year) checking every device, plus biologist/hygiene audit visits — https://ppconline.org/sector/accreditation-standards-in-focus-pest-risk-assessments-ppc (search extract)
- BRCGS documentation expected on 4.14: a site map of device locations, contracts or competency records, and monitoring logs for all devices (vendor summary, Rentokil Poland) — https://www.rentokil.com/pl/blog/informacje-branza-ddd/pest-management-standardy-brc-ifs-fssc-aib (search extract)
- Off-the-shelf audit checklists exist for BRCGS v9 4.14 (SafetyCulture library) — https://public-library.safetyculture.io/products/brcgs-v9-4-14-pest-management-5iqnc1adysrutdop (search extract)
- BPCA advises food clients to ask for prices per technician or biologist visit rather than per annum, because visit frequency changes with the risk assessment (UK) — https://bpca.org.uk/news-and-blog/Page-12/a-pest-control-checklist-for-food-clients/276378 (search extract)

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure

- Issue 9 changed "survey" to "assessment" in the 4.14 annual pest review (consultancy summary) — https://ascfoodsafety.com/brcgs-for-food-safety-changes-site-standards/ (search extract)

- Retailer pressure (example, not a mandate): Tesco deployed Rentokil PestConnect across stores and warehouses and reported a **40% cut in rodenticide use** (UK, April 2021) — https://retailtechnology.co.uk/news/7449/tesco-turns-to-tech-for-pest-control/ ; https://thegrocer.co.uk/news/rat-burglar-alarms-cut-harmful-rodenticide-use-across-tesco-estate-by-40/655458.article (search extract)

## Researcher observations
- Strong: food-site pest control is regular, audited (BRCGS 4.14, 8–24 visits/yr) and enforced (prosecutions, £12k–£60k fines); the UK industry is mostly SMEs; subscriptions are normal.
- Weak: the "independents can't offer digital" gap appears largely filled — Syngenta SecureChoice retrofits any existing bait station and is promoted via NPTA/BPCA; Pelsis/Edialux (TrapMe, EverSmart) and Pestfix (TrapMe Fenrir £94.64, TrapSensor) sell to independents. No audit requirement for digital monitoring was found, and BPCA urges caution on replacing visits.
- Unverified leads (search summaries, not tied to a URL): Bayer Digital Pest Management offered rental per trap per month including two gateways, portal, app and data (2022); Anticimex entered the UK by buying Pestokill, whose MD said the market has one national player and is otherwise small local firms.
- Better angle for the breeder: a **low-cost, vendor-neutral portal** that aggregates several brands' sensors for an independent, or a much cheaper retrofit sold through distributors with price transparency (none of the incumbents publish subscription prices).
