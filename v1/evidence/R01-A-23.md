## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Commercial wood-pellet boilers (about 50–500 kW) draw from a silo or bunker that often has no continuous level reading. When the store empties, the boiler stops on a fuel fault, the building loses heat, and the site may pay for an urgent delivery and an engineer restart. Bulk deliveries normally need several days' notice.
- Who suffers / who pays: Occupants of schools, care homes, rural estates and holiday parks; site caretakers; biomass maintenance contractors. The site owner or estates/facilities budget pays.
- Sensor concept: A dust-tolerant time-of-flight or ultrasonic level sensor at the top of the store plus a boiler status contact, over LTE-M. It forecasts days-to-empty, alerts on low level and can push the forecast to the pellet supplier.

## 2. Frequency
- In the Highlands, one primary school's biomass boiler problems led to **828 hours** lost to breakdowns (Press and Journal, "Schools miss out on teaching time due to broken down boilers"; Scotland). The extract attributes this to breakdowns generally, not specifically to fuel run-outs — https://www.pressandjournal.co.uk/fp/news/inverness/413078/biomass-off-the-boil-across-highlands/ (search extract)
- Highland Council briefing note (6 March 2018): tenants with biomass heating were "experiencing issues with wood pellet supply", particularly bagged pellets; the note links this to the UK being a net importer and says the council would review options "for improving resilience through direct supply by the Council" (Scotland) — https://www.highland.gov.uk/membersintranet/download/downloads/id/148/briefing_note_-_biomass_wood_pellet_supply_issues.pdf (search extract)
- A school in Macomer, Sardinia closed because there was no fuel for the heating boiler after a switch from wood chip to pellets; the report blamed supply problems linked to the war in Ukraine (Italy) — https://www.unionesarda.it/en/sardinia/no-heating-school-closed-in-macomer-the-fault-of-the-war-in-ukraine-q52gmfw7 (search extract)
- Pellet boilers have a dedicated "lack of fuel in the tank" error state (Tetan KP-12S manual, section 9.12, fix: add fuel) — shows a run-out is a designed-for fault condition, not a rare edge case — https://www.manualslib.com/manual/1101624/Tetan-Kp-12s.html?page=68 (search extract)
- **Gap:** no statistic was found for how often commercial pellet stores run empty per site per year. The recurring task that a sensor would replace (weekly visual checks of the store) is described in the idea but was not sourced.

## 3. Current workarounds
- Supplier-side level alarms already exist: Balcas Energy (formerly Forever Fuels) sells the DELOX SiCon level system for pellet stores; a high alarm (typically 90%) "warns delivery-drivers when the store is nearly full" and a low alarm (typically 10%) "reminds you to re-order" (UK/Ireland; vendor product listing) — https://www.environmental-expert.com/products/pellet-level-sensors-222203 (search extract)
- Point (full/empty) sensors are the cheap option: a 2006 bulk-handling forum post says continuous measurement such as radar "is very expensive" while a rotating paddle switch is a cheaper full/empty indicator (old source) — https://www.bulk-online.com/en/node/63240 (search extract)
- Back-up fuel is another workaround: one US school's pellet system provides 100% of the winter heat load, with a fuel-oil boiler kept as backup (US, Dovetail Partners case study) — https://mff.forest.mtu.edu/Biomass/BiomassDocs/Dovetail-BirchGrove.pdf (search extract)
- Public bodies tender maintenance and pellet supply together, e.g. "Maintenance of Biomass Boilers & Supply & Delivery of Biomass Pellets" (UK public tender, 2022) — https://bidstats.uk/tenders/2022/W26/777846914 (search extract); separate pellet-supply tender "Biomass Wood Pellets for Boiler System" (UK, 2022) — https://bidstats.uk/tenders/2022/W48/787742903 (search extract)
- When a pellet boiler stops, owners report restart difficulty: a Woodpecker pellet boiler owner reported it "fails to ignite" because it was "not feeding pellets in on restart" (US forum, hearth.com) — https://www.hearth.com/talk/goto/post?id=2170053 (search extract)
- **Gap:** no source quantified caretaker time spent checking stores, or the frequency of manual dip checks.

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- Ofgem (Non-Domestic RHI): every participant using biomass must keep current fuel-use records; "Accurate biomass fuel records must be available at all times as they are needed to pass the audit", and payments may be lost if records are inaccurate (UK) — https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/guidance/biomass-fuel-records-record-it-keep-it-dont-lose-it (search extract)
- Domestic RHI biomass sustainability info sheet: non-compliance can lead to withheld payments or further sanctions (UK, Feb 2015) — https://Www.Gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/403105/Biomass_Sustainability_Requirements_-_Info_Sheet_-_Domestic_RHI_Feb_15_Final.pdf (search extract)
- Biomass Suppliers List (BSL) audits suppliers and participants; a Gemserv/BSL presentation reported average non-compliances per audit of 1.21 for self-suppliers and 3.29 for commercial suppliers (Jan–May 2020), and major non-compliances typically lead to revocation of BSL authorisation (UK) — https://r-e-a.net/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/S3-WH2020-Imogen-Jamie-Gemserv-and-Biomass-Suppliers-List-Update-from-BSL.pdf (search extract); BSL guidance on audit findings — https://biomass-suppliers-list.service.gov.uk/?p=158244 (search extract)
- Ofgem published updated Sustainability Self-Reporting Guidance (7 Oct 2024) for RHI biomass participants (UK) — https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2024-10/Sustainability_Self-Reporting_Guidance_Final_07_Oct_2024.pdf (search extract)
- Trade press: "UK: are your RHI records audit worthy?" (HortiDaily) — https://www.hortidaily.com/article/9204635/uk-are-your-rhi-records-audit-worthy/ (search extract)
- **Important caveat:** these RHI duties concern fuel *sustainability and quantity* records, not store *level*. No regulation, insurer condition or care-home rule was found that requires monitoring of pellet-store level. Weak link to the sensor's core job.

## 6. Distribution channel
- **Pellet suppliers already sell level monitoring with delivery:** Scandbio (Sweden) offers business customers a "level watcher" — ultrasonic or radar sensors feed a web portal; Scandbio "takes care of ordering, requests and delivery", with a shared calendar for delivery notes and planned shutdowns — https://www.scandbio.com/en/business-customer/level-sensor/ (search extract)
- Balcas Energy (UK/Ireland pellet producer, formerly Forever Fuels) sells pellet-store level sensors to its customers (see section 3) — https://www.environmental-expert.com/products/pellet-level-sensors-222203 (search extract)

## Researcher observations
- The pain is real but the evidence is thin: no sourced UK run-out incident or cost. RHI audit pressure is about fuel records, not store level, so c4 is weak.
- The strongest finding is the channel: pellet suppliers (Balcas in UK/IE, Scandbio in SE) already sell or bundle level monitoring to drive vendor-managed delivery. That suggests the real buyer is the **pellet supplier** (route optimisation, fewer emergency runs) rather than the site — but it also means incumbents already occupy the channel.
- Many council school systems run on wood chip, not pellets, shrinking the "schools" segment; the same problem in chip stores (harder to measure) or in domestic/estate pellet systems may be the larger niche.
- ATEX/DSEAR risk for an electronic sensor inside a pellet-dust headspace should be checked before scoring c8 high.
