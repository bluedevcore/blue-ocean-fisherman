# R02-D-02 — Independent jewellers can't prove the block-policy 'stock in safe out of hours' warranty after a break-in

> Research conditions (2026-11-05): WebFetch is blocked by this environment's network policy, so no page was
> fetched. Every bullet below rests on WebSearch result titles, snippets and summaries and is marked
> "(search extract)". Where a search summary drew on several pages without saying which one carried a line,
> the bullet says so. 8 queries were run for this idea (the budget cap).

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Jewellers' block policies commonly require display and window stock to be kept in a safe or strongroom outside business hours, and the alarm to be set. After a burglary or smash-and-grab, compliance with these conditions may decide whether a claim is paid. The idea assumes there is no time-stamped record that trays left the window and that the safe was shut.
- Who suffers / who pays: Owners of independent jewellers, watch dealers and pawnbrokers (the payer), and their staff who close up.
- Sensor concept: Pressure pads under window and display trays, a reed and bolt-throw contact on the safe door, and a light sensor. An LTE-M uplink with battery backup alerts if trays or the safe remain unsecured after closing time and issues a nightly "stock secured" record for the broker or insurer.

## 7. Competitors & market gap
- The incumbent is the intruder alarm. Safe door contacts, safe vibration/seismic sensors and lock-switch (bolt-throw) monitoring are standard alarm zones on jewellers' safes. With a monitored alarm, the receiving centre already logs set/unset times and zone states (search extract) — https://forums.cabling-design.com/security-physical/security-suggestions-for-jewelry-store-17796-.htm ; https://dach.assaabloy.com/ch/en/product-catalogue/effeff-t172607-microswitch-for-alarm-function
- **GAP (explicit):** The "safe shut and bolted" half of the concept largely overlaps with what an alarm-installed safe contact and lock switch already record. The apparent gap is narrower: evidence that **window and display trays are empty** at closing, plus a broker-facing nightly certificate. No product doing tray-presence monitoring for jewellers was found. However, only one query targeted competitors, so absence is not proven.
- Not found: prices for alarm-company add-on safe zones or monitoring packages for jewellers.

## Researcher observations
- The strongest evidence is that out-of-hours safe-custody terms are a real feature of jewellers' block wordings, with exclusions or sub-limits for stock left out (India and Malaysia wordings; US guidance on application answers becoming warranties). UK-specific wording was not found.
- The weakest point is competitive overlap. Safe door contacts and lock-switch monitoring are already standard alarm zones, so the monitored alarm log arguably already proves "safe shut". The novel part is tray-in-window detection.
- Much recent UK loss is daytime robbery and ram raids, where an overnight warranty record does not help. The case for value rests on the overnight-burglary subset, which was not quantified.
- Better channel hypothesis: the specialist broker (TH March and similar) could bundle this as a risk-management service, or the NSI alarm installer could add tray zones to the existing panel. Both would beat selling a standalone LTE-M box to the owner.
