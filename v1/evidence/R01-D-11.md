# R01-D-11 — Pool dosing-chemical drum runs dry overnight — disinfectant drops and the pool can't open in the morning

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Leisure centre, hotel and school pools dose sodium hypochlorite and acid from drums, IBCs or bulk tanks. If a container runs dry, or dosing stops, the disinfectant residual falls and the pool may fail its pre-opening test and close or delay opening, with lost sessions and lesson refunds. Staff check container levels by eye on plant-room rounds, and suppliers deliver on schedules.
- Who suffers / who pays: Duty managers and pool plant operators handle it; swimmers and swim schools lose sessions. The operator (leisure trust, council, hotel, school) pays lost income and emergency deliveries.
- Sensor concept: A weighing platform under drums/IBCs (or ultrasonic level in an IBC) plus dosing-pump pulse/current monitoring, over LTE-M. Low-stock and "dosing stopped" alerts, consumption trends, and a feed to the supplier for auto-replenishment.

## Researcher observations
- Weak on c1/c2: the specific failure (container runs dry overnight) has no sourced incident. Pool closures from chemical supply and dosing faults are real, and the 2022 shortage was widespread, but it was a national supply event that a level sensor would not fix.
- Competitively crowded: modern pool dosing controllers (ProMinent, SEKO, Dinotec, Fluidra, Grundfos) already offer suction-lance level sensing and cloud alarms. What remains is retrofitting older plant rooms and multi-site visibility.
- The better buyer is the chemical distributor (Brenntag/Monarch, Univar, Pool Pro, Woburn), as a vendor-managed-inventory tool: they already run telemetry in other sectors and gain from route optimisation. Multi-site leisure trusts are a secondary buyer.
- A "dosing stopped" alarm (pump current/pulse) may matter more than stock level, given the Southampton overnight dosing-error closure and the US chlorine-gas incident.
