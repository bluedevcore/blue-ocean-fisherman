## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Holiday parks and glamping sites with many lodge hot tubs must control Legionella and other pathogens under HSE guidance HSG282, which covers hot tubs hired out to successive guests. Staff test disinfectant and pH by hand, typically at least daily and at changeovers, and keep paper logs that may be incomplete. Hot-tub-linked illness can lead to closures, enforcement, prosecution and compensation claims.
- Who suffers / who pays: Guests suffer illness. Housekeeping and hot-tub staff do the testing. The park operator (operations or maintenance manager) pays for labour, chemicals and any liability.
- Sensor concept: A floating or inline probe pod (ORP for disinfectant, pH, temperature) with replaceable probes, over LTE-M. It flags tubs out of range before guests arrive, builds the HSG282 log per tub, alerts on heater or pump failure, and offers a dashboard for service contractors.

## Researcher observations
- Strong: frequency (twice-daily testing written into park job adverts), regulation (HSG282 plus council and Crown Office enforcement) and serious incident costs (£54k, £120k, £1m fines; lodge outbreaks in Wales and Devon).
- Main risk is buildability and opex. ORP/pH probes drift, need calibration every 2–6 months and last about 9–24 months, at $30–$580 each. With dozens of tubs per park, probe upkeep could exceed the labour saved. The product probably still needs a weekly manual check to satisfy HSG282 and staff.
- Competitive signal: Park Leisure Solutions already pitches real-time monitoring and dosing to holiday parks, and spa brands embed connectivity. The opening is brand-agnostic and fleet-level, with HSG282 log output.
- A better buyer may be the hot-tub service contractors who look after many holiday-let tubs for remote owners. They bear the visit cost, and remote alerts let them skip mid-stay visits (cf. the £120+VAT changeover package).
