# R02-C-03 — Care homes can't evidence that soiled and infected linen reached thermal-disinfection temperature (65°C/10 min or 71°C/3 min)

> Research note: WebFetch is blocked in this environment, so no page was opened in full. Every
> bullet below relies on the title and extract shown in WebSearch results and is marked
> "(search extract)". 8 queries were run (the per-idea budget). Geography is England/UK unless
> stated. Where a search summary drew on several results and I cannot tell which URL holds a
> given quote, the bullet says "attribution approximate".

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Guidance for health and social care (HTM 01-04) expects foul and infected linen to be
  thermally disinfected (commonly given as 65°C for 10 minutes or 71°C for 3 minutes, with
  longer times quoted when mixing time is included). Care-home staff may pick the wrong
  programme, overload the machine, or use domestic-grade machines, and most homes have no
  per-cycle record that the temperature was reached.
- Who suffers / who pays: Residents (infection risk), laundry assistants, the infection-
  prevention lead; the provider pays for outbreaks (admissions closures) and faces inspection
  findings.
- Sensor concept: Clamp-on surface thermistor on the outer tub or drain plus a current clamp on
  the heater; firmware estimates time above 65/71°C per cycle and flags failures; LoRaWAN/LTE-M;
  monthly infection-control audit report. It gives an indicative reading, not a validated one.

## 1. Cost of incident
- Hospital analogue (England): gastroenteritis outbreaks in Avon in 2002–2003 cost about
  £635,000 per 1,000 beds in lost bed-days and staff absence; restricting admissions caused
  5,443 lost bed-days (search extract) —
  https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/eid/article/10/10/03-0941_article
- Same Avon study in the LSHTM repository (search extract) —
  https://researchonline.lshtm.ac.uk/9795
- Scottish medicine-of-the-elderly hospital: one prolonged norovirus outbreak was costed at
  £341,534 (lost bed-days, staff absence, outbreak management) (search extract) —
  https://journalclub.wustl.edu/2016/03/24/lessons-learned-from-a-prolonged-and-costly-norovirus-outbreak-at-a-scottish-medicine-of-the-elderly-hospital-case-study/
- News: "Staggering cost of norovirus as five nursing homes closed and 87 bed days lost" (headline
  only in extract) (search extract) —
  https://www.inkl.com/news/staggering-cost-of-norovirus-as-five-nursing-homes-closed-and-87-bed-days-lost
- The idea's "~£1k+/day in lost fees" per closed home was NOT confirmed by any source found, and
  no source links a specific outbreak to failed linen disinfection.

## 2. Frequency
- Closure is common when outbreaks happen: a 2016 Scottish survey found 73.3% (n=55) of
  independent care homes that had an outbreak closed the entire home (attribution approximate)
  (search extract) — https://core.ac.uk/download/293880869.pdf
- Community IPC policy for care homes: once closed, homes should stay closed to admissions until
  the outbreak is over (attribution approximate) (search extract) —
  https://www.cumbria.gov.uk/elibrary/Content/Internet/327/38541/38547/45140124756.pdf

  closure reduces outbreak duration (abstract only) (search extract) —
  https://oalib-perpustakaan.upi.edu/Record/doaj_dfbb8e4438bc4e63b2442d54eae8094a/Similar

  dedicated care-home laundries; no frequency statistic found).

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- HTM 01-04 temperatures as summarised by suppliers: laundry held at 65°C for at least ten
  minutes or 71°C for at least three minutes during the wash (attribution approximate) (search
  extract) — https://www.electroluxprofessional.com/au/thermal-disinfection-guide/
- JLA gives the longer figure: 65°C for no less than 18 minutes or 71°C for no less than 11
  minutes including mixing time (attribution to this JLA post approximate) (search extract) —
  https://jla.com/blog/2025/01/24
- IHEEM: the main wash held at 65°C or 71°C for a minimum of 18 and 11 minutes respectively;
  HTM 01-04 published March 2016 (search extract) — https://iheem.org.uk/?p=41019
- Supplier article: CQC inspectors "will check that laundry is carried out in accordance with"
  HTM 01-04 (commercial source; attribution approximate) (search extract) —
  https://www.electroluxprofessional.com/gb/?p=8597
- Cleaning Matters (Miele): CQC inspectors have penalised care homes for disordered laundries,
  lack of soiled-laundry separation, lack of quality and safety audits and "lack of effective
  equipment"; a machine must reliably reach temperature to comply with HTM 01-04 (search
  extract) — https://www.cleaning-matters.co.uk/miele-Infection-prevention
- Scotland: the Care Inspectorate said a laundry room at an Ayrshire care home posed a "risk of
  infection", citing lack of investment (search extract) —
  https://inkl.com/news/laundry-room-at-ayrshire-care-home-posed-risk-of-infection-as-inspector-complains-of-lack-of-investment
- US analogue: a CMS citation where a facility failed to prevent infection spread by not
  monitoring washing-machine hot-water temperatures and had no policy for it (US) (search
  extract) — https://marketing-prod-cpbgh0gea2gsc3e3.eastus-01.azurewebsites.net/citation/1492665
- NHS precedent for per-cycle records (washer-disinfectors, not washer-extractors): NI Estates
  alert says independent monitoring should show critical parameters and log the cycle number and
  error codes for failed cycles; permanent records kept (search extract) —
  https://www.health-ni.gov.uk/sites/default/files/publications/health/EFA-2010-010.pdf
- NHS Scotland washer-disinfector logbook records each cycle's disinfection time and temperature
  (SHTM 01-05) (search extract) —
  https://www.nss.nhs.scot/media/6263/washer-disinfector-daily-and-weekly-logbook-v2-sept-25.pdf
- I found no CQC published guidance in these results that explicitly requires per-cycle
  temperature records for care-home linen.

## 7. Competitors & market gap
- Miele Sapphire can configure washers to "operate and record the use of thermal disinfection
  programs to meet (HTM) 01-04 requirements" (vendor; attribution approximate) (search extract)
  — https://laundryandcleaningnews.com/?p=15577
- Miele fact sheet recommends "a validation tool that monitors the two key metrics - temperature
  and time - in every wash, along with the proof-of-delivery of detergent" (vendor) (search
  extract) —
  https://www.miele.co.uk/media/ex/gb/Professional/Brochures/CPD_Handout_Architects_updated_June_2021.pdf
- Temp°Check: third-party validation tool to check full thermal disinfection took place; stores
  validation reports for up to 1,000 cycles (vendor; attribution approximate) (search extract)
  — https://www.cleaning-matters.co.uk/page_898411.asp
- WASHCO WASHCONNECT: real-time temperature validation and monitoring of which cycles are used
  (vendor; attribution approximate) (search extract) —
  https://www.laundryandcleaningnews.com/analysis/health-and-safety-first-9477687/
- JLA offers ozone disinfection on a cool wash as an alternative to thermal disinfection (search
  extract) — https://ie.jla.com/?p=2592
- Gap (researcher assessment from the sources above): per-cycle temperature validation is already sold by machine makers (Miele) and
  third-party tools (Temp°Check, WASHCONNECT). No prices were found for any of them. A
  retrofit clamp-on logger for older or domestic-grade machines may be the only open space, and
  it would give a less accurate reading than in-drum validation tools.

## Researcher observations
- Compliance (c4) is real but mostly reported second-hand by suppliers; the CQC link rests on
  supplier articles. The disagreement over hold times (10/3 vs 18/11 minutes) means any device
  must state which rule it checks.
- c7 risk is high: Miele already records thermal disinfection programmes, and Temp°Check and
  WASHCONNECT sell per-cycle validation. The retrofit niche is older machines and small homes
  with domestic-grade machines. Those homes are the least likely to pay.
- Better channel: laundry rental firms (JLA, Armstrong) that already have multi-year council and
  care-group contracts could add monitoring to the rental. Hospices are an adjacent buyer (JLA
  publishes a hospice guide).
