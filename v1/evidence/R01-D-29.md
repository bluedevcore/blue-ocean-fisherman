## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Since the Glue Traps (Offences) Act 2022 came into force (31 July 2024), rodent glue traps may only be used in England under a Natural England licence. Licence conditions require frequent physical inspections and the licensee to stay on site unless every trap has remote alert equipment. This can mean repeat or overnight attendance on jobs where glue boards are used.
- Who suffers / who pays: Licensed pest technicians and their firms (time on site); clients in the permitted settings (e.g. hospitals, aircraft); trapped rodents (welfare).
- Sensor concept: A glue-board holder with a capture sensor (pressure/motion, IR or capacitive) and cellular radio, giving a capture alert and a timestamped inspection/response log for licence records.

## 2. Frequency
- **Licences are rare.** The NPTA reports that Natural England "only granted **8 individual licenses and 14 class licenses** throughout the whole of 2025" (England; trade association, not an official Natural England statistic) — https://www.npta.org.uk/?p=84768 (search extract)
- Individual licences are issued only "in exceptional circumstances, for an indoor location, where public health or safety is at risk" (England; gov.uk) — https://www.gov.uk/guidance/glue-trap-licences (search extract)
- The class licence (CL53) covers narrow, time-critical indoor situations such as inside aircraft or hospital operating rooms (England) — https://www.gov.uk/guidance/glue-trap-licences ; https://www.killgerm.com/?p=23889 (search extract)
- The NPTA also suggests licensed use is a small share of actual glue-trap use (its own estimate) — https://www.npta.org.uk/?p=84768 (search extract)

## 3. Current workarounds
- The licensee must remain on site while traps are in use unless remote alert equipment is fitted to every trap; traps must be physically inspected **at least every 6 hours**; a triggered trap must be checked **within 2 hours** of an alert (England; BPCA guidance and Defra principles) — https://1env.com/media/bss/productattachment/BPCAguidancedocumentGluetrapoffencesactversion1.3.pdf ; https://pestcontrolnews.com/?p=28736 (search extract)
- If traps will not be inspected at the required intervals they must be taken up, even temporarily; a named emergency competent person must be able to remove traps if the licensee cannot (England) — https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0598/4010/2597/files/BPCAguidancedocumentGluetrapoffencesactversion1.3.pdf (search extract)
- The main practical workaround is not using glue boards: the licence route is limited to exceptional cases, so most technicians use other methods (inferred from gov.uk scope and NPTA licence numbers) — https://www.gov.uk/guidance/glue-trap-licences ; https://www.npta.org.uk/?p=84768 (search extract)

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- Glue Traps (Offences) Act 2022: creates the offence of setting a glue trap to catch a rodent in England; the explanatory notes say licence conditions can include "the regular monitoring of glue traps that have been set" (England) — https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2022/26/notes/division/6/index.htm ; https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2022/26/section/2/data.html (search extract)
- Remote-monitoring exemption: the licensee must stay on site "unless a trap alert pressure or motion system, or other remote monitoring equipment, is used on every glue trap" to notify the licensee of a catch (England; CL53 conditions as summarised by gov.uk/BPCA) — https://www.gov.uk/guidance/glue-trap-licences ; https://1env.com/media/bss/productattachment/BPCAguidancedocumentGluetrapoffencesactversion1.3.pdf (search extract)
- Records: detailed records and location plans must be kept on site for all glue traps and updated after every inspection (England; BPCA/Defra principles) — https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0598/4010/2597/files/BPCAguidancedocumentGluetrapoffencesactversion1.3.pdf (search extract)
- Class-licence reporting: users must notify the licensing authority within 5 working days after each use (England) — https://www.gov.uk/guidance/glue-trap-licences (search extract)
- Training: licences go only to holders of RSPH Level 2 in pest management who have also completed a Natural England-approved glue-trap course (England) — https://www.killgerm.com/?p=23889 (search extract)
- Insurance angle (headline only): "Insurance warning to pest controllers as new law comes into force" (BPCA) — https://bpca.org.uk/news-and-blog/sector/Page-12/insurance-warning-to-pest-controllers-as-new-law-comes-into-force/278013 (search extract)
- **Shrinking geography:** Wales banned glue-trap use from 17 October 2023 — https://www.pestmagazine.co.uk/news/wales-to-ban-snares-and-glue-traps-from-october-17.html ; Scotland's ban on use, supply and possession of rodent glue traps took effect 1 July 2026 — https://news.stv.tv/scotland/scotlands-glue-trap-ban-comes-into-force ; https://bpca.org.uk/news-and-blog/pest-watch/page-2/scottish-bill-to-ban-the-sale-and-use-of-rodent-glue-boards-published/276682 (search extract)

## Researcher observations
- The licence wording is real and favourable (remote alert on every trap removes the stay-on-site duty; 6-hour checks; 2-hour response; records). But the market is close to zero: about 22 licences in all of 2025 in England, outright bans in Wales (2023) and Scotland (July 2026), and BPCA caution about remote monitoring.
- The same capture-alert hardware is more valuable for **other trap types** where time-to-check matters (live-catch traps, snap traps in multi-catch stations, wildlife-licence traps), or for the **food-site digital monitoring** market (see R01-D-31). The breeder should fold this into a general "trap alert" product rather than a glue-board one.
- If kept, the only buyer niche is hospitals and aircraft (CL53 settings), where the hospital estates/infection-control team might value proof of response times.
