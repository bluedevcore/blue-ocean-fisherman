# R01-A-32 — Community defibrillator guardians walk to cabinets weekly; failed heaters and unreturned AEDs still go unnoticed

Note on method: WebFetch is blocked in this environment by network policy, so no page was fetched
directly. Every bullet relies on WebSearch result titles/snippets/summaries and is marked
"(search extract)". Where the search summary did not tie a claim to one specific URL, the bullet
says so. Geography is UK unless stated.

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Community public-access defibrillators (CPADs) in outdoor cabinets are looked after by volunteer "guardians" who visit periodically (weekly or monthly, depending on the local scheme) and log status on The Circuit (BHF national defibrillator network). Between visits, a cabinet heater can fail in cold weather, an AED can be taken to an emergency and not returned, or a cabinet can be damaged, and the problem may only be found at the next visit or during an emergency.
- Who suffers / who pays: Volunteer guardians; parish/town councils, village hall and church committees, community groups and small businesses that own the device; patients in cardiac arrest if the device is missing or not ready.
- Sensor concept: Cabinet module with door switch, temperature sensor and an optical sensor reading the AED's status indicator, battery LTE-M; updates readiness automatically; alerts on door opening (likely use), cold cabinet or status-light fault.

## 2. Frequency
- Scale: over 120,000 defibrillators are registered on The Circuit, maintained by roughly 81,000 guardians (RCUK Check Your Defib Week material, 2026) (search extract) — https://www.resus.org.uk/about-us/news-and-events/lives-risk-rcuk-launches-check-your-defib-week-tackle-unprepared-devices
- Regional count: South Central Ambulance Service reports 8,215 defibrillators registered on The Circuit in its region (search extract) — https://www.scas.nhs.uk/check-your-defib-because-every-minute-counts/
- Theft/vandalism is rare: a Circuit flyer says only 0.3% of defibrillators on The Circuit have theft or vandalism records, 76% of theft/vandalism incidents involved locked cabinets, and many "stolen" devices were actually deployed in an emergency but not yet returned (search extract) — https://cdn.asp.events/CLIENT_Nineteen_F7A1E73F_5056_B725_6BE81DEAAC036B43/sites/ESS2025/media/libraries/exhib-brochures/145816-the-circuit-unlocked-cabinets-a5-flyer-jun-25.pdf
- Wales: of 7,612 CPADs registered on The Circuit, eight were vandalised (0.1%) ("Missing in action: debunking risk perceptions of theft and vandalism", Wales) (search extract) — https://pure.cardiffmet.ac.uk/en/publications/316-missing-in-action-debunking-risk-perceptions-of-theft-and-van/ ; https://aace.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/P_316_ERC2024_v3.pdf
- NHS England cites evidence that less than 1% of unlocked cabinets are vandalised (search extract) — https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/accessing-community-public-access-defibrillators-cpads/
- RCUK launched "Check Your Defib Week" (June 2026) specifically to tackle "unprepared devices", citing damaged pads, flat batteries, missing parts and expired pads (search extract) — https://aace.org.uk/news/lives-at-risk-rcuk-launches-check-your-defib-week-to-tackle-unprepared-devices/
- No published rate of heater failures or of AEDs not returned after use was found (see "not found").

## 3. Current workarounds
- SCAS guardian checklist lists weekly tasks, e.g. confirm the defibrillator is in its cabinet and check the battery ("If less than 50% order new battery"); an older version lists a weekly check that the "ready" symbols are active (search extract) — https://www.scas.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/DEFIBRILLATOR-GUARDIAN-CHECKLIST_Nov2025.pdf ; https://www.scas.nhs.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Guardian-Checklist-Accessible.docx
- Cadence varies by scheme: Great Western Air Ambulance guardians "are responsible for conducting monthly checks" and for getting the defibrillator back online after use (search extract) — https://greatwesternairambulance.com/?p=28652
- RCUK five-step guardian check covers presence/visibility, accessibility, pad expiry, the status indicator, and updating The Circuit; checks and maintenance are logged and The Circuit sends reminders (search extract) — https://www.resus.org.uk/sites/default/files/2026-06/FINAL_Checklist%20%28print%20version%29.pdf
- Post-use process is manual: Acton Burnell Parish Council policy says after deployment the guardian should check the unit "as soon as possible to ensure that the unit is still in place and functioning correctly and replace the used pads" and do a status check on The Circuit (search extract) — https://www.actonburnell-pc.gov.uk/sites/default/files/defibrillator_maintenance_policy.pdf
- Councils adopt written defib maintenance policies (Pontesbury PC Feb 2025; Ellesmere TC 2025) (search extract) — https://www.pontesbury-pc.gov.uk/sites/default/files/defib_maintenance_policy_-_february_2025.pdf ; https://ellesmere-tc.gov.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/9-Defib-Maintenance-Policy-2025.pdf
- Winter workaround is a manual heater check: vendor guidance suggests winter checks including "testing cabinet heating and seals" and inspecting pad packaging for moisture (search extract) — https://defibwarehouse.co.uk/2023/12/ensure-your-aed-is-protected-this-winter/
- Ambulance-service notification after use is the current "door-open" signal, but it is not always given: a Falkirk first-responder group said the ambulance service normally alerts them when a unit is used but had not done so in that case (search extract) — https://www.inkl.com/news/missing-defibrillator-replaced-after-desperate-appeal-to-falkirk-community
- No source quantified guardian time per check (volunteer labour, so not costed).

## 8. Buildability (components, BOM, certification)
- Retrofit feasibility is shown by existing products: Turtle's "Universal" cabinet monitoring system and DefibMonitor sense temperature, humidity, door and defib presence (search extract) — https://turtledefibcabinets.co.uk/defibrillator-cabinets/defibmonitor-subscription/

## Researcher observations
- Strong on c2/c3 (periodic guardian visits are documented by ambulance trusts and RCUK) and on public pain for unreturned AEDs. Weak on c1: hardware losses are £500–£2,000 and theft/vandalism affects only ~0.1–0.3% of devices per the Circuit/Welsh data.
- c7 risk is real: Turtle, Rotaid (via Community Heartbeat Trust) and Duchy already sell UK cabinet monitoring with temperature/door/presence sensing and annual fees (£126–£190 range for support/monitoring). The unproven differentiator is reading the AED status light and writing readiness to The Circuit automatically.
- The most natural buyer/channel is the managed-package provider (CHT, Duchy, ambulance trusts' guardian schemes) rather than individual parish clerks; councils already pay ~£130–£190/yr per site for support.
- A better niche might be "deployed and not returned" detection feeding ambulance-service re-activation, since the Circuit flyer says many "stolen" devices were really deployed and not yet returned.
