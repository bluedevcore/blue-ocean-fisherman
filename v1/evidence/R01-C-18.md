# R01-C-18 — GP-to-lab pathology boxes cook in courier vans: rejected samples and repeat bleeds

> Research note: WebFetch is blocked in this environment, so no page was opened in full. Every
> bullet below relies on the title and extract shown in WebSearch results and is marked
> "(search extract)". 8 queries were run (the per-idea budget). Geography is UK unless stated.
> Note: the evidence found points mainly at COLD exposure (winter) rather than heat as the main
> cause of spurious potassium in GP samples.

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Blood samples from GP surgeries travel for hours to a laboratory. Exposure to cold or
  heat, and delay before centrifugation, can raise measured potassium (pseudohyperkalaemia) or
  cause haemolysis. Results may be wrong, samples rejected, and patients recalled or referred.
- Who suffers / who pays: Patients (repeat bleeds, unnecessary referrals), GP practices
  (recalls), pathology labs (quality, workload), couriers (blamed without data).
- Sensor concept: Temperature logger plus GNSS in each transport box, LTE-M, automatic check-in
  at the lab, and per-route excursion reports linked to rejection and spurious-result rates.

## 1. Cost of incident
- Milton Keynes University Hospital FOI (2019): phlebotomy "around £4 per bleed", about 50p for
  handling and consumables per sample, and an estimate of "~£16.50/ sample" for a wrong-blood-in-
  tube event (search extract) — https://www.mkuh.nhs.uk/document/it-divices-response-4826-attachment-2
- Avoidable admissions: an NHS Grampian audit found inappropriate admissions for
  pseudohyperkalaemia fell "from 22 cases to 6" after GP practices centrifuged at source across 86
  practices (Scotland; the extract blended two Aberdeen papers, so attribution to this URL is
  approximate) (search extract) — https://sage.cnpereading.com/doi/10.1177/0004563212474566
- Recall scale (anecdotal, patient forum): a post reports "nearly 2,500 patients have been
  recalled for extra tests after problems with a merged pathology service in Kent" (secondary,
  non-authoritative) (search extract) —
  https://healthunlocked.com/thyroiduk/posts/139011993/blood-test-problems-as-though-we-dont-have-enough
- Non-UK context: a Singapore hospital quality project says "the repeated blood taking process is
  chargeable to both the organization and patients" (Singapore) (search extract) —
  https://isomer-user-content.by.gov.sg/622/27fa5345-92eb-4681-a626-d89d93497f2a/C_556_NHCS_SHM_2021_Reduce_Rejected_Specimen_Rate.pdf
- I found no UK figure for the cost of a GP recall appointment for a repeat bleed.

## 2. Frequency
- Portsmouth (Sinclair et al., 2003): mean daily potassium in GP samples rose as winter
  temperatures fell and dropped in summer, while inpatient results stayed flat; transport
  temperature "strongly affects measured serum potassium" (search extract) —
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1769952
- Follow-up audit (2011) "Seasonal pseudohyperkalaemia: no longer an issue?": biggest gap between
  on-site and lab-centrifuged results in January 2011, when average daily temperature was 2.5°C
  (search extract) — https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1258/acb.2011.011122
- BJGP audit of about 224,000 GP-requested potassium results: pseudohyperkalaemia "almost
  invariably associated with pre-analytical factors"; warns centralisation could increase it
  "unless heated transport is used" (search extract) — https://bjgp.org/content/58/549/278
- Black Country Pathology (2025): an "increased incidence of spuriously raised potassium results",
  linked to bagged rather than racked samples, longer routes to the Wolverhampton hub and slower
  centrifugation; "Longer transport times will be particularly problematic in cold weather"
  (search extract) —
  https://myconnect.swbh.nhs.uk/news/increased-incidence-of-spuriously-raised-potassium-results/
- York audit (2017) compared GP U&E hyperkalaemia rates in June vs December across 52 surgeries
  sending more than 20 requests (search extract) — https://www.yorkhospitals.nhs.uk/seecmsfile/?id=2870

## 3. Current workarounds
- On-site centrifugation at GP practices (Grampian audit: share centrifuged at source rose from 20%
  to 95%) (attribution approximate) (search extract) —
  https://sage.cnpereading.com/doi/10.1177/0004563212474566
- Synnovis (London) introduced temperature-controlled vans but says it "cannot mitigate for the
  seasonal variation" before pick-up; couriers reported samples stored in fridges on some sites
  (search extract) —
  https://www.synnovis.co.uk/sites/default/files/upload/Seasonal%20variation%20in%20potassium%20results.pdf
- Norfolk and Waveney SusQI report (2025): audit of raised potassium across 10 GP surgeries;
  team considered reusable insulated transport bags (search extract) —
  https://production.networks.sustainablehealthcare.org.uk/sites/default/files/2025-09/Clinical%20sciences%20SusQI%20Report.pdf
- Brent scrutiny report (2013): linked seasonal potassium to storage temperature at GP surgeries
  and proposed bike-to-hub plus temperature-controlled vans (search extract) —
  https://democracy.brent.gov.uk/documents/s19500/7-HOSC%20update%20sept%202013.pdf
- Clinical workaround: York community hyperkalaemia pathway tells clinicians to check first whether
  a result is spurious; most patients at 5.4–5.9 mmol/L do not need emergency admission (search
  extract) — https://hnyppr.org.uk/web/york/w/hyperkalaemia-in-adult-patients-in-the-community-pathway
- Validation runs with traditional loggers: a US case study says loggers were downloaded after each
  run, with no real-time status, before moving to a GPS tracker (US, single site) (search extract)
  — https://www.medlabmag.com/article/1513

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- ISO 15189:2022 has "more detailed" pre-analytical requirements and references ISO 20658 for
  sample collection and transport (Eurachem/LabRulez note) (search extract) —
  https://gcms.labrulez.com/paper/32956
- European Accreditation training on ISO 15189 Chapter 7 says pre-examination requirements became
  less prescriptive and more goal-oriented, including sample stability and time monitoring where
  relevant (EU) (search extract) —
  https://www.sas.admin.ch/dam/sas/de/dokumente/Wie%20wird%20meine%20Stelle%20akkreditiert/Gesetzliche%20Grundlagen/ea15189_kap7.pdf.download.pdf/EA%20LC%20TTT%20ISO%2015189%20-%20Chapter%207.pdf
- NHS procurement: an NHS South West notice for pathology and pharmacy courier services says "the
  implementation of a tracking system will be paramount" and requires samples to travel in
  "validated temperature-controlled vehicles or transport containers" (search extract) —
  https://www.find-tender.service.gov.uk/Notice/000758-2023
- US analogue: a CMS survey report cites a lab for failing to define and monitor specimen
  transport temperature (US CLIA, not ISO 15189) (search extract) —
  https://wbrvbcploiiwmgaexcni.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/cms-2567-pdfs/10D2224368_20240206_XCRR11.pdf
- I did not find the ISO 15189:2022 clause text or UKAS guidance that explicitly requires
  continuous transport temperature records.

## Researcher observations
- Strong clinical evidence that transport temperature and delay cause spurious potassium in GP
  samples, and it is getting worse with hub consolidation (Black Country 2025). But the evidence
  points to COLD and delay, not heat; the idea's framing should change.
- Weak cost evidence: a bleed costs about £4, so the value is avoided admissions, recalls and
  clinical risk, which is hard to price.
- Courier firms (CitySprint, EMED) already sell tracking and temperature-controlled vehicles. A
  gap remains at the bag/box level and at the GP surgery before pick-up.
- A better buyer may be the pathology network's logistics manager (Band 7) during courier
  re-tendering, or the courier firm wanting proof for its SLA. A cheaper product could be a
  per-surgery "time and temperature since draw" logger rather than per-box GNSS.
