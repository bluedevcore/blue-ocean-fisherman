# R01-D-18 — Little-used pool and spa changing-room showers — weekly Legionella flushing and monthly temperature checks done by hand

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Leisure centres, gyms and spas have many showers and thermostatic mixing valves (TMVs). Under HSE ACoP L8 and HSG274 Part 2, outlets that are little used are normally flushed weekly, and sentinel outlet temperatures are checked monthly. In practice, duty staff walk round flushing and water-hygiene contractors take temperatures, and records can be incomplete.
- Who suffers / who pays: Duty staff do the flushing rounds. The Legionella responsible person or duty holder at the leisure operator carries the legal risk. The operator pays staff time and contractor monitoring fees.
- Sensor concept: Strap-on dual pipe thermistors on each shower/TMV supply detect use and log hot and cold temperatures, over LoRaWAN or LTE-M. The system flags outlets unused for 7 days (only those need flushing), logs sentinel temperatures continuously and exports an L8 record.

## 2. Frequency
- HSE: "Check water temperature at sentinel outlets on a monthly basis. Check representative outlets for temperature as above on a rotational basis"; hot water >50 °C (>55 °C in healthcare) after 1 minute; cold <20 °C after 2 minutes; where TMVs are fitted, measure at the hot water inlet to the TMV (UK, regulator) (search extract) — https://www.hse.gov.uk/legionnaires/things-to-consider.htm
- Weekly flushing of infrequently used outlets: a council water-services log book defines an infrequently used outlet as "one that is used less than once a week", and says spray heads/hoses must be removed before flushing; the same log sets a weekly check of TMV blended temperatures (UK; attribution inferred to the Redditch sample log book) (search extract) — https://moderngovwebpublic.redditchbc.gov.uk/documents/s3679/Water%20services%20log%20book%20Sample%20-%20Appendix%202.pdf
- Government guidance (Defence Infrastructure Organisation): "Flush all outlets weekly until occupancy; if a phased occupancy, those outlets not in use must be flushed weekly" (UK; attribution inferred) (search extract) — https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6023860a8fa8f514727afb2b/TB_2021-01_-_Legionella_-_water_hygiene_guide.pdf
- Caveat: a healthcare webinar transcript notes "In HSG274, there isn't a definition for 'little use' or 'infrequently used'" (search extract) — https://his-website.azurewebsites.net/media/2944/waterborne-infections-webinar-transcript.pdf
- Sports-centre practice: "staff have to travel to the sites every week to undertake flushing and temperature control", with monitoring also carried out by a contractor (Northern Ireland news on automatic shower units) (search extract) — https://www.londonderrysentinel.co.uk/news/automatic-shower-units-help-flush-out-legionella-risk-sport-centres-1082001

## Researcher observations
- Strong on compliance and frequency: HSE web guidance (monthly sentinel checks), weekly flushing practice in council and government documents, and a leisure-centre-specific HSE prosecution (Tendring: shower Legionella, monitoring failures, £27k plus costs). Recent shower closures at Jersey, Hampstead Heath, Mansfield and Hythe give public evidence of pain.
- Weak on c1 for this niche: the leisure prosecution fine is modest (£27k). Large fines are in housing and prisons. Labour cost of flushing rounds was not quantified.
- Crowded category: many UK LoRaWAN/Sigfox pipe-temperature vendors (Nano Sensorics, SPICA, PTSG, Invisible Systems, SOCOTEC, Connexin, InferSens, CreevX), some with auto-flush. Water-hygiene contractors (PTSG, SOCOTEC) are themselves becoming competitors.
- Better angle: sell through the water-hygiene contractor already doing monthly visits, or pair use-detection with auto-flush valves (Sfella/aquaflush model) so staff rounds go away. Multi-site leisure operators (GLL, Everyone Active, Places Leisure) would be the target buyers, but no evidence about their procurement was found.
