## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Food outlets' grease traps fill with fat, oil and grease (FOG) between scheduled emptying visits; FOG passing into the public sewer causes blockages, sewer flooding and pollution. UK water companies prosecute under s111 Water Industry Act 1991, and operators often cannot show the trap was maintained.
- Who suffers / who pays: Food-outlet owners/operators (fines, clearance recharges); water-company network teams; residents affected by sewer flooding.
- Sensor concept: ToF/ultrasonic level sensor in the trap lid, lid reed switch logging each service, temperature probe for hot-fat dumps; LTE-M, battery; alerts the contractor to book an early pump-out and auto-logs services as evidence.

## 2. Frequency
- 300,000 sewer blockages a year across the UK (Water UK) (search extract) — https://water.org.uk/fatsblockpipes
- Thames Water (Sept 2026): its network protection team made over 19,000 visits to restaurants and food businesses in the past year; more than 2,500 food businesses installed grease-management equipment in that period; three businesses successfully prosecuted in five years with fines/costs totalling over £50,000; some traps were cut into so they looked compliant while FOG still flowed (UK) (search extract) — https://www.thameswater.co.uk/news/2026/sep/food-business-outreach-helps-prevent-thousands-of-fatbergs

## Researcher observations
- Strong: s111 prosecutions with £ figures, including one for an installed-but-unmaintained trap (China Panda), plus water-company inspection volume (19,000 visits a year at Thames) give c1/c4/c10 evidence.
- Weak: prosecutions are rare (three in five years at Thames), fines are small (£5k–£16k for independents), and the UK already has grease-trap alarms (Kingspan SmartServ, Aquasentry). The EM310-UDL is end-of-life at some retailers.
- Better buyer: drainage franchises (Metro Rod) or grease-management contractors, who could use fill data to move from fixed-interval to on-demand routes, and multi-site pub/restaurant groups (Mitchells & Butlers was fined £75k). Water companies themselves may fund sensors at repeat-blockage hotspots as cheaper than 19,000 inspection visits — worth testing as a buyer.
