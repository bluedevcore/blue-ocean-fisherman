## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: UK employers must assess and control hand-arm vibration (HAV) exposure under the Control of Vibration at Work Regulations 2005. Many vibrating tools used on sites are hired, and employers usually estimate trigger time on paper. Monitoring systems exist but are bought or rented separately from the hired tool.
- Who suffers / who pays: Operatives (HAVS, a permanent condition); employers (HSE fines, civil claims, health-surveillance costs); contractor H&S managers. The payer would be the contractor, possibly through the hire company's invoice.
- Sensor concept: Tag fitted by the hire depot to each vibrating tool. It measures trigger time and vibration, links to an operative ID over BLE or LTE-M, and calculates daily exposure points against the EAV and ELV, with records kept.

## 2. Frequency
- HSE IIDB statistics (GB): 220 new HAVS cases in 2024, 215 in 2023 and 300 in 2022, down from 1,210 in 2010. HSE says IIDB "will tend to underestimate" incidence and excludes the self-employed (search extract) — https://hse.gov.uk/statistics/causdis/vibration
- HSE statistics page (GB): a 1997–98 MRC survey estimated 288,000 people with vibration white finger, a prevalence figure (old) (search extract) — https://hse.gov.uk/vibration/hav/statistics.htm
- Building Safety Group (UK): "33% increase in Hand Arm Vibration breaches on construction sites" (search extract, title; 2019 per the Project Plant URL) — https://cpnonline.co.uk/?p=6377 ; https://projectplant.co.uk/2019/02/havs-breaches-jump-by-third-safety-organisation-warns/
- Counterpoint: a field survey of UK utilities operatives found none above the ELV and 91.07% below the EAV (search extract) — https://www.open-access.bcu.ac.uk/8977/

## 3. Current workarounds
- Hire firms publish colour-coded HAV guides that give each tool's magnitude and the time to reach the EAV and ELV. Frank Key advises using its "HAV Colour Code guide" to "select the lowest vibration tool" (UK) (search extract) — https://www.frank-key.co.uk/safety/
- National Tool Hire Shops "recommends the use of the points system introduced by the Health & Safety Executive" (UK) (search extract) — https://nationaltoolhireshops.co.uk/knowledge/site/what-is-hand-arm-vibration-syndrome
- GAP Group (hire) offers an online HAV calculator (search extract, title) — https://www.gap-group.co.uk/hav-calculator
- Safe Work Australia guide: trigger time "is often much shorter than the overall time on the job and is usually over-estimated by workers" (AU) (search extract) — https://www.safeworkaustralia.gov.au/system/files/documents/1703/guidetomeasuringassessinghandarmvibration.pdf
- Canadian Acoustics paper citing Palmer et al.: self-evaluated exposure time "is not a reliable way" to estimate exposure; workers estimate task length rather than trigger time (search extract) — https://jcaa.caa-aca.ca/index.php/jcaa/article/download/2344/2093/2483
- HASpod blog: "Most people estimate trigger times… but this can be inaccurate" (UK) (search extract) — https://haspod.com/blog/vibration/understanding-havs-trigger-times

- HSE Buxton evaluated six tool timers for timing accuracy in the lab and in field trials, as reported in IOA proceedings (UK) (search extract) — https://www.ioa.org.uk/system/files/proceedings/24._hawker_a.pdf

## Researcher observations
- Enforcement evidence is strong: HSE prosecutions of £32k–£600k, regular in 2025–26, under statutory exposure values. Civil claims are typically £8k–£60k and occasionally six figures.
- The "hire-fleet-embedded" gap looks weak. Reactec has been in Speedy's fleet since about 2008 and half the top-8 hire firms took HAVmeters in about 2012. GAP stocks EAVE ToolWarden tags. Cheap timers cost about £91–£100 with no subscription, which caps pricing.
- Prosecuted defendants are often landlords, NHS trusts and grounds-maintenance teams, not contractors. These operators own their tools and may be a better niche: estates and grounds teams with no H&S specialist.
- HSE's guidance on where measurement is taken (palm or tool versus wrist) could favour a tool-mounted sensor that measures actual magnitude. Validating that against HSE Buxton testing would be the differentiator.
