# R02-A-01 — Licensed boarding kennel blocks overheat in heatwave afternoons; licence expects 10–26°C but nobody sees the 3 pm peak

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Animal-activity licence conditions for dog/cat boarding set a sleeping-area temperature band (minimum 10°C; many council documents also give a 26°C maximum) and expect staff to watch dogs for heat stress. In hot weather, uninsulated kennel blocks may exceed that band in the afternoon when no one is checking. A heat-related death can bring prosecution, owner compensation and a worse inspection rating.
- Who suffers / who pays: Licence holder / owner-operator of a licensed boarding kennel or cattery; staff; boarded animals and their owners.
- Sensor concept: 2–4 battery LoRaWAN or LTE-M temperature/humidity sensors per block with one gateway; escalating phone alerts at ~25°C and ~28°C; an automatic daily min/max record for council inspections; possibly sold through pet-business insurers.

## 3. Current workarounds
- Min/max thermometers: Gwynedd's boarding conditions require "a minimum-maximum thermometer in each kennel block and isolation unit" (Wales; 2012-era document) (search extract) — https://rhagolwg.gwynedd.llyw.cymru/en/Businesses/Documents-Busnes/Permits-and-licences/Animals/Boarding-Establishments-Conditions-Guidance-Dogs.pdf
- Rhondda Cynon Taf's conditions (based on May 2012 guidance) say a minimum-maximum thermometer must be provided in isolation kennels (Wales) (search extract) — https://www.rctcbc.gov.uk/EN/Business/LicencesandPermits/Taxilicences/RelatedDocuments/DogBoardingLicenceconditionsandguidance.pdf
- Staff-based heat protocols: one US operator's protocol keeps water bowls full, has staff carry a water jug through boarding areas, and keeps let-outs for "at risk" dogs short (US) (search extract) — https://pawpartner.com/paw-partner/blog/29/hot-weather-protocols-for-pet-daycare-and-boarding

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- England (2018 regime) kennel guidance, as reproduced by Wigan Council: kennels must aim to keep the ambient temperature in the sleeping area above an absolute minimum of 10°C, and "dogs must be monitored to check if they are too hot or too cold" (UK–England) (search extract) — https://www.wigan.gov.uk/Docs/PDF/Business/Licensing-Permits-Registrations/Animals/Guidance-for-providing-boarding-for-dogs.pdf
- Upper limit of 26°C appears in several council documents. Wigan home-boarding guidance gives 10°C as an absolute minimum and 26°C as the maximum. Harborough's home-boarding self-assessment says sleeping areas should be between 10°C and 26°C (UK–England, home boarding) (search extract) — https://wigan.gov.uk/Docs/PDF/Business/Licensing-Permits-Registrations/Animals/Guidance-for-home-boarding-for-dogs.pdf ; https://www.harborough.gov.uk/download/downloads/id/8397/self-assessment_form_-_providing_home_boarding_for_dogs.docx
- Vale of Glamorgan's dog-boarding conditions require the kennel bedding area to be kept between 10°C and 26°C (Wales, c.2014) (search extract) — https://www.valeofglamorgan.gov.uk/Documents/Working/Business%20Support/EU%20Services%20Directive/Dog%20Boarding%20Conditions.pdf
- Isolation kennels: Rhondda Cynon Taf and Gwynedd say the isolation-kennel temperature "must not be allowed to rise above 26 C or fall below 13 C" (Wales) (search extract) — https://www.rctcbc.gov.uk/EN/Business/LicencesandPermits/Taxilicences/RelatedDocuments/DogBoardingLicenceconditionsandguidance.pdf
- Day care: Caerphilly's day-boarding conditions treat 26°C as a limit that should not be exceeded in normal circumstances, with a 10°C minimum (Wales) (search extract) — https://caerffili.gov.uk/CaerphillyDocs/Licensing/Day_boarding_establishments_conditions.aspx
- Records: the licence holder must keep all records required by the licence available for inspection. Kirklees' kennel self-assessment checklist says such records must be kept for at least three years (UK–England) (search extract) — https://www.kirklees.gov.uk/beta/licensing/pdf/providing-boarding-for-dogs-in-kennels-self-assessment-form.pdf
- The star rating sets licence length: a risk × compliance matrix gives low-risk, higher-standard premises up to 5 stars and a 3-year licence, and high-risk, minimum-standard premises 2 stars and a 1-year licence (UK–England) (search extract) — https://www.southnorfolkandbroadland.gov.uk/licensing/licences/animal-activity-licences ; https://www.plymouth.gov.uk/animal-activities-licence
- Some councils publish lists of star-rated establishments, so the rating is visible to the public (UK) (search extract) — https://www.kirklees.gov.uk/beta/licensing/animal-welfare-licensing.aspx ; https://www.nwleics.gov.uk/pages/animal_welfare_star_ratings
- Insurance: Cliverton's licensed kennel and cattery policy covers claims for "injury, loss or death of an animal while in the care, custody or under the control of you or an employee" (UK) (search extract) — https://cliverton.co.uk/policies/licensed-kennel-and-cattery-insurance
- A boarding policy summary on Keymedia lists exclusions such as death following illness after more than 90 days' boarding, and death due to illness of animals aged 8 or over at the start of boarding (jurisdiction not confirmed) (search extract) — https://cdn-res.keymedia.com/insurr/ProgramForm/21592.pdf
- Gap: no source showed an insurer requiring or discounting temperature monitoring for kennels.

## Researcher observations
- Strong on regulation (temperature bands, a monitoring duty, three-year records, star rating linked to fee and licence length). Weak on incident frequency and cost: the only UK prosecution found is an unlicensed home boarder with a £2,000 fine.
- The 26°C upper limit is clearly evidenced in Wales and in home-boarding and day-care documents. For England kennels the evidence is "monitor for too hot/too cold" plus a 10°C minimum, so pitches should avoid claiming a statutory 26°C ceiling in England.
- The incumbent risk is high: generic $20–$260 alarms already do the alerting. The defensible part is an inspection-ready record and star-rating evidence. Kennel insurers (Cliverton, Protectivity) are the most plausible channel already serving this buyer.
- A possibly stronger niche for the same problem is home boarders and dog day-care operators (also licensed, more numerous, and less able to check temperature during the day). The only UK conviction found came from this segment.
