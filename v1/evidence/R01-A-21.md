## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Commercial and institutional sites store heating oil above ground. Secondary containment (bunds) can fill with rainwater or leaked oil unnoticed, and tanks or pipework can leak slowly. Insurance may exclude gradual pollution or clean-up costs, so the site owner bears remediation and any regulatory action.
- Who suffers / who pays: Owners and managers of schools, churches, farms, care homes and small businesses off the gas grid; councils and dioceses with many sites.
- Sensor concept: Battery bund sensor that tells oil from water, plus tank level-loss analysis, on cellular LPWAN; alerts on oil in the bund or unexplained level loss; annual compliance pack for insurer and regulator.

## 1. Cost of incident
- Withernsea High School (East Riding of Yorkshire): oil leaked from a heating oil tank after a delivery and reached a stream; the council was fined £18,000 plus £3,939 costs (almost £22,000); the EA installed booms before it reached the estuary; the council then surveyed oil tanks across all its sites (search extract) — https://iema.net/articles/costly-oil-leak-for-council
- Suffolk malt producer fined £20,000 plus costs after a tank leak killed fish in the River Lark (search extract) — https://www.rivertac.org/?p=5924
- Romsey company fined £1,500 plus £1,500 costs after heating oil reached the River Test via a fish farm (2005) (search extract) — https://pick-upau.org.br/site_english/environmental_panorama/2005/2005.05.31/environment_agency_prosecutes_romsey.htm
- Jersey: La Hougue Farm fined £1,000; stream quality fell from good/excellent to poor, and residual oil was still in the stream bed nearly a year later (2013) (search extract) — https://www.gov.je/News/2013/Pages/FuelSpillPrevention.aspx
- Chubb (insurer) premises pollution liability example: a heating oil tank at a logistics warehouse with an underground pipe fracture, "estimated loss to the business could be £800,000" (insurer marketing example) (search extract) — https://chubb.com/uk-en/business/products/environmental-insurance/premises-pollution-liability-insurance.html

## 3. Current workarounds
- Annual "Oil Storage Compliance Inspection" to OFTEC 600A covers pipework, gauges, bund condition, supports, fill points, vents and spill kit, with a written report and a separate quote for remedial work (search extract) — https://gptenvironmental.co.uk/oftec-inspections.html
- OFTEC-registered tank inspection services are also offered by Tradebe and Crown Oil (search extract) — https://www.tradebe.co.uk/node/2019 ; https://www.crownoil.co.uk/services/environmental/tank-inspections/
- OFTEC TI/133D is the risk-assessment form for whether a domestic tank needs secondary containment and fire protection; commercial sites fall under the 2001 Regulations instead (search extract) — https://certbox.pages.dev/certificate/ti133d-oil-tank-risk-assessment/
- Fuel distributor Craggs Energy runs a "When did you last check the health of your fuel tank?" campaign (title) (search extract) — https://www.craggsenergy.co.uk/?p=3430
- Wired/battery float-switch bund alarms (Watchman Bundman, CTS Economy, Atkinson) detect any liquid in the bund, with local audible/visual warning (search extract) — https://tanks.ie/products/watchman-bundman ; https://www.atkinsonequipment.com/?p=4210
- No source on the labour cost of manual bund checks or rainwater pumping was found (search budget ran out before this was searched).

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- The Control of Pollution (Oil Storage) (England) Regulations 2001 (SI 2001/2954) were made on 21 Aug 2001, came into force on 1 Mar 2002 and show no outstanding effects on legislation.gov.uk (search extract) — https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/2954
- The 2001 guidance says the Regulations mainly affect above-ground storage with a 200 litre lower limit "on industrial, commercial and institutional premises" (search extract; copy hosted by Crown Oil) — https://www.crownoil.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/oil_store.pdf
- gov.uk marks the 2001 guidance note as withdrawn and points to newer oil storage guidance (search extract) — https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/oil-storage-guidance
- Third-party guides: a bund should hold at least 110% of the largest container or 25% of total volume stored, whichever is greater (not an official source) (search extract) — https://hydepark-environmental.com/news/what-is-bunded-storage-uk-legal-requirements-explained/ ; https://hydepark-environmental.com/media/wysiwyg/England_Bunded_Storage_Quick_Guide_v1.pdf
- The Diocese of Rochester publishes guidance on the oil storage regulations for churches (title) (search extract) — https://rochester.anglican.org/content/pages/documents/oil-storage-regulations-2002.pdf
- OFTEC: most home insurance covers lost oil and damage to the home after a spill "but NOT the environmental investigation and clean-up costs"; owners are told to check cover for leaks, third-party damage and clean-up (domestic) (search extract) — https://www.oftec.org/consumers/are-you-insured-for-an-oil-tank-leak
- Broker material: many wordings give "insufficient cover… as regards gradual pollution and clean up costs"; Environmental Impairment Liability is sold as separate cover (search extract; summary attribution to this broker page is probable, not certain) — https://www.hislbrokers.com/insurance-coverages/environmental-impairment-liability/
- Forum user: package insurers will cover loss of oil "provided it's from a single event"; a gradual leak would typically be excluded (user opinion) (search extract) — https://singletrackworld.com/forum/off-topic/stwers-with-oil-heating-are-you-insured-for-liability
- Chubb sells premises pollution liability cover that addresses own-site clean-up (search extract) — https://chubb.com/uk-en/business/products/environmental-insurance/premises-pollution-liability-insurance.html

## Researcher observations
- Regulatory basis (2001 Regs, 200 L threshold for commercial/institutional) and insurance gaps (clean-up often excluded or limited) are reasonably supported. Prosecution fines are modest (£1k–£22k), and the larger risk is the clean-up bill.
- Competition is strong on level monitoring (cheap consumer monitors with drop alerts, £25–£60/yr subscriptions) and moderate on bund alarms. Differentiation rests on oil/water discrimination plus a compliance/insurer pack.
- Multi-site estates look like the better buyer than single sites: councils (the Withernsea case led to a survey of all tanks), dioceses with church tanks, academy trusts. Oil distributors (Crown Oil, Craggs) and OFTEC inspectors are plausible channels.
- Insurer-led distribution (pollution liability carriers like Chubb) is a possible route, but untested here.
- Research is incomplete (6 queries) because the shared search budget ran out.
