# R01-B-02 — Cooling-tower biocide drum runs dry between monthly water-treatment visits

Note on method: WebFetch is blocked in this environment, so no page was fetched. Every bullet below
relies on WebSearch result titles, snippets or the search tool's summary of them, and is marked
"(search extract)". Where the search summary did not make clear which of several listed URLs carried a

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: On a cooling tower or evaporative condenser, the biocide or inhibitor day tank can run empty, or the dosing pump can stop or airlock. The system may then run undosed until someone notices at a weekly check or the contractor's next visit. Microbial counts and Legionella risk rise, and remedial disinfection or enforcement can follow.
- Who suffers / who pays: The duty holder (the site operator, usually the engineering or facilities manager) pays for remediation and faces enforcement. Water-treatment contractors can also be prosecuted. Staff and the public carry the health risk.
- Sensor concept: Ultrasonic/ToF level (or load cell) on each chemical day tank, a vibration/current sensor proving the dosing pump strokes, and a sump temperature probe. NB-IoT/LTE-M or LoRaWAN. Alerts the contractor and site when dosing stops, and fills in the weekly check record automatically.

## 1. Cost of incident
- HP Bulmer (Hereford): following guilty pleas after an HSE investigation into cooling towers at the plant, the companies were "each fined £300,000 with costs of £50,000". The case centred on failure to maintain an effective cleansing/disinfection regime for seasonally used towers (UK, 2008) (search extract) — https://processengineering.co.uk/article/1306929/hse-warning-after-bulmer-legionnaire-prosecution ; https://www.birminghampost.co.uk/news/local-news/hereford-cider-firm-fined-300000-3957140 ; https://www.irwinmitchell.com/newsandmedia/2008/july/fine-for-legionnaires-cider-firm
- Faltec Europe, a car-parts manufacturer, was "fined £800,000 after a legionnaires' disease outbreak at its factory", traced to two cooling towers at Boldon. The firm then "shut the cooling towers down and replaced them with water chillers" (UK) (search extract) — https://www.coolingpost.com/uk-news/800000-fine-for-ld-outbreak/
- Riaar Plastics (West Bromwich) was fined £50,000 plus £11,000 costs at Birmingham Magistrates' Court on 2 June 2023, after six people were infected with Legionnaires' disease in September 2020. The case concerns tower condition rather than dosing specifically (UK) (search extract) — https://www.expressandstar.com/news/local-hubs/sandwell/west-bromwich/2023/06/06/black-country-firm-fined-50000-after-members-of-the-public-infected-with-legionnaires/ ; https://www.shponline.co.uk/in-court/riaar-plastics-fined-following-outbreak-of-legionnaires-disease/ ; https://www.coolingpost.com/uk-news/plastics-firm-fined-50000-for-ld-outbreak/
- Chromalloy UK was "fined a total of £110,000 (£55,000 for each breach) with £77,252 in costs". The HSE inspector said its water treatment programme and management arrangements were "severely ineffective" (UK, c.2015) (search extract) — https://www.coolingpost.com/uk-news/firm-fined-110000-for-legionella-lapses/
- Isher Hangers (Birmingham, c.2019): the search summary says an HSE inspector described the firm "operating a cooling tower without biocide", that concerns raised by its own water-treatment consultants were ignored, and that two partners were each fined £12,115 including victim surcharge. A blog reports suspended sentences. The summary did not say which of the two URLs carries the inspector quote (UK) (search extract) — https://phil-sss.blogspot.com/2019/11/partners-given-suspended-sentence-for.html ; https://cqms-ltd.co.uk/?p=6390
- Contractor exposure: the search summary reports that Eaton Ltd was prosecuted over its water cooling systems and that the water-treatment contractor Aegis Ltd "was also found to have failed significantly in its duties". The title "Double sentence for Legionella failure" fits this, but the attribution to that URL is not confirmed (UK) (search extract) — https://www.hsmsearch.com/Double-sentence-for-Legionella-failure

## 2. Frequency
- Minnesota (2017 CSTE abstract): a community outbreak was linked to an industrial cooling tower "using an automated biocide delivery system". Low periodic demand "may have contributed to intermittent biocide, stagnation, and Legionella amplification". This is the closest match to a dosing-delivery failure (US) (search extract) — https://cste.confex.com/cste/2017/webprogram/Paper7508.html
- 2014 CSTE abstract: an electronic control system turned off cooling-tower pumps during low demand, "preventing delivery of disinfectant by a timed-release system" (US) (search extract) — https://cste.confex.com/cste/2014/webprogram/Paper3089.html
- Catalonia outbreak, 2005: inspectors found "inadequate disinfectant doses of biocide, non-existent maintenance records on weekends and wrong sample points". Proper maintenance "could have prevented the outbreak" (Spain). The summary attributes this to Eurosurveillance, but the exact URL is inferred (search extract) — https://www.eurosurveillance.org/content/10.2807/esm.12.03.00691-en
- South Bronx 2015: the city health department cited "inadequate maintenance and inadequate levels of biocide" as possible contributors (US) (search extract) — https://foodpoisoningbulletin.com/2015/details-on-south-bronx-legionnaires-outbreak-investigation/
- Central Harlem 2025 health-department report: one tower had "major lapses in routine monitoring, had no treatment adjustments for out-of-range water quality, and had missed required legionella sampling" (US) (search extract) — https://www.cidrap.umn.edu/sites/default/files/page-files/PHA-Legionnaires%20Harlem062326.pdf
- Edinburgh (UK): an improvement notice was served for "a failure to devise and implement a sustained and effective biocide control programme" in one tower, which was not identified as the outbreak source (search extract) — https://www.acr-news.com/improvement-notice-served-on-edinburgh-firm-as-ld-search-continues
- Installed base: there is no national count of notified towers in the results. Registers are held council by council. For example, Bassetlaw lists one premises with "9 (8 Hyperbolic & 1 Forced draught)" towers, and Stockport publishes per-site counts (UK) (search extract) — https://www.bassetlaw.gov.uk/environmental-health/pollution-control/cooling-towers-and-evaporative-condensers/ ; https://www.stockport.gov.uk/cooling-towers-and-evaporative-condensers
- No per-site rate of dosing outages (e.g. "drum empty X times a year") was found.

## 3. Current workarounds
- An SPX Cooling summary of ACoP L8 / HSG274 Part 1 lists weekly checks of cooling and make-up water: oxidising biocide (ppm), conductivity, pH, microbiological activity ("Dipslide (incubate 48hrs @30oC) or Total viable count cfu/ml") and checks of control equipment (UK, vendor summary) (search extract) — https://spxcooling.com/wp-content/uploads/Obligations-ACoP-L8-HSG274-part-1-v3.pdf
- A Hong Kong EMSD checklist schedules a weekly check of "water treatment dosing equipment and conductivity sensors" and of the chemicals' adequacy (non-UK, supporting context) (search extract) — https://bestpractice.emsd.gov.hk/en/hvac-installations/useful-forms
- Councils issue paper self-assessment forms for wet cooling systems, e.g. Rushmoor's "control of legionella in wet cooling systems self assessment form" (title only) (UK) (search extract) — https://www.rushmoor.gov.uk:443/media/cvnmd2xk/control-of-legionella-in-wet-cooling-systems-self-assessment-form.pdf
- In public tenders, monthly contractor work is described as "primarily monthly monitoring and inspection of water systems", and tenders require Legionella Control Association (LCA) accreditation. The summary did not say which notice each phrase came from (UK) (search extract) — https://www.find-tender.service.gov.uk/Notice/002067-2025 ; https://www.contractsfinder.service.gov.uk/Notice/30ba555e-a9df-494b-ab2e-ea3f924f5177

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- The Notification of Cooling Towers and Evaporative Condensers Regulations 1992 require non-domestic premises to notify the local authority, in writing on an HSE-approved form. A "notifiable device" is any cooling tower or evaporative condenser except where it contains no water exposed to air and its water and electrical supplies are not connected (UK) (search extract) — https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1992/2225/body/made?view=plain ; https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1992/2225/regulation/2/made?view=plain
- Council guidance says notification exists mainly "to assist inspectors in investigating outbreaks". The summary did not say which council page carries the quote (UK) (search extract) — https://www.ceredigion.llyw.cymru/media/u4wbgrx0/notification-of-cooling-towers-and-evaporative-condensers-guidance-notes.pdf ; https://scambs.gov.uk/environmental-health/cooling-towers
- HSE's 2024 HSG274 Part 1 update bulletin changed the record-keeping threshold wording to "if there are five employees or fewer". It flags the DPD No. 1 test and says "pH correction of free-halogen test results is a vital part in ensuring that you have sufficient biocide in your system" (UK) (search extract) — https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/UKHSE/bulletins/393edc2
- Enforcement is real and reaches both operators and contractors: see Bulmer (£300k each), Faltec (£800k), Chromalloy (£110k) and Eaton/Aegis above (UK) (search extract) — https://www.coolingpost.com/uk-news/800000-fine-for-ld-outbreak/ ; https://www.hsmsearch.com/Double-sentence-for-Legionella-failure
- Public buyers require LCA-accredited contractors in Legionella tenders (UK) (search extract) — https://www.contractsfinder.service.gov.uk/Notice/30ba555e-a9df-494b-ab2e-ea3f924f5177
- No insurer requirement specific to cooling-tower dosing records was found.

## Researcher observations
- Strong on c4 (statutory notification, HSG274 weekly checks, a long list of UK prosecutions with £50k–£800k fines) and on outbreak evidence that interrupted biocide delivery is a real failure mode.
- Weak on per-incident cost below outbreak level and on frequency of "drum ran dry" events. Neither has a sourced figure.
- Competition is significant: dosing controllers (ProMinent, JUMO) already log and alarm, and US chemical suppliers (State Industrial, ChemREADY) bundle tank-level telemetry with supply. The product would need to target sites whose existing controllers have no remote tank or pump alarm.
- A better buyer may be the water-treatment contractor or chemical supplier, who visits monthly, sells the chemicals, can be prosecuted (Aegis), and gains reorder automation. An OEM/white-label kit for UK LCA-accredited contractors looks like the most credible channel.
