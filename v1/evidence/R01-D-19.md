## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Construction sites must provide toilets, washing water and drinking water (CDM 2015 Schedule 2). Welfare units (and chemical toilets / waste tanks) are hired out and usually serviced on a fixed, typically weekly, schedule. The idea claims busy sites can fill the waste tank or run out of water between visits, while quiet sites get visits they don't need. The idea's figure of ~£65–£120 per visit is **not verified**.
- Who suffers / who pays: Site workers (no usable toilet/water); principal contractor (legal welfare duty); hire company (emergency call-outs, wasted visits, driver time). Proposed buyer: hire company's service / operations manager.
- Sensor concept: Ultrasonic level sensor on the waste tank, level/pressure on the fresh-water tank, optional gas-bottle weight and generator run-hours, cellular (LTE-M); service-on-demand routing, pre-overflow alerts, proof-of-service and welfare records for the contractor.

## 2. Frequency
- Building Safety Group (BSG) reported a **47% increase in welfare breaches** on construction sites (Q4 2025 vs Q1 2026, drawing on ~10,000 inspections over six months); common inadequacies included sites with only cold-water taps and no designated eating facilities (UK; page is a BSG release on a third-party host) — https://bsg.barques.dev/news/bsg-reports-47-increase-in-welfare-breaches-on-construction-sites/ (search extract)
- Earlier BSG figures moved the other way: a **41% drop** in welfare breaches (Nov 2024 release) — https://bsg.barques.dev/news/bsg-reports-41-drop-in-welfare-breaches-on-construction-sites/ (search extract); and an **8% rise** (Q4 2017 to Q1 2018) — https://www.pbctoday.co.uk/news/health-safety-news/bsg-reports-8-rise-in-welfare-breaches-on-construction-sites/40302/ (search extract)
- The search summary stated that BSG cites **BS 6465-1:2006**, which recommends 1 toilet per 7 persons "on sites where workers are working a 40 hour week and where portable toilets are emptied once a week" — i.e. weekly emptying is the planning norm. The summary attributed this to BSG coverage but did not tie it to one result URL; likely one of the BSG/pbctoday pages above or https://theconstructionindex.co.uk/news/view/site-welfare-breaches-on-the-rise (search extract — attribution uncertain)

## Researcher observations
- Strong: the welfare duty is statutory and actively prosecuted (several HSE fines), weekly emptying is the planning norm (BS 6465-1 via BSG), and the hire company's own drivers already visit every unit, so install and channel are cheap.
- Weak: the prosecuted failings are about contractors not providing facilities, not about hired units filling up between services. The pain for the *hire company* (wasted or emergency visits) is unquantified. Portable-toilet fill sensors already exist (Sensoneo, Satellite Sense), so this would be an adaptation for UK welfare units rather than a new category.
- Better niche for the breeder: standalone **site waste tanks** (Site Equip charges emptying separately, weekly or ad hoc) and **tanker/vacuum-service operators** (e.g. GAP "Welfare Tankering Services") whose routing is the direct cost line; or events/festival toilets where fill rates swing widely.
- Unverified lead (no URL could be tied to it): a 2018 Unite survey reportedly found 40% of workers said site toilets and showers were not cleaned daily.
