# R02-D-30 — Vet practices run out of oxygen mid-anaesthetic because nobody saw the cylinder bank empty

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Many small-animal practices supply anaesthetic oxygen from cylinders on the machine or a small manifold. If the duty cylinder empties and the reserve is low or shut, staff must change cylinders mid-procedure. The patient may become hypoxic, and an emergency delivery may be needed.
- Who suffers / who pays: Patients, nurses and surgeons; the practice pays for spare cylinders, emergency deliveries and any complaint or claim.
- Sensor concept: Load-cell cradle under each cylinder estimates contents by weight, with nothing fitted to the gas line. LoRaWAN/LTE-M. Low-contents and reorder alerts, plus a pre-list check record per theatre.

## 1. Cost of incident
- No UK figure was found for a claim, settlement or complaint cost caused by a veterinary practice running out of oxygen. The idea's "~£2k–£20k" is unsourced.
- Context: a VDS submission to the CMA says client disputes are often best pursued through the small claims court. That points to modest individual claim values (UK) (search extract) — https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69c184887e02b81c0d1c76b6/Veterinary_Defence_Society.pdf
- Anaesthetic mortality baseline: a 2022 VetCompass study of 157,318 UK dogs having anaesthetic or sedative events found greater age, poorer ASA status and more urgent procedures associated with higher odds of death. It does not attribute deaths to oxygen-supply failure (search extract) — https://rvc-repository.worktribe.com/OutputFile/1574902
- Oxygen spend, the cost the sensor would sit beside: a UK vendor workbook models 1,200 cases a year with 680 L E-cylinders at £16 each, about £2,372 a year in cylinder oxygen against about £356 for a concentrator (vendor claim, UK; labels partly garbled) (search extract) — https://res.cloudinary.com/dh7irias3/image/upload/v1/65b9eea6e1cc6bb9f0cd2a47751a186f/cost-savings-workbook-pdf-1700735094
- In a Burtons testimonial, a UK practice says it was "spending around £8000 per year on bottled gas, almost 90% of which was oxygen" before installing a piped/concentrator system (vendor testimonial, UK) (search extract) — https://burtonsveterinary.com/procedures-care/anaesthesia/concentrators-piped-gas.html

## 2. Frequency
- An empty or under-pressurised oxygen tank is listed as a standard anaesthesia problem. Guidance: "check the oxygen tank volume and pressure prior to procedure and monitor the flow meter every 5 minutes" (VIN troubleshooting article, US) (search extract) — https://www.vin.com/doc/?id=8249951
- A retrospective audit of veterinary anaesthesia machines lists "Oxygen source: cylinder — improperly installed or inspection expired" among common faults (search extract) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7779558
- A case report describes a horse that became severely hypoxaemic during surgery when an oxygen concentrator failed. The theatre switched to a cylinder, and the horse later died of a complication. Supply failure happens with concentrators as well as cylinders (Veterinary Record Case Reports, 2018) (search extract) — https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/196569
- A specialist recommends swapping the machine and using a "reserve full oxygen E-cylinder" when a fault cannot be found quickly. Practices are expected to hold a full reserve (search extract) — https://www.vin.com/doc/?id=8249951
- US research-animal regulatory complaints recommend "two sources of oxygen be available during all anaesthetic procedures". This came after a primate death (search extract) — https://www.peta.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/2023-08-07-WA-DOH-VET-BOARD-COMPLAINT_WaNPRC-Macaque-Death-June-2023-002.pdf
- No incidence statistic (per practice per year) for running out of cylinder oxygen mid-GA was found.

## 3. Current workarounds
- Checking tank volume and pressure before the procedure and watching the flowmeter every 5 minutes (search extract) — https://www.vin.com/doc/?id=8249951
- Holding a full reserve cylinder (search extract) — https://www.vin.com/doc/?id=8249951
- Switching to oxygen concentrators or piped systems, which removes cylinder changes for the duty supply. UK distributors (Burtons, Atlas Copco) market these to vets (search extract) — https://burtonsveterinary.com/procedures-care/anaesthesia/concentrators-piped-gas.html ; https://atlascopco.com/en-uk/compressors/industry-solutions/veterinary-oxygen

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- BCGA Leaflet 22, "Medical Gases. Selecting a Medical Gas Cylinder Supplier", is aimed at dentists, vets and first-aid responders who buy single medical-oxygen cylinders. It followed an MHRA finding that some dental practices had been supplied falsified medical oxygen. It covers purchasing, not contents monitoring (UK) (search extract) — https://mrcvs.co.uk/en/news/22825/BCGA-releases-guide-addressing-gas-supply-concerns ; https://gasworld.com/story/bcga-releases-new-guidance-to-address-oxygen-supply-concerns

## 7. Competitors & market gap
- BOC/Linde LIV IQ: an integrated-valve medical oxygen cylinder with a digital display showing live contents, selected flow, time remaining, and visible and audible low-contents warnings. A Linde-era article describes an IoT version as a plan, not a feature (UK/IE) (search extract) — https://www.boconline.ie/en/gases-and-equipment/medical-gases-and-equipment/liv-iq-digital-therapy-device.html ; https://www.news-medical.net/news/20131120/Linde-Healthcare-introduces-intelligent-digital-oxygen-cylinders-with-integrated-pressure-regulators.aspx
- Air Liquide TAKEO/TAKEO2: an integrated cylinder with a digital gauge that shows time remaining at the selected flow and "emitting a warning sound when the oxygen level is low" (search extract) — https://sg.airliquide.com/medical-gases/takeotm ; https://au.healthcare.airliquide.com/takeo2
- Rotarex: remote monitoring on medical oxygen regulators via Bluetooth, or cloud over 3G/Sigfox (vendor) (search extract) — https://rotarex.com/blogs/remote-monitoring-medical-valves-medical-oxygen-regulators
- CylConnect: wireless cylinder scales and gateways with Wi-Fi or cellular, aimed at gas distributors and industrial users (vendor) (search extract) — https://www.gasworlddirectory.com/files/a81855a8ca/4287/documents/brochure-4pg_CylConnect-Pulsa.pdf
- Praxair: a wireless pressure-monitoring service for cylinders (gauges, routers, gateways) (search extract) — https://www.gasworld.com/praxair-announce-new-remote-cylinder-monitoring-service/1159.article
- Substitute: oxygen concentrators and generators (Burtons, Atlas Copco, BERG OXYBERG) replace the duty cylinder. A UK vendor puts concentrator cost at roughly £356–£668 a year against about £2,372 for cylinders (vendor) (search extract) — https://res.cloudinary.com/dh7irias3/image/upload/v1/65b9eea6e1cc6bb9f0cd2a47751a186f/cost-savings-workbook-pdf-1700735094 ; https://atlascopco.com/en-uk/compressors/industry-solutions/veterinary-oxygen
- GAP (explicit): no product was found that remotely alerts a veterinary practice when its anaesthetic oxygen cylinders run low. However, (a) the big gas suppliers already ship digital-gauge cylinders with local low-contents alarms and time-remaining displays (LIV IQ, TAKEO), (b) Rotarex and CylConnect already sell remote cylinder telemetry to gas distributors, and (c) the market is moving to concentrators, which removes the duty-cylinder problem. The gap is narrow: a networked alert, for practices still on standard (non-digital) cylinders. A gas supplier could close it by enabling connectivity on its own valves.

- No price was found for the load cell or cradle itself. I could not cite a retail load-cell price in budget.
- Academic designs already estimate oxygen-cylinder level with a weight sensor and raise alerts for low level, blockage or leak. The concept is technically proven but simple (search extract) — https://archives.christuniversity.in/items/show/20190
- Physics caveat (my inference, not sourced): a medical-oxygen cylinder's gas mass is a small fraction of its tare weight, so the load cell needs good resolution and a per-cylinder tare. Cylinder tare varies by size and supplier.
- Certification: a cradle with no gas-path contact avoids medical-gas-pipeline standards. Whether a veterinary-only monitor needs medical-device marking was not researched; vet equipment is generally outside UK MDR (not confirmed).

## Researcher observations
- Weak on c1, c2 and c7: no monetised incidents were found, mid-GA run-outs are anecdotal, and the problem is being engineered away by concentrators and by digital-gauge cylinders (LIV IQ, TAKEO) with local alarms.
- The stronger buyer for the same problem is the gas distributor (BOC, Air Liquide, regional medical-gas resellers), who wants to cut delivery runs (CylConnect's "up to 30%" pitch). The practice is the weaker buyer.
- A possible adjacent niche: practices that have moved to concentrators still need a monitored backup cylinder, and the concentrator itself can fail (equine case report). A monitor covering "concentrator output plus reserve cylinder" may be more defensible than cylinder weighing alone.
- Buildability is easy (Dragino SN50v3-LB with HX711 at about €48), but measurement resolution on heavy steel cylinders needs validating.
