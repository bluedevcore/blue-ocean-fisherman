## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Freezer and blast-freezer stores sit on insulated slabs. Underfloor heating (electric mats or cables, glycol loops or air vents) keeps the ground beneath from freezing. If the heating fails and nobody notices, the subsoil can freeze and ice lenses can lift the slab. Floors crack, racking goes out of plumb, and the store may have to be emptied for floor replacement.
- Who suffers / who pays: The owner of the food factory or cold store, its engineering or maintenance team, and the users of the store. Insurers may dispute the claim.
- Sensor concept: Thermistor strings dropped into existing under-slab probe tubes, plus a current clamp on the heater supply or glycol pump, over LoRaWAN. It alerts on a falling subsoil temperature or a heater that is off, and produces a monthly record for the insurer and the refrigeration contractor.

## 1. Cost of incident
- Repair scope, from trade sources: repairs range "from just mending the underfloor heating system to removing and replacing the wear slab". If frost heave is caught before the structure is compromised, the standard repair is "to replace the entire floor and repair the under-floor heating system", and "frozen soils are remediated and re-compacted prior to re-pouring new concrete floors" (US trade press/contractor; attribution inferred across these pages) (search extract) — https://refrigeratedfrozenfood.com/articles/83915-what-lies-beneath ; https://www.manufacturing.net/home/article/13184088/how-to-prevent-and-repair-frost-heave-under-your-freezer-floor ; https://stellarfoodforthought.net/how-to-prevent-and-repair-frost-heave-under-your-freezer-floor/
- Worst case: the ground can freeze so that floors "heave and crack, resulting in significant repair costs and even the loss of the entire facility". Severe frost heave "can result in an entire building being condemned and demolished" (trade press; attribution inferred) (search extract) — https://www.plantservices.com/equipment/process-cooling/article/11288132/how-to-prevent-frost-heave-in-cold-storage-spaces ; https://facilityexecutive.com/preventing-frost-heave-in-cold-storage-facilities/
- Downtime: repairs "can take several weeks in some cases and obviously disrupts productivity" (trade source; attribution inferred) (search extract) — https://stellarfoodforthought.net/how-to-prevent-and-repair-frost-heave-under-your-freezer-floor/
- Insurance dispute (Australia): a business blamed frost heave in its freezer-room floor on failed under-room heating. The insurer accepted frost heave as the cause but said there was no evidence the heating failure caused it. Its engineer pointed to drainage, plumbing leaks and design or construction faults, and the claim was declined. AFCA criticised the insurer for making only a visual inspection, and the headline reports the insurer was made to pay (AU) (search extract) — https://insurancenews.com.au/daily/more-dough-please-insurer-to-pay-up-after-freezer-fight
- Gap: no source gave a £/$ repair figure. The idea's "~£100k–£1m" is unverified.

## 3. Current workarounds
- Monthly checks are the advice: "check your system monthly and understand that time is of the essence", and "A monthly check of the system's operation and effectiveness will ensure that any malfunctions are detected quickly" (trade sources; attribution inferred) (search extract) — https://stellarfoodforthought.net/how-to-prevent-and-repair-frost-heave-under-your-freezer-floor/ ; https://www.manufacturing.net/home/article/13184088/how-to-prevent-and-repair-frost-heave-under-your-freezer-floor
- Sensor practice: a freezer maintenance guide says "if your facility has underfloor temperature sensors, make sure they are working properly and program your sensors to alarm if the sub soils fall below 40 degrees Fahrenheit" (US; attribution inferred) (search extract) — https://stellarfoodforthought.net/how-to-prevent-and-repair-frost-heave-under-your-freezer-floor/
- Controller set point: an AKO anti-frost-heave manual recommends a ground-temperature set point of 5°C, with the probe "at the most unfavourable point and at an equal distance between the two heating cables" (EU manufacturer manual) (search extract) — https://www.notice-facile.com/en/manual/1721017/ako+5231
- Visual warning signs are the de facto detector: "cracks in the floor and a perceptible change in the floor's grade", tilted racking, and forklifts struggling on uneven floors (trade press; attribution inferred) (search extract) — https://refrigeratedfrozenfood.com/articles/83915-what-lies-beneath ; https://facilityexecutive.com/preventing-frost-heave-in-cold-storage-facilities/
- Gap: no source quantified the staff time for manual sub-floor readings, and none confirmed the idea's claim that SME factories skip them.

## Researcher observations
- Severity is well evidenced in kind ("loss of the entire facility", floor replacement, weeks of downtime) but not in money, and frequency is low and unquantified. Compliance pressure is absent, which leaves c2 and c4 weak, as the idea's notes expected.
- Heat-trace incumbents (nVent Raychem, Emerson EasyHeat, AKO) already ship controllers with alarm relays for new systems. The retrofit gap is older mat, glycol or vent systems, and generic LoRaWAN probes (about €65–€100) make the hardware easy to copy.
- The strongest angle may be insurance and claims evidence. The AFCA case turned on proving that heater failure caused the heave. Cold-store insurers' surveyors, FM Global-insured sites or the heat-trace installers (nVent and EasyHeat partners) may be better buyers or channels than the SME maintenance manager.
