## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: UK scaffolds used as working platforms must be inspected at least every 7 days and after events such as adverse weather, with a written report. Scaffold contractors inspect by visiting each site, and a storm creates many inspections at once. Ties or bases can move between inspections, and collapses lead to HSE prosecutions.
- Who suffers / who pays: Scaffold contractors (inspection labour, travel, liability); site workers and the public; principal contractors who must not use an uninspected scaffold.
- Sensor concept: Clamp-on tilt/accelerometer sensors on key standards and tie points plus a site wind sensor (LoRaWAN/LTE-M). Alerts on movement, a local wind record to show which scaffolds need a post-storm inspection first, and a digital inspection register. It would triage inspections; the law still requires a competent person to inspect.

## Researcher observations
- Strong: the statutory duty (7-day + after adverse weather, written report, 24 h) is clear and recurring; storm collapses recur (2016, 2019, 2024) and draw NASC/HSE attention; cheap LoRaWAN tilt sensors (€57–€130) exist; no scaffold-specific movement product found.
- Weak: the sensor cannot remove the legal inspection, so the saving is triage and liability evidence, not labour; fines found are small (£4k–£24k); digital tag software is cheap (A$1/tag/week), which sets a low price anchor.
- Better channel/niche for the breeder: scaffold **security-alarm hire firms** (already put wireless kit on scaffolds) could add a tilt channel; or target **long-duration, high-consequence scaffolds** (over public highways, schools, rail/utility sites) where principal contractors or asset owners (e.g. Yorkshire Water, which employs its own inspectors) pay for monitoring.
