# R02-D-27 — Vet anaesthetic scavenging left off or failing exposes nurses to isoflurane between annual tests

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Small-animal practices give general anaesthetics daily on isoflurane/sevoflurane. If the active scavenging (AGSS) fan is off, a hose is disconnected or flow drops, waste anaesthetic gas (WAG) enters the room. Checks today are an annual service/LEV test and occasional personal badge sampling.
- Who suffers / who pays: Veterinary nurses and surgeons (including pregnant staff) are exposed; the practice owner or group pays for controls, testing and any claim.
- Sensor concept: Differential-pressure sensor in the scavenging duct, a current clamp on the AGSS fan, and an "anaesthesia in progress" signal from the O2 flowmeter. LoRaWAN/LTE-M. Alert when GA runs with scavenging off or under-flowing, plus a monthly per-theatre COSHH record.

## 1. Cost of incident
- No UK figure was found for a claim, fine or settlement over veterinary WAG exposure (see "Searched for but not found"). The cost case rests on health risk and compliance, not documented losses.
- Health-risk basis: a 2021 risk-assessment review of isoflurane in hospital and veterinary settings reports dose-dependent effects on cardiac haemodynamics and pulmonary function. It says isoflurane can potentially cross the utero-placental barrier, and it calls for much lower release (peer-reviewed, global) (search extract) — https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33865128/
- A NIOSH Health Hazard Evaluation of a US veterinary hospital looked at 12 miscarriages among seven employees. It found one isoflurane overexposure (an employee recovering an animal after surgery) but could not link the miscarriages to work. This shows that an exposure concern brings an investigation cost even when causation is not proven (US) (search extract) — https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/77685
- Badge sampling cost (the current control): Burtons Veterinary offers a first set of WAG badges for £50 with lab analysis free (promotional vendor price, UK) (search extract) — https://burtonsveterinary.com/waste-gas-monitoring
- Older US badge prices were $39–$75 each including lab analysis (Clinician's Brief, 2005; dated) (search extract) — https://www.cliniciansbrief.com/article/waste-anesthetic-gas

## 2. Frequency
- Exposure: in the NIOSH miscarriage HHE, one measured isoflurane overexposure occurred during recovery after surgery. Other personal samples were below the lowest occupational limit (US) (search extract) — https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/77685
- NIOSH carried out a further veterinary-hospital evaluation (sevoflurane, 2024) after staff raised concerns. It advised pregnant employees, or those planning pregnancy, to share the SDS and report with their healthcare providers. Staff concern recurs (US) (search extract) — https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/172297
- Passive canister scavenging performance "varies greatly" between brands and between individual canisters (Taylor & Mook, JAVMA, cited in a US university policy) (search extract) — https://research.utsa.edu/compliance/_documents/_iacuc/IACP-Policy%20020%20Isoflurane%20Waste%20Anesthetic%20Gas%20Policy_Revision%204%2003-13-26.pdf
- No statistic was found for how often UK veterinary AGSS fans are left off or ducts disconnected. The frequency of the specific failure mode is unevidenced.

## 3. Current workarounds
- Annual service: a UK service provider (Vet-Tech Solutions) describes an anaesthetic machine visit that checks "airflow indicators, speed control, fan operation" and ends with a formal test certificate (search extract) — https://linkedin.com/company/vettech
- Burtons offers active or passive scavenging system servicing as a separate step from anaesthesia equipment servicing (UK vendor) (search extract) — https://dev.burtons.uk.com/procedures-care/anaesthesia/piped-gas-agss.html
- Badge monitoring: Burtons' badges are worn for an 8-hour continuous period, collected, and lab-analysed in about 14 days. Its annual service agreement sends badges out "when the monitoring is next due". This implies periodic, typically annual, sampling (UK vendor) (search extract) — https://burtonsveterinary.com/waste-gas-monitoring
- Badge limitation: passive badge sampling "may capture the amount of exposure an employee receives over an 8 hour time weighted average but is not useful in detecting specific leak sources, peaks in exposure throughout the day and ceiling levels" (Drexel thesis) (search extract) — https://researchdiscovery.drexel.edu/esploro/outputs/graduate/An-analytical-comparison-of-isoflurane-levels/991014632521904721
- Pre-use checks: US university guidelines recommend inspecting scavenging systems before each use and checking for leaks (US) (search extract) — https://ehso.emory.edu/sso/documents/isoflurane-guidelines.pdf

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- UK limit: a UK Veterinary Medicines Directorate (VMD) product information document for an isoflurane product states the limit "has been set at 50 ppm on an 8-hour weighted average". It says operating and recovery areas should have adequate ventilation or scavenging (UK) (search extract) — https://www.vmd.defra.gov.uk/ProductInformationDatabase/files/QRD_Documents/QRD-Auth_1290632.PDF
- Other limits are far lower: NIOSH recommends a 2 ppm ceiling for any halogenated agent over ≤1 hour, and some European 8-h values are 10 ppm (search extract) — https://ehs.princeton.edu/laboratory-research/animal-research-health-and-safety/administration-of-biological-and/safe-use-of-isoflurane-animal-research ; https://www.uhs.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/GuidanceforWasteAnestheticGases062916.pdf
- COSHH re-commissioning (human healthcare context): BeaconMedæs states that "Under COSHH regulations these systems require re-commissioning on an annual basis" for AGSS/LEV (UK vendor leaflet) (search extract) — https://acprodbponlinebcc5.blob.core.windows.net/bp-public-files/bp_editor_div_mgs/SalesMarketing/Bmed_AGSS_LEV_HTM_Sales_Leaflet_EN_4233500129.pdf
- MHRA has issued a safety alert on active AGSS, which "incorporate a mechanical pump to assist the flow of waste gas". It concerns human-theatre equipment and a gap in the standard on pressure relief (UK regulator) (search extract) — https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5485ac3c40f0b602440002a5/con076137.pdf
- RCVS PSS: a supplier article says the RCVS added anaesthetic-gas requirements to the PSS environmental standards, which aim to cut gas usage. The search did not surface the RCVS's own wording on scavenging tests or exposure monitoring (UK, second-hand) (search extract) — https://www.nvsweb.co.uk/?p=28741
- Not found: the HSE EH40 entry, an HSE veterinary WAG guidance page, and the exact LEV thorough-examination interval (14 months under COSHH reg. 9). None appeared in results, so they are not cited here.

## 8. Buildability (components, BOM, certification)
- Current-clamp node, priced: Milesight CT101 LoRaWAN smart current transformer at €75.90 ex VAT (iot-shop.de), €69.94 net / €83.23 incl. VAT (industry-electronics.de) and £81.00 (Innon, UK) (retailer prices) (search extract) — https://iot-shop.de/en/shop/mil-ct101-868m-milesight-ct101-lorawan-intelligenter-stromwandler-6989 ; https://industry-electronics.de/milesight-iot/ct101-868m-100a-smart-current-transformer-lorawan-primary-druckansicht-1156620.htm ; https://www.innon.com/products/ct101-868m-smart-current-transformer
- Caution: the CT101 is a 100 A-primary device. A small AGSS fan draws well under 1 A, so on/off detection needs a lower-range CT or a different sensing method. This is my inference, not sourced.
- Pressure node, priced (gauge, not differential): Milesight EM500-PP LoRaWAN pipe-pressure sensor at €237.90 (iot-shop.de, marked not available) and €259.00 (another EU listing); A$399 ex GST (Australia) (retailer prices) (search extract) — https://iot-shop.de/en/shop/mil-em500-pp-milesight-em500-pp-lorawan-pressure-sensor-5044 ; https://iot-store.com.au/products/milesight-em500-pp-lorawan-pipe-pressure-sensor
- A pipe-pressure sensor in MPa ranges is wrong for AGSS duct flow, which is a few hundred Pa. A low-range differential-pressure element would be needed. No priced LoRaWAN differential-pressure node was found in this search budget. See the R02-D-32 evidence for low-range DP options.
- Certification: if the device only monitors the scavenging duct and fan and does not connect to the patient breathing system, medical-device classification seems unlikely, but this is not confirmed. An O2-flowmeter "GA in progress" sensor fitted to the anaesthetic machine may raise service-warranty or MHRA questions (not confirmed).

## Researcher observations
- Weak c1: the evidence shows health concern and investigations, but no monetised UK losses. The current spend it would replace is about £50 a year in badges plus an annual service visit.
- Strong channel: the UK vet-equipment distributors (Burtons, NVS, Vet-Tech) already sell AGSS servicing and badges. They could bundle a fan/flow logger with the annual service.
- Buildability is fine but needs the right low-range DP or low-current sensing. Off-the-shelf 100 A CTs and MPa pressure nodes do not fit.
- Better niche for the same problem: research animal facilities (universities and CROs), where institutional EHS policies already require WAG monitoring and inspection before each use. The many US university SOPs in the results point this way, and these buyers have EHS budgets.
- A better-evidenced angle is the RCVS PSS environmental standard: logging per-theatre gas use and scavenging could serve both sustainability reporting and staff safety.
