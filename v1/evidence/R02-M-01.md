# R02-M-01 — Hot-tub service firms make paid mid-stay visits to holiday-let tubs because they can't see whether each tub is heated, covered and circulating

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Independent hot-tub service companies look after many holiday-let tubs for absentee owners. They visit at changeovers, and sometimes mid-stay or on call-out, because they cannot see remotely whether a tub has tripped, lost temperature, had its cover left off, lost water or stopped circulating. A missed fault can mean a guest refund. Poor control can mean a Legionella risk for which the owner or operator is prosecuted.
- Who suffers / who pays: The service company pays for travel and technician time. The absentee owner pays refunds and carries the legal liability. Guests suffer cold or unsafe tubs.
- Sensor concept: A per-tub LTE-M node with no wetted chemistry probes. It has a clamp-on pipe temperature sensor, a CT clamp on the pump or heater supply, a reed switch on the cover and a low-water float. A fleet dashboard ranks which tubs need a visit and auto-builds an operational log. Technicians add test-strip readings in the app.

- Gap assessment: OEM systems (ControlMySpa, SmartTub, Connextion, Hot Spring) already give dealers or owners fault alerts, but each is tied to one brand or OEM and needs that brand's control pack. Holiday-let fleets are mixed-brand. A brand-agnostic, clip-on fleet monitor for contractors is therefore a real but narrow gap. A UK pilot (Sentinel) already targets it, and cheap software logs (YourSpa) exist. No prices were found for ControlMySpa, SmartTub or Sentinel subscriptions.

## Researcher observations
- Strong points: per-visit costs are concrete (£69–£225 per call-out, £65 an hour), refunds and complaints are well documented, and the Legionella liability is real (Piperdam £120k, Irwin Mitchell claim). The contractor buyer feels the cost directly.
- Weak points: OEM connectivity (Balboa ControlMySpa dealer portal, SmartTub, Caldera Connextion) already gives dealers fault alerts on newer tubs. A UK "Sentinel" pilot targets exactly this holiday-let fleet niche. The claimed advantage rests on mixed-brand and older fleets, so c7 should be scored with that in mind.
- A no-probe node cannot satisfy chemical testing (Jacuzzi's own caveat), so the HSG282-log claim is limited to circulation, temperature and drain or refill events. The visit-saving case for contractors is stronger than the compliance case.
- Possible better channel: holiday-let agencies (Sykes, Hoseasons) or holiday-home insurers (Schofields), which absorb refund disputes across thousands of mixed-brand tubs.
