## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Under JCQ rules, exam centres must keep confidential exam papers in inspected, approved secure storage with a small number of keyholders, and must keep logs of how materials are received and moved. Access records are mostly paper-based. Security breaches lead to awarding-body investigations, possible sanctions, and replacement papers.
- Who suffers / who pays: Exams officers and heads of centre (investigations, inspection stress); candidates if papers are compromised; awarding bodies (cost of replacement papers).
- Sensor concept: Reed contacts on the secure-room door and safe with a cellular uplink. Alerts on openings outside timetabled windows, and exports a tamper-evident access log for the JCQ inspection.

## 1. Cost of incident
- England: in 2018, in 24 cases exam boards had to mitigate the effect of an error, by replacing papers, issuing a correction before candidates sat the paper, or changing mark schemes (search extract) — https://www.tes.com/magazine/archive/gcse-and-levels-68-security-breaches-and-71-errors
- England (2019): Pearson replaced a forthcoming A-level further maths paper after it was found opened at a school already under investigation, and made separate arrangements for that centre's students. It found no evidence that questions had leaked (search extract) — https://www.tes.com/news/edexcel-maths-level-paper-replaced-after-new-breach
- England (2019): the Edexcel A-level maths paper leak was referred to prosecutors and investigated by police. Ofqual called it the "most significant security breach in 2019", and the individual responsible was debarred for life from involvement with Pearson exams (search extract) — https://feweek.co.uk/2019/04/23/edexcel-maths-a-level-paper-leaks-referred-to-prosecutors ; https://www.tes.com/news/police-investigating-criminal-level-maths-leak ; https://schoolsweek.co.uk/ofqual-investigates-allegations-of-malpractice-in-edexcel-a-level-maths-paper/
- Centre-level consequences: while an investigation runs, results or certificates can be withheld; if malpractice is confirmed, certificates can be recalled and declared invalid (school policy restating JCQ rules) (search extract) — https://www.stanchester-academy.co.uk/SiteAssets/Files/Policy%20and%20Statements/2022GM/Malpractice%20Policy.pdf
- I found no £ figure for the cost of a replacement paper, an investigation, or a centre sanction (see "Searched for but not found").

## 2. Frequency
- England: Ofqual reported 68 security breaches in the 2018 summer series, down from 114 in 2017, and 59% of the 2018 breaches resulted from schools or colleges opening (and sometimes handing out) the wrong paper (search extract) — https://www.tes.com/news/gcse-and-levels-68-security-breaches-and-71-errors ; https://schoolsweek.co.uk/big-drop-in-exam-security-breaches-despite-high-profile-social-media-leaks
- England: cases of "an actual or potential question paper security breach" rose from 14 in 2018 to 24 in 2019. The summary did not say which page carried this; the Ofqual Chief Regulator's Report (Sept 2019) is the likely source (search extract) — https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/853828/Chief_Regulator_s_Report_25_Sep_2019_final.pdf
- England: there are 6,011 centres for GCSE, AS and A level in England (Ofqual delivery report 2025: more than 1.3 million students and more than 17 million scripts) (search extract) — https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/ofqual-delivery-report-2025/ofqual-delivery-report-2025
- Reading: roughly 24–114 breaches a year across about 6,000 centres is well under 2% of centres a year. Most breaches involve opening the wrong paper at the wrong time in the exam room, which a door contact on the secure room would not detect.
- Newer threat: police investigated exam papers taken from Pearson and OCR systems in a cyber-attack and "sold online". This is a digital leak, not a secure-room one (search extract) — https://schoolsweek.co.uk/police-investigate-stolen-exam-papers-after-cyber-attack

## 3. Current workarounds
- Keyholders: access to inspected, approved secure storage must be limited to 2–6 authorised keyholders (OCR's ICE document), and one keyholder must be the exams officer (school policy restating ICE) (search extract) — https://ocr-live-prd95.cambridgeassessment.org.uk/Images/145317-ocr-instructions-for-conducting-examinations-ocr-ice.pdf ; https://richardhale.herts.sch.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Examination-Policy.pdf
- Paper access logs: an Essex school records keyholders on a "Secure Room/Storage Facility: Keyholder and Access Log" held by the exams officer. The summary did not say which of the school's PDFs carried this (search extract) — https://www.clarity.essex.sch.uk/_files/ugd/b163ac_b3753c0403b04f7fa74670473ca43f61.pdf
- Escorts: staff who are approved but are not keyholders must be accompanied by a keyholder at all times (school policy) (search extract) — https://www.mggs.org/wp-content/uploads/Examinations-Policy-2025-2026.pdf
- JCQ logs: a 2026 Exams Office/JCQ presentation cites ICE para 1.5 (receipt log with exact date and time of receipt), para 2.4 (a checking log kept by the exams officer), and logging of staff collecting materials from secure storage by name and time stamp (search extract) — https://www.theexamsoffice.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/TEO-JCQ-presentation-2026-Final.pdf
- JCQ paper-journey infographic: if no keyholder is on site, do not accept the delivery; it points to the JCQ Exam Materials Receipt Log (search extract) — https://www.jcq.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/JCQ-Infographic-Paper-Journey-Final-version.pdf
- CCTV is used in some centres (idea record). I did not search for CCTV prevalence.

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- JCQ "Instructions for Conducting Examinations" (ICE) 2025–26 is the governing document (search extract) — https://www.jcq.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/JCQ-Instructions-for-conducting-examinations-2025_6_FINAL.pdf ; https://www.jcq.org.uk/knowledge-hub/intructions-for-conducting-examinations/
- JCQ Centre Inspection Service: inspectors visit centres throughout the year to check compliance, and the visits are unannounced (per TEO). Inspectors view exam rooms and the secure storage room, and centres must "demonstrate the receipt, secure movement and secure storage of question papers and confidential materials" (search extract) — https://www.theexamsoffice.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/TEO-JCQ-presentation-2026-Final.pdf ; https://www.theexamsoffice.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/TEO-SLT-JCQ-CIS-Presentation-Jan-26-LDpptx.pdf
- For a new or changed secure storage location, confidential materials are not despatched until a satisfactory inspection has taken place, and JCQ CIS "reserve the right to charge a fee" where a further inspection is needed because of inaccurate information (search extract) — https://jcq.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Are-you-ready-Checklist-CoA_CoSS.pdf ; https://www.jcq.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/JCQ-Centre-Inspection-Service-Changes.pdf
- The secure room must be accessible throughout an exam series and available for inspection (search extract) — https://www.theexamsoffice.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/TEO-JCQ-presentation-2026-Final.pdf
- AQA: if there is a problem with secure storage, tell the awarding body and/or JCQ as soon as possible (search extract) — https://www.aqa.org.uk/onboarding-hub-exam-officers/question-paper-security ; https://www.aqa.org.uk/exams-administration/new-exams-officer-hub/question-paper-security
- Sec-Ed best-practice article on secure storage of exam materials, which a search summary says states the secure room must be for the sole use of exams (attribution probable) (search extract) — https://www.sec-ed.co.uk/best-practice/secure-storage-of-exam-materials
- Reading: JCQ requires logs of receipt, checking and collection, but the extracts do not show a requirement for an electronic door-opening record. A sensor log would be supporting evidence, not a mandated record.

## 7. Competitors & market gap
- Traka electronic key cabinets (ASSA ABLOY) record who removed a key and when it was returned, with a full audit trail and per-user key permissions by PIN, card or biometrics. No price seen (search extract) — https://traka.com/global/market-documents/brochures/en/traka-key-management.pdf ; https://www.traka.com/global/en/products/intelligent-key-cabinets/traka-automotive/traka-16-bit
- Salto XS4 Locker Lock: for "cabinets, boxes and show cases where access control and full audit trailing are required" (search extract) — https://saltosystems.com/en/products/xs4-locker-lock/
- Trade press: "Secure storage and access control" (HSM), covering lockers and access control with audit (search extract) — https://www.hsmsearch.com/?p=12880
- Overseas parallel: Tasmania's TASC exam-centre rules require a dedicated secure storage room with restricted access after materials arrive, and TASC must be notified if emergency access happens (search extract) — https://tasc.tas.gov.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/TASC-Exam-Centre-Memorandum-of-Understanding-MoU-Procedures-and-Guidelines.pdf
- **Gap (explicit):** I found no product marketed for JCQ secure-room access logging. But electronic access control with an audit trail (Salto, Traka, and many schools' existing card-access and intruder-alarm systems with event logs) already does the job wherever a school has fitted it to the exam room door. A cheap standalone door-contact logger is technically trivial; the gap is a JCQ-formatted export and timetable-aware alerts, not detection. Many schools may already log the door through their existing alarm or access system; I did not verify this.

## 9. Recurring revenue
- Exam series run every year (summer plus the November resits), and the JCQ inspection is ongoing, so an annual "inspection-ready log" subscription is plausible (search extract) — https://www.theexamsoffice.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/TEO-JCQ-presentation-2026-Final.pdf
- The value is concentrated in a few weeks a year (May–June and November). Outside the series the secure room is mostly idle, so the subscription value per centre is low (inference).

## Researcher observations
- c4 is real but soft: JCQ mandates keyholders, approved storage, inspections and paper logs, but not an electronic access record. c1 is weak: no £ figures were found, and breaches are rare (24–114 a year across about 6,000 centres), with most caused by opening the wrong paper in the exam room, which a door sensor cannot detect.
- c7: the incumbent is electronic access control (Salto, Traka, or the school's own card or alarm system with event logs), often already installed. The new part would only be timetable-aware alerts and a JCQ-format export.
- If the breeder keeps this idea, a stronger version might be a "secure storage pack" for independent and small centres, or for overseas Cambridge/Pearson international centres (Pearson publishes an international certificate visit report form: https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/Support/Quality%20Assurance/test-inspection/pearson-international-certificate-visit-report-form.pdf) where inspection is remote and evidence is harder. It could also combine door, safe and room temperature/humidity monitoring. I did not research these.
- Recommend a low score unless other evidence is found; the idea record itself flagged it as a long shot.
