# R01-B-04 — Emergency safety showers and eyewashes: unproven weekly flush, unseen real activations

> Method note: WebFetch was blocked in this environment (ENOTFOUND for all external hosts, incl. www.osha.gov). Every bullet below relies on WebSearch result titles/snippets only and is marked "(search extract)". No page was fetched.

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Plumbed emergency showers and eyewashes at chemical-handling sites are meant to be activated (flushed) on a regular schedule — weekly under ANSI/ISEA Z358.1 in the US — and each activation is evidence of either a test or a real chemical exposure. Weekly tests are recorded by hand on tags/sheets, and a real activation in an unstaffed area is not automatically known to anyone.
- Who suffers / who pays: Operators handling acids/caustics (injury, infection risk from stagnant water); the site EHS manager and employer (OSHA citations in the US; HSE prosecution in the UK).
- Sensor concept: Clamp-on pipe temperature and/or flow/vibration sensing on the shower/eyewash supply to detect every activation and duration, sent over LoRaWAN/NB-IoT; auto-builds the weekly flush log, alerts on unscheduled activation, flags long stagnation.

## 1. Cost of incident
- US: OSHA's maximum penalty for a "serious" violation rose from $16,131 to $16,550 per violation from 15 January 2025; willful/repeat maximum rose to $165,514 (search extract) — https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDOL/bulletins/3cc9f3f
- US: Same 2025 penalty adjustment confirmed in OSHA's own trade release (search extract) — https://www.osha.gov/news/newsreleases/osha-trade-release/20250114
- UK: Flowchem UK Ltd (Nottingham) fined £50,000 after an agency worker was doused with corrosive drain un-blocker; HSE noted eye-wash bottles were provided but there was no shower (search extract, HSE press release, Feb 2026) — https://press.hse.gov.uk/2026/02/13/chemical-company-fined-after-agency-worker-suffers-chemical-burns/
- UK: Granville Oil & Chemicals (Rotherham) fined £100,000 after a worker suffered chemical burns; findings included no drench shower on site (search extract, trade press) — https://www.hsmsearch.com/?p=16541
- Note: both UK cases are about showers being ABSENT, not about un-tested showers; I found no £/$ case specifically tied to an un-flushed or faulty-but-present shower (see "Searched for but not found").
- No reliable $ figure for the cost of a workplace chemical eye injury surfaced in search (see "Searched for but not found").

## 2. Frequency
- US: BLS recorded 42,286 occupational eye injuries/illnesses with days away from work in 2002; chemical burns ranked second (with abrasions) behind surface wounds/foreign bodies (search extract, BLS CWC, dated) — https://www.bls.gov/opub/mlr/cwc/nonfatal-occupational-injuries-involving-the-eyes-2002.pdf
- Spain: a cohort of 604 workplace eye injuries (2014–15) found ocular chemical burns were the second most common type at 12.68% (search extract) — https://ddfv.ufv.es/entities/publication/2d7844cd-13a0-4793-aa7e-8cb2a998d42c
- US: 29 CFR 1910.151(c) (eye/body flushing) appears on OSHA's FY2014 "most frequently cited serious violations in general industry" list (search extract; slide deck reproducing OSHA data, not OSHA's own page) — https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/fy2014-mfc-general-industry-12015/44133281
- US: ISHN (2016) reports that repeat violations for previously cited emergency shower and eyewash conditions "are on the rise" (search extract) — https://ishn.com/articles/82517-wash-it-out
- Frequency of the test task itself: weekly per unit — "shall be activated weekly for a period long enough to verify operation and ensure that flushing fluid is available" (search extract quoting ANSI Z358.1 wording) — https://ishn.com/articles/86572-eyewash-safety

## 3. Current workarounds
- Weekly manual activation recorded on a paper inspection form/tag by staff; examples of university weekly eyewash inspection logs (search extract) — https://www.unmc.edu/ehs/safety/lab-safety/EyewashWeeklyInspectionLog.pdf
- Digital checklist templates (SafetyCulture "weekly eyewash audit") replace paper but still rely on a person doing and ticking the test (search extract) — https://safetyculture.com/library/health-care/weekly-eyewash-audit
- Auditors detect skipped tests after the fact: a university lab-safety guide says if activating the eyewash produces rusty water or particulates, "it is unlikely the eyewash has been tested regularly" (search extract) — https://ehs.unl.edu/lab-safety-compliance-survey-companion-guide/lab-safety-compliance-survey-companion-guide-59
- UK/EU: supplier service visits — Hughes Safety Showers offers service visits every 3, 6 or 12 months under "Essential"/"EssentialPlus" contracts, with a site survey report per asset (vendor claim, search extract) — https://hughes-safety.com/gb/hub/protect-your-investment
- Local alarm kits (flow switch + horn/strobe) are the existing workaround for "unseen activation" in remote areas, e.g. Haws 9001 "recommended ... where a shower or eyewash is installed in a remote or noisy location where user is unable to summon help" (search extract) — https://costcutindustrial.ca/product/haws-9001-emergency-shower-eyewash-alarm-system-each/
- Labour cost of the weekly round: not found (see below).

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- US: OSHA 29 CFR 1910.151(c) requires suitable facilities for quick drenching/flushing where workers may be exposed to injurious corrosive materials; OSHA letters of interpretation index (search extract) — https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/interlinking/standards/1910.151(c)/standard_interpretations
- US: OSHA's standard itself does not specify testing; ANSI Z358.1 is used as recognized guidance — discussion of whether OSHA enforces ANSI Z358.1 (search extract) — https://jjkellercompliancenetwork.com/regsense/is-ansi-z358-1-enforced-by-osha
- US (California): Cal/OSHA 8 CCR 5162 requires plumbed eyewash/shower activation at least monthly (search extract, California AG document and Title 8 text) — https://oag.ca.gov/sites/all/files/agweb/pdfs/cci/safety/eyewashes.pdf and https://dir.ca.gov/title8/5162.html
- UK/EU: BS EN 15154-5:2019 (emergency safety showers — water overhead body showers for sites other than laboratories) is listed by BSI as current (search extract) — https://knowledge.bsigroup.com/products/emergency-safety-showers-water-overhead-body-showers-for-sites-other-than-laboratories
- UK/EU: Hughes states EN 15154 and ANSI Z358.1-2014 "call for weekly testing plus annual servicing" (vendor claim, search extract) — https://www.hughes-safety.com/gb/service
- UK: legal duty is general (COSHH 2002 duty to prevent or adequately control exposure); emergency showers are not named as a specific COSHH requirement in the vendor summary found (search extract, vendor page) — https://denios.co.uk/services/denios-magazine/emergency-showers-faqs
- Water hygiene: OSHA info sheet warns Acanthamoeba, Pseudomonas and Legionella "thrive in stagnant, untreated water" in eyewash stations (search extract) — https://osha.gov/Publications/OSHA3818.pdf
- Water hygiene: peer-reviewed study of 50 emergency eyewash/shower stations found 89% PCR-positive for Legionella spp. and protozoa incl. Acanthamoeba (search extract, J Ind Microbiol 1998) — https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/sj.jim.2900491
- Insurance: no insurer requirement for safety-shower monitoring was found.

## Researcher observations
- Strong: the weekly activation duty is clear in the US (ANSI Z358.1 used by OSHA) and the competitive set (Emerson WirelessHART, Honeywell, Apprion) shows large plants pay for activation alerting — but those systems are integrator-led and aimed at process plants with control rooms.
- Weak: incident money is thin. Found UK fines concern sites with NO shower, which a sensor does not fix. No insurer pressure found. The per-unit value of the weekly log is low unless sites have many units.
- Better niche suggestion for the breeder: US multi-unit sites (water/wastewater utilities, universities with hundreds of lab eyewashes, battery plants) where weekly flush labour across many units is the cost, sold through shower OEM service fleets (Hughes, Haws) or Legionella water-hygiene contractors who already flush outlets weekly.
