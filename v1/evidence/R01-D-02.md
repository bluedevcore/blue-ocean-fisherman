# R01-D-02 — Liquid nitrogen storage dewar boils down between weekly top-ups — cryopreserved cell lines and vials lost

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Standalone liquid-nitrogen (LN2) storage dewars lose nitrogen continuously. If a top-up is missed, a lid is left ajar or the vacuum jacket degrades (raising boil-off), the vapour-phase temperature rises and stored cell lines/samples can be lost. Checks are often a periodic dipstick reading or a temperature probe that only alarms once the LN2 is nearly gone.
- Who suffers / who pays: Core facility / cryostore technicians and PIs whose material is lost; the institution (core facility or lab manager budget) pays for monitoring.
- Sensor concept: Low-profile load-cell weighing platform under each dewar measuring LN2 mass and boil-off rate, LTE-M uplink; alerts on low level, abnormal boil-off (vacuum failure) and missed top-ups; level shared with the LN2 supplier; automatic fill log.

## 2. Frequency
- Peer-reviewed study (US, fertility cryotanks): in an intact tank a 10% LN2 weight loss took 4.2–4.9 days; warming to −185 °C followed ~3 h after the weight alarm; in a fast-loss simulation the tank emptied in under 3 minutes (search extract) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6504988
- Vendor document: an unattended/not-functioning dewar "will lose all of its nitrogen after 20 to 120 days depending on the brand and model and the condition of the tank"; in one highly insulated dewar the temperature rose "from -150 to 0 in eight hours" once dry (vendor claim, search extract) — https://www.networkedrobotics.com/documentation/Monitoring-Liquid-Nitrogen-Storage-Dewars-by-Weight.pdf
- Repeat incident: KI's internal review found "the exact same issue" (LN2 flow shut off during maintenance and not restored) had already happened in early 2022, before the Dec 2023 loss (Sweden, search extract) — https://universitetslararen.se/2024/04/18/a-chain-of-issues-reason-behind-freezer-failure/
- Canadian fertility-society guidance recommends LN2 storage tanks "be refilled at least once a week" — i.e. a weekly task per tank (Canada, search extract) — https://cfas.ca/_Library/_documents/CFAS-Guidance-Document-on-Cryo-Storage-June-2018.pdf
- Litigation scale from one event: more than 550 legal actions followed the 2018 Pacific Fertility tank failure (US, search extract, title) — https://www.fertilitybridge.com/news-articles/chart-inc-pfc-tank-failure-settlement-lawsuits
- No source found giving a per-dewar annual failure rate (see "Searched for but not found").

## 3. Current workarounds
- US patent on LN2 level monitoring states standard practice "has included daily visual inspection, dipstick level checks, and electronic alarms", and that electronic monitoring is "performed almost exclusively by thermocouple probes", which can miss a rapid leak because residual vapour keeps the reading acceptable (search extract) — https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10753821
- University of Wisconsin BME project: "Temperature probes are widely used but fail to send alarms until after liquid nitrogen levels are nearly depleted" (US, search extract) — https://bmedesign.engr.wisc.edu/projects/s19/liquid_nitrogen_alarm
- Supplier-side workaround: BOC's Cryospeed service offers scheduled, hands-free LN2 delivery with drops planned around the customer's usage pattern; lab dewars 2–50 L (Ireland brochure, search extract) — https://www.boconline.ie/en/images/BOC-Cryospeed-Brochure_tcm674-60679.pdf
- Large/auto-fill sites rely on bulk-tank auto-fill, which itself failed at KI when a bulk-tank valve was closed during external service work and not reopened; the SMS alarms "got stuck in the server and never arrived" (Sweden, search extract) — https://news.ki.se/several-causes-of-the-freezer-failure-according-to-internal-report
- Vacuum-failure detection today is visual: manuals cite heavy frost/sweating on the outer shell and faster-than-normal LN2 consumption as signs of lost vacuum (search extract) — https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0268/0428/9635/files/11624417-T_MNL_OPERATING_INSTR_LN2_DEWARS.pdf ; https://www.cfht.hawaii.edu/Instruments/Spectroscopy/FTS/section2_7_2.html

## Researcher observations
- Strong: cost of loss is very well sourced at the catastrophic end (KI SEK 30m rebuild fund; $15m US verdict), and the technical case for weight over temperature is peer-reviewed (alarm days earlier in slow loss).
- Weak: weight-based dewar monitoring is not novel — Networked Robotics, CryoScout/CryoGuardian and Columbia's deployment already exist; wireless level/temperature sensors (Planer, Sonicu, Fisher) are cheap ($80–$213/yr subscriptions). Differentiation would rest on price, cellular simplicity and supplier integration.
- The two biggest documented losses (KI, Pacific Fertility) were auto-fill / bulk-supply or controller failures, not missed manual top-ups of standalone dewars — the idea's specific "weekly top-up missed" framing is less evidenced than "auto-fill/supply interruption undetected".
- Better niche for the breeder: IVF/fertility clinics (regulated, litigated, CFAS requires level or temperature sensors with external call-out) and hospital/NHS cryostores; or sell the boil-off feed to the LN2 supplier as vendor-managed inventory.
