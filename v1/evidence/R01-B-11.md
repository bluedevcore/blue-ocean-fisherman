# R01-B-11 — Warehouse sprinkler pump-house weekly test log faked or skipped, so the insurer challenges the claim

Note on method: WebFetch is blocked in this environment, so no page was fetched. Every bullet below
relies on WebSearch result titles, snippets or the search tool's summary of them, and is marked
"(search extract)". Where the search summary did not make clear which of several listed URLs carried a

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Sprinkler installations to BS EN 12845 / LPC Rules (TB203) need a weekly pump start test, diesel run, fuel/oil checks and pressure readings, all recorded. On lightly staffed distribution centres these checks can be skipped or recorded inaccurately. Frost in the pump house, low diesel or a flat starter battery can go unnoticed until a fire.
- Who suffers / who pays: DC facilities teams, landlords and tenants (stock), and property insurers. The payer would be the site facilities manager or property manager.
- Sensor concept: Pressure transducers on the installation/trunk main, vibration/current detection of jockey and main pump runs, diesel tank level, pump-house temperature and starter-battery voltage. NB-IoT/LTE-M. Builds the weekly test record automatically, alerts on a missed test, frost or low fuel, and can be shared with the contractor and insurer.

## 1. Cost of incident
- Primark is reported to be "seeking about £50m in compensation" three years after a fire at its Leicestershire distribution centre. The fire destroyed half its stock in 15 minutes, and the sprinkler system failed to activate (cause not stated in the extract) (UK) (search extract) — https://www.retail-week.com/primark-seeks-and16350m-in-damages-for-distribution-centre-fire/1845038.article
- ASOS Barnsley warehouse fire: "the fire destroyed around 20% – some £22m worth – of the company's stock". The headline says the arson was "less destructive thanks to sprinklers" (UK) (search extract) — https://www.ifsecglobal.com/fire/asos-arson-less-destructive-thanks-sprinklers/amp/
- The search summary gives an estimate that warehouses without sprinklers cause "a direct financial loss to business of £230m per year". The summary did not say which result carried it; the BAFSA item is the likeliest (UK) (search extract) — https://bafsa.org.uk/?p=75241
- Coverage denial (US): in Frankenmuth Mutual v. Fun F/X II (7th Cir., 2023), a notice-of-impairment exclusion barred coverage because the insured knew of a sprinkler impairment and did not tell the insurer. Headline: "sprinkler malfunction leads to $7M in fire damages" (US) (search extract) — https://theindianalawyer.com/?p=207697 ; https://www.carltonfields.com/insights/blogs/propertycasualtyfocus/imported/seventh-circuit-finds-notice-of-impairment-exclusion-bars-coverage-for-warehouse-fire ; https://www.businessinsurance.com/insurer-not-required-to-pay-for-fire-where-sprinkler-was-off-frankenmuth-mutual/
- Coverage denial (US): in Garvin St. Warehouse v. Bridgeway (Ind. Ct. App., July 2026), a frozen, broken sprinkler line went unreported and the court agreed the insurers could deny the fire claim. Headline: "Ascot and insurers can deny fire claim without proving harm" (US) (search extract) — https://www.butler.legal/indiana-appeals-court-upholds-insurers-denial-of-warehouse-fire-claim-over-sprinkler-notice-requirement/ ; https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/risk-compliance-legal/ascot-and-insurers-can-deny-fire-claim-without-proving-harm-582654.aspx
- UK precedent described by BAFSA: a 2005 fire at a Magna Park (Lutterworth) warehouse. Sprinklers failed when a common water supply was cut by damage to underground pipes. Insurers relied on an exclusion for failing to report impairments, and this was "confirmed on appeal". BAFSA adds that property insurers "invariably impose obligations on insured persons via policy wording, either as a warranty or a condition precedent". The case is not named in the extract (UK) (search extract) — https://www.bafsa.org.uk/sprinkler-news/news/sprinkler-system-maintenance

## 3. Current workarounds
- Insurer test cards kept in the pump house. The Allianz Automatic Sprinkler System Pump Test Card covers a 30-minute diesel run and a 15-minute electric run, pump run time, engine oil level, total hours run, fuel top-up, and notification to the Fire Authority or Alarm Receiving Centre before the test (UK) (search extract) — https://riskmanagement.allianz.co.uk/content/dam/onemarketing/azuk/allianzcouk/risk-management/docs/automatic-sprinkler-system-pump-test-card_acom102_12.pdf ; https://www.allianz.co.uk/content/dam/onemarketing/azuk/allianzcouk/risk-management/docs/automatic-sprinkler-system-pump-test-card_acom102.pdf

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- The FPA says weekly pump testing "is required by standards BS EN 12845, clause 20, and LPC Technical Bulletin TB203". Electric pumps run "a minimum of 15 minutes"; diesel engines "shall be run for 30 minutes, or for the time/interval recommended by the supplier, whichever is the greater" (UK) (search extract) — https://www.thefpa.co.uk/advice-and-guidance/advice-and-guidance-articles/sprinkler-pump-maintenance-requirements
- FPA explainer on the TB203 changes (title only) (UK) (search extract) — https://www.thefpa.co.uk/news/lpc-sprinkler-rules-technical-bulletin-203-understanding-the-changes
- Under TB203 the servicing contractor checks tank ball valves and tank condition quarterly, and a detailed tank inspection is due every two years by a specialist (UK) (search extract) — https://www.johnsoncontrols.co.uk/building-insights/blogs/sprinkler-tank-inspections
- BS EN 12845 requires a quarterly inspection of heads, pipework and supports and a flow test. The summary drew this from a BAFSA-linked guide (UK) (search extract) — https://bafsa.org.uk/assets/Downloads/BIF16b.pdf
- Pump-house temperature: minimum "4°C, except in sprinkler pump houses containing diesel engines where 10°C is required" (UK fire authority guide). The Allianz card sets the same thresholds (search extract) — https://www.dwfire.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/BIF27_A-guide-for-owners-and-occupiers.pdf ; https://riskmanagement.allianz.co.uk/content/dam/onemarketing/azuk/allianzcouk/risk-management/docs/automatic-sprinkler-system-pump-test-card_acom102_12.pdf
- Insurer winter precautions: an Aviva loss prevention standard checklist for sprinkler winter precautions exists as a SafetyCulture template (title only) (UK) (search extract) — https://public-library.safetyculture.io/products/sprinkler-systems-winter-precautions-checklist-aviva-loss-prevention-standard-v10
- Insurer policy terms: warranties or conditions precedent on sprinkler maintenance (BAFSA, above) (UK) (search extract) — https://www.bafsa.org.uk/sprinkler-news/news/sprinkler-system-maintenance
- Independent inspection: the periodic impartial inspection "cannot be done by the owner, occupier, insurer, installer, or the maintenance provider itself" (UK) (search extract) — https://governmentbusiness.co.uk/node/30958
- An automated-testing standard exists. FM Approvals' new standard FM 1330, "Fire Pump Monitoring and Automated Testing", is intended to improve reliability and reduce inspection and testing staff time (US/global) (search extract) — https://fmapprovals.com/Resources/Insights/FM-1330

## Researcher observations
- Strong c4: BS EN 12845 clause 20 and TB203 mandate weekly tests (diesel 30 min). Insurers print the test cards and bind policies with warranties or conditions precedent. FM 1330 now standardises automated pump monitoring and testing.
- Strong c1 at the tail (Primark ~£50m claim, ASOS £22m stock), but fires are rare per site. The per-site value case rests on insurance terms and labour, not frequent incidents.
- Competition: pump OEMs (SPP FireEye, Peerless FireConnect) already offer remote monitoring, and FM 1330 creates an approval bar that a start-up must clear. The opening is more likely a retrofit kit for existing pump sets, sold through LPS 1048 maintenance contractors.
- Better buyer or channel: insurers' risk engineering teams (Allianz, Aviva, Ecclesiastical already issue test cards), or landlords and fund managers with large DC portfolios, who could mandate monitoring across tenants.
