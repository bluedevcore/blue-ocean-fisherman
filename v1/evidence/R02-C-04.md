# R02-C-04 — Kitchen extract ducts cleaned on a guessed TR19 interval because nobody records daily hours of use; insurers contest grease-fire claims

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: BESA TR19 Grease links kitchen-extract cleaning frequency to daily hours of use
  (roughly 12-monthly for light, 6-monthly for moderate, 3-monthly for heavy use), and many
  insurers make TR19 cleaning a policy condition. Usage is usually estimated verbally when the
  cleaning contract is set, so the interval may be too long. After a duct fire, the insurer may
  argue the interval was wrong.
- Who suffers / who pays: Restaurant, pub and hotel operators (fire damage, closure, disputed
  claims); kitchen staff and occupants of flats above.
- Sensor concept: Current clamp on the extract-fan motor plus a duct thermistor near the canopy;
  LTE-M logs daily run-hours and heat load, works out the TR19 interval, alerts when a clean is
  due or the fan fails, and keeps evidence for the insurer.

## 3. Current workarounds
- Contractor sets the interval from stated hours; guides repeat the TR19 bands: heavy (12–16 h/
  day) every 3 months, moderate (6–12 h) every 6 months, light (2–6 h) every 12 months, as
  minimums (search extract) — https://fanrescue.co.uk/how-often-to-clean-commercial-kitchen-ducts/
- Post-clean evidence: providers supply grease-thickness measurements, photos and a TR19
  compliance certificate (attribution approximate) (search extract) —

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- BESA: for kitchen extract systems, TR19 sets a maximum grease deposit threshold of 200 microns
  (search extract) — https://www.thebesa.com/besa-blogs/kitchen-extract-system-cleaning
- TR19 uses a 200 μm mean across the system; any single reading above 500 μm calls for urgent
  local cleaning; measured with a deposit thickness test (attribution approximate) (search
  extract) — https://www.thebesa.com/besa-blogs/ventilation-hygiene-standards
- BESA: TR19 gives "a structured framework to help building operators, facilities managers, and
  contractors schedule inspections and cleaning activities based on risk, system type, and use"
  (attribution approximate) (search extract) —
  https://www.thebesa.com/besa-blogs/duct-cleaning-best-practice
- Under fire safety law, the Responsible Person's duties include ensuring ductwork is cleaned in
  accordance with TR19 Grease (attribution approximate) (search extract) —
  https://www.thebesa.com/besa-blogs/ventilation-hygiene-standards
- Insurer condition wording quoted in trade release: the insurer "is entitled to refuse claims
  ... unless you can demonstrate compliance with all the conditions" (HVCA; cites a 2011 Woking
  pub fire) (search extract) —
  https://news.cision.com/next-step-marketing/r/insurers-get-tough-on-ductwork-fire-hazards,c9138179
- The Caterer: "Insurers may not pay out on pub fires if dirty ductwork is to blame" (search
  extract) —
  https://www.thecaterer.com/archive/insurers-may-not-pay-out-on-pub-fires-if-dirty-ductwork-is-to-blame
- FOS DRN-5074719: insurer declined a kitchen fire claim for breached conditions; ombudsman held
  it could not rely on a breach that did not increase the risk of the loss (fire largely
  contained in the duct) — shows outcomes turn on causation and evidence (search extract) —
  https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/decision/DRN-5074719.pdf
- Paine v Catlins [2004] EWHC TCC: hotel fire dispute over whether a monthly or annual cleaning
  obligation applied to the extract duct (England) (search extract) —
  https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/tcc/2004/3054
- Ireland: FSPO 2018-0097 — takeaway fire claim declined over a fryer warranty; breach of a
  condition precedent discharges the insurer even if not material (search extract) —
  https://fspo.ie/complaint-outcomes/decisions/documents/2018-0097.pdf
- Gas interlock (BS 6173:2009) already requires fan-running proof (current or airflow monitoring)
  in many commercial kitchens (search extract) —
  https://www.ducting-express.co.uk/attachments/product/1995/Commercial_Kitchen_Gas_Interlock_System_Kit_Data_Sheet.pdf

## Researcher observations
- c4 is strong: BESA TR19, the fire-safety Responsible Person duty, insurer conditions and two FOS
  decisions on uncleaned ducts. c10 is strong for London (many LFB pages, 2025 closures).
- The specific claim that insurers argue about the usage band is NOT evidenced. The FOS cases are
  about no cleaning or overdue cleaning, so the product's value is "proof of cleaning on time",
  with run-hours as supporting evidence.
- c7: Halton and Quintex already log run times and duct temperature, but only in premium
  demand-controlled systems. Retrofit to existing kitchens looks open.
- Best channel: TR19 contractors (CCS, Interduct, Swiftclean type), who could fit a clamp at the
  first clean and use the data to justify more frequent cleans. Extra-care/housing kitchens
  (Housing 21 tender) are an adjacent buyer.
