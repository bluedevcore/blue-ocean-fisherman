# R01-A-06 — Haulage depot oil separator overfills unnoticed and discharges diesel to surface-water drains

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Depots with diesel refuelling and vehicle wash bays drain through oil separators (interceptors). If oil or silt builds up past capacity, or the high-level/oil alarm is missing or faulty, oil can pass into surface-water drains and watercourses. Emptying is usually booked on a fixed interval rather than on measured need.
- Who suffers / who pays: Haulage, bus and coach operators (depot manager, SHEQ manager) bear prosecution, fines, costs and clean-up; the interceptor service / waste-tanker contractor is paid per visit.
- Sensor concept: Battery probe kit in the separator: oil-layer probe plus liquid/silt level, LPWAN/cellular backhaul; alerts on oil depth and high level; empty-on-demand reports for the contractor and an evidence log for the regulator.

## 1. Cost of incident
- Taw and Torridge Coaches (Barnstaple, Devon coach depot) were fined £25,500 and ordered to pay over £80,000 costs after diesel leaked from tanks at the depot into the Taw estuary; reported Oct 2024. Note: the source was the fuel tanks/pumps, not a separator (search extract) — https://www.gov.uk/government/news/taw-and-torridge-coaches-fined-for-diesel-leak-into-taw-estuary ; also https://www.iota.org.uk/blog/coach-company-fined-for-diesel-leak-into-taw-estuary
- Haulage firm DJM Logistics pleaded guilty to polluting the River Weaver Navigation with oil and was fined £15,000 plus £4,633.84 costs (≈£19,600 total, matching the article title "firm faces £19,600 penalty") (search extract; date not shown) — https://www.dailypost.co.uk/news/local-news/firm-faces-19600-penalty-oil-2877168
- Cavalier Haulage (Anglia) fined £2,000 plus £1,918 Environment Agency costs for oil pollution threatening an SSSI; officers found oil-stained sawdust and an oil sheen across the yard; Jan 2006 (search extract) — https://pick-upau.org.br/site_english/environmental_panorama/2006/2005.01.20/cavalier_behaviour_led.htm
- Nigel Rice Transport (Beverley) pleaded guilty after diesel sprayed from a vent during a fuel delivery and ran into a drain (Commercial Motor, 8 Apr 2004) (search extract) — https://archive.commercialmotor.com/article/8th-april-2004/14/diesel-flows-into-waterway
- An older PPG3 guide (quoted in search extract) warns that if the oil level alarm fails, "excessive oil could pass through the separator… This could result in substantial cleanup costs and legal action being taken under the Water Resources Act 1991" (search extract) — https://spelproducts.co.uk/are-oil-separator-alarms-a-legal-requirement-uk-legal-requirements-for-oil-separator-alarms/
- Observation on the evidence: the fines found are mostly modest (£2k–£25.5k) with costs sometimes larger (£80k+); none of the found cases was explicitly a separator overflow at a haulage depot.

## 3. Current workarounds
- BS EN 858-2 practice: separators serviced/inspected about every six months by qualified people, with a full integrity test at least every five years (vendor guide) (search extract) — https://gptenvironmental.co.uk/bs-en-8582-guidance.html
- GPT Environmental (an interceptor service firm) argues "there is no regulatory requirement to send a tanker to empty your interceptor of water every 6 months unless oil or silt levels are critical" — i.e. calendar emptying is often unnecessary (vendor claim) (search extract) — https://gptenvironmental.co.uk/interceptor-servicing.html
- Separator inspection & maintenance is sold as a routine contracted service by Adler & Allan (search extract) — https://www.adlerandallan.co.uk/services/assets-infrastructure/separators/inspection-maintenance
- Interceptor emptying is quote-based (CSG, Bay Oils); no public per-visit price (search extract) — https://www.csg.co.uk/industrial-commercial-waste/drainage-waste/fuel-oil-interceptor-emptying ; https://bayoils.co.uk/interceptor-waste-collection

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- Defra update: from 1 January 2021 it has been a legal requirement for oil separators supplied in England, Wales and Scotland to be fitted with an automatic warning device or high-level alarm (search extract) — https://bivdanewsletter.com/regulatory-affairs-newsletter/uk-government-updates-regulatory-affairs-newsletter/defra-weekly-update-wc-08-jan/
- SPEL ties the alarm requirement to the Construction Products Regulations / BS EN 858 (a supply-side duty, not an operator duty to keep it working) (vendor) (search extract) — https://spelproducts.co.uk/are-oil-separator-alarms-a-legal-requirement-uk-legal-requirements-for-oil-separator-alarms/
- GPP 3 "Use and design of oil separators in surface water drainage systems" (NetRegs, 2022 update) supersedes PPG 3; extract notes GPP documents are not endorsed by the EA as regulatory guidance in England (search extract) — https://www.netregs.org.uk/media/1899/guidance-for-pollution-prevention-3-2022-update-v2.pdf ; https://netregs.org.uk/environmental-topics/guidance-for-pollution-prevention-gpp-documents/gpp-3-use-and-design-of-oil-separators-in-surface-water-drainage-systems
- gov.uk still hosts "Choosing and using oil separators (PPG3)" guidance on choosing, installing and maintaining separators (search extract) — https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/choosing-and-using-oil-separators-ppg3-prevent-pollution
- Practitioner guidance lists the Environmental Permitting (England and Wales) Regulations 2016 and says the warning alarm should be tested as part of servicing (search extract) — https://www.adlerandallan.co.uk/knowledge/insight/how-to-achieve-oil-separator-compliance
- No insurer requirement for separator monitoring was found.

## Researcher observations
- Compliance pressure is real but mostly on the supply side (alarm fitted since 2021). Fines found are modest, and the bigger costs appear as prosecution costs.
- The competitive field is crowded for oil/high-level alarms (Kingspan, Labkotec, Aquasentry, Afriso, GSM/solar units at ~£300–£1,800). Any differentiation would have to come from silt measurement and need-based emptying.
- The stronger buyer may be the interceptor service contractor (Adler & Allan, GPT, CSG), who visits every site and is already pushing "don't empty on a calendar". A contractor-fleet tool for multi-site operators (fuel retailers, bus groups, supermarkets) looks more promising than selling to individual haulage depots.
- ATEX/Ex rating may be needed in separator chambers handling petrol, which raises cost.
