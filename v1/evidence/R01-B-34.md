## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Commercial property/theft policies for shops, trade counters and small warehouses can carry "protections" conditions (locks, shutters and the intruder alarm in use outside business hours). If a premises is left with a shutter partly up or the alarm unset, a theft claim may be declined or reduced.
- Who suffers / who pays: Business owners and branch managers (uninsured loss); brokers handling declined claims; insurers handling disputes.
- Sensor concept: Shutter-position sensor at the bottom rail plus alarm-set state from a panel output on a cellular node; alerts if the premises is not secured by a set time after closing, with a nightly compliance log for the broker.

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- "Theft Protections" condition precedent: a UK ombudsman decision quotes a policy (NIG) making the operation of locks, shutters and other fittings outside business hours a condition precedent to liability (UK; commercial; attribution likely to DRN-5385299) — https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/decision/DRN-5385299.pdf (search extract)
- Court of Appeal, Milton Furniture v Brit Insurance [2015] EWCA Civ 671: the policy required that "the whole of the protections including any Burglar Alarm provided for the safety of the premises shall be in use at all times out of business hours"; the insurer refused the claim relying on two alarm-setting conditions precedent (UK; outcome not shown in the extract) — https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewca/civ/2015/671 ; https://7kbw.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/20150707-MILTON-v-BRIT-INSURANCE-FINAL.pdf (search extract)
- Anders & Kern v CGU (Norwich Union): a business burglary claim where the insurer argued over the alarm condition (UK, 2007) — https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/comm/2007/377 ; https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewca/civ/2007/1481 (search extract)
- A C Ward & Son v Catlin: commercial theft policy with a **Burglar Alarm Maintenance Warranty** relied on by insurers (UK; outcome not in extract) — https://vlex.co.uk/vid/c-ward-ltd-v-794034653 (search extract)
- Insurance Act 2015, section 11 "Terms not relevant to the actual loss": a breach must relate to the loss before an insurer can decline on that basis; commentary uses a burglar-alarm requirement as a standard example (UK) — https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/4/section/11/enacted/data.pdf ; https://www.pinsentmasons.com/out-law/analysis/insurance-act-2015-changes-to-conditions-precedent (search extract)
- FOS applies the same logic: where an alarm requirement is aimed at reducing theft, non-compliance cannot be relied on if the loss would have happened anyway (UK; consumer policy; attribution likely to DRN-4644953) — https://financial-ombudsman.org.uk/decision/DRN-4644953.pdf (search extract)
- Shop-front case DRN-3358096: the insurer declined a claim involving a damaged roller shutter and window; the ombudsman upheld the decline on the evidence and noted that if the shutter was not in place the premises were not secured as the policy required (UK; commercial) — https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/decision/DRN-3358096.pdf (search extract)
- Most commercial policies also require theft to involve **forcible and violent entry or exit** (UK; AXA claims guide) — https://www.axaconnect.co.uk/siteassets/commercial-lines/commercial-claims/commercial-property/making-claims-clear_theft-forced-and-violent-entry_v3.pdf (search extract)
- US parallel: an Appellate Division court dismissed a fire claim where the insured failed to comply with a policy warranty on operational burglar-alarm protection (US) — https://www.barclaydamon.com/alerts/Appellate-Division-Dismisses-Fire-Claim-Where-Insured-Failed-to-Comply-with--Policy-Warranty-Regarding-Operational-Burglar-Alarm-Protection (search extract)

## Researcher observations
- Strong: protections/alarm conditions are real and litigated (Milton v Brit, Anders & Kern, A C Ward v Catlin, many FOS decisions); a nightly duty suits a recurring log.
- Weak: the Insurance Act 2015 s.11 and FOS practice mean insurers must link the breach to the loss and make the condition clear, which softens the threat; existing alarm systems with shutter contacts and ARC late-to-close add-ons already cover much of the job; the business burglary trend was falling to 2014.
- Unverified leads (search summaries not tied to specific DRNs): a domestic case where the alarm had not been in operation for at least 10 days before a burglary; an RSA business stock-theft decline because alarm sensors did not cover the stock area; an earlier-noted commercial case where the insurer relied on a protections condition requiring key-operated roller shutters plus an alarm condition. Opening the DRN PDFs listed would confirm which is which.
- Better angle for the breeder: sell through **alarm installers/ARCs** as a "warranty compliance report" add-on to existing panels (shutter contact + set/unset events already exist), or target **unalarmed or partly alarmed** sites (yards, compounds, trade counters with roller shutters but no shutter contact), where the gap is larger.
