# R01-A-03 — Small frozen-food and ice-cream distributors lack the transport temperature records quick-frozen food law requires

Note on method: WebFetch was blocked in this environment (ENOTFOUND for all external hosts,
tested on legislation.gov.uk). Every bullet below relies on WebSearch result titles/snippets and
is marked "(search extract)". No page was fetched directly.

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: UK/EU quick-frozen food law requires transport to carry recording instruments and keep dated temperature records for at least a year. Small frozen-food and ice-cream wholesalers running converted 3.5–7.5t vans may rely on cab displays, paper sheets or printer tickets; a mid-round fridge failure can spoil a load with no clear record of when the excursion started.
- Who suffers / who pays: Owner / transport manager of an independent frozen-food or ice-cream wholesale distributor; drivers; customers receiving goods.
- Sensor concept: Per-compartment air temperature probes plus door switch, LTE-M with GNSS, continuous log for record retention, alerts on rising temperature/door open, per-drop PDF, fault view for the refrigeration service agent.

## 1. Cost of incident
- UK enforcement case: Cardiff food supplier Global Foods Ltd was fined £120,000 (reduced on appeal to £80,000) for delivering high-risk food in a vehicle that was not refrigerated; the officer found "the driver didn't have any temperature control equipment on the vehicle" (UK; chilled meat, not frozen) (search extract) — https://www.duncanlewis.co.uk/Legal_News/Food_supplier_who_delivered_food_without_refrigeration_has_fine_reduced_on_appeal_(27_March_2017).html ; also https://news.wales/south/cardiff-council/global-foods-ltd-fined-120-000-2017-01-26545.html
- Load value indicators (theft cases, not refrigeration failures): headline "£5000 of frozen food snatched" — van stolen in Warwickshire while driver made a delivery (UK) (search extract) — https://www.coventrytelegraph.net/news/local-news/5000-of-frozen-food-snatched-3126480 ; a refrigerated van of pies "worth about £25,000" taken from Melmerby near Ripon, North Yorkshire, Dec 2024 (UK) (search extract) — https://amp.cbc.ca/news/world/pie-heist-uk-1.7401023

## 2. Frequency
- Swiss inspection campaign (~900 refrigerated vehicles): about one in six (17%) not holding temperature, ≥3 °C above the regulated limit; most failures in smaller local transport, worst in the 3.5–12 t class, with vans a large share of stops (Switzerland, 2015) (search extract) — https://www.coolingpost.com/world-news/17-of-refrigerated-vehicles-over-temperature/
- IIR corroborates: "in 17% of cases the shipments were 3°C or more above the regulated temperature" (Switzerland) (search extract) — https://iifiir.org/en/news/in-switzerland-17-of-refrigerated-vehicles-above-temperature-regulation
- Recording is continuous by law: Reg (EC) 37/2005 Art 2.1 requires recording instruments that monitor air temperature "at frequent and regular intervals" — i.e. every journey generates a record (UK retained / EU) (search extract) — https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2005/37/data.html
- Customer goods-in checks happen at every drop: delivery-record templates set a frozen maximum (e.g. −13 °C) to be checked and recorded per delivery (template, non-official) (search extract) — https://public-library.safetyculture.io/products/tpb03-delivery-record
- No per-van annual fridge-failure rate for UK vans was found (see "not found").

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- Commission Regulation (EC) No 37/2005, Art 2.1: "the means of transport, warehousing and storage of quick-frozen foodstuffs shall be fitted with suitable recording instruments"; records "shall be dated and stored by the food operator for a period of at least one year, or for a longer period taking into account the nature and the shelf life" (retained in UK law; EU) (search extract) — https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2005/37/data.html ; https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2005/37/oj/eng
- Instruments must comply with EN 12830, EN 13485 and EN 13486 (from 1 Jan 2006) (search extract) — https://iifiir.org/en/news/monitoring-of-temperatures
- The Quick-Frozen Foodstuffs (England) Regulations 2007 (in force 1 March 2007) implement this in England (search extract) — https://legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2007/191/schedules
- Northern Ireland 2007 Regulations: each food operator must make the Art 2 records available to an authorised officer on request (search extract) — https://legislation.gov.uk/nisr/2007/65/schedule/1/made
- Scotland equivalent amendment regulations 2007 (search extract) — https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ssi/2007/106
- General hygiene duty: Reg (EC) 852/2004 Annex II Chapter IV covers transport conveyances (temperature wording not confirmed from snippet) (search extract) — https://legislation.gov.uk/eur/2004/852/annex/II/chapter/IV/adopted?view=plain
- Enforcement in transport temperature control (UK): Global Foods £120k/£80k fine (above); Highland Council news items "Food Delivery Company ignored food safety rules" and "Food business fined for food safety rules after ignoring previous warning" (a hired vehicle found transporting high-risk foods without adequate temperature control) (search extract) — https://www.highland.gov.uk/news/article/9508/food_delivery_company_ignored_food_safety_rules ; https://www.highland.gov.uk/news/article/10703/food_business_fined_for_food_safety_rules_after_ignoring_previous_warning
- Customer contracts: supermarkets/DCs "require full, signed logs to accept deliveries" (vendor claim, UK) (search extract) — https://www.glaciervehicles.com/wiki/delivery-note-with-temp-logs
- No enforcement case found specifically citing the Quick-Frozen Foodstuffs Regulations recording duty against a small van operator.

## Researcher observations
- Strong c4/c9 on paper: a primary statute (Reg 37/2005) mandates recording instruments and ≥1-year retention, and comparable per-vehicle subscriptions (£18–£30/month) exist.
- Weak on c7 and c8: the category is crowded (Transcan, Carrier, Thermo King, Orbcomm, Microlise, generic telematics), refrigeration OEMs now pipe unit data into telematics directly, and a new device must achieve EN 12830 conformity.
- c1/c2 per-site evidence is thin — the Swiss 17% excursion rate is the best frequency datum; no UK per-van failure rate or ice-cream audit-failure evidence was found.
- Better channel: all-makes transport refrigeration service networks (e.g. Marshall Fleet Solutions) and van converters (CoolKit, Glacier) already sell loggers; an entrant would likely need to white-label through them rather than sell to owners directly.
