## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Fire services and insurers attribute most thatch fires to chimneys and solid-fuel appliances, through sparks or embers landing on the thatch or heat transfer from flues. Owners of thatched holiday lets and pubs cannot see how guests or staff run the stove between sweeps.
- Who suffers / who pays: Owners of thatched holiday lets and pubs, guests, and thatch insurers.
- Sensor concept: A thermocouple strap on the flue pipe with an LTE-M uplink. It would alert on overfiring or chimney-fire temperature signatures, count burn hours so sweeping can be scheduled by use, and produce an insurer report.

## 2. Frequency
- Aviva "warns of a thatched fire a day. Chimneys to blame", and puts the chimney-related share at 80%, attributing it to wood-burning stoves or open fires (UK; insurer) (search extract) — https://insurancetimes.co.uk/thatch-roofs-go-up-in-flames/1386882.article
- Devon & Somerset FRS attended 26 thatched-roof fires in a year, and "the vast majority – 23 – originated in the chimney" (UK, South West) (search extract) — https://www.ifsecglobal.com/global/thatched-cottage-fire-menace-prompts-chimney-safety-warning-from-devon-somerset-frs/
- A study of 103 thatch fires found that 82 originated in the thatch, with "a strong association... with wood burning stoves (66) or open fires (13) in use" (UK) (search extract; the summary did not attribute this line to one page; the likely source is the Building Conservation article "Thatch Fires" on stoves) — https://buildingconservation.com/articles/thatch-fire-stoves/thatch-fire-stoves.htm
- Burgoynes (forensic engineers, 2013): about 15 fires a year in thatched buildings, a small share of building fires but with disproportionately high losses (UK) (search extract) — https://burgoynes.com/articles/2013/12/fires-in-thatched-buildings
- A thatch-insurance firm investigates around 30 thatch fires a year, and totals swing considerably (UK) (search extract; the summary did not name the firm; the candidate page is shown) — https://hawkins.biz/?p=9146
- Note: annual counts conflict, from about 15 to about 365 ("a fire a day"). They cover different areas, years and definitions.

## 3. Current workarounds
- Sweeping: insurer guidance treats annual sweeping as "the bare minimum" for thatched homes (Jensten). Essex Fire says a chimney in regular use should be swept twice a year, and HETAS lists twice-yearly sweeping for wood-burning use (UK) (search extract) — https://jensteninsurance.co.uk/business-insurance/property-insurance/thatched-property-insurance/ ; https://essex-fire.gov.uk/thatched-properties ; https://www.hetas.co.uk/?p=384178
- Flue temperature checks: Suffolk County Council (August 2025) advises checking stove-pipe temperatures, because if they are "too hot... sparks can escape, too cool and you risk dangerous build-ups of soot and tar" (UK) (search extract) — https://www.suffolk.gov.uk/council-and-democracy/council-news/opinion-protecting-suffolks-thatched-treasures-from-fire
- Spark arrestors: Thatching Advisory Services advises against installing one, and to keep any existing one clean. NACS (cited by Northants Fire) suggests quarterly cleaning of a fitted arrestor on a chimney in regular use (UK) (search extract) — https://www.thatchingadvisoryservices.co.uk/chimneys-flues-and-fires ; https://www.northantsfire.gov.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/Thatched-property-fire-safety-booklet.pdf
- Not found: what holiday-let operators do in practice to control guest stove use, such as house rules or log limits.

## 7. Competitors & market gap
- Aduro Smart Response: a wireless two-sensor stove thermometer with an app that notifies the user when to add wood or "if the stove's temperature is too high or low". A retailer lists it at £311.99 incl VAT. It estimates flue-gas temperature algorithmically (UK/EU; consumer) (search extract) — https://adurofire.com/accessories/aduro-smart-response ; https://nationalflues.com/dev/product/aduro-smart-response-the-intelligent-thermometer-for-your-stove-sku-232-53315
- Tempsure WiFi stove monitor: a forum user called it "kind of pricy: $25 for a plastic mounting bracket and $55 for the probe" (US; about 2021) (search extract) — https://www.hearth.com/talk/threads/tempsure-wifi-stove-monitor.188760
- Hearth.com forum users have built Wi-Fi flue-probe solutions "under $150" (US; DIY) (search extract) — https://www.hearth.com/talk/threads/wifi-app-thermometer-solution-for-stove-pipe-probe-temperatures-plug-and-play-under-150.208308/
- Generic wireless stove-pipe thermometers with washer sensors are sold online (search extract; title) — https://www.desertcart.sg/zh/products/89003685-wireless-thermometer-for-stove-pipe-chimney-outdoor-wood-boiler-1
- **GAP (explicit):** Cheap consumer Wi-Fi flue-temperature monitors with over-temperature alerts already exist (Aduro £312; Tempsure about $80; DIY under $150). They target the homeowner standing next to the stove. What was **not** found is a landlord- or portfolio-facing product: remote multi-property alerts over cellular (no guest Wi-Fi), burn-hour counting for usage-based sweep scheduling, and an insurer-ready safe-use report. That gap is real but narrow, and a cheap incumbent covers the core sensing.

## Researcher observations
- The causal chain is strongly evidenced: chimneys and stoves cause most thatch fires (Aviva 80%; D&S FRS 23/26; the 103-fire study, 66 linked to wood-burners). Losses are large (£750k–£1m+ rebuilds), and premiums have quadrupled as insurers leave.
- The weakness is competition on the core sensing. Consumer Wi-Fi flue-thermometer alarms already exist at about £80–£312. The differentiator has to be landlord or insurer reporting plus burn-hour sweep scheduling, which no incumbent was found doing.
- Better channel: the specialist thatch insurer or MGA (PEN Underwriting, Howden Elite, Jensten), as a condition or discount on thatch-with-stove risks, which is the riskiest subset. Fire-service thatch programmes, which already push flue-temperature checks, are a credible endorsement route.
- Holiday-let operators with absent owners are the best buyer fit; single-home owners are at home and can use cheap consumer devices.
