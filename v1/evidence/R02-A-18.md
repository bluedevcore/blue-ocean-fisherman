# R02-A-18 — Waste-wood, green-waste and SRF stockpiles self-heat between twice-daily probe rounds and burn the site

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Permitted sites storing combustible waste (waste wood, green waste/compost, SRF/RDF) must, under Environment Agency Fire Prevention Plan (FPP) guidance, manage self-heating and monitor temperature on piles kept for longer periods. Manual probe or infrared checks are periodic and may miss deep-seated heating between rounds. Fires lead to clean-up, enforcement and insurance consequences.
- Who suffers / who pays: Site manager / operations director of an independent permitted waste-wood, green-waste or recycling site; neighbours; fire and rescue services; insurers.
- Sensor concept: Rugged steel lances with 2–3 thermistors at 1–2 m depth and a LoRaWAN head left in piles, a solar LTE gateway, alerts above ~50°C or on rising trends, and an automatic FPP monitoring log for the EA officer and insurer surveyor.

## 3. Current workarounds
- Manual probes at shift end: a gov.uk FPP alternative-measures case study accepted a 50°C probe trigger for processed-wood windrows, checked at the end of each working shift. Another case used thermal imaging of pre-crushed wood stacks with a 75°C trigger (UK) (search extract) — https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/fire-prevention-plans-environmental-permits/fire-prevention-plans-case-study-examples-of-alternative-measures
- An EA permit decision document describes a site using a 49°C trigger and cites a critical temperature of 94°C for a large wood-chip pile (10 m × 50 m × 50 m, 10–75 mm particles) (UK) (search extract) — https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a81ef96e5274a2e87dc0457/EPR-BP3739YW-A001-DD.pdf
- Operator FPPs filed with the EA set temperature trigger levels and monitoring frequency, e.g. Veolia ES Hampshire's composting FPP variation (Dec 2023) (UK) (search extract) — https://consult.environment-agency.gov.uk/psc/so20-6bu-veolia-es-hampshire-ltd-epr-rp3695hc-v004/supporting_documents/Application%20Variation%20%20Fire%20Prevention%20Plan%20%2019122023.pdf
- Fixed thermal cameras: a German waste facility installed two FLIR FC-Series R cameras over a 25 m × 25 m storage area with a 65°C alarm, on its insurer's advice (DE; vendor case study) (search extract) — https://www.flir.com/discover/security/industrial/flir-fc-series-r---german-waste-facility-case-study/
- US insurer fact sheet on wood-chip piles: monitoring options include "long probe thermometers, wireless temperature sensors, thermal imaging cameras" (US) (search extract) — https://www.acadiainsurance.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Wood-Chip-Piles-Fire-Prevention-FactSheet.pdf
- Not found: a source showing "twice-daily" probe rounds as the standard practice (the idea's claim), or the labour cost of probe rounds.

## Researcher observations
- Strong regulatory pull (EA FPP monitoring for piles stored 3+ months, permit questions about monitoring), real enforcement (permit suspensions, £26.8k–£230k penalties) and insurer pressure. c1, c4 and c10 look well supported.
- c7 is weaker than the idea implies: in-pile wireless probe systems already exist and one (PREVENTiT) is used by a UK wood recycler to win FPP approval. Off-the-shelf LoRaWAN probe kits (OneTemp/Milesight TS201, ~£55 per sensor) make a DIY route cheap. The gap may be price, ruggedisation and an EA-ready log for small sites.
- Better angle from the evidence: sell through FPP consultants and insurers' risk surveyors, and position the monitoring as an "alternative measure" that lets operators store longer or larger piles under the EA case-study route. That turns compliance cost into an operational benefit.
- Green-waste and compost sites (6-month storage, hot piles at 60–90°C) are a large adjacent niche where probes are already normal.
