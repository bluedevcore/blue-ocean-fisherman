# R01-C-06 — Utility roadworks: barriers left on site after the job ends trigger s74 overrun charges up to £10k/day

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Under section 74 of the New Roads and Street Works Act 1991 (NRSWA), highway authorities in England can charge utilities (undertakers) per day when works occupy the highway longer than notified. Disputes can turn on when a site was actually cleared, and signs or barriers left behind after work ends can count against the undertaker.
- Who suffers / who pays: Utility street works teams and their traffic-management (TM) subcontractors pay overrun charges and fixed penalty notices (FPNs). Council street works inspectors spend time proving or disputing dates.
- Sensor concept: GPS and motion tracker fitted in barrier or sign feet, LTE-M, multi-year battery. It would record geofenced "site live" and "site cleared" timestamps, match them to the Street Manager permit, and log overnight proof that signing was present (Red Book compliance).

## 1. Cost of incident
- Cambridgeshire County Council issued "almost half a million pounds" (£483,000) in fines to utility companies and developers for non-compliant and over-running roadworks in 2024–25. This total mixes overrun charges with other non-compliance fines, so it is not purely s74 (search extract) — https://www.cambridgeshire.gov.uk/news/almost-half-a-million-pounds-in-fines-issued-to-combat-roadwork-disruption
- Shropshire: gas, water and electricity firms were fined "more than £850,000" for late roadworks between April and September 2016, across 268 sites. That works out to roughly £3.2k per site, calculated by the researcher (search extract) — https://www.shropshirestar.com/news/2016/09/17/firms-fined-850000-over-late-shropshire-roadworks
- Shropshire 2017: utilities were fined "more than half a million pounds" since April across 224 sites (search extract) — https://shropshirestar.com/news/politics/2017/09/19/firms-fined-more-than-500000-for-overrunning-roadworks
- Black Country / West Midlands: the headline reads "Firms hit with £300k bill for roadwork delays" (2015). Only the title was seen (search extract) — https://www.expressandstar.com/news/transport/firms-hit-with-ps300k-bill-for-roadwork-delays-6332764
- Charge levels are contested between sources:
  - A DfT consultation summary lists proposed charges for traffic-sensitive streets in road category 0 or 1 of £5,000 per day for the first days and £10,000 from the fourth day (search extract) — https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a78fc8240f0b676f4a7cf65/summary-of-responses.pdf
  - The 2012 impact assessment describes an option to raise the maximum daily overrun charges on traffic-sensitive streets to £25,000, £8,000 and £1,000 by road category (search extract) — https://legislation.gov.uk/ukia/2012/2024/pdfs/ukia_20122024_en.pdf
  - The idea record's "£5k then £10k/day" may therefore be the older proposal level. The in-force figure was not confirmed.
- Low end: Knowsley's fee schedule lists "Section 74 Overrun charges (Cat 3 & 4 Non Traffic Sens)" at £250 (per day) (search extract) — https://www.knowsley.gov.uk/streets-roads-and-transport/roads/highways/highway-fees-and-charges
- From 5 January 2026, DfT amended the s74 regulations "to extend charging for overruns to weekends and public holidays". This raises the cost of a site that sits uncleared over a weekend (search extract) — https://static.hauc-uk.org.uk/downloads/HAUCEngland-Changes-to-Street-Works-Legislation-from-5-January-2026.pdf
- FPN level: the Street Works (Fixed Penalty) (England) Regulations 2007 set £120, or £80 if paid early, for failing to give a notice required under s74 regulations. These are the original figures and may since have been uprated (search extract) — https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2007/1952/made
- Signs left behind (Norfolk): coverage of Anglian Water's 165 overrun fines from Norfolk County Council also cites a maximum fine of £100 "where five or fewer signs are left on site and they are cleared within two days of the company being informed". So a few left-behind signs can attract a small penalty, while a full overrun is charged daily (search extract) — https://www.greatyarmouthmercury.co.uk/news/25349786.huge-roadwork-delays-caused-failure-collect-signs

## 2. Frequency
- Anglian Water received 165 fines from Norfolk County Council for overrunning roadworks. In some of the worst cases it "failed to remove road signs after the completion of works" (search extract) — https://www.greatyarmouthmercury.co.uk/news/25349786.huge-roadwork-delays-caused-failure-collect-signs
- The same story was carried by trade press: "Norfolk roadwork delays caused by 'failure to collect signs'" (search extract) — https://highways-news.com/?p=44292
- Shropshire overrun fines covered 268 sites in about 5 months of 2016 and 224 sites in a similar window of 2017. That is roughly 45–55 charged sites per month in a single county (search extract; rate calculated by researcher) — https://www.shropshirestar.com/news/2016/09/17/firms-fined-850000-over-late-shropshire-roadworks
- Gloucestershire added a public-reporting category for "abandoned materials", meaning signs, barriers and cones left on site after work is complete. This suggests the problem is common enough to warrant its own report category (search extract) — https://www.gloucestershire.gov.uk/council-and-democracy/latest-news-for-members-from-council-and-partners/new-way-to-report-utility-company-streetworks/
- No per-crew or per-utility rate (e.g. "x% of jobs leave kit behind") was found.

## 3. Current workarounds
- Councils rely on resident reports and inspections:
  - Gloucestershire asks the public to report utility streetworks, including abandoned signs and barriers. Its streetworks teams then inspect the sites and issue fines (search extract) — https://www.gloucestershire.gov.uk/county-council-newsletters/councils-connected/december-2024/new-way-to-report-utility-company-streetworks
  - Bromley asks residents to photograph the works and report them via its online system, saying whether they relate to a utility company (search extract) — https://www.bromley.gov.uk/traffic-management/roadworks/4
- Disputes are settled by evidence the promoter supplies. Buckinghamshire's overrun guidance says the works promoter bears the burden of proving that a physical overrun did not occur. If no evidence is provided, s74 charges are applied based on the information in the notice (search extract) — https://www.buckinghamshire.gov.uk/parking-roads-and-transport/highway-licences/permit-scheme-for-road-works-and-street-works/overrun-charging-regime/
- Councils' own contracts handle it by replacement cost. A Lincolnshire item reports that if contractors fail to collect signs and cones after work finishes, they must replace them at their own expense. The source attribution within the result set is likely this article but was not confirmed (search extract) — https://www.lincsonline.co.uk/grantham/news/concerns-raised-over-council-flytipping-after-roadwork-sig-9405171
- FPNs can be withdrawn on representations. Under NRSWA Schedule 4A, the street authority must consider representations and may withdraw an FPN and repay the penalty (search extract) — https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2004/18/schedules

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- NRSWA s74 lets a highway authority charge an undertaker where works in the highway are "unreasonably prolonged" (search extract) — https://legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1991/22/section/74/2019-10-25?view=plain
- Charging rules:
  - The Street Works (Charges for Unreasonably Prolonged Occupation of the Highway) (England) Regulations 2009 set the per-day amount (search extract) — https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2009/303/regulation/9/made
  - From 5 January 2026, overrun charging applies on weekends and public holidays, and FPNs for late notifications can apply each day (search extract) — https://static.hauc-uk.org.uk/downloads/HAUCEngland-Changes-to-Street-Works-Legislation-from-5-January-2026.pdf
- Gloucestershire states that "all signs and barriers must be removed from the site once the job is complete" and that non-compliance is fined (search extract) — https://www.gloucestershire.gov.uk/council-and-democracy/latest-news-for-members-from-council-and-partners/new-way-to-report-utility-company-streetworks/
- Safety at Street Works and Road Works (the "Red Book") is the statutory code of practice for signing, lighting and guarding:
  - The 2013 edition came into force on 1 October 2014 (search extract) — https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/safety-at-street-works-and-road-works
  - The search summary states that the code has had statutory backing since 1 February 2002 and that failing to follow its signing, lighting and guarding requirements is a criminal offence (search extract) — https://www.ciht.org.uk/news/safety-at-street-works-and-road-works-code-of-practice/
  - DfT has consulted on an update (search extract) — https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/updating-the-safety-at-street-works-and-road-works-code-of-practice
- Gateshead publishes a street works protocol page on enforcement and penalties (title only) (search extract) — https://www.gateshead.gov.uk/article/25094/Enforcement-and-penalties

## 7. Competitors & market gap
- **HRS Intelliframe (UK)** — the closest match:
  - It is a GPS-equipped electronic frame for permit, diversion and TM signs. It alerts when a sign changes condition or "when a job is about to overrun its permitting conditions", and can integrate with street works information portals to update roadworks status (vendor claim) (search extract) — https://ccsbestpractice.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/INTELLIFRAME-ELEVATOR-201120-V2.pdf
  - A contractor reportedly uses 100 units monitored remotely on GPS. No price is published (vendor claim) (search extract) — https://ccsbestpractice.org.uk/entries/intelliframe-system/

## Researcher observations
- The money is real and recurring but spread thin. County totals of £0.3m–£0.85m a year across hundreds of sites imply low thousands of pounds per site. The "left-behind signs" offence itself may be as small as £100 (Norfolk coverage). The big per-day charges apply only to genuine overruns on traffic-sensitive streets.
- HRS Intelliframe already pitches "alert before overrun" with GPS signs and portal integration in the UK. A newcomer would need to win on price per asset or barrier-foot form factor, not on concept.
- A better buyer may be the TM subcontractor or barrier-hire firm. They physically own the kit, lose it when it is left behind, and could use the same tracker for fleet inventory (as in the Zonar use case). The s74 evidence would be a premium add-on sold to the utility.
- Unconfirmed lead: a council meeting item in this result set (probably Wokingham, https://wokingham.moderngov.co.uk/mgAi.aspx?ID=37674) reportedly said there are about 5,300 roadworks a year in the borough, 75% by utilities, and that utilities do leave signs and equipment behind. The attribution was not confirmed, so it is not used as evidence above.
