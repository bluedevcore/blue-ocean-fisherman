## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: During sewer relining or repair, flows are overpumped around the work with temporary (often diesel) pumps for days or weeks. If a pump stops or runs out of fuel overnight, sewage can back up into gardens and homes or escape to watercourses. Contractors manage this with attendants and regular checks.
- Who suffers / who pays: Residents; the overpumping/rehabilitation contractor; the water company client (Ofwat sewer-flooding performance commitments, compensation, EA pollution enforcement).
- Sensor concept: clamp-on accelerometer (pump running), float switch in the upstream manhole (level rising), fuel-tank level sensor; LTE-M, battery; SMS/call cascade to the on-call crew and an event log for the client.

## Researcher observations
- Strong: the regulatory cost of an internal flood is well documented (GSS £2,000+; draft ODI ~£60k per incident per UU), and pump-failure pollution fines are frequent and large. The sensors are cheap off-the-shelf parts.
- Weak: no evidence was found of overpumping-specific failures, the attendant cost is unsourced for the UK, and the big hire fleets (Xylem FST, Selwood SelWatch, Pump Supplies) already offer telemetry with SMS/phone alarms — c7 is the main risk.
- Better niche: a fleet-agnostic retrofit kit sold to the water company client (or its framework contractors) as a contract requirement, logging upstream manhole level regardless of whose pumps are on site. Permanent pumping-station backup-pump status (the Yorkshire Water £350k case — backup pump dead for five months) may be a stronger, recurring problem for the same sensors.
