# R01-D-36 — Farm rodenticide stewardship — CRRU and Red Tractor demand bait inspection records that farmers don't keep

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: UK farmers who buy professional rodenticides must follow the UK Rodenticide Stewardship Regime (CRRU). Permanent outdoor baiting needs documented justification and re-inspection at least every four weeks. Red Tractor farm assurance audits bait plans and inspection records. Rats also eat and spoil feed.
- Who suffers / who pays: Assured livestock and arable farmers (the payer, at audit); pest controllers who service farms; wildlife exposed to secondary poisoning (e.g. barn owls).
- Sensor concept: A bait-station activity sensor (IR or lid) with a bait-take estimate, on LoRaWAN or LTE-M. It would keep an automatic inspection and activity log in CRRU/Red Tractor format and alert when bait is taken, so pulsed baiting can be timed.

## 1. Cost of incident
- US extension and trade figures: an adult rat eats about 1 oz of feed a day (about 23 lb a year) and contaminates more, so the total loss can reach about 46 lb a year. At $300 a ton that is about $6.90 per rat per year (US) (search extract) — https://www.thepoultrysite.com/articles/how-much-does-it-cost-to-feed-a-rat ; https://swineweb.com/how-much-does-it-cost-to-feed-a-rat/
- Mississippi State University Extension, "Controlling Rodents on the Poultry Farm" (P2824): repeats the feed arithmetic and cites Berry (2003), reporting USDA estimates that the real cost of rats' feed wastage and damage "is as high as $25 per rat per year". It also lists gnawing damage to structures, curtains, cool-cell pads, insulation and wiring (US) (search extract) — https://extension.msstate.edu/sites/default/files/publications/publications/P2824_web.pdf
- No UK (AHDB or similar) cost-per-rat or cost-per-farm figure was found. No figure was found for the financial penalty of losing a Red Tractor certificate over vermin records.

## 2. Frequency
- CRRU permanent-baiting guidance (2019 update, reported by BPCA and Killgerm): permanent outdoor bait points loaded with rodenticide "continue to require re-inspection at least every four weeks". Beyond that minimum, inspection frequency is for the technician in charge of the programme (UK) (search extract) — https://bpca.org.uk/news-and-blog/feature/critical-update-to-permanent-rodenticide-baiting-conditions/231859 ; https://www.killgerm.com/critical-update-to-permanent-rodenticide-baiting-conditions/
- Red Tractor vermin-control standard: in one sector version, "A site survey is completed at least quarterly", recording date, locations inspected, findings, actions required and date actions completed (UK) (search extract; the summary drew on several Red Tractor vermin-control pages and did not say which sector version carries this line) — https://redtractorassurance.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/VC.b_CROPS-_Vermin-Control.pdf ; https://redtractor.org.uk/assurance/standards/vermin-control-3/
- Council farm pest contracts are built on a few visits a year. Carlisle City Council's farm contract covers rats and mice with "four routine visits throughout the year plus one additional visit if we think it necessary". Gwynedd's standard rodent contract includes 4 visits and 4 revisits. Tameside's includes 8 visits a year (UK) (search extract) — https://www.carlisle.gov.uk/Residents/EnvironmentalProblems/PestControl ; https://www.gwynedd.gov.uk/en/Businesses/Environmental-health-and-trading-standards/Pest-control/Pest-control-contracts.aspx ; https://tameside.gov.uk/pestcontrol
- No statistic was found on how many UK farms run permanent baiting, or how often Red Tractor audits raise vermin non-conformities.

## 3. Current workarounds
- Paper bait plan under Red Tractor: "Where baits are used a Bait Plan identifies: location of bait points, bait used, bait point inspection, replenishment dates". The plan "must be kept up-to-date" and "kept for 2 years following changes" (UK) (search extract; drawn from Red Tractor vermin-control pages; exact sector page not identified) — https://redtractorassurance.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/VC.b_CROPS-_Vermin-Control.pdf ; https://redtractorassurance.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/VC_LM_Vermin-Control.pdf
- Contracting out: some Red Tractor items "can be N/A if using a professional vermin control contractor", but "Where an external pest contractor is used, a documented agreement is in place" (UK) (search extract; Red Tractor pages, exact page not identified) — https://redtractor.org.uk/assurance/standards/vermin-control-3/ ; https://redtractorassurance.org.uk/standards/vermin-control-13
- Council contract prices: Gwynedd annual rodent contract £250 + VAT (£300) for 4 visits and 4 revisits (2024/25); Tameside from £287.50 + VAT (£345) for 8 visits a year (UK) (search extract) — https://www.gwynedd.gov.uk/en/Businesses/Environmental-health-and-trading-standards/Pest-control/Pest-control-contracts.aspx ; https://tameside.gov.uk/pestcontrol
- Scottish Borders Council offers annual contracts for farms and says its paperwork meets farm and feed assurance criteria. Farm prices are not published (UK) (search extract) — https://www.scotborders.gov.uk/pestcontrol
- CRRU early-warning practice: non-toxic material in fixed bait boxes can detect new rat activity, but Dr Alan Buckle notes this "demands regular inspection". If non-toxic bait is taken and mice and voles are ruled out, it can be replaced temporarily with rodenticide (UK) (search extract) — https://www.farminguk.com/news/farms-alerted-to-end-of-routine-long-term-rodenticide-baiting_38918.html
- Labour cost of a farmer's own bait rounds: not found.

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- CRRU permanent baiting (2019): a professional user responsible for the site must "identify and document 'a high potential for reinvasion where other methods have proved insufficient'". Permanent baiting is allowed only with products whose labels specifically permit it (UK) (search extract) — https://bpca.org.uk/news-and-blog/feature/critical-update-to-permanent-rodenticide-baiting-conditions/231859 ; https://www.killgerm.com/critical-update-to-permanent-rodenticide-baiting-conditions/
- FarmingUK, "Farms alerted to end of routine long term rodenticide baiting": "Except as a justifiable last resort against clear and sustained threats to human or animal health, the UK Rodenticide Stewardship Regime rules out long term rodenticide baiting around the outside of farm buildings" (UK) (search extract) — https://www.farminguk.com/news/farms-alerted-to-end-of-routine-long-term-rodenticide-baiting_38918.html ; https://www.thinkwildlife.org/farms-alerted-to-end-of-routine-long-term-rodenticide-baiting/
- Red Tractor: an environmental risk assessment "must be undertaken before laying bait"; "Permanent baiting is not routinely undertaken and toxic bait is removed when treatment is finished"; all monitoring points, toxic baits and EFKs "are accurately recorded on a site plan". Grain-store baiters must have read Red Tractor's "Safe mouse control in a grain store" guidance, and the training "must be documented" (UK) (search extract; Red Tractor vermin-control pages, sector page not identified) — https://redtractorassurance.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/VC.b_CROPS-_Vermin-Control.pdf ; https://redtractorassurance.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/VC_CC_Vermin-Control.pdf
- Point-of-sale control: proof of competence is "either a certificate from a CRRU approved course, or membership of a CRRU approved farm assurance scheme". Farm-assurance membership documents count as certification at the point of sale (UK) (search extract) — https://www.nfuonline.com/cross-sector/science-and-technology/crop-protection/crop-protection-key-content/how-to-buy-rodenticide-in-2018/ ; https://www.thinkwildlife.org/stewardship-regime/point-of-sale/
- In 2017, 11 farm assurance schemes were announced as compliant with the stewardship regime for 2018 (UK) (search extract; titles) — https://www.thepigsite.com/news/2017/12/2018-rodenticide-compliance-for-11-farm-assurance-schemes-1 ; https://bpca.org.uk/news-and-blog/sector/page-67/eleven-assurance-schemes-compliant-with-the-uk-rodenticide-stewardship-regime/189766
- No insurer requirement linked to farm rodent control was found.

## 5. Buyer & budget
- Red Tractor says the bait plan "should be completed by the person with responsibility for vermin management", which is the farmer or farm manager, or a contractor under a documented agreement (UK) (search extract; Red Tractor pages, exact page not identified) — https://redtractorassurance.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/VC.b_CROPS-_Vermin-Control.pdf
- Budget scale: public council farm and commercial rodent contracts cost about £300–£345 a year (Gwynedd, Tameside). That is a benchmark for what a farmer pays a third party today (UK) (search extract) — https://www.gwynedd.gov.uk/en/Businesses/Environmental-health-and-trading-standards/Pest-control/Pest-control-contracts.aspx ; https://tameside.gov.uk/pestcontrol
- No farm-specific private contract price or farm budget-holder evidence was found.

## 6. Distribution channel
- Agricultural merchants and other rodenticide sellers are already inside the stewardship system. Sellers who buy stewardship-labelled products to sell onward need a "Supply Chain Compliance Declaration". CRRU runs an independent audit of supplier point-of-sale compliance (UK) (search extract) — https://www.thinkwildlife.org/stewardship-regime/point-of-sale/ ; https://www.nfuonline.com/cross-sector/science-and-technology/crop-protection/crop-protection-key-content/how-to-buy-rodenticide-in-2018/
- BPCA: sellers of professional-use rodenticides to pest controllers, farmers and gamekeepers must register for a stewardship point-of-sale audit by 31 December. The page is undated (UK) (search extract; the summary called it "one BPCA item" without naming it; it is one of the BPCA stewardship pages in the results, most likely this one) — https://bpca.org.uk/news-and-blog/sector/Page-67/new-year-arrangements-for-farm-assurance-schemes-announced/190990 ; https://bpca.org.uk/news-and-blog/sector/page-67/eleven-assurance-schemes-compliant-with-the-uk-rodenticide-stewardship-regime/189766

## 8. Buildability (components, BOM, certification)
- Bait-take estimation is still a research problem. The Hong Kong EMSD innovation wish-list asks for a "Rodent Bait Consumption Monitoring Device", a small sensor that sends consumption rate over LPWAN (HK) (search extract) — https://inno.emsd.gov.hk/en/it-wish-list/index_id_397.html
- Research prototype: "An IoT Smart Rodent Bait Station System Utilizing Computer Vision" (RatSpy, Sensors 2020) reports bait-station levels and intruders, with a design target of a bait-level estimate within 15% (search extract) — https://mdpi.com/1424-8220/20/17/4670
- Patents describe capacitive sensing near the bait post, weight-change sensing, and a capacitance tray that logs rodent passage with time stamps (search extract; the summary did not map each method to a patent number) — https://patents.google.com/patent/US12376581 ; https://patents.google.com/patent/US12213474 ; https://patents.google.com/patent/US10897887

## 10. Public evidence of pain
- CRRU wildlife monitoring: liver analysis of 100 dead barn owls from 2018 found anticoagulant residues in 87%, mostly low. The 2020 samples showed 88%, against a 2006–2012 baseline average of 81%. ## Researcher observations
- The compliance hook is real and specific: a 4-weekly re-inspection for permanent outdoor baiting (CRRU), and a Red Tractor bait plan with inspection dates kept for 2 years. But CRRU steers farms away from permanent baiting, which shrinks the population of always-baited points a sensor would log.
- Weak on money: the only cost-per-rat figures are US ($6.90–$25 per rat per year). Farm pest budgets look small (council contracts about £300–£345 a year), and Bell dropping its app fee suggests subscription pricing pressure.
- Buildability risk: a reliable bait-take estimate is still at prototype or patent stage (EMSD wish-list, RatSpy ±15%). A visit sensor is cheap and available, but visits are not consumption.
- Better buyer and channel for the same problem: pest-control contractors who service many assured farms, and food or feed mills (Rentokil already sells PestConnect into audited food sites). The CRRU point-of-sale audit puts merchants in touch with every farm buyer and could carry a "stewardship record" product.
