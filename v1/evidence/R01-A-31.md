## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Charity and commercial textile banks are stolen from (contents or the whole steel bank), and banks that fill up attract side-waste and complaints from host sites and councils. Collections usually run on fixed rounds, so a bank can sit full or be raided between visits.
- Who suffers / who pays: Charity trading companies and textile merchants that own bank networks; host sites (supermarkets, schools, councils' bring sites); councils that clear fly-tipping. The bank operator pays for collection, replacement banks and lost stock.
- Sensor concept: Battery LTE-M/NB-IoT unit with ultrasonic fill sensing, a door/chute tamper accelerometer and GNSS to detect bank removal; feeds collection-route planning and theft alerts with evidence.

## 1. Cost of incident
- Textile Recycling Association (TRA) put clothing-collection crime at a "conservative estimate of £15 million a year", about 10% of donated clothing (UK). Resource article on the 2014 TRA London study, which found theft had decreased but "10 per cent still represents a reasonably high percentage" — https://www.resourcemedia.eco/article/textile-theft-has-decreased-london-6710 (search extract; summary combined results, attribution of the £15m wording to this page is inferred)
- letsrecycle: charities "are believed to be losing as much as £15 million a year" to textile crime (UK; minister pledges zero tolerance) — https://www.letsrecycle.com/news/minister-steps-in-over-clothes-collection-concerns/ (search extract)
- An earlier estimate: up to 15% of used clothing donations stolen, costing UK charities around £50 million a year (The Ecologist, Feb 2011; UK) — https://theecologist.org/2011/feb/01/charities-suffer-criminal-gangs-target-lucrative-clothing-recycling-sector (search extract). The same results report one recycler spent "more than 250,000" (currency as given) modifying bins to stop break-ins (attribution to this page inferred).
- Whole-bank theft: TRAID (London charity) had up to 20 steel banks stolen in two months, "each bank valued at up to £1,000"; banks are taken for scrap steel or repainted and sold to another charity (Sensolus customer story, vendor; UK) — https://www.sensolus.com/knowledge-hub/success-stories/protecting-traid-charity-donation-banks-with-peak-technology/ (search extract)
- Lontex (London textile collector) recovered a stolen bank using a tracker, saving "the £800 it would have cost to have the bank replaced" (letsrecycle; UK) — https://www.letsrecycle.com/news/stolen-textile-bank-recovered/ (search extract)
- Replacing a bank costs "up to £1,500" and takes six to eight weeks (UK; figure appears in results on the 2018 wave of bank thefts — Huddersfield Examiner / Civil Society; exact page not confirmed) — https://www.examiner.co.uk/news/how-audacious-thefts-textile-banks-14851653 (search extract)
- City of London Police arrested four men and seized £20,000 in a case against a gang suspected of stealing "hundreds of thousands of pounds worth of used clothing" (Civil Society; UK) — https://www.civilsociety.co.uk/news/arrests-made-and-20-000-seized-after-string-of-charity-clothes-bank-thefts.html (search extract)
- Logistics cost is a large share of the business: "the major charities can spend around 20% of their income on logistics" (University of Southampton CORMSIS event on an Oxfam bank-sensor trial, 2015; UK) — https://www.southampton.ac.uk/cormsis/news/events/2015/0611.page (search extract); a Czech operator says collection is about 60% of operating costs (Sensority, vendor; CZ) — https://www.sensority.eu/en/?p=1962 (search extract)

## 3. Current workarounds
- **Fixed rounds and frequent visits:** SATCoL visits banks "regularly, often daily" (see section 2) — https://www.essex.police.uk/news/essex/news/news/2025/december/basildon-thurrock-charity-theft-arrests/ (search extract)
- **Collectors clear side-waste on each visit:** at Bordars Road, Hanwell, the Salvation Army said its collectors clear fly-tipping at each collection, yet residents kept asking Ealing Council to remove the banks (UK) — https://www.ealing.news/?p=22686 (search extract)
- **Forensic marking:** SATCoL sprays banks with SmartWater "to help catch thieves and deter future thefts" (letsrecycle; UK) — https://www.letsrecycle.com/news/salvation-army-uses-smartwater-to-cut-textile-theft (search extract)
- **Bank redesign:** a 2014 TRA study credited better bank design (and falling clothing prices) for a drop in London theft — https://www.resourcemedia.eco/article/textile-theft-has-decreased-london-6710 (search extract)
- **GPS trackers already fitted by some operators:** TRAID (Sensolus via Peak Technologies) — https://www.peaktech.com/case-studies/peak-case-study-tracking-asset-theft/ (search extract); Lontex — https://www.letsrecycle.com/news/stolen-textile-bank-recovered/ (search extract)
- **Sensor-triggered collections trialled:** an Oxfam trial using daily fill data and a 50–60% fill trigger suggested time and distance savings "of up to 30%" against fixed schedules (University of Southampton, 2015; UK) — https://www.southampton.ac.uk/cormsis/news/events/2015/0611.page (search extract). A peer-reviewed study of a UK charity's textile and book banks found dynamic scheduling cut bank visits and raised profit by up to 2.4% (McLeod et al.) — https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/en/publications/dynamic-collection-scheduling-using-remote-asset-monitoring-a-cas/ (search extract)

## Researcher observations
- Pain is well evidenced in public (police, trade press, residents, councils) and per-incident costs (£800–£1,500 per stolen bank) are sourced, but no compliance driver exists and the economics of textile collection collapsed in 2025–26 (SATCoL pausing collections and removing banks). Buyers are cutting networks, not adding sensors.
- The category is crowded: Nordsense, Sensoneo, FarSite and Sensority already do fill sensing for textile banks, and Sensolus does theft tracking; SATCoL already trialled sensors in 2016.
- A better angle for the same problem may be the **host site or council** (fly-tipping clearance cost at bring sites) or the **textile merchant** that now charges councils for collection — but neither was evidenced as a buyer here.
