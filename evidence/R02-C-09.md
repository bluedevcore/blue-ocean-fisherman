# R02-C-09 — Independent funeral homes' body fridges fail over a weekend with no temperature log, ahead of statutory inspection in England and Scotland

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: After the Legacy (Hull) case and the Fuller Inquiry's call for statutory regulation,
  councils inspected funeral premises, and Scotland already has an Inspector of Funeral Directors
  and a statutory code. If a body-store fridge fails when the premises are unstaffed (e.g. a
  weekend), the deceased can deteriorate before anyone notices. Many independents keep no
  continuous temperature record.
- Who suffers / who pays: Bereaved families; the funeral director (refunds, reputation,
  complaints, inspection findings); mortuary staff.
- Sensor concept: LoRaWAN/LTE-M temperature probe per body store plus a door reed switch;
  out-of-hours phone/SMS alert; daily signed log and a monthly inspector-ready report.

- Funeral-home case: a grandfather's body decomposed at a funeral home after its fridge switched
  off; the family could no longer dress or embalm him or hold an open-casket service; Ashton
  Funeral Service said the fridge was checked on the Friday evening but no one works weekends,
  so nobody noticed (no date or compensation given) (search extract) —
  https://inkl.com/news/grandad-s-body-decomposed-in-funeral-home-after-fridge-switched-off

  down" (search extract) —
  https://www.inkl.com/news/family-devastated-after-grandad-s-body-decomposes-when-funeral-home-fridge-breaks-down
- Public mortuary damages: Bradford Council, which runs Bradford Public Mortuary, agreed to pay a
  family £73,250 in damages (October 2019) after a body deteriorated; the mortuary accepted a
  Human Rights Act breach (attribution approximate) (search extract) —

## 3. Current workarounds
- Manual checks only: in the Ashton case the fridge was checked on Friday evening and not again
  until after the weekend (search extract) —
  https://inkl.com/news/grandad-s-body-decomposed-in-funeral-home-after-fridge-switched-off
- Some independents have no refrigeration and rely on off-site arrangements: NAFD "does not
  currently require members to install on-site refrigeration but does expect members relying on
  off-site refrigeration" to make arrangements (attribution approximate) (search extract) —
  https://www.nafd.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/NAFD-response-to-Scot-Gov-Funeral-Director-Code-of-Practice-Consultation-200919.pdf
- CMA interviews: some recent entrants started with no premises, and there was no consensus on
  whether to prioritise investment in refrigeration units (search extract) —
  https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5e32d57740f0b60912e21eee/Summary_of_visits.pdf
- Generic GSM text alarms are used for fridges (see section 7); I found no data on how many
  funeral homes use one.

  inspector-ready log (Scottish 4–7°C code, council inspection). The difference from incumbents
  is the record, not the alert, so the critic should treat c7 as contested.

## Researcher observations
- c4 and c10 are strong and rising: the Fuller Phase 2 report, the Scottish statutory code
  (4–7°C) with inspection powers, council inspections after Legacy, and a 2026 push by MPs for
  regulation after a further case.
- c7 is a real risk: generic GSM alarms at £53–£90 already solve the weekend alert. The only open
  space is an inspector-ready record matched to the Scottish code and future England rules.
- Better buyer/channel: mortuary fridge suppliers and servicers (Mortuary Fridge Ltd, Ferno,
  Leec, Hadrian), who could bundle monitoring with installation and PPM. Scotland is the first
  market, since the code and inspector already exist. Hospital and council mortuaries (Belfast
  Trust tender, HTA-licensed) are an adjacent buyer with budgets, but they face stronger
  incumbents.
