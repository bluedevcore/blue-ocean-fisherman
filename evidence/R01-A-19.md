# R01-A-19 — LPG cylinder cages at retail stockists are raided for scrap and run empty between delivery runs

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: LPG cylinders held in locked outdoor cages at garages, garden centres, marinas and farm shops are stolen (for scrap/export or resale), and cages can run out of stock between scheduled restock visits. Stock is counted by hand on each delivery.
- Who suffers / who pays: Cylinder owners (Calor, Flogas and their distributors) lose cylinder assets; stockists lose sales and stock; distributor drivers and sales staff do the counting.
- Sensor concept: Cage-mounted battery module (door/tamper sensing plus a full-cylinder count estimate), cellular LPWAN, with after-hours intrusion alerts and stock levels feeding restock routing.

## 3. Current workarounds
- Police and safety guidance: store cylinders upright in locked, well-ventilated outdoor enclosures (search extract) — https://neighbourhoodwatchscotland.co.uk/?p=4098
- Locks and cages are being defeated by cutting padlocks and cage mesh (Braunston, Ryde cases above) (search extract) — https://rugbyobserver.co.uk/?p=49870
- Calor warned shipping carriers in 2012 that stolen cylinders were being exported, mainly to Africa, and highlighted the explosion risk (search extract) — https://felixstowedocker.blogspot.com.au/2012/08/gas-hazard-warning-to-carriers.html
- Restocking: Blue Rhino (US) gives current retailers an online delivery-ordering option; no sensing is mentioned (search extract) — https://bluerhino.com/retailers
- No source was found describing UK distributor drivers' manual cage counts or their labour cost.

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- UKLPG/Liquid Gas UK Code of Practice 7 covers storage of full and nominally empty LPG cylinders at retail outlets, service stations and depots, including display in retail environments (secondary summary) (search extract) — https://cdn.asp.events/CLIENT_Nineteen_F7A1E73F_5056_B725_6BE81DEAAC036B43/sites/installershow-2026/media/libraries/exhibitor-brochures/205505-Liquid-Gas-UK_Codes-of-Practice.pdf
- Lockable open-air wire cages are described for retail/temporary sites holding up to 400 kg, subject to separation distances (search extract) — https://registeredgasengineer.co.uk/?p=7759
- Jersey government LPG minimum standard: "for a small amount of LPG cylinders, a lockable cage will be sufficient" (search extract) — https://gov.je/SiteCollectionDocuments/Education/ID%20Liquefied%20Petroleum%20Gas%20(LPG)%20-%20Minimum%20Standard%2020221207%20MW.pdf
- Flogas guidance: nominally empty cylinders should be treated as full and stored 3 m from full cylinders; a lockable cage may be appropriate (search extract) — https://res.cloudinary.com/flogas-prd/image/upload/v1778177448/Uploads/Flogas/prd/33533_Flogas_LPG_Code_A4_96276d57e9.pdf
- HSE page on LPG and ATEX (relevant to any electronics mounted inside or next to an LPG cage) (title) (search extract) — https://hse.gov.uk/electricity/atex/lpg.htm
- None of these require monitoring; the compliance driver is safe storage, not stock or theft records.

## Researcher observations
- Theft pain is real and recurrent, but the evidence is anecdotal local news, and the only big £ figure is old.
- The owner of the asset (Calor/Flogas) loses the cylinder, so the cylinder company or its distributor is the payer, not the stockist. Calor's cylinder-network sales roles are the most plausible buyer persona found.
- Otodata already sells a cage restock monitor in North America at low monthly fees. A UK entrant would need theft detection plus stock count to stand apart, and would face very low per-unit price expectations.
- The ATEX/DSEAR zone around LPG cages may raise hardware cost. Mounting outside the cage could avoid it.
- Possible better niche: propane exchange networks in the US (Blue Rhino, AmeriGas-type) at much larger scale, where cage telemetry is already an accepted category.
