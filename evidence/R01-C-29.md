## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Insurers covering unoccupied property commonly impose conditions such as regular internal and external inspections, draining the water system or keeping minimum heating, and keeping records. If the conditions are not met, escape-of-water, theft or malicious-damage cover may be lost. Social landlords hold many void homes and pay security firms for screens, alarms and inspections.
- Who suffers / who pays: Housing association or council voids and asset teams and insurance managers (the payer); incoming tenants delayed by void damage.
- Sensor concept: Plug-in hub with battery backup: temperature, leak rope, PIR/door contact, screen-removal vibration and mains-loss detection over LTE-M. It would produce an automated weekly "inspection-equivalent" report, heating-threshold proof, and intrusion and leak alerts.

## 2. Frequency
- Federato (US guide on seasonal and vacant properties): "most insurers expect seasonal properties to be inspected periodically", and policies "may specify inspection frequency, such as every 7, 14, or 30 days". ## Researcher observations
- Strong on cost and pain: burst-pipe claims average above £17k (ABI 2023), escape-of-water repairs about £12.8k (Zurich), and FOS shows insurers decline unoccupied escape-of-water claims on condition breaches. Councils see hundreds of burst pipes in a freeze.
- Caveat: FOS mainly covers consumers and small businesses. Large housing associations and councils usually buy commercial or self-insured programmes, so the FOS evidence may not reflect their policy terms. Social-landlord policy wording is still unfound.
- The decisive unknown remains insurer acceptance. Only a US vendor says sensors "often meet insurer requirements". Without acceptance, the product adds to inspection cost rather than replacing it.
- Competition is closer than the idea assumed: SafeSite sells a £35-a-week void alarm, and Eddisons (RemoteZone, under its insurance arm) and Corps Security (Beespace sensors) already sell sensor-based vacant-property monitoring.
- Better niche or channel for the same problem: insurance-linked property services such as Eddisons, which can make sensor evidence part of the policy condition. Or charity, almshouse and church landlords on standard unoccupancy forms, where owners do the inspections themselves.
