## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Insurers covering unoccupied property commonly impose conditions such as roughly weekly internal and external inspections, draining the water system or keeping minimum heating, and keeping records. If the conditions are not met, escape-of-water, theft or malicious-damage cover may be lost. Social landlords hold many void homes and pay security firms for screens and inspections.
- Who suffers / who pays: Housing association or council voids and asset teams and insurance managers; incoming tenants delayed by void damage.
- Sensor concept: Plug-in hub with battery backup: temperature, leak rope, PIR/door contact, screen-removal vibration and mains-loss detection over LTE-M. It would produce an automated weekly "inspection-equivalent" report, heating-threshold proof, and intrusion and leak alerts.

## Researcher observations
- The insurer condition (weekly inspection, heating or drain-down, evidenced records) looks real across several insurer and charity documents. The FOS blog shows insurers do decline unoccupied escape-of-water claims. This supports c4 at about 7, but social-landlord policy wording was not found.
- The incumbent void-security firms (VPS, Clearway, SafeSite) already visit weekly and log visits digitally. They are the natural channel, and also the competitor whose inspection revenue the sensor would cut.
- The decisive unknown is whether any insurer accepts sensor evidence instead of a physical visit. Without that, the product adds to the inspection cost rather than replacing it. A better first niche may be PRS/charity landlords insured under standard unoccupancy forms (almshouses, Masonic, church), where the condition is explicit and the owner does the inspections personally.
