## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Brass/copper landing valves and inlet fittings on dry risers in residential tower blocks are stolen for scrap. One missing valve can make the whole riser unusable for firefighters, and the loss may go unnoticed until the next inspection or a fire.
- Who suffers / who pays: Residents and firefighters bear the safety risk. The landlord (council, ALMO or housing association) pays for replacement valves, security measures, patrols and contractor call-outs. Landlords of high-rise residential buildings in England are the "responsible person" under the Fire Safety (England) Regulations 2022.
- Sensor concept: Tamper/vibration and tilt tags on each landing valve (or a wire loop through the handwheel), plus door contacts on inlet/landing boxes. LoRaWAN with a cellular gateway in the riser. Instant theft alert and a monthly-check record.

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- Fire Safety (England) Regulations 2022, reg 7(1): "The responsible person in relation to a high-rise residential building must undertake monthly routine checks of lifts for use by firefighters, evacuation lifts and essential fire-fighting equipment within the building." (England; primary legislation; search extract; not fetched — blocked) — https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2022/547/regulation/7
- Reg 7 also requires the responsible person to "make a record of the monthly checks" and make it "accessible to the residents of the building", and to "take steps to rectify the fault". (search extract; not fetched — blocked) — https://legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2022/547/regulation/7/made/data.xht
- "Essential fire-fighting equipment" includes key fire-fighting equipment under reg 6(7), which per the extract covers dry and wet rising main inlets and outlets. A high-rise residential building is one at least 18 m high or with at least 7 storeys, and two or more sets of domestic premises. (search extract; not fetched — blocked) — https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2022/547
- London Fire Brigade: responsible persons "must undertake monthly checks of firefighting lifts and other firefighting equipment" and "must report any defective lifts or firefighting equipment to the fire service". ## Researcher observations
- Strong points: c4 (statutory monthly check and record for exactly this equipment in England), c10 (police, council, trade-press and inquiry sources) and a real channel (riser test contractors such as PTSG already visit and do anti-theft remedials).
- Weak points: c1 is small per incident (£109–£270 per valve; about £3.5k for a 2-block spree). The main loss is unpriced life-safety risk. Thefts come in clusters, not a steady monthly rate. Cheap welding and bolting has already cut thefts (Islington), which undermines a subscription.
- The record-keeping duty (reg 7) is broader than theft. A better framing for the breeder may be "automated reg 7 monthly check of all essential fire-fighting equipment in high-rise residential" (riser box doors, valve presence, smoke-vent status, firefighting-lift status). The channel would be the riser/fire contractors who already hold the six-monthly contracts, with theft alerts as a feature rather than the product.
