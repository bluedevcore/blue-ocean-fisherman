# R01-C-27 — HMO weekly fire alarm tests skipped or unrecorded: civil penalties up to £30k per offence

> Research note: WebFetch is blocked in this environment, so no page was opened in full. Every
> bullet below relies on the title and extract shown in WebSearch results and is marked
> "(search extract)". 8 queries were run (the per-idea budget). Geography is England unless stated.

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: HMO managers must keep fire alarm systems in working order, and many councils' licence
  conditions or guidance expect weekly tests recorded in a logbook plus 6-monthly servicing.
  Agents either visit each HMO or rely on tenants; faults can go unnoticed. Councils can fine or
  prosecute for management-regulation breaches.
- Who suffers / who pays: Landlords and letting/managing agents (penalties, visit time);
  tenants (exposure to faulty alarms).
- Sensor concept: Box near the alarm panel that detects the sounder and reads fault/power LEDs,
  LTE-M, automatic weekly test record, fault/power-loss alerts and an evidence pack for licence
  renewal.

## 2. Frequency
- Weekly testing: Oxford City Council says BS 5839-6 treats weekly testing as best practice for
  large (3+ storey) Grade A LD1 HMO systems (search extract) —
  https://www.oxford.gov.uk/info/20244/safety_requirements_in_hmos/931/fire_alarm_testing_and_maintenance
- Great Yarmouth: in licensable HMOs with Grade A systems the licence holder or manager carries out
  weekly checks, with servicing six-monthly by a qualified engineer and records in a logbook
  (search extract) — https://www.great-yarmouth.gov.uk/fire-precautions-in-dwellings/record-keeping
- Market size: Local Authority Housing Statistics 2023-24 estimate about 472,823 HMOs in England,
  131,061 subject to mandatory licensing and 93,319 licensed (secondary; which of these two URLs
  carries the figures is approximate) (search extract) — https://payprop.com/blog/where-did-the-hmos-go
  and https://www.property118.com/?p=162380
- Enforcement volume (single councils): Leicester FOI lists 25 civil penalties in 2021 and 44 in
  2022 (search extract) — https://directory.leicester.gov.uk/media/cybh3du3/foia-35128.doc
- Wigan: 188 licensed HMOs at end-2024 (116 at end-2020) and 2 fines/civil penalties in 2024
  (search extract) — https://www.wigan.gov.uk/Docs/PDF/Council/Disclosure-Logs/2025/17666.pdf

## 3. Current workarounds
- Cherwell: weekly fire alarm tests and visual checks by "cleaning/maintenance staff, or a
  competent tenant", recorded in a logbook; six-monthly service by a competent professional under
  BS 5839-1 (search extract) —
  https://www.cherwell.gov.uk/info/245/hmos/593/hmo-standards-and-fire-safety/3
- Bedford: weekly test "by a competent person (this could be the landlord, manager or a person
  who has been" trained) (search extract) — https://www.bedford.gov.uk/files/firealarmtesting.pdf/download
- Oxford requires an annual signed self-declaration that alarms were tested and maintained; Coventry
  warns a false declaration can lead to enforcement (search extract) —

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- Duty basis: council policies cover the Management of HMOs (England) Regulations 2006 under
  section 234(3) Housing Act 2004 as a civil-penalty offence (Oadby & Wigston appendix) (search
  extract) —
  https://moderngov.oadby-wigston.gov.uk/documents/s35939/Appendix%202%20-%20Civil%20Penalties%20under%20the%20Renters%20Rights%20Act%202025%20and%20Other%20Housing%20Legislation%202026%20.pdf
- Swale's model policy (ACEHO, for 1 May 2026) lists "failure to comply with management regulations
  in respect of HMOs" among penalty-eligible breaches; the extract did not give the band (search
  extract) —
  https://democracy.swale.gov.uk/documents/s33318/ACEHO%20branded%20Model%20Civil%20Financial%20Penalty%20Policy%20including%20RRA%20breaches%20for%201%20May%202026%20NO%20SELECTIV.pdf
- Penalty levels: council policies updated for the Renters' Rights Act 2025 describe breaches
  "subject to a civil penalty with a statutory maximum of £40,000" (listed examples are
  electrical-safety duties; one policy cites up to £40,000 under the Smoke and CO Alarm
  Regulations). The £30,000 figure in the idea may now be out of date, but I could not confirm the
  band for HMO management breaches (search extract) —
  https://www.dorsetcouncil.gov.uk/w/policy-for-civil-penalties-under-the-renters-rights-act-2025-and-other-housing-legislation
- Standards conflict: Joblogic says BS 5839-6 covers HMOs but BS 5839-1 applies to shared common
  parts; councils refer to both (search extract) — https://www.joblogic.com/blog/fire-alarm-testing
- Counter-view: a landlord association argues landlords "DO NOT need to test fire alarms weekly",
  subject to the fire risk assessment (search extract) —
  https://pdpla.com/news/guidance-on-testing-of-fire-alarms
- I did not find the LACORS guide text or regulation 4 wording directly.

## Researcher observations
- Strong enforcement evidence with real fines, including against letting agents; Gateshead
  explicitly cites missing checks and records.
- Weekly testing is guidance or licence-condition based and is contested (PDPLA view), so the
  requirement varies by council; Grade A panel HMOs (3+ storeys) are the clearest target.
- Aico already dominates Grade D connected alarms in rented housing with a ~£190 gateway and a low
  annual fee, so smaller HMOs are partly served. The gap is larger panel-based HMOs.
- Best channel is likely the six-monthly fire alarm maintainer, who could fit the box and resell
  records; HMO licensing teams could also accept the log as evidence.
