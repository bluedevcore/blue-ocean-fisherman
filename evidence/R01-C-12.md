# R01-C-12 — Thousands of Type 3 TMVs serviced on fixed cycles because nobody can prove they're still mixing correctly

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: NHS healthcare premises fit Type 3 thermostatic mixing valves (TMVs) to protect patients from scalding. The valves are specified under HTM 04-01 and its D08 performance specification, and must be tested in service and serviced. Trusts pay water-hygiene contractors to service valves on fixed cycles. A valve that drifts or fails between visits can deliver water that is too hot (scald risk) or too cool (Legionella risk).
- Who suffers / who pays: NHS estates water-safety teams and trusts pay for the servicing contracts. Patients and residents bear the scald risk, and providers face prosecution after incidents.
- Sensor concept: A clamp-on thermistor pair on the mixed-outlet and hot-inlet pipes. It would detect each use and its peak mixed temperature and send readings over LoRaWAN, with a 5-year battery. Alerts would fire on drift above 44°C or fail-safe failure, and the data would provide evidence for moving servicing to risk-based intervals.

## 1. Cost of incident
- Corporate manslaughter, care home (England): Aster Healthcare was fined £1.04m plus £184,513 prosecution costs after a resident's scalding death at Birdsgrove Nursing Home.
  - The bath was not fitted with the correct type of TMV, and the TMV that was fitted "had not been serviced and was not working correctly".
  - The company later falsified water temperature records submitted to CQC and HSE.
  - (search extract) — https://thecarehomeenvironment.com/story/36727/aster-healthcare-fined-1m-care-staff-sentenced-after-scalding-death
- Housing association (England): headline "Housing association to pay £135,000 over scalding death" (search extract) — https://www.shponline.co.uk/in-court/housing-association-to-pay-135-000-over-scalding-death/
- Scotland: a care home was fined £30,000 after a pensioner was scalded (search extract) — https://www.shponline.co.uk/in-court/scottish-care-home-fined-30000-pensioner-scalded/
- Wales: Greencroft Nursing Home (Deeside) was fined £5,000 after an 88-year-old resident was lowered into a scalding bath and later died. The report notes that a Type 3 TMV is required on hot taps when bathing vulnerable people (search extract) — https://www.deeside.com/aston-greencroft-nursing-home-directors-fined-resident-lowered-scalding-bath-died-burns/
- NHS hospital (England, 2008): Oxford Radcliffe Hospitals NHS Trust was fined £8,000 and ordered to pay £2,786 compensation after an elderly woman was scalded in a bath (search extract) — https://www.irwinmitchell.com/newsandmedia/2008/july/nhs-trust-fined-after-woman-scalded
- Routine servicing spend (the cost the sensor aims to optimise):

## 2. Frequency
- Powys THB tender: "every 6 months, all TMVs must be fully cleaned and checked". TMVs are a mix of standalone valves and valves within taps (search extract) — https://bidstats.uk/tenders/2023/W13/795738352
- D08 sets the first in-service TMV test "between 6 and 8 weeks after" commissioning. This is stated by a TMV servicing provider citing NHS Model Engineering Specification D08 (search extract) — https://smsewebsite.azurewebsites.net/news-events/how-often-should-a-tmv-be-serviced
- HSE monthly checks: an archived HSE temperature-regime table lists monthly checks at sentinel outlets. For TMV-fitted outlets, the supply to the TMV should reach at least 50°C within one minute (search extract) — https://hse.gov.uk/foi/internalops/sims/pub_serv/07-12-07/appendix3.htm
- A water hygiene policy states hot water should exceed 50°C (>55°C in healthcare premises) after one minute. This comes from a non-NHS employer's policy document (search extract) — https://colleaguesconnect.midcounties.coop/siteassets/working-here/people--policies/health--safety-policies/water-hygiene-policy-v-2025-appendix-final.pdf
- Scale per site: Northampton General Hospital had about 1,700 TMVs needing a full service (search extract; attribution probable) — https://bidstats.uk/tenders/2023/W03/791150767
- No published per-trust TMV failure rate or scald-incident rate was found (see below).

## Researcher observations
- The largest sourced costs (£1m Aster, £135k, £100k-class fines) are in care homes and supported housing, not NHS hospitals. In the one NHS hospital case found, the fine was £8k. Care home groups may be a sharper buyer than NHS estates for the same sensor: they have higher prosecution exposure, CQC scrutiny and less in-house estates capacity.
- The servicing-cost saving is modest. At roughly ≤£50/valve/year, a sensor would need a very low per-valve price to pay back on servicing reduction alone. The value case rests on scald and Legionella liability and record integrity (the falsified records in Aster).
- Competition is real: Factorylab already markets clip-on LoRaWAN TMV monitoring with scald warning and predictive maintenance, and several legionella monitoring firms sell into NHS estates.
- Water-hygiene contractors are the clear channel, but they earn from fixed-cycle servicing visits, so they may resist a product designed to reduce those visits.
