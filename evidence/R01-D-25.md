## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: After escape-of-water claims, restoration contractors put dehumidifiers and air movers in homes for days to weeks. The idea says insurers question how long kit ran and when drying was achieved, homeowners claim back the electricity, and contractors make frequent visits to take moisture readings. The idea's figure of £5–£15 per unit per day for electricity is **not verified** (one contractor estimate of ~£4/day was found).
- Who suffers / who pays: Restoration contractors (visits, documentation), insurers and loss adjusters (claim cost, disputes), homeowners (electricity, disruption).
- Sensor concept: Inline power-logging plug per drying unit, ambient T/RH sensor, optional wall-moisture sensor, LTE-M hub; remote drying curve, run-hours and kWh per unit, drying certificate, fewer site visits.

## 1. Cost of incident
- Insurers "pay out £1.8 million every day" for escape-of-water damage, attributed to the ABI (UK; Clyde & Co, July 2023) — https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2023/07/the-rising-cost-of-escape-of-water-claims (search extract)
- Insurance Times (ABI warning on burst-pipe claims in cold weather): the search summary says the article reports an average claim for this type of incident of over **£17,000** (UK; year of the figure not clear in the extract) — https://www.insurancetimes.co.uk/news/abi-warns-of-costly-burst-pipe-claims-as-cold-weather-hits/1454094.article (search extract)
- Post magazine headline: "Sector must tackle rising escape of water claims, warns ABI" (UK; headline only) — https://www.postonline.co.uk/insurer/3359441/sector-must-tackle-rising-escape-of-water-claims-warns-abi (search extract)
- Electricity per unit: a UK drying/insurance-repair contractor estimates a large dehumidifier costs **approximately £4.00 per day** to run, and says the insurer "would normally reimburse you for the additional cost of electricity consumption whilst equipment is at your property" (UK; contractor marketing estimate) — https://bakersofdanbury.co.uk/services/insurance-repairs/frequently-asked-questions/ (search extract)
- Ombudsman: in DRN-4561814 the FOS agreed the insurer should repay extra electricity from the drying process and awarded **£600** for distress and inconvenience over many weeks; the policyholder had been switching the machines off because he could not afford the power (UK) — https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/decision/DRN-4561814.pdf (search extract)
- **Gap:** no figure found for the cost of drying (equipment hire per day or per claim), the cost of a monitoring visit, or the total electricity reimbursed per claim.

## 2. Frequency
- Go.Compare analysis: escape of water accounted for **28.63% (almost one in three)** of home insurance claims declared in 2024 (UK; comparison-site data, not ABI) — https://press.gocompare.com/news/water-woes-new-data-reveals-escape-of-water-tops-home-insurance-claims (search extract)
- "One in three home insurance claims is for escape of water" (UK; headline) — https://yourmortgage.co.uk/insurance/news-insurance/one-in-three-home-insurance-claims-is-for-escape-of-water (search extract)
- Drying duration: in FOS DRN-4694073, drying a home after a large escape of water took **about three months**; equipment was supplied promptly, but the insurer should have housed the family elsewhere because of mould risk (UK) — https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/decision/DRN-4694073.pdf (search extract)
- **Gap:** no source found for the number of drying jobs per year or the number of moisture-reading visits per job.

## 3. Current workarounds
- Allianz's escape-of-water claim guide (2025): the customer may be able to claim back extra electricity used by drying equipment, and "your contractor will provide the documents and usage details" needed for reimbursement (UK; insurer) — https://www.allianz.co.uk/content/dam/onemarketing/azuk/allianzcouk/allianz-home-insurance/claim-guides/0042392-2025-Allianz%20Claims%20escape%20of%20water%20guide%20Final%20-%202.pdf (search extract)
- Insurer alternative to drying on the customer's supply: in FOS DRN-4673821 the ombudsman found it reasonable for the insurer to offer a cash settlement instead of installing drying equipment, noting it would still have repaid the extra electricity (UK) — https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/decision/DRN-4673821.pdf (search extract)
- Homeowner refusal: in FOS DRN-7867279 the policyholder would not let contractors use her electricity for dehumidifiers, which contributed to claim delays (UK) — https://www.financial-ombudsman.org.uk/decision/DRN7867279.pdf (search extract)
- Software documentation (no hardware): Encircle's "Hydro" feature (2021) helps restorers build drying plans and log moisture and psychrometric readings; the vendor says its reports "meet and exceed the insurance industry's strict documentation requirements" (US/Canada; vendor claim; the search summary placed this in a 2021 Business Wire release) — https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20210909005955/en/ (search extract — attribution likely but not certain)
- **Gap:** no source found on how often contractors visit to take readings, or what a visit costs.

## Researcher observations
- Strong: escape of water is roughly one in three UK home claims and very costly; electricity reimbursement relies on contractor-supplied usage data; FOS cases show real friction (homeowners switching kit off, refusing their supply, months-long drying).
- Weak: the gap is narrow. Polygon (with Deutsche Telekom) already sends insurers automatic power-consumption reports, and Dri-Eaz builds Wi-Fi/Bluetooth logging into its flagship dehumidifier. The idea's "insurers dispute run days" claim was not evidenced.
- Better angle for the breeder: a **self-powered or battery drying kit with its own metering** that does not draw on the homeowner's supply (DRN-4673821 and DRN-7867279 show power use itself is the friction), or a retrofit kit aimed at **small independent UK restorers** whose older kit is not connected, sold through equipment hire/distributors.
