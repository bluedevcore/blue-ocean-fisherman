# R02-A-07 — Farm and equine vets' car-boot medicines cook in summer and freeze in winter with no records

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Ambulatory farm and equine vets carry medicines and vaccines in vehicles. Vehicle interiors can exceed label storage temperatures in summer and fall below them in winter. UK veterinary regulators and the RCVS Practice Standards Scheme expect medicines in vehicles to stay within label range and to be monitored and recorded, and inspectors often find this missing.
- Who suffers / who pays: Practice manager / clinical director of a farm or equine practice, or the compliance lead at a corporate vet group; ambulatory vets; farm and equine clients.
- Sensor concept: BLE or LTE-M temperature logger in the drug box (and fridge drawer if fitted), with phone alerts at ~25°C/~30°C and an automatic daily per-vehicle record for PSS/VMD inspection.

## 2. Frequency
- UK car-based practice study (Carter et al., Open Veterinary Journal 2020; 4 vehicles over 2 years): internal car temperature ranged from −7.4°C to 54.5°C. It exceeded typical drug storage recommendations (>25°C) in every month, and peaks occurred between 14:00 and 17:00 (UK) (search extract) — https://www.pubmed.gov/32821666/ ; https://doaj.org/article/0008ead860594c0a820a0a7c71f6c951 ; https://irep.ntu.ac.uk/id/eprint/40364
- US large-animal practice vehicles (Ondrak, Jones & Fajt 2015; 24 vehicles, summer 2013): storage-unit maxima were 54.4°C (Texas) and 47.7°C (Nebraska), with an overall mean of 29.1°C. In Nebraska at least one reading above 25°C was recorded on a mean of 111 of 124 days, and 63% of all logger readings were above 25°C (US) (search extract) — https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4590276/
- Compliance gap frequency: the search summary reports that in 47% of PSS assessments, assessors found a lack of evidence of temperature monitoring for medicines. This appears to come from the RCVS FAQ on common temperature-monitoring issues; the attribution came from the summary (UK) (search extract) — https://www.rcvs.org.uk/faqs/what-are-the-common-issues-with-temperature-monitoring
- VMD (2026): "All too often, inspectors find that temperature monitoring and recording in practice vehicles is either absent or wholly inadequate" (UK) (search extract) — https://vmd.blog.gov.uk/2026/08/13/heatwaves-temperature-control-and-veterinary-medicines-how-to-transport-medicines-safely/

## 3. Current workarounds
- Max/min thermometers read and reset by hand: an RCVS Knowledge farm-vet-champions transcript describes recording max and min readings and then resetting the device, daily for cold-chain products (UK) (search extract) — https://knowledge.rcvs.org.uk/document-library/farm-vet-champions-storage-of-medicines-transcript-lee-grist/storage-of-medicines-at-practice-and-on-farm.pdf
- A VMD inspection report says a digital min/max device should be read and reset daily so that coverage between readings is continuous (UK) (search extract) — https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/68fa5753c18f97edd2b6374c/inspection_report.pdf
- Behavioural workarounds: the same RCVS presentation suggests parking in the shade on sunny days to reduce the temperature in the car (UK) (search extract) — https://knowledge.rcvs.org.uk/document-library/farm-vet-champions-storage-of-medicines-transcript-lee-grist/storage-of-medicines-at-practice-and-on-farm.pdf
- Cool boxes or refrigerated units: the VMD practice-premises inspection criteria give "use of a cool box or refrigerated unit" as an example measure for transport (UK) (search extract) — https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a81de20ed915d74e62348af/_816190-v4-VMD_L4_IAT_INS_218_C_-_Inspection_Criteria_for_Veterinary_Practice_Premises.pdf
- Passive insulated equine medical box listed at $59.99 (US) (search extract) — https://www.minitack.com/products/classic-equine-insulated-medical-box
- No source quantified vet or nurse time spent on manual vehicle temperature logging.

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- RCVS PSS (2024 change list), SA 10.1.6: "Monitoring and recording of environmental temperatures wherever medicines are stored must be undertaken (including consulting rooms, prep rooms, refrigerators and vehicles)" (UK) (search extract) — https://rcvs.org.uk/document-library/pss-vmr-updates-9-september-2024-change-list/pss-vmr-updates-9-september-2024-change-list.pdf
- VMD blog (13 Aug 2026): "Temperature control of veterinary medicines is no longer just good practice, it is a legal requirement." Storage conditions in vehicles must suit all medicines carried, including on home, farm and yard visits. Ambient storage within vehicles should also be tested and monitored, and cold can cause condensation in packaging (UK) (search extract) — https://vmd.blog.gov.uk/2026/08/13/heatwaves-temperature-control-and-veterinary-medicines-how-to-transport-medicines-safely/
- VMD inspection report: VMPs stored in vehicles must stay within their SPC temperature ranges. Medicines left in personal vehicles when the vet is off duty are flagged as a deficiency (UK) (search extract) — https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/68fa5753c18f97edd2b6374c/inspection_report.pdf
- Alarm set-points recommended in a VMD inspection report: fridge alarms at 2.5°C min and 7.5°C max, and an ambient max alarm at 24.5°C. The summary did not say which of these FOI-released reports it came from (UK) (search extract) — https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69bae0d7bfd6895605dad850/FOI202600193_-_CJ_Hall_report.pdf ; https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69bae08dbfd6895605dad84e/FOI202600193_-_Ham_report.pdf
- RCVS FAQ: ambient storage is generally expected at 8°C to 25°C, which matters most when outside temperatures are extreme (UK) (search extract) — https://www.rcvs.org.uk/faqs/what-are-the-common-issues-with-temperature-monitoring
- VMD practice premises inspection criteria: during transport, "measures should be taken to ensure that veterinary medicines remain within the temperature range specified on their SPCs" (UK) (search extract) — https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a81de20ed915d74e62348af/_816190-v4-VMD_L4_IAT_INS_218_C_-_Inspection_Criteria_for_Veterinary_Practice_Premises.pdf
- GDP analogue: transport temperature monitoring equipment "must be maintained and calibrated at regular intervals at least once a year". This applies to wholesale distribution, not practice vehicles directly (UK) (search extract) — https://www.gov.uk/guidance/good-distribution-practice-for-veterinary-medicinal-products-in-great-britain/transport
- VMD premises registration and inspection is a standing regime (UK) (search extract) — https://www.gov.uk/guidance/registration-and-inspection-of-veterinary-practice-premises
- Human-medicine analogue: USP <1070> covers storage of preparations in EMS vehicles and ambulances (US; title only) (search extract) — https://www.drugfuture.com/Pharmacopoeia/USP32/pub/data/v32270/usp32nf27s0_c1070.html

## Researcher observations
- Strongest evidence: a clear, current regulatory duty (PSS SA 10.1.6 names vehicles; VMD 2026 calls it a legal requirement) and published UK and US measurements showing vehicles above 25°C in every month and peaking at 54°C.
- Weakest areas: incident cost (no £ loss data) and incumbents (sub-£30 BLE loggers with free apps exist). The product must win on automatic per-vehicle compliance records across a practice, not on sensing.
- The same problem also applies to small-animal home-visit vets and all practice vehicles (VMD wording covers home visits). The buyer may be corporate-group compliance (CVS, IVC etc.) rather than individual farm practices. Next-day wholesaler delivery (NVS, Covetrus) is a plausible bundling channel.
