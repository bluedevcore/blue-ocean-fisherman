# R01-C-36 — Monthly emergency lighting flick tests in small blocks cost a contractor visit each time

Note on method: WebFetch is blocked in this environment, so no page was fetched directly. Every
bullet below relies on WebSearch result titles, snippets or the search tool's summary of them, and
is marked "(search extract)". Where the search summary did not say which of several listed URLs

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: BS 5266-1 calls for a monthly functional test and an annual full-duration test of emergency escape lighting, with a logbook. In the common parts of blocks of flats this falls under the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005. Managing agents and housing associations with many small blocks may pay a contractor, or send a caretaker, to each block every month to operate the test key and record results.
- Who suffers / who pays: The block's Responsible Person (freeholder, managing agent, housing association) organises it. Leaseholders typically pay through the service charge. Contractors carry the travel time.
- Sensor concept: Retrofit relay on the existing test key-switch circuit plus stick-on photodiode tags on each emergency fitting, over LoRaWAN/LTE-M. Monthly and annual tests are triggered remotely and logged automatically as pass/fail per fitting.

## 1. Cost of incident
- Prosecution, Morecambe flats (Lancashire): a landlord was fined £10,500 with £12,000 costs (headline total £22,500); £4,500 of the fine was "for failure to ensure that the emergency lighting was subject to a suitable system of maintenance" and £6,000 for not complying with an enforcement notice (UK) (search extract) — https://www.landlordtoday.co.uk/news_features/Lancashire-landlord-who-ignored-enforcement-notice-ordered-to-pay-%C2%A322-500
- Prosecution, Broadway House, Bedford (an office block, not flats): the enforcement notice cited no evidence that the fire alarm and emergency lighting had been maintained; the landlord/Responsible Person was "fined over £40,000 for multiple and repeated breaches of the Fire Safety Order" (UK) (search extract) — https://www.thefpa.co.uk/news/bedford-landlord-prosecuted-for-non-compliance-of-fire-regulations
- Ongoing cost of testing (the main "cost" for this idea): a London electrical contractor lists a function test at £50.99 for up to 10 fittings, an annual duration test at £89.99 and extra fittings at £3 each; another page from the same firm gives £50.99 for up to 20 fittings and £1.50 per extra fitting (vendor, pages conflict) (search extract) — https://electrical-london.pages.dev/emergency-lighting-london ; https://electrical-london.pages.dev/communal-area-electrical-london
- Small-block example: a leaseholder in a six-flat block reported their housing association wanted "about £1000 per year (including vat) for our testing. This includes a monthly test and 1 annual test" (leaseholder forum post; attribution inferred to the Safelincs "emergency lighting testing costs" thread) (search extract) — https://www.safelincs-forum.co.uk/topic/4790-emergency-lighting-testing-costs/
- Vendor estimate: Whitecroft Lighting says monthly function tests plus the annual duration test "can easily be in the region of £3000 a year", and estimates at least £5,670 a year for routine testing in a larger commercial building with staff at premium rates (vendor claim) (search extract) — https://www.whitecroftlighting.com/content/files/knowledge_source/page_121_1.pdf

## 2. Frequency
- The monthly test is a short functional test "simulating failure of the mains power supply which ensures the lamp switches on and illuminates correctly" (FPA) (search extract) — https://www.thefpa.co.uk/advice-and-guidance/advice-and-guidance-articles/how-to-test-emergency-lighting
- Grenfell Tower's emergency lighting test procedure (a social-housing tower block) describes an annual test lasting the full system duration, 1 or 3 hours depending on the system, and (under the 2011 edition) a six-monthly test of 1 hour for 3-hour systems (search extract) — https://assets.grenfelltowerinquiry.org.uk/TMOM00001911_Grenfell%20Tower%20Emergency%20Lighting%20Installation%20-%20Information%20%26%20Test%20Procedure.pdf
- A housing association sets a monthly emergency-lighting test for its higher-risk schemes (Longhurst Group; landlord policy, not a statutory minimum) (search extract) — https://longhurst-group.org.uk/me-and-my-home/your-tenancy-useful-information/fire-safety/fire-safety-in-communal-areas

## 3. Current workarounds
- Caretaker with a test key: a Safelincs forum answer says "a normal caretaker could use a test key to cut power to the lights and check they stay on", with an electrician needed for repairs; a later reply said no special qualification is needed to test (attribution inferred to the qualifications thread) (search extract) — https://www.safelincs-forum.co.uk/topic/4156-qualifications-for-emergency-lighting-testing/
- Fish-key test switches are commonly installed so emergency lights can be tested without switching off all power (Safelincs; attribution inferred to its testing category page) (search extract) — https://www.safelincs.co.uk/category/testing-emergency-lighting
- Split arrangement in flats: a forum thread describes a competent person doing monthly functional checks and a 1-hour six-monthly test, with a certificating company doing the annual checks (search extract; attribution inferred) — https://safelincs-forum.co.uk/topic/747-emergency-light-testing

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- Scope: the Fire Safety Order 2005 brought the common parts of blocks of flats within mainstream fire safety law; the fire risk assessment covers the common parts, not the flats (Fire Safety in Purpose-Built Blocks of Flats guide; attribution inferred to the government update and the RICS-hosted copy) (search extract) — https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/65e9def95b6524001af21bcc/Fire_Safety_in_Purpose_Built_Blocks_of_Flats_Guide-update.pdf ; https://www.rics.org/content/dam/ricsglobal/documents/to-be-sorted/fire_safety_in_purpose_built_blocks_of_flats_guide.pdf
- The TPI advice note says emergency lighting "in accordance with BS 5266 is required in common parts stairs and corridors, plant and service rooms" (search extract) — https://www.tpi.org.uk/media/iwaopbj5/advice-note-fire-safety-in-flats.pdf
- Enforcement uses Article 17 (maintenance): the Morecambe case fined failure to keep emergency lighting "subject to a suitable system of maintenance" (search extract) — https://www.landlordtoday.co.uk/news_features/Lancashire-landlord-who-ignored-enforcement-notice-ordered-to-pay-%C2%A322-500
- London Fire Brigade publishes enforcement notices; one for a purpose-built block required that "adequate lighting is provided to illuminate emergency routes and exits" (which notice ID is not confirmed) (search extract) — https://www.london-fire.gov.uk/community/public-notices/public-notice-detail?id=7212 ; https://www.london-fire.gov.uk/community/public-notices/public-notice-detail?id=8559
- Fire services track this: Lincolnshire published an FOI record titled "Enforcement of emergency lighting regulations between the period of April 2022 to March 2025" (title only) (search extract) — https://www.lincolnshire.gov.uk/directory-record/81458/enforcement-of-emergency-lighting-regulations-between-the-period-of-april-2022-to-march-2025
- The monthly test does not legally require an electrician (see section 3). This weakens the "paid contractor visit is unavoidable" premise.

## 7. Competitors & market gap
- Self-test luminaires (the main substitute) cost about the same as standard fittings: Lyco £36.99 vs £28.99; Safelincs Emerald LED self-test bulkhead £29.99 inc VAT; SparksDirect 3W self-test 3h bulkhead £15.00 ex VAT (UK retail) (search extract) — https://www.lyco.co.uk/indoor-lighting/emergency-light-fittings.html ; https://www.safelincs.co.uk/emerald-led-bulkhead-light-self-test ; https://www.sparksdirect.co.uk/product/ip65-3w-led-emergency-bulkhead-self-test--3h-maintainednon-maintained-with-legend-set-amp-remote-6000k
- Advanced LuxIntelligent: addressable test panel with optional cloud monitoring; each panel has "up to four easily-wired lighting loops supporting up to 249 luminaires"; marketed as a retrofit (vendor) (search extract) — https://www.iheem.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/04/LuxIntelligent-2020.pdf
- Hochiki FIREscape Nepto (2023): a self-testing, self-monitoring emergency lighting range; Hochiki said a cloud-based subscription service was in development (vendor/trade press) (search extract) — https://electricalcontractingnews.com/?p=36559 ; https://www.fsmatters.com/Hochiki-Europe-launches-FIREscape-Nepto-range
- EMC (US): a wireless retrofit that attaches to existing lights/signs, with "proactive maintenance analytics to identify lumen and battery degradation" (US, vendor) (search extract) — https://emcllc.com/download_file/621/0
- Mobile-managed emergency light testing was already being marketed in 2020 (IFSEC directory item, title only) (search extract) — https://directory.ifsecglobal.com/mobile-managed-emergency-light-testing-from-news082978.html
- No subscription prices were found for any connected emergency lighting system.
- Gap suggested by the evidence (inference): self-test fittings remove the manual test but still need someone to read and record status LEDs, unless networked. Networked systems need rewiring or new fittings. A no-rewire retrofit (key-switch relay plus optical tags) fits between the two, but has to beat "replace with £30 self-test fittings plus a caretaker".

## Researcher observations
- Frequency (monthly, mandated) and compliance (FSO Article 17; prosecutions exist) are well evidenced.
- The economic premise is weak. The monthly test needs no qualification, so a caretaker with a fish key can do it. Self-test fittings cost about £8 more than standard ones. A monthly contractor visit is only £50–£100 at sourced vendor prices, so savings per block are perhaps £600–£1,200 a year at most.
- The product's real competitor is "swap fittings for self-test at the next battery replacement". The relay-plus-tag retrofit wins only where re-fitting is costly or the agent has no caretaker.
- The better buyer may be the fire/electrical maintenance contractor serving hundreds of blocks: remote tests cut their travel, and they could resell the logged results. The FIA is a channel to them.
