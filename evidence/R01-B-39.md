# R01-B-39 — Server-room gas-suppression cylinders leak agent between 6-monthly services

> Research note: WebFetch is blocked in this environment, so no page was opened in full. Every
> bullet below relies on the title and extract shown in WebSearch results and is marked
> "(search extract)". 8 queries were run (the per-idea budget). Geography is UK/EU unless stated.

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Clean-agent (FM-200/HFC-227ea, Novec 1230/FK-5-1-12) and inert-gas suppression
  cylinders can lose agent slowly. Contents are normally checked at 6-monthly service visits. A
  cylinder that has lost agent may not reach design concentration in a fire, and refilling HFC
  agent is affected by the HFC phase-down.
- Who suffers / who pays: The site (data-centre, server-room, archive owner) pays for the system,
  its service contract and any refill. The suppression service contractor carries out checks.
- Sensor concept: Load cell under each cylinder or a temperature-compensated pressure transducer,
  LoRaWAN/NB-IoT, giving a continuous agent-quantity trend, leak alerts and a records export for
  the service contractor.

## 1. Cost of incident
- HFC price pressure (dated, non-UK, secondary): a notice from fire-protection firm Alberta Group
  says the EU's "37% reduction for 2018" in HFC supply "resulted in the manufacturers raising their
  prices by a minimum of 180% per Kg of FM200" (circa 2018) (search extract) —
  https://thebigredguide.com/news/alberta-group-announces-updates-gas-regulation-co-4870-ga.1608696279.html
- Reclaimed agent exists as a lower-cost route: A-Gas sells "FM-200R", reclaimed FM-200 with "the
  same extinguishing concentrations as FM-200" and FM approval; no price shown (search extract) —
  https://www.agas.com/us/products-services/fire-protection-halon-products/fm-200r/
- Scale of a small installation: a UK public tender for a "Gas suppression system for Server room"
  shows a contract value of "£50,000", covering supply, installation and maintenance (search
  extract) — https://stotles.com/explore/notices/39ca454a-826d-4faf-b690-1375155d4c44/gas-suppression-system-for-server-room
- I found no sourced UK figure for the cost of refilling one FM-200 or Novec cylinder, and no
  sourced loss figure for a fire where a leaked cylinder failed to reach design concentration
  (see "Searched for but not found").

## 2. Frequency
- Inspection interval: secondary sources summarising BS EN 15004-1 say cylinders "must be checked
  semi-annually for pressure and weight" and that a container must be refilled or replaced when
  agent quantity drops by "more than 5%" or pressure by "more than 10%" (clause 9.2.1 as quoted by a
  third party; standard text not seen; the extract blended these two pages, so which page carries
  which phrase is approximate) (search extract) —
  https://www.designingbuildings.co.uk/wiki/Fire%20suppression%20system%20readiness%20between%20inspections
  and https://kordfire.com/?p=28601
- A US regulatory text (46 CFR 147.65, vessels) uses the same idea: "A container showing a loss in
  net weight of more than 5 percent should be refilled or replaced" (US) (search extract) —
  https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-46/chapter-I/subchapter-N/part-147/subpart-B/section-147.65
- Leak behaviour in practice: an Eng-Tips forum thread describes a CO2 system whose pilot bottle
  gauge read about half scale; the advice was to weigh the cylinders because they may be empty
  (search extract) — https://eng-tips.com/threads/co2-cylinder-valve-function-of-the-quot-push-button-cap-quot.451790
- I found no published statistic for how many clean-agent cylinders are found below the 5%
  threshold at service visits.

## 3. Current workarounds
- The semi-annual task is to "verify agent quantity by weighing or using a liquid level device"
  (secondary summary) (search extract) —
  https://www.designingbuildings.co.uk/wiki/Fire%20suppression%20system%20readiness%20between%20inspections
- Portable ultrasonic liquid-level indicators are the usual tool for liquefied agents, e.g.
  Coltraco's Portalevel range (product pages; no prices shown) (search extract) —
  https://firebuyer.com/coltraco-portalevel
- Cylinder supervisory pressure switches exist for some systems, e.g. Kidde-Fenwal datasheets for
  pressure switches (US manufacturer) (search extract) —
  https://kidde-fenwal.com/Media/Data%20Sheets/K-90-9047_BA_print.pdf
- A UK supplier says its service contracts cover "gas supply integrity testing", detector inspection
  and other safety checks; no prices given (search extract) —
  https://parr-group.co.uk/fire-safety/fire-suppression-supply-installation-maintenance

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- F-gas (UK): GOV.UK guidance covers fire protection systems and says about 1.6 kg of HFC-227ea
  equals 5 tonnes CO2e (the leak-check threshold), and that fitting leak detection can halve the
  check frequency (search extract) —
  https://www.gov.uk/f-gas-in-refrigeration-air-conditioning-and-fire-protection-systems
- NetRegs (UK) says that for 5–50 tonnes CO2e, leak checks are due every 12 months, or every 24
  months if a leak detection system is fitted, and that leak checks must be done by certified
  personnel (search extract) —
  https://www.netregs.org.uk/environmental-topics/air-pollution/fluorinated-gases-f-gases/fire-protection-equipment/
- Important limit on the pitch: the Fire Industry Association (2015) says fire protection systems
  inspected to ISO 14520 or EN 15004 are deemed to meet the F-gas leak-check requirement, so the
  existing 6-monthly service already satisfies F-gas (UK) (search extract) —
  https://www.fia.uk.com/news/f-gas-portables-what-do-the-changes-in-the-regulation-mean-for-the-uk-market-.html
- Record keeping: GOV.UK says records are generally kept for five years (page lists refrigeration
  and air-conditioning explicitly; fire-protection scope not confirmed) (search extract) —
  https://www.gov.uk/guidance/record-f-gas-in-equipment-you-own-or-service
- Contractor competence: UK public tender notices returned by one search include a university
  fire alarm and gas suppression maintenance tender requiring that "all operatives working on Gas
  Suppression Systems must be F-Gas certified", and a 2024 tender asking for "BAFE SP203 Part 3 -
  Gas Suppression System Design, Installation, maintenance". The extract did not say which notice
  holds which wording, so attribution between these two notices is approximate (search extract) —
  https://www.contractsfinder.service.gov.uk/Notice/0644b101-9649-4591-b4e6-89a72051fadf and
  https://www.contractsfinder.service.gov.uk/Notice/87b2ebd2-fbf5-42da-ac78-794e82bc1758
- Agent supply: 3M announced in December 2022 it would stop making Novec 1230 by end-2025 (PFAS
  exit); Fike says it offers SF 1230 as the same chemical (US vendor) (search extract) —
  https://www.fike.com/knowledge-center/3m-novec-1230-discontinued-what-now/

## 7. Competitors & market gap
- Coltraco Permalevel Featherweight (UK): constant monitor for CO2, inert gases and clean agents
  that infers agent mass from pressure at known temperature; marks a cylinder "LEAKING" after a 1%
  mass drop and alarms at 5% loss; one unit covers up to 12 cylinders, 48 with four units (vendor
  claim; no price published) (search extract) —
  https://www.sensors.nl/en/vare/permalevel-featherweight-voor-constante-bewaking-brandblussystemen
- Coltraco "PermaMass Featherweight" product listing (search extract) —
  https://www.firesafetysearch.com/permamass-featherweight/
- TLX / Thames-side style weight-monitoring units: suspended and platform weight-monitoring
  systems "accurate within 100 grams", alarm to the fire panel below a threshold, up to 20 units
  per control panel, wireless on request (vendor claim) (search extract) —
  https://firebuyer.com/?p=78312
- Rotarex markets automatic 24/7 suppression readiness monitoring ("Be Sure Your Fire Suppression
  System is Ready 24/7/365 — Automatically") (vendor) (search extract) —
  https://rotarex.com/blogs/be-sure-your-fire-suppression-system-ready-247365-%E2%80%94-automatically
- NAFFCO sells a CO2 gas weight monitoring system with continuous gas weighing devices, one per
  cylinder, max 20 per unit (UAE vendor) (search extract) —
  https://www.naffco.com/downloads/pdf/co2-system-gas-weight-monitoring-system.pdf
- Kidde-Fenwal holds a "cartridge weight monitoring" patent covering a monitoring assembly that
  sends alarms to a panel (US) (search extract) — https://patents.google.com/patent/US12097395
- Gap: none of the listings found publish a price or a cloud/LPWAN records service. The incumbents
  are wired to the fire panel and sold through suppression OEM channels.

## 8. Buildability (components, BOM, certification)
- Pressure-based mass inference is already proven by Coltraco ("<1%" precision vs "~2.5%" for
  typical gauges, vendor claim) (search extract) —
  https://www.sensors.nl/en/vare/permalevel-featherweight-voor-constante-bewaking-brandblussystemen
- Load-cell platforms under cylinders are already sold (100 g accuracy, vendor claim) (search
  extract) — https://firebuyer.com/?p=78312
- Gas storage rooms can be oxygen-deficient and actuators can fail unnoticed (WorkSafe WA
  bulletin), which matters for any device fitted near the valve (Australia) (search extract) —
  https://worksafe.wa.gov.au/publications/dangerous-goods-safety-bulletin-no-0117-gas-storage-automatic-fire-suppression-systems
- I found no component pricing for load cells or transducers in these searches, and no source on
  whether adding a transducer to a gauge port affects system approval (FM/LPCB listing).

## Researcher observations
- Weak compliance angle: the FIA says EN 15004/ISO 14520 servicing already satisfies F-gas leak
  checks, so continuous monitoring is not required by regulation. The value is risk reduction and
  agent-cost protection, which is harder to sell.
- The market is already served by suppression-specific products (Coltraco, TLX/Thames-side,
  NAFFCO, Rotarex) sold through OEM/installer channels. The gap may be only price and cloud
  reporting.
- A stronger buyer is probably the BAFE SP203-3 contractor, who could sell it as a premium tier
  and as protection for scarce agent. Large multi-cylinder inert-gas sites in colocation data
  centres are better targets than single small server rooms.
- Before building, check whether adding a transducer to a listed system affects its approval.
