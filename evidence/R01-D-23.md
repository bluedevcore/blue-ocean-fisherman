## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Construction and utility sites rely on hired mesh panel fencing (Heras-type). Wind or intruders can knock panels over or unclip them out of hours, leaving the perimeter open. Teams usually find out on the next morning's walk-round.
- Who suffers / who pays: Principal contractors and site managers (duty to prevent unauthorised access, theft losses); members of the public, especially children; fencing hire firms (call-outs to re-erect).
- Sensor concept: Clip-on tilt, shock and clamp-open sensors on some panels and gates, linked by a LoRaWAN gateway with LTE-M backhaul. Out-of-hours alerts go to a keyholder or security firm, and a daily perimeter-integrity log is kept.

## 7. Competitors & market gap
- Heras (Heras Deutschland) G-fence: detectors on each fence panel analyse vibration and movement in three axes to detect cutting, pulling and climbing. This is permanent perimeter intrusion detection (PIDS) and the snippet does not say whether it is wireless (search extract) — https://www.heinze.de/produktserie/perimeter-detektion-fuer-zaunanlagen-heras/11405079/drucken/
- HERMEQ "Temporary mesh Pulse fencing" (UK page): temporary mesh fence with monitored panels that detect climbing, cuts and panel movement, with an optional alarm relay, aimed at contractors. This is the closest direct competitor (search extract; price not shown) — https://hermeq.com/uk/en/temporary-mesh-pulse-fencing.html
- Ajax-based construction-site monitoring (Swiss integrator): solar-powered tower with 360° perimeter coverage, wireless and scheduled to arm out of hours (search extract) — https://www.aesag.ch/en/blog/alarmanlagen-funk-draht/ueberwachung-absicherung-von-baustellen-mit-ajax

## Researcher observations
- The regulatory hook is real but general. Reg. 13(4)(b) demands "necessary steps", not monitoring or records, so the sensor is a nice-to-have unless insurers require it.
- A direct temporary-fence competitor already exists (HERMEQ Pulse fencing), and permanent PIDS from Heras covers high-value sites. CCTV towers already serve larger sites.
- Contractor SHE alerts (Morgan Sindall 2025) and BSG commentary show wind blow-over is a recognised UK problem. The stronger angle may be wind and stability (a temporary works failure) rather than intrusion. Event fencing near crowds, where collapse injures the public, could be a better niche.
