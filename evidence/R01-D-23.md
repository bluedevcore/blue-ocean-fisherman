# R01-D-23 — Hired temporary fencing panels blown over or unclipped overnight — site perimeter open to the public

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Construction and utility sites rely on hired mesh panel fencing (Heras-type). Wind or intruders can knock panels over or unclip them out of hours, leaving the perimeter open. Teams usually find out on the next morning's walk-round.
- Who suffers / who pays: Principal contractors and site managers (CDM duty to prevent unauthorised access, theft losses, insurance conditions); members of the public, especially children; fencing hire firms (re-erection visits, lost panels).
- Sensor concept: Clip-on tilt, shock and clamp-open sensors on some panels and gates, linked by a LoRaWAN gateway with LTE-M backhaul. Out-of-hours alerts go to a keyholder or security firm, and a daily perimeter-integrity log is kept.

## Researcher observations
- The pain is better evidenced than in the first pass. HSE and COPFS prosecutions (£600k, £800k) explicitly turn on inadequate or unmaintained perimeter fencing, and a FOS decision shows theft cover lost when the enclosure was incomplete out of hours. Together these make "proof the fence was intact overnight" valuable to both the safety and insurance cases.
- Economics are tight. Panels hire for about £3.50–£10.90 a week, so a $50 sensor per panel is uneconomic. Only sparse placement (gates, corners, every Nth panel) or a gate-only product works, and that weakens detection of a single unclipped panel.
- A direct competitor exists (HERMEQ Pulse temporary fencing with panel-movement detection), and CCTV/alarm towers (BauWatch, Reconeyez) own the larger-site security budget.
- Better buyer and channel for the same problem: the national hire networks (National Tool Hire Shops, Lakeside) could add a rentable "smart gate/corner" kit to fence orders. Or the security-tower firms could add fence-tilt nodes to their existing out-of-hours monitoring, rather than selling to site managers directly.
