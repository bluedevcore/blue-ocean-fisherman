## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Mobile crushing and screening plant in England and Wales runs under local-authority (Part B / LAPPC) mobile-plant permits based on Defra's Process Guidance Note PG3/16. Typical permit conditions require water sprays at the feed and discharge points while crushing, and stopping if the water supply fails. Operators may crush with sprays off or the water supply empty, and there is no objective record that sprays ran.
- Who suffers / who pays: Neighbours and site workers (dust exposure), and the permit holder (prosecution, enforcement, loss of contracts).
- Sensor concept: A pressure switch or pulse flow sensor on the spray manifold and an accelerometer on the crusher chassis to tell crushing from idling. LTE-M, powered from the machine's 24 V supply. Logs minutes crushed without water, alerts the foreman, and exports a per-site compliance record.

## 1. Cost of incident
- UK (Cheltenham, 2015): after neighbours complained about dust from a mobile crusher at a redevelopment site, the council found it "operating without the correct water sprays to control dust emissions" and without the correct permit. The firm was fined £10,355 in total, plus a £120 victim surcharge and £450 costs (search extract) — https://www.cheltenham.gov.uk/news/article/1651/plant_operator_fined_for_not_obtaining_environmental_permit
- UK (Gloucester, 2016): a plant hire firm admitted using a mobile stone crusher without a permit and was fined £2,000 with £1,200 costs. The council said such machinery "should only be operated with water sprays to control the dust" (search extract) — https://www.punchline-gloucester.com/articles/aanews/plant-hire-firm-fined-over-mobile-stone-crusher
- US (Pennsylvania): DEP fined Knorr Contracting $2,000 after an inspector saw a crusher operating "with no water connected to the machine's water spray dust suppression system" (search extract) — https://www.recyclingtoday.com/news/pennsylvania-dep-fines-contractor
- Both UK cases combine "no permit" with "no sprays", so neither isolates the fine for a spray failure alone. I did not confirm the idea's "fines up to £50k in the magistrates' court" (see "Searched for but not found").
- Recurring cost of compliance: annual LAPPC subsistence charges for mobile screening and crushing plant, for example Ipswich's first and second permits at £626 / £1,034 / £1,551 (low / medium / high risk). A worse risk rating raises this fee (search extract) — https://www.ipswich.gov.uk/sites/ipswich/files/m-files/fees_and_charges_lappc_part_b.pdf

## 2. Frequency
- Crushing is a daily activity on demolition and recycling sites while a job runs. Permits require the regulator to be notified before the plant operates at each new site, which shows frequent moves (search extract) — https://www.maldon.gov.uk/publications/ppc/data_folder/Concrete%20Crushers/Maldon%20Demolition%20Ltd%20(Two)/Permit-Variations%20etc/Maldon%20Demolition%20-%20003.pdf

## 3. Current workarounds
- Permit conditions require weekly maintenance checks of the water-spray dust suppression equipment, with faults and repairs recorded, and adequate frost protection in winter. These are paper records; the summary did not say which of the East Suffolk, Coventry or Charnwood permits carried the weekly-check wording (search extract) — https://www.eastsuffolk.gov.uk/assets/Environment/Environmental-Protection/Environmental-permitting/Environmental-Permits-Directory/N/Norfolk-and-Suffolk-Ltd-Permit.pdf ; https://coventry.gov.uk/downloads/file/7761/mineral-improvements-ltd-163-permit ; https://charnwood.gov.uk/files/documents/permit_ref_no_157_cliffe_house_farm/Permit%20Ref%20No.%20157%20-%20Cliffe%20House%20Farm.pdf
- Council inspections use checklists. A Harlow inspection form asks whether the crusher is fitted with water suppression and whether a suitable water supply is available, which is a point-in-time check (search extract) — https://www.harlow.gov.uk/sites/default/files/documents/RB%20Whitbread%20inspection%202016.pdf
- Boundary dust monitoring is used on urban demolition sites under planning conditions. For example Camden uses PM10 action levels of 190 µg/m³ (1 hour) and 250 µg/m³ (15 minutes), with SMS and email alerts on breach (search extract) — https://camdocs.camden.gov.uk/CMWebDrawer/Record/10556767/file/document
- Statutory nuisance is the fallback: councils must investigate dust complaints under Part III of the Environmental Protection Act 1990, and serve an abatement notice if a nuisance is confirmed (search extract) — https://www.cumberland.gov.uk/your-environment/nuisance/odour-and-dust-nuisance/dust-nuisance ; https://www.netregs.org.uk/environmental-topics/nuisances/nuisance-from-dust-insects-noise-odour-and-pathogens/dust-nuisance/

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- Statutory guidance: "Process Guidance Note 3/16(12): Statutory guidance for mobile crushing and screening" (Defra, Sept 2012), which supersedes PG3/16(04) (search extract) — https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a7f778a40f0b62305b87529/mobile-crushing-and-screening-process-guidance-note-3-16_12_.pdf
- Northern Ireland has an equivalent DAERA statutory guidance for mobile crushing and screening (search extract) — https://www.daera-ni.gov.uk/publications/statutory-guidance-mobile-crushing-and-screening
- Typical permit conditions (East Suffolk, Coventry and Charnwood permits): "High pressure, low volume water spray dust suppression equipment shall be fitted to the machine feed hopper and the final discharge points". In one example, "if water of the required pressure is not available for use on the suppression system, then the process shall not operate", and crushing must stop if the water supply fails until it is restored (search extract) — https://www.eastsuffolk.gov.uk/assets/Environment/Environmental-Protection/Environmental-permitting/Environmental-Permits-Directory/T/TRUPermit-2020.pdf ; https://coventry.gov.uk/downloads/file/7764/hydrex-uk-ltd-163-transferred-permit ; https://charnwood.gov.uk/files/documents/permit_ref_no_157_cliffe_house_farm/Permit%20Ref%20No.%20157%20-%20Cliffe%20House%20Farm.pdf
- Legal basis: permits are issued under Regulation 13 of the Environmental Permitting (England and Wales) Regulations 2016 (Sheffield permit, Stuart Hamilton Plant) (search extract) — https://www.sheffield.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2024-09/stuart_hamilton_plant_ltd.pdf
- Coventry permit: "you will be liable for prosecution if you fail to comply with the conditions of this permit" (search extract) — https://www.coventry.gov.uk/downloads/file/37138/permit-221-a14-idt-as-of-09102019
- Fees depend on risk rating: subsistence charges for mobile plant step down with fleet size (permits 1–2, 3–7, 8+) and vary by low, medium or high risk. Harlow's 2026/27 first-permit fees are £646 / £1,034 / £1,506 (search extract) — https://www.harlow.gov.uk/sites/default/files/documents/Fees%20and%20charges%202026%20to%202027.pdf ; https://www.ipswich.gov.uk/sites/ipswich/files/m-files/fees_and_charges_lappc_part_b.pdf
- Gap in the mandate: permits require the sprays and a weekly check record, but not continuous logging. A spray log is voluntary evidence, and it could support a lower risk rating and fee (my inference; not confirmed).

## 5. Buyer & budget
- Permit holders include demolition contractors, plant hire firms, farms and quarry operators. Examples are Maldon Demolition Ltd, Stuart Hamilton Plant, ATE Farms (mobile crusher) and Cemex (search extract) — https://www.harborough.gov.uk/directory_record/4165/ate_farms_mobile_crusher ; https://www.coventry.gov.uk/downloads/file/37104/permit-210-cemex-cromwell ; https://www.sheffield.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2024-09/stuart_hamilton_plant_ltd.pdf
- Hire model: a Maldon permit (EvoQuip Cobra 230) says the plant "may be hired out as required with or without an Operator at demolition or sites", so the permit holder (the hire firm) carries liability while a hirer's operator runs the machine. That makes the permit holder a motivated buyer (search extract) — https://www.maldon.gov.uk/publications/ppc/data_folder/Concrete%20Crushers/Maldon%20Demolition%20Ltd%20(Two)/Permit-Variations%20etc/Maldon%20Demolition%20-%20003.pdf

- I found no job adverts for "compliance manager" at crushing contractors. Most permit holders look like SMEs where the MD holds the budget (inference).

## 7. Competitors & market gap
- OEM telematics (Sandvik My Fleet, Metso Metrics): the search extracts mention engine, location, utilisation and crusher/feeder/conveyor data, but no spray or dust-suppression data (search extract) — https://hub-4.com/news/launch-of-my-fleet-telematics-system-at-hillhead-2018 ; https://cdrecycler.com/news/metso-outotec-lokotrack-metrics-digital-system-aggregates-recycling
- Spray systems are standard on the machines: a used Metso LT200HP listing describes a "water spraying system with pump for binding dust particles at crusher cavity, under the crusher and at the discharge end of main conveyor". The hardware exists; the question is whether it is switched on (search extract) — https://www.machinerytrader.com/listing/for-sale/261554039/metso-lt200hp-crusher-aggregate-equipment
- Boundary dust monitors measure the outcome, not whether sprays ran: Mabey Hire's Enviroguard (PM10 and PM2.5 around the clock, hire or managed service) and BES Group packages with SMS alerts. No hire prices were found (search extract) — https://www.mabeyhire.co.uk/application/monitoring/dust-monitoring/ ; https://strainstall.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/BES-AR-NDV-Leaflet-Jul-25-2.pdf
- Osiris light-scattering monitors (MCERTS, PM10/PM2.5/PM1) are named in Camden-approved schemes (search extract) — https://camdocs.camden.gov.uk/CMWebDrawer/Record/10556767/file/document
- **Gap (explicit):** I found no product that logs whether a mobile crusher's dust-suppression sprays were running while it crushed. OEM telematics already capture crushing activity (crusher, feeder and conveyor data) but appear not to log spray pressure or flow. Boundary dust monitors measure ambient PM10 and are bought by the main contractor, not the crusher permit holder. The gap looks real, but OEMs could close it cheaply with a firmware or input change, since they already sense crushing state.

## Researcher observations
- c4 is well supported: specific, quotable permit conditions (sprays at feed and discharge, "process shall not operate" without water pressure, weekly checks recorded) and a real UK prosecution that combines dust complaints with no sprays. c1 is moderate: the fines found are £2k–£10k.
- c7 looks open today, but the main risk is OEM telematics. Sandvik, Metso and Finlay already sense crushing state and could add one input for spray pressure. A brand-agnostic retrofit for older and mixed fleets (EvoQuip, Rubble Master, Keestrack, older Finlay) is the defensible niche.
- A better buyer may be the plant hire firm that holds the permit but hires the crusher out with or without an operator (Maldon example). It carries the legal risk while someone else runs the machine, so it has the strongest motive to buy a spray logger.
- Channel idea for the breeder: councils' public registers give a complete, free list of permit holders, and the annual subsistence charge renewal (1 April) is a natural moment to sell.
