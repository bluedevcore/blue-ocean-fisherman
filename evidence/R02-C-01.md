# R02-C-01 — Spa and salon towels with massage oil self-heat in folded stacks overnight after a dryer cycle is cut short before cool-down

> Research note: WebFetch is blocked in this environment, so no page was opened in full. Every
> bullet below relies on the title and extract shown in WebSearch results and is marked
> "(search extract)". 8 queries were run (the per-idea budget). Geography is UK unless stated;
> Australian, NZ, Singapore and US sources are labelled.

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Towels used in spas, salons, massage clinics and hotel spas carry treatment oils that
  low-temperature washes may not remove. If a dryer cycle is stopped before its cool-down phase
  and warm towels are folded and stacked, the stack can self-heat and ignite, often after the
  premises close.
- Who suffers / who pays: Salon/spa owners and operators (property damage, lost trading,
  insurance excess and premium), staff who do the laundry, neighbouring businesses.
- Sensor concept: Current clamp on the dryer supply to detect cycles aborted before cool-down,
  plus a LoRaWAN/LTE-M temperature tag on the clean-towel shelf or trolley; alert if stacked
  laundry stays hot after close; monthly "dryer discipline" report.

## 3. Current workarounds
- Fire-service advice only: Shropshire FRS recommends not leaving freshly dried laundry stacked
  overnight and not storing warm, damp laundry in sealed bags (search extract) —
  https://www.shropshirefire.gov.uk/news/tumbled-dried-towels-could-pose-fire-risk
- Shropshire FRS "Self Heating of Towels" leaflet (search extract) —
  https://www.shropshirefire.gov.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Self-Heating-of-Towels-1.pdf
- Fenland/Cambridgeshire: crews urged beauty and spa businesses to wash oily items at higher
  temperatures before drying (search extract) —
  https://www.wisbechstandard.co.uk/news/22669430.beauty-spas-restaurants-pubs-urged-wash-tumble-dryer-fire-risk-oils/
- Cambridgeshire FRS said it would contact beauty and spa treatment centres to warn about tumble
  drying oil-contaminated items (search extract) —
  https://340-588b6f14179be.radiocms.com/news/local-news/fire-service-warns-of-oil-residues-causing-fires-in-tumble-dryers

  (search extract) — https://manuals.online/miele/dryer/pt-8301-sl/user-manual/1xxcz

  quickly removed and spread out (attribution to this Electrolux manual approximate) (search
  extract) — https://manuals.online/electrolux/dryer/t4900/user-manual/1zdks
- I found no source on how often staff abort cool-down, or on labour time spent checking.

- JLA commercial dryers: sensor-activated system that "automatically dowses flames on detection

- US9573003 "Tumble dryer with passive extinguishing system" (search extract) —
  https://patents.google.com/patent/US9573003

  (search extract) —
  https://iot-shop.de/en/shop/mil-ct101-868m-milesight-ct101-lorawan-intelligenter-stromwandler-6989

## Researcher observations
- Pain evidence (c10) is strong: many LFB incident pages and fire-service warnings, plus insurer
  guidance (NFU Mutual, Zurich, Ecclesiastical). Cost evidence (c1) is weak in the UK.
- The hazard spans pubs, restaurants (tea towels, NFU Mutual), hotels and leisure-centre spas,
  not only salons. Hotel/leisure spa laundries (LFB City of London hotel, Twickenham leisure
  centre) may be a better buyer with facilities budgets than small independent salons.
- Channel: laundry equipment suppliers (JLA) already sell service contracts and an in-drum
  suppression feature, so they could add a stack-temperature tag. They are also a potential
  competitor if they add one.
- Hardware is cheap (CT clamp about €76–€98), so the product is mainly the alert and the record.
