# R02-B-04 — RPZ backflow valves stick open and dump water through the relief port for months between annual tests

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Reduced-pressure-zone (RPZ) valves protect the public water supply from fluid category 4 risks and must be tested (usually annually) by an approved tester. If a check valve fouls, the relief valve can discharge continuously through the tundish between tests, wasting water and, in larger failures, flooding plant rooms. Sites may also bypass a faulty RPZ, which breaches the Water Fittings Regulations.
- Who suffers / who pays: Site owners / FM teams paying for water and flood damage; the water undertaker protecting the network.
- Sensor concept: two-electrode water-presence sensor or flap flow detector in the RPZ tundish/air-gap drain plus pipe temperature probe; LoRaWAN or LTE-M; alerts on sustained discharge and appends a monthly log to the annual test certificate.

## 1. Cost of incident
- Discharge volumes (vendor/utility worked examples, US): a New York rural water association example estimates a 2-inch RPZ at 80 psi discharges about 40 gpm, and that an ~8,378-gallon room would fill in 54 minutes if the drain can't keep up (search extract) — https://www.nyruralwater.org/sites/default/files/Farewell_Spring%202020.pdf
- Watts-sourced articles: mechanical-room drains are often undersized or clogged and unable to remove water during a catastrophic relief discharge; average commercial flood claims cited at around $75,000 "but can easily extend into the millions" (vendor claim, US; the figure is in one of these two Watts articles) (search extract) — https://empoweringpumps.com/watts-sentry-plus-alert-system-reduced-pressure-zone-backflow-preventers/ ; https://empoweringpumps.com/watts-water-technologies-mitigating-reduced-pressure-zone-backflow-discharge
- Engineering note that in some cases it is preferable to install an RPZ outside the building and spill to grade (US engineering firm) (search extract) — https://www.nv5.com/knowledge/reduced-pressure-zone-basics/
- Repair cost: WaterSafe says RPZ valves typically start around £60–£80 ex VAT depending on size and manufacturer, with annual testing fees set by the approved tester (UK) (search extract) — https://www.watersafe.org.uk/blog/posts/rpz-valves-explained/
- No UK figure was found for water wasted (litres or £) by a continuously "dribbling" RPZ between tests — the idea's "£ thousands a year" is unsourced.

## 2. Frequency
- Continuous discharge under no-flow is a known, listed failure mode: Aalberts IPS manual lists "relief valve continuously discharging during no-flow conditions" with the #1 check valve fouled with debris as a cause (manufacturer, US) (search extract) — https://www.aalberts-ips.us/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/ES1593_IOM_BFMM4000_rev.b.pdf
- Apollo manuals list a #2 check fouled with debris plus back-pressure, and a damaged diaphragm, as causes of continuous discharge (manufacturer) (search extract) — https://www.cooneybrothers.com/userfiles/documents/Apollo_4ALF20A03R1_Manual.pdf ; https://manualsnet.com/apollo-valves/40-200-series
- Trade article "Troubleshooting backflow assembly relief valves" (Plumber magazine, Oct 2021) (search extract) — https://www.plumbermag.com/online_exclusives/2021/10/troubleshooting-backflow-assembly-relief-valves
- No statistic was found for the share of UK RPZs failing their annual test, or how often relief valves discharge between tests.

## 3. Current workarounds
- Annual test by an approved tester is the main control: "RPZ valves must be tested annually, sometimes more frequently"; intervals are set by the local water undertaker (UK water-company guidance) (search extract) — https://www.yorkshirewater.com/business/water-regulations/rpz/ ; https://www.anglianwater.co.uk/business/help-and-advice/water-fittings-regulations/reduced-pressure-zone-valves
- Yorkshire Water: a compliance test must not be carried out earlier than 30 days before the due date; late testing "is not acceptable and may result in enforcement action" (search extract) — https://www.yorkshirewater.com/business/water-regulations/rpz/
- Reporting: SES Water asks for the test report within 10 working days; Northumbrian Water requires approved testers to submit test data via its NWL RPZ Portal (search extract) — https://seswater.co.uk/-/media/files/seswater/your-water/water-regulations/fact-sheet-_rpz-valve.pdf ; https://www.nwl.co.uk/business/water-regulations/rpz-valves/
- Mechanical mitigation: electric override shut-off valves that close the supply upstream when discharge persists and need manual reset (MIFAB ACV) (search extract) — https://mifab.com/Catalog/Library/ACV_-_Electric_Override_Safety_Shut-Off_Valve_System.pdf
- Between tests, no routine inspection of the tundish was evidenced (absence of evidence, not proof).

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- Water Supply (Water Fittings) Regulations 1999: RPZ valves are recognised protection for risks up to and including fluid category 4; commissioning and testing by an accredited tester approved by the water supplier; WRAS publishes the tester list (UK water-company guidance) (search extract) — https://seswater.co.uk/-/media/files/seswater/your-water/water-regulations/fact-sheet-_rpz-valve.pdf ; https://www.stwater.co.uk/my-supply/water-fittings-regulations/rpz-valves
- Installation, testing and maintenance follow the WRAS Approved Installation Method; Southern Water cites AIM-08-01; WRAS consulted on a 2024 RPZ AIM issue 3 (search extract) — https://www.southernwater.co.uk:443/help-advice/rpz-valves ; https://wras.co.uk/downloads/public_area/news/consultation/2024-rpz-aim/2024-rpz-aim-issue3-consultation-version-feb-2024.pdf
- Wessex Water: if a passed test certificate is not received within 14 working days of the due date it will contact the owner, and "the supply may be switched off until the issue with the RPZ valve can be resolved" (search extract) — https://www.wessexwater.co.uk/your-water/reduced-pressure-zone-valves
- Enforcement under the Regulations (backflow cases, not RPZ-bypass specifically): an installer convicted at Sheffield magistrates of two breaches (Reg 3(2)(i), Reg 7(1)(a)) after a borehole cross-connection, fined £15,102.40 incl. costs (search extract) — https://www.waterregsuk.co.uk/downloads/publications/case_studies/cs-borehole-cross-connection_apr2021_.pdf
- Severn Trent "strawberry pink water" cross-contamination at Newent: Haygrove Ltd ordered to pay £11,707.78 incl. costs; root cause of such incidents "normally associated to backflow" (search extract) — https://waterregsuk.co.uk/news/newsletter/categories/case_studies/strawberry_pink_water_cross_contamination
- Worcester: a company ordered to pay £5,776.94 after contaminating the supply to 83 apartments, for breaches of the 1999 Regulations (search extract) — https://www.pbctoday.co.uk/news/health-safety-news/water-quality-failings/53556/
- Veolia: a man prosecuted for ten offences including using a fitting without adequate backflow prevention (search extract) — https://www.veolia.co.uk/press-releases/illegal-water-connection-not-tolerated-water-company
- No prosecution specifically for bypassing a faulty RPZ was found.

## Researcher observations
- Strong: the compliance machinery is real and well documented (annual approved-tester tests, water-company portals, supply cut-off threats), and the failure mode (fouled check → continuous relief discharge) is in every manufacturer's troubleshooting guide. Sensor and BOM are trivial (~€55 leak sensors).
- Weak: the economic loss is not sourced for the UK; the dramatic losses are US "catastrophic discharge" plant-room floods, for which Watts already sells a $1.6k–$3.5k kit. c7 depends on Watts not selling SentryPlus in the UK.
- Better angle: sell to approved RPZ testers as a per-valve monitoring add-on that pre-fills the water-company test submission (NWL portal style) and flags fouled checks early — the tester gets a repair visit, the site avoids a supply cut-off. Plant rooms in hospitals and labs (catastrophic flood risk) are the higher-value niche.
