# R01-A-36 — Floor-stored grain heats in hot spots between weekly spear probes; loads rejected for insects or mycotoxins

Note on method: WebFetch is blocked in this environment by network policy, so no page was fetched

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: On-farm floor-stored and bin grain (wheat, barley, oilseed rape) can heat locally (insects, mites, moulds) between manual spear-probe readings. Heating can lead to infestation, ochratoxin A risk, downgrades or merchant rejection. Red Tractor requires records of temperature/moisture monitoring in store.
- Who suffers / who pays: Arable farm managers / owners and grain-store staff pay rejection charges and losses; merchants and hauliers bear disruption.
- Sensor concept: Wireless temperature (plus RH) spears left in the heap (battery LoRaWAN/LTE-M); hot-spot alerts; aeration-fan advice; automatic assurance-ready monitoring record.

## 2. Frequency
- A Midlands haulier said rejections jumped from one or two a week to five out of 66 loads in a single week (search extract) — https://fwi.co.uk/news/rejections-rise-worries-grain-trade
- Mycotoxin (historic): in a survey of 306 farm-stored wheat, barley and oat samples from the 1997 UK harvest, OTA "was detected in 21 % of the samples", with 2.7% exceeding 5 µg/kg; concentrations related to moisture content and storage time (search extract) — https://ibl.kb.nl/article/0000000121605/16/7/6
- Storage fungus Penicillium verrucosum can produce OTA at ≥18% moisture content and, "where grain is stored above 18% MC, these levels can be exceeded in two weeks, if the temperature is sufficiently high" (AHDB) (search extract) — https://ahdb.org.uk/knowledge-library/how-the-grain-store-environment-affects-pest-fungi-insects-and-mites-levels
- AHDB notes at least twelve mite species infest UK stored grain and that "the direct physical presence of storage pests may also make the grain unacceptable to the customer" (search extract) — https://ahdb.org.uk/knowledge-library/how-the-grain-store-environment-affects-pest-fungi-insects-and-mites-levels
- Monitoring cadence (drives c2): AHDB says check temperature every few days until the cooling target is reached, then weekly; insect/mite checks weekly until grain reaches and stays at 5°C, then monthly (search extract) — https://ahdb.org.uk/knowledge-library/cooling-targets-for-grain-storage ; https://ahdb.org.uk/knowledge-library/monitoring-insect-and-mite-populations-in-grain-stores
- No current national rate of farm-store heating or of insect rejections per farm per year was found.

## 3. Current workarounds
- Manual probing at fixed points: AHDB says measure at the same location each time, where cooling is slowest, usually ~0.5 m below the surface and midway between ducts, using a calibrated probe (search extract) — https://ahdb.org.uk/knowledge-library/cooling-targets-for-grain-storage
- Cooling targets drive aeration: below 12°C within 3–4 months of harvest to stop insect activity and below 5°C by end of December (AHDB; trade press citing AHDB also gives "below 15C within two weeks of harvest") (search extract) — https://ahdb.org.uk/knowledge-library/cooling-targets-for-grain-storage ; https://www.fwi.co.uk/arable/crop-storage/whats-in-your-grain-store-tackling-pests-before-harvest
- Organic guidance: weekly grain temperature checks once at target, weekly pitfall-trap checks, at least monthly moisture checks (Soil Association) (search extract) — https://soilassociation.org/farmers-growers/farming-news/2016/grain-storage-in-trying-times
- Manual probe hardware: 2 m digital grain temperature probe £285 ex VAT; 1.5 m fibreglass probe £104.50 ex VAT (SW Seeds) (search extract) — https://www.swseeds.co.uk/temperature-probes/digital-grain-temperature-probe-2m/ ; https://www.swseeds.co.uk/temperature-probes/1.5-fibreglass-grain-temperature-probe/
- Paper template: Red Tractor publishes a grain storage strategy template and example (search extract) — https://redtractor.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/sites/10/2021/09/Grain-storage-strategy-template-and-example.docx
- Pre-outloading checks/sampling: Dealey's pre-outloading checklist (search extract) — https://dealey.co.uk/public/files/pre-outloading-checklist-201126101332.pdf
- No source quantified labour time per probing round.

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure

## Researcher observations
- c4 is well evidenced (Red Tractor CR.ST.3/CR.ST.4 and weekly post-harvest monitoring records) and c2/c3 are evidenced via AHDB's weekly/monthly schedule.
- c1 is weaker than the idea claims: rejections cost £15–£30/t (hundreds of pounds per load), though sector-wide figures (~£20m in a record year) and OTA risk add weight.
- c7 is the main risk: Martin Lishman (Barn Owl, AgroLog, upcoming Multi-Sense), Robydome, Farmex and Javelot already serve UK stores with wireless probes and subscriptions (£180/device/yr; £525/yr).
- Better channel suggestion: grain merchants (Frontier is already buying Javelot for its own stores) could push low-cost spears to supplying growers to cut their rejection handling costs; the NFU rejections hub also shows a merchant–grower friction point.
