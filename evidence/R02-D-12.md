# R02-D-12 — Smouldering fires after hot works on historic roofs: the 60-minute fire watch is unprovable and the roof void is unwatched overnight

> Research conditions (2026-11-05): WebFetch is blocked by this environment's network policy, so no page was
> fetched. Every bullet below rests on WebSearch result titles, snippets and summaries and is marked
> "(search extract)". Where a search summary drew on several pages without saying which one carried a line,
> the bullet says so. 8 queries were run for this idea (the budget cap).

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Hot work on historic roofs (lead welding, torches, plumbing) can ignite dry roof timbers. A smoulder may develop out of sight in a void and break out after the post-work fire watch has ended and the site is locked. Contractors work under hot-work permits that set fire-watch periods, but proving afterwards that the watch was kept relies on paper.
- Who suffers / who pays: Owners of heritage buildings and their insurers suffer the loss. Conservation roofing and leadwork contractors carry permit compliance and liability.
- Sensor concept: A clip-on battery node with an 8x8 thermopile array, a rate-of-rise heat sensor and an optical smoke sensor, using LTE-M. It would log the fire-watch period, keep monitoring for 12–24 hours and alert the site manager or keyholder. Hired per day, with a permit-compliance PDF.

## 1. Cost of incident
- Uppark (National Trust, West Sussex), 30 August 1989: devastated by fire "caused by a workman's blowtorch whilst repairing lead flashing on the roof, just two days before the work was due to be completed" (UK) (search extract) — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppark
- The Art Newspaper: the National Trust "has issued a writ jointly with its insurers against the builders", claiming negligent use of oxyacetylene equipment and the cost of reconstruction. It calls this the largest restoration the Trust has undertaken (UK) (search extract) — https://www.theartnewspaper.com/1991/06/01/the-external-restoration-of-uppark-house-in-west-sussex-is-virtually-complete-two-years-after-the-fire-which-half-gutted-the-building-in-august-1989
- Uppark repair cost: a secondary page puts the cost of repairing the house and contents at "nearly £20 million, largely met from insurance". Cardinus gives "over £20m". Cardinus also says the fire was electrical in origin, which conflicts with the other sources, so treat it with caution (UK) (search extract) — https://m.koolearn.com/liuxue/ielts/read/12118.html/ ; https://www.cardinus.com/?p=5697
- St Mary's, Beachamwell (Norfolk, Grade I, about 1,000 years old), 2022: workers replacing stolen lead said "they had been welding lead when a spark had landed on another part of the roof – which was thatched – and caught light". "The spire has gone, the roof has gone, the windows have gone." The cause is reported as believed, not formally confirmed (UK) (search extract) — https://bbc.in/3Hr8iKQ ; https://premierchristian.news/en/news/article/medieval-norfolk-church-destroyed-in-devastating-blaze
- Zurich claims data, reported by IFIC Forensic via AIRMIC: 164 major hot-work fires over ten years linked to £69.8m of losses (commercial and industrial, not heritage-specific) (UK) (search extract) — https://airmic.com/node/40304
- Surrey: the fire service said a fire at a Grade II-listed school building "started through roofers on the job" (UK; no final determination seen; headline "contractors responsible roof fire") (search extract) — https://www.getsurrey.co.uk/news/local-news/contractors-responsible-roof-fire-4833119

## 3. Current workarounds
- Fire watch, as NFU Mutual's hot-works guide recommends: monitoring by a trained competent person "for at least 60 minutes after the work ends", then checks at intervals of no more than 20 minutes up to 120 minutes before sign-off (UK; insurer guidance) (search extract) — https://www.nfumutual.co.uk/globalassets/business/rms/2021-update/hot-works-fire-safety-guide.pdf
- Ecclesiastical's heritage hot-works guidance: an intermittent fire watch "for up to 180 minutes" with checks every 15–30 minutes, the period set by the fire risk assessment (UK; heritage insurer) (search extract) — https://www.ecclesiastical.com/media/kjsdmasv/heritage-hot-works.pdf
- HSB/Munich Re: a minimum of 30 minutes' continuous watch after work, then periodic inspection for at least another 30 minutes (UK & Ireland; engineering insurer) (search extract) — https://www.munichre.com/hsbeil/en/insights/guides-to-loss-prevention1/construction-guides-to-loss-prevention/fire-caused-by-hot-work---guide-to-loss-prevention.en.html
- Temporary wireless site fire alarms: Safe Site Facilities hires "wireless construction site fire alarms" at £21.00 per week ex VAT, with internal batteries that "will last up to 3 years" (UK) (search extract) — https://safesitefacilities.co.uk/products/construction-site-wireless-fire-alarms
- Avoidance: NFU Mutual's guide for historic and listed buildings says hot work should generally not be permitted in historic buildings, and recommends a detached workshop at least 7 m away where possible (UK) (search extract) — https://www.nfumutual.co.uk/link/4bd1710fe850445b8a65ad2976f0b6f0.aspx
- Not found: the labour cost of a fire watch, for example operative day rates spent standing watch.

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- The Joint Code of Practice "Fire Prevention on Construction Sites" is in its 10th edition (August 2022, Amendment 1 January 2023) (UK; FPA/insurers) (search extract) — https://wallbarn.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/FPA-Fire-Prevention-on-Construction-Sites-Joint-Code-of-Practice-10th.pdf ; https://miller-insurance.com/News-and-insights/Latest-insights/Fire-prevention-on-construction-sites-10th-Edition-of-the-Joint-Code-of-Practice
- Ecclesiastical says the Code calls for a Hot Works Permit system for all hot work and for choosing a contractor with heritage experience (UK) (search extract) — https://www.ecclesiastical.com/media/kjsdmasv/heritage-hot-works.pdf
- **Important correction to the idea:** the search did not confirm that the Joint Code itself sets a 60-minute watch. Insurer guidance varies: 60 minutes plus checks to 120 (NFU Mutual), 30 plus 30 (HSB), and up to 180 minutes (Ecclesiastical, heritage). The idea's "at least 60 minutes" is one insurer's figure, not a universal rule (search extract) — https://www.nfumutual.co.uk/globalassets/business/rms/2021-update/hot-works-fire-safety-guide.pdf ; https://www.munichre.com/hsbeil/en/insights/guides-to-loss-prevention1/construction-guides-to-loss-prevention/fire-caused-by-hot-work---guide-to-loss-prevention.en.html
- IFIC Forensic, via AIRMIC: "Failure to comply with best practice can invalidate cover" (UK; headline) (search extract) — https://airmic.com/node/40304
- CITB short training standard "Hot working permits and risks including insurers' requirements" (roofing) covers the causes of roof hot-work fires, valleys and box gutters, and references NFRC Safe2Torch (UK) (search extract) — https://citb.co.uk/standards-and-delivering-training/training-standards/short-training-standards-search/roofing-common/hot-working-permits-and-risks-including-insurers-requirements
- Historic England publishes fire advice covering fires caused by hot works and permit-to-work systems (England) (search extract) — https://historicengland.org.uk/advice/technical-advice/emergency-and-fire/fire-advice
- In the US, FM Approvals Standard 3270, "Remote Fire Watch Systems" (January 2021), sets performance requirements for "hot work robots which detect products of combustion... during and after hot work operations". This means a recognised approval route exists for electronic fire watch (US/global) (search extract) — https://fmapprovals.com/Resources/Insights/remote-firewatch

## 5. Buyer & budget
- FPA Hot Work Passport: a national benchmark of fire-safety knowledge for "employees involved in carrying out hot work or with responsibility for supervising or managing hot work". - CITB's "managing hot work" guidance puts the duty on the employer to determine tasks needing a permit, establish a permit system and ensure compliance. ## 7. Competitors & market gap
- Ramtech WES3 (UK) wireless site fire alarm with an EN54-certified heat detector. It has battery life "over three years" and thermistor rate-of-rise detection, and is marketed for hot-work risk; its marine brochure says welding and cutting are linked to "approximately 85% of hot work-related fires". No price was visible: Fire Detection Shop showed a £0.00 placeholder and LGM asks for a quote (UK) (search extract) — https://www.firedetectionshop.co.uk/products/wes3-heat-detector-site-alarm ; https://rt-staging-files.s3.amazonaws.com/downloads/pdf/wes3_marine_brochure.pdf
- Safe Site Facilities: wireless site fire alarms at £21.00/week ex VAT (UK) (search extract) — https://safesitefacilities.co.uk/products/construction-site-wireless-fire-alarms
- Fike Fire Watch: FM-approved (October 2024), with IR3 flame detectors, an HD camera and video-analytics smoke detection; "up to 18.7 hours of continuous monitoring"; marketed as "a cost-effective alternative to hiring contractors for fire watch duties". No price found (US/global) (search extract) — https://fike.com/knowledge-center/fike-fire-watch-fm-approval-press-release ; https://fireandsafetyjournalamericas.com/fike-fire-watch-receives-global-fm-approval-for-temporary-fire-detection-system/
- BlackStarTech FireSight: FM 3270-approved (March 2025), combining multi-spectrum IR flame detection and smoke video analytics as "an alternative to traditional fire watch methods". No price found (US) (search extract) — https://www.fmapprovals.com/Resources/Insights/BlackStarTech-FireSight
- **GAP (explicit):** Cheap UK temporary wireless site alarms (WES3; Safe Site at £21/week) **already** provide battery heat and smoke detection that can stay in place overnight after hot work. What they apparently lack is a thermal-array view of the work zone and a permit-compliance log or PDF; this was not confirmed, since product feature pages weren't fetched. FM-approved camera-based remote fire-watch units (Fike, FireSight) exist but are US-oriented and unpriced. The idea's distinct gap is therefore narrow: "evidence of the watch plus thermal-array detection in a void, cheaply hired". It is not "no cheap detection exists".

## Researcher observations
- The pain is real and well documented: Uppark (lead flashing, blowtorch, £20m-class loss), Beachamwell (lead welding spark into thatch), and insurers publishing heritage hot-work guidance with fire watches of up to 180 minutes.
- The competitive gap is weaker than the idea states. Cheap UK temporary wireless site alarms with EN54 heat and smoke detectors (WES3; £21/week hire) already cover "unwatched overnight". FM-approved camera fire-watch units exist for the high end. The novel part is the thermal array and the permit-evidence log.
- Better buyer or channel: heritage insurers such as Ecclesiastical, which insures most Anglican churches, could require or subsidise post-hot-work monitoring as a permit condition. Bundling into the existing site-alarm hire fleet, for example as a WES3-compatible add-on, may beat a standalone product.
- Event frequency per contractor is low, which favours a hire model over sale, but hire is anchored by about £21/week alarm rental.
