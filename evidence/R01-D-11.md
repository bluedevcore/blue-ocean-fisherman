# R01-D-11 — Pool dosing-chemical drum runs dry overnight — disinfectant drops and the pool can't open in the morning

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Leisure centre, hotel and school pools dose sodium hypochlorite and acid from drums or IBCs. When a drum runs dry overnight, disinfectant falls and the pool fails its pre-opening test (lost sessions and swim-school refunds, ~£1k–£5k a day for a busy leisure pool), and the emergency delivery costs extra. Staff check drum levels by eye; chemical suppliers deliver on fixed rounds.
- Who suffers / who pays: Duty managers, swimmers, swim schools / buyer as proposed: Leisure Centre / Pool Plant Manager (or hotel Chief Engineer), via the chemical supply contract
- Sensor concept: Weighing platform under drums/IBCs (or ultrasonic level in an IBC) plus dosing-pump pulse/current monitoring, LTE-M; low-stock and 'dosing stopped' alerts, consumption trends (detects over-dosing or leaks), feed to supplier auto-replenishment.
