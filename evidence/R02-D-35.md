# R02-D-35 — Small dairies' batch-pasteuriser chart recorders jam or run out of ink, so the batch can't be released

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Farm dairies, ice-cream makers and cheesemakers that pasteurise in vats have to show that each batch reached the required time and temperature (for example 63°C for 30 minutes). This is usually done with an automatic chart recorder. Paper recorders can jam, lose pens or drift. A batch without a valid record may have to be held, reprocessed or discarded, and a missing record can lead to enforcement.
- Who suffers / who pays: The owner-operator of a small dairy, cheese or ice-cream business. Their customers and regulators depend on the record.
- Sensor concept: A calibrated PT100 probe in the vat thermowell plus current sensing on the agitator, sending over LTE-M. It records time at temperature for each batch, alerts in real time if the holding temperature drops, and produces a signed digital batch chart for EHOs and customers.

## 2. Frequency
- Every batch needs a record. Minnesota statute requires "a dated recording-thermometer chart for each batch of mix" to be kept at the plant (US; attribution inferred among the Minnesota URLs) (search extract) — https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/?id=32.64&year=2005
- Recorder consumables wear out monthly: Eurotherm's paper-vs-paperless calculator says each circular chart recorder uses 3 pens and that pens last 30 days (vendor claim) (search extract) — https://www.eurotherm.com/ko/food-and-beverage-cpg/pasteurization-paper-recording-cost-savings-calculator/
- Calibration checks recur. New York requires recording thermometers to be tested "at least once each 3 months by regulatory agency; daily by plant operator" (US; attribution between appendices 4 and 9 inferred) (search extract) — https://regulations.justia.com/states/new-york/title-1/appendices/appendix-4
- Outbreaks caused by real pasteurisation failures are uncommon. A UK case-study paper notes that outbreaks of gastrointestinal disease from milk pasteurisation failures "are rare in the UK" (UK, peer-reviewed) (search extract) — https://www.cambridge.org:443/core/product/F6CB111787FFE1B14663A576CBDF7F63/core-reader
- Gap: no statistic was found on how often chart recorders jam, run dry or drift per site per year.

## 3. Current workarounds
- Paper charts are collected and archived by hand: Yokogawa says operators "must manually retrieve paper charts periodically and archive them for audit", with "no redundant backup if the paper chart is lost" (vendor; attribution among Yokogawa notes inferred) (search extract) — https://yokogawa.com/ca/library/resources/application-notes/data-acquisition-for-milk-and-dairy-pasteurization
- Paper upkeep cost is rising: Eurotherm says "the cost of operation and upkeep of paper chart recorders is increasing every year", citing on-site record storage and dearer pens and charts (vendor claim) (search extract) — https://www.eurotherm.com/food-and-beverage/fda-compliance-to-pmo-criteria-for-the-evaluation-of-electronic-data-loggers/
- Manual calibration checks: the US PMO method checks the recording thermometer against an indicating thermometer, with the recorder required to read within 0.5°C (1°F). The test immerses the sensing element in boiling water for at least 5 minutes, or 2 minutes for electronic recorders (US; attribution inferred to the PMO PDF) (search extract) — https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/eot2008/20080916121147/http://www.cfsan.fda.gov/~acrobat/pmo99-4.pdf
- Canada's critical-process test puts the probe in a circulating bath at processing temperature, and any deviation must be documented with the corrective action (Canada) (search extract) — https://inspection.canada.ca/en/preventive-controls/dairy-products/critical-process-test-procedures
- Operators are told to annotate charts: CFIA calls the process control record "the legal record of the pasteurization process" and asks operators to note the reasons for all unusual occurrences (Canada) (search extract) — https://inspection.canada.ca/en/preventive-controls/dairy-products/batch-pasteurization
- Gap: no source quantified the staff time spent changing charts and pens or writing up thermometer reads.

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- UK time/temperature standard (original 1995 text): pasteurised cream must be heated to not less than 63°C and held for not less than 30 minutes (Dairy Products (Hygiene) (Scotland) Regulations 1995, Sch. 6, Part II, as made; current retained-law text not checked) (search extract) — https://legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1995/1372/schedule/6/part/II/made
- Ice cream (1995 text): the mix must be heated to not less than 65.6°C, held for not less than 30 minutes, then cooled to no more than 7.2°C within 1½ hours (Sch. 6, Part V, as made; Scottish and England & Wales versions) (search extract) — https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1995/1372/schedule/6/part/V/made?view=plain ; https://legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1995/1086/schedule/6/part/V/made
- Record-keeping expectation: ABB's UK case study says food-quality regulations require several pasteuriser parameters to be "accurately recorded and the paper record/chart stored for several years" (UK, vendor) (search extract) — https://new.abb.com/control-systems/industry-specific-solutions/food-beverage/paperless-recorders-for-daisy-co-uk
- UK trade code: the Specialist Cheesemakers Association's Assured Code of Practice (Edition 3, 2025) is available to paid-up members and enforcement officers. Its pasteurisation and recorder sections did not surface (UK) (search extract) — https://specialistcheesemakers.co.uk/about-us/assured-code-of-practice
- US vat rule: Florida requires the recording chart to show the full holding period, and at least 30 minutes when cooling starts in the vat (US) (search extract) — https://ccmedia.fdacs.gov/content/download/82003/file/Requirements-Batch-Vat-Pasteurization.pdf
- Canada: records should show a 30-minute hold after filling, with no more than 0.5°C difference between the centre and the coldest milk in the vat during holding (Canada) (search extract) — https://inspection.canada.ca/en/preventive-controls/dairy-products/batch-pasteurization
- Retention and electronic records: Virginia requires recorder charts to be kept for at least six months and allows electronic records with the regulator's prior approval (US; attribution inferred) (search extract) — https://law.lis.virginia.gov/admincode/title2/agency5/chapter510/section650/
- Eurotherm reads the 2013 PMO as treating electronic data recorders as suitable replacements for paper chart recorders (US, vendor reading) (search extract) — https://www.eurotherm.com/food-and-beverage/fda-compliance-to-pmo-criteria-for-the-evaluation-of-electronic-data-loggers/
- Gap: no current FSA or UK local-authority guidance was found stating that a small vat pasteuriser must have an automatic recorder rather than a manual log, and no insurer requirement was found.

## Researcher observations
- Strong points: the per-batch record is a hard legal requirement in US and Canadian rules and is expected in UK practice. A recorder failure stops production or triggers a recall or embargo, and there is a real UK case (Daisy & Co) and a US recall caused by a paper jam.
- Weak points: c7 is exposed. ABB, Yokogawa, Eurotherm and especially Anderson-Negele (PPR plus Legendary cloud) already sell paperless and cloud-backed pasteurisation recorders, and recorders can last 17 years. Accuracy (0.5°C) and tamper-evidence demands raise the build bar above a generic LoRaWAN PT100.
- The best wedge may be the UK pasteuriser OEMs and installers (e.g. Dairy Machinery UK) and instrument distributors (A M Sensors). They could fit a low-cost logging and alert module as standard on new small vats, or sell it as the upgrade when a paper recorder fails. The SCA, as the trade body whose code reaches EHOs, could endorse the record format.
