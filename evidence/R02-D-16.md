# R02-D-16 — Listed buildings next to basement digs and demolitions: party-wall awards demand crack and tilt readings taken by hand

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Party-wall awards and planning conditions for basements and demolitions next to existing (often listed or historic) buildings commonly require crack and movement monitoring of the neighbouring building. This is usually done by surveyors visiting weekly to monthly to read tell-tales, studs and levelling targets. Movement between visits is not recorded.
- Who suffers / who pays: Adjoining owners carry the risk of damage. The building owner or developer pays for monitoring and safeguarding under the Party Wall etc. Act 1996, and contractors manage it.
- Sensor concept: A battery crack-displacement sensor (linear potentiometer) plus a MEMS tilt sensor, reporting hourly over LoRaWAN or LTE-M. Alerts at the award's trigger levels, with a monthly report to both surveyors.

## 3. Current workarounds
- Manual monitoring as specified in Camden basement documents: schedules of condition with photographs before and after works, and "attachment of tell tales or demec studs to accurately record movement of significant cracks". Some use "micrometer measurements between 3 studs glued to wall adjacent to crack" (UK) (search extract; pooled Camden documents; candidates shown) — https://camdocs.camden.gov.uk/CMWebDrawer/Record/5187067/file/document ; https://camdocs.camden.gov.uk/CMWebDrawer/Record/8641579/file/document
- Priced manual monitoring: one Camden-filed monitoring schedule lists "Site Set up and Base readings... £695", "Regular Monitoring of targets on site... £200 per visit", £100 per visit for processing and presenting results, and £30 per crack gauge installed, including team, equipment, insurance and travel (UK; one contractor's project-specific rates) (search extract) — https://camdocs.camden.gov.uk/CMWebDrawer/Record/4691172/file/document
- A 2026 party-wall cost guide puts extra inspections at about £100–£300 per visit, and says basement excavations sit "at the top end of party wall agreement cost" (UK; consumer guide) (search extract) — https://www.bookabuilderuk.com/blog/party-wall-agreement-cost-2026
- A structural survey guide lists "Crack monitoring (installation and 6-month reporting): £300–£600" (UK; general, not party-wall-specific) (search extract) — https://crownarchitecture.co.uk/resources/structural-survey-guide

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- Party Wall etc. Act 1996, s.6: the building owner must serve notice for excavation within 3 m or 6 m depending on depth, and under s.6(3) must, if required by the adjoining owner, underpin or otherwise safeguard the neighbour's foundations at their own expense (England & Wales) (search extract; the summary pooled the London law pages; candidates shown) — https://osborneslaw.com/blog/basement-developments/ ; https://www.jonathanlea.net/blog/london-neighbour-building-works-damage-legal-rights/
- Camden Basement Construction Plan guidance (CPG4) covers "timing and frequency of monitoring, method of monitoring, trigger levels for action" alongside construction sequence and temporary works (UK; older guidance version) (search extract; pooled Camden documents; candidate shown) — https://camdocs.camden.gov.uk/CMWebDrawer/Record/6453751/file/document
- Trigger levels in Camden basement specifications use green, amber and red tiers. Examples: "Green/Amber Trigger Levels shall be 5mm in any plane... Amber/Red Trigger Levels shall be 10mm"; vibration amber 10 mm/s and red 20 mm/s PPV. Trigger points are "to be agreed with party wall surveyor" (UK) (search extract; pooled Camden documents; candidates shown) — https://camdocs.camden.gov.uk/CMWebDrawer/Record/6531875/file/document ; https://camdocs.camden.gov.uk/CMWebDrawer/Record/7092277/file/document
- RBKC Basements SPD (April 2016) expects a construction method statement from a qualified civil or structural engineer. A commercial guide says RBKC policy makes excavation **underneath** a listed building unacceptable, so the listed-building risk is mostly about adjacency (UK) (search extract) — https://www.rbkc.gov.uk/media/document/basements-spd-april-2016 ; https://hampsteadrenovations.co.uk/guides/chelsea-basement-lower-ground-refurbishment/
- An RBKC committee report on a Chelsea Conservation Area basement (25 Ovington Street) asked for additional Construction Method Statement information "to address differential movement within the terrace" (UK) (search extract) — https://www.rbkc.gov.uk/pdf/Document%2070%20-%2025%20Ovington%20Street%20SW3%20-%20Planning%20Committee%20Report.pdf

Surveyors may not accept their resolution and stability against 5 mm or 10 mm triggers, or for tilt in arc-seconds. Award acceptance by both surveyors is the real certification hurdle. ## Researcher observations
- The compliance hook is real and specific. Camden basement specifications set monitoring frequency (weekly to monthly, up to readings every shift) and green, amber and red trigger levels (5 mm and 10 mm). Party-wall surveyors agree the triggers, and the building owner pays (s.6(3)). Manual monitoring is priced at about £200–£300 per visit plus £695 set-up, which is a clear target to undercut.
- The volume pool is London basements (about 240 applications a year in RBKC alone historically; 7,328 approvals in London 2008–2019), but it is falling since policies tightened. The listed-building subset is unknown.
- The competitive gap is the weakest point. UK remote crack monitors (Montec via South Survey; SPPS subBOX) exist and appear aimed at surveyors, and their prices are unknown. Surveyors' acceptance of low-cost MEMS tilt data is a technical risk.
- Better channel: the monitoring surveyor firm already doing the £200 visits could deploy cheap wireless nodes to cut visit counts. Selling to them as a tool may beat selling to the contractor. The insurer and subsidence market (SPPS) is a second pool with steadier demand.
