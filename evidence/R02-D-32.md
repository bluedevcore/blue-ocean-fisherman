# R02-D-32 — High-care rooms lose positive air pressure unnoticed between once-a-shift gauge readings, raising Listeria risk in ready-to-eat food

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Chilled ready-to-eat (RTE) producers segregate high-risk and high-care zones and supply filtered air. In high-risk zones, BRCGS expects positive pressure relative to lower-risk areas. A failed fan, clogged filter or propped door can collapse or reverse the differential, and it is usually checked on a dial gauge at intervals.
- Who suffers / who pays: Consumers (especially vulnerable groups), the technical team and the producer (recall, closure, delisting).
- Sensor concept: Digital differential-pressure sensor (Sensirion SDP-class) across each high-care/high-risk wall or door, plus door contacts. LoRaWAN. Alarm if the differential collapses for more than N minutes, and a continuous record for BRCGS auditors and EHOs.

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- BRCGS (Issue 9): high-risk facilities must be supplied with sufficient changes of filtered air and be under positive air pressure. A risk assessment defines the filter specification, air-change frequency and the specification for positive pressure (UK consultancy summary of the Standard) (search extract) — https://techni-k.co.uk/risk-based-facilities/high-risk-and-high-care-facilities
- Important limitation: an interpretation of clause 4.4.13 states, "Whilst there is no specific requirement for an air over pressure" for high-care areas (text truncated). The positive-pressure obligation applies firmly to high-risk only, not high-care (search extract) — https://techni-k.co.uk/risk-based-facilities/high-risk-high-care-ambient-high-care-controls ; https://ascfoodsafety.com/?p=17459

## Researcher observations
- Weak causal link: the headline outbreak (2019) is attributed to cleaning and hygiene and to contaminated meat from a supplier, not air pressure. c1 is indirect, as the idea admits.
- Scope correction: BRCGS mandates positive pressure for high-risk zones. For high-care there is "no specific requirement for an air over pressure". The target is high-risk (cooked meats, some RTE), a smaller population than "high-care".
- The BOM is cheap (SDP810 about €25 plus a LoRa node about €48) against wired monitors at about $1,600–$2,400. Price headroom exists, but wireless food-focused DP monitors already exist (Monnit, Sonicu, Leap).
- Better channel: AHU/HVAC maintenance contractors, who could sell filter-loading alerts with the room differential. Better niche: high-risk cooked-meat and RTE plants that supply retailers demanding a specific Pa level, since retailer codes are stricter than BRCGS.
