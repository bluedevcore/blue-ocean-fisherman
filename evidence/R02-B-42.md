# R02-B-42 — Radon sump fans in schools in radon Affected Areas fail silently, letting levels climb back above the action level

> Research note: WebFetch is blocked in this environment, so no page was opened in full. Every
> bullet below relies on the title and extract shown in WebSearch results and is marked
> "(search extract)". 8 queries were run (the per-idea budget). Geography is Great Britain
> (England unless stated); Highland Council (Scotland), Northern Ireland, Ireland and US sources
> are labelled.

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Under the Ionising Radiations Regulations 2017 (IRR17), employers in radon Affected
  Areas must measure radon and act where the annual average exceeds 300 Bq/m³. Many schools
  installed an active radon sump (a fan pulling air from under the floor). If the fan fails or is
  switched off, radon can rise again without anyone noticing until the next retest, if one is
  done.
- Who suffers / who pays: Staff and pupils (long-term exposure); the employer (local authority,
  multi-academy trust or independent school governors) carries the legal duty and pays for
  testing and remediation.
- Sensor concept: Differential-pressure switch or current clamp on the sump fan, with an
  optional low-cost radon sensor; LoRaWAN or LTE-M; alert on fan failure and a monthly "system
  operating" record.

## 1. Cost of incident
- Enforcement precedent: Kingswood School, Bath (2023) was fined £50,000 for exposing seven
  children and two adults to high radon levels; HSE found the school had not monitored radon
  for eight years, and the problem surfaced only after HSE intervened in 2018 (England) (search
  extract) —
  https://itv.com/news/westcountry/2023-07-21/boarding-school-fined-for-exposing-pupils-and-staff-to-radioactive-gas
- The same £50k fine reported in a Somerset Council external news page ("Bath school given 50k
  fine for exposing pupils to radioactive gas") (search extract) —
  https://external.somerset.gov.uk/bath-school-given-50k-fine-for-exposing-pupils-to-radioactive-gas
- Cost to re-do remediation: UK Radon guidance (archived copy) gives a typical cost of about
  £800 for an active sump with fan, "up to £2,000"; a DIY active sump about £300 (up to £700)
  (domestic figures; UK) (search extract) —
  https://tnaqa.mirrorweb.com/ukgwa/20260505145242mp_/https://www.ukradon.org/information/reducelevels
- Checkatrade cost guide: "most radon sump systems cost around £800", can climb to £2,000 (UK,
  domestic) (search extract) — https://www.checkatrade.com/blog/cost-guides/radon-test-cost
- Fan replacement only (US): HomeAdvisor lists radon fan replacement at roughly $110–$600 (US;
  approximate range from the extract) (search extract) —
  https://www.homeadvisor.com/cost/environmental-safety/radon-fan-replacement-cost
- The main harm (lung-cancer risk) is long-term and not priced per incident; I found no
  per-incident cost of a failed school sump fan.

## 2. Frequency
- England ran a national, multi-year programme to measure and reduce radon in schools — "the
  first national radon campaign within a single occupational sector"; councils in high-radon
  areas such as Cornwall and Devon have "mature and ongoing" measurement and mitigation
  programmes (search extract) — https://www.irpa.net/members/TS10c.7.pdf
- HPA presentation: the cross-government schools programme ran from 2008 (ages 5–16), and the
  original three-year timescale was extended (search extract) —
  https://www.irpa.net/members/1030%20thu%20forth%20gooding%20TS10c.7.pdf
- Isle of Man: radon found in five schools; a UKHSA spokesperson said results are "similar to
  many other schools and workplaces tested across the UK" (search extract) —
  https://gold.manxradio.com/news/isle-of-man-news/radon-found-in-five-manx-schools/
- Highland Council (Scotland): Ardgour, Carrbridge and Newtonmore primaries measured above the
  (then) 400 Bq/m³ action level; Ardgour to receive three active radon sumps using a fan and
  piping (search extract) —
  https://scottishconstructionnow.com/article/measures-announced-to-tackle-radon-levels-in-highland-schools
- Highland Council: after sumps were fitted, retesting at one site showed levels below the
  action level but above the target level, so a further two sumps were installed; another
  site's first attempt failed and needed further 90-day testing — evidence that remediation
  does not always hold (attribution of each detail to a specific briefing approximate) (search
  extract) —
  https://www.highland.gov.uk/news/article/9472/radon_works_to_highland_council_properties_%E2%80%93_briefing_may_2016
- I found no statistic on how often school radon sump fans fail or are switched off.

## 3. Current workarounds
- U-tube manometer: in a working system the two liquid legs sit at different heights; if level,
  the fan has stopped; a typical reading is 0.5–2.0 in. water column (US guidance site) (search
  extract) — https://inspectandtest.net/guides/radon-mitigation-fan-replacement/
- Periodic retest: Camden Council document recommends radon levels be "re-monitored after
  installation and annually" for a remediation system (search extract) —
  https://camdocs.camden.gov.uk/CMWebDrawer/Record/9611029/file/document
- Derbyshire County Council's radon safety policy sets monitoring for six months after works,
  then annually for three years (council policy) (search extract) —
  https://www.derbyshire.gov.uk/site-elements/documents/pdf/council/policies-plans/radon-safety-policy-and-guidance.pdf
- Somerset Council Property arranges radon monitoring for LA-maintained schools through a
  contractor, PropertECO (search extract) — https://external.somerset.gov.uk/radon-guidance-update
- Labour cost of these retests (detector packs, contractor visits) was not found.

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- HSE: where radon is reduced by engineered means, the employer should ensure levels are
  "remeasured immediately after installation"; "Regular maintenance of the engineering controls,
  supported by occasional (for example annual) measurements will ensure the system remains
  effective" (GB) (search extract) — https://www.hse.gov.uk/radiation/ionising/radon.htm
- Stroud District Council: where radon is controlled by an engineered solution such as a sump,
  "your risk assessment should include regular checks that the control measures are functioning
  correctly and are properly maintained" (search extract) —
  https://www.stroud.gov.uk/business/health-and-safety/radon-in-the-workplace/
- BIR advice sheet: if the annual average exceeds 300 Bq/m³ the employer must notify HSE under
  IRR17 (search extract) —
  https://www.bir.org.uk/media/533928/advice_sheet_3__radon_lb2024_v2.pdf
- Legal commentary (Mondaq, UK): above the action level schools are "required to implement
  measures to reduce radon levels", including radon sump systems (search extract) —
  https://www.mondaq.com/uk/employee-rights-labour-relations/1678726/school-estate-and-workplace-risk-is-radon-on-your-radar
- UK National Radon Action Plan (2nd edition) sets the policy framework (search extract) —
  https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/655ca137046ed400148b9d4a/UK-national-radon-action-plan-second-edition.pdf
- Ireland (for comparison): EPA says schools/workplaces use a 300 Bq/m³ reference level and
  advises remediation and retest within 12 months, and retesting previously high rooms every 5
  years (search extract) — https://www.epa.ie/environment-and-you/radon/radon-testing/
- Insurer requirements for radon fan monitoring: not found.

## Researcher observations
- Compliance (c4) is solid: HSE explicitly expects "regular maintenance" and periodic
  measurement of engineered controls, and councils write their own monitoring policies.
- Cost evidence is weak: one £50k fine (for not monitoring at all), and remediation is cheap
  (£800–£2,000). Local fan alarms cost about $54, which caps hardware price expectations.
- Better buyer: council property teams and radon contractors (PropertECO, Lucion type) that
  already run managed monitoring contracts. A fan-status feed could be bundled into those
  contracts rather than sold to schools directly. Housing associations with sumps in social
  homes (e.g. LiveWest radon standards) are a possible adjacent niche:
  https://www.livewest.co.uk/sites/default/files/2023-07/Radon%20Safety%20Standards%2023_0.pdf
  (search extract).
