# R01-D-37 — Food factory external doors propped open — the main pest ingress route that pest reports and auditors keep citing

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: External dock and fire doors at food factories and warehouses are left or propped open. That lets rodents, birds and insects in. Auditors and pest contractors cite open doors, but sites do not measure how long doors stand open.
- Who suffers / who pays: Food-site technical managers, who own BRCGS compliance and the pest contract (the payer, possibly through the pest contractor); pest contractors; the business, through fines, recalls and lost certification.
- Sensor concept: A magnetic or accelerometer door-open sensor with a dwell timer and local buzzer, on LoRaWAN or LTE-M. It alerts after N minutes open and produces a weekly open-hours-per-door report correlated with pest activity.

## 3. Current workarounds
- Pest contractor reports: guidance says conducive conditions must be documented on every service report. "Cultural conditions" are staff practices that let pests in, "e.g., leaving doors open". Items stay on the report until resolved (US trade) (search extract) — https://giepublication-test.azurewebsites.net/article/documenting-conducive-conditions
- Food Engineering: a facility will never be free of pests unless the manufacturer pays for the right service level and manages proofing and housekeeping. Rapid-closing doors "only work effectively when they are not left open", which is a common problem (US trade) (search extract; the summary attributed this to "one food safety source", and Food Engineering is the matching trade article in the results) — https://www.foodengineeringmag.com/articles/98244-a-good-pest-control-program-can-help-protect-the-bottom-line
- Usual physical fixes: tight-fitting, self-closing doors, door sweeps and screens where doors must stay open (US regulator guidance) (search extract) — https://www.nyc.gov/assets/doh/downloads/pdf/rii/keep-pests-out-of-food-establishments.pdf
- Technical Manager job adverts list pest control among duties, e.g. "Manage pest control visits and ensure recommendations are implemented" (UK) (search extract; the summary did not say which advert carries this line; one of several adverts in the results) — https://findajob.dwp.gov.uk/details/17414093
- Cold-store practice: a door-open alarm that sounds after four minutes and emails the responsible person (US vendor guidance) (search extract) — https://www.dwyeromega.com/en-ca/resources/how-trigger-cold-storage-alarm

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure

## Researcher observations
- Audit pressure is real and evidenced: doors and pest prevention (4.4.8) are a recurring BRCGS non-conformity, with "doors left open" a typical finding. Pest infestation prosecutions in UK food businesses are frequent, with fines from about £16k to £134k.
- Hardware is a commodity (LoRaWAN door contacts £55–£70; consumer alarms about £13), so any value is in the pest-programme and audit report, not the sensor.
- Better channel for the same problem: the large pest contractors (Rentokil, Anticimex) already sell connected monitoring into audited food sites by subscription. A door-dwell module sold as an add-on to their platforms fits better than a standalone product. Cold-store operators already buy door-open alarms (energy and temperature), which could be an easier wedge.
- Weak point: no evidence that sites will pay specifically to measure door-open time, and the exact BRCGS clause text was not confirmed.
