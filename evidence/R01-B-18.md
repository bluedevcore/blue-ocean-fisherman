## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Last-mile delivery hubs, e-bike/e-scooter operators and e-bike distributors charge or store many lithium-ion packs, often overnight. A thermal-runaway fire can damage stock and premises, and insurers publish guidance (and in some cases tighten cover) for battery charging areas.
- Who suffers / who pays: Hub/depot operators and their insurers; landlords of the units; e-bike retailers and distributors facing cover restrictions.
- Sensor concept: Thermistor strip along charging racks, off-gas (electrolyte VOC / H2) sensor, current monitoring per charger bank, cellular link; alerts to a keyholder, automatic charger cut-off, and a charging log for the insurer.

## 2. Frequency

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- UK law: LFB's #ChargeSafe guidance is aimed at Responsible Persons under the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005 and covers storage and charging of e-bikes and e-scooters (search extract) — https://www.london-fire.gov.uk/safety/lithium-batteries/chargesafe-guidance-for-responsible-persons/
- UK: LFB Guidance Note 103 (charging and storage of electric powered personal vehicles) "does not create new legal obligations" and tells responsible persons to bring in a competent person or fire risk assessor. This is guidance, not a monitoring mandate (search extract) — https://www.london-fire.gov.uk/media/q1ygpvjg/gn_103-charging-and-storage-for-electric-powered-personal-vehicles.pdf
- UK insurer standard: Aviva's Loss Prevention Standard on electric bicycles (v2.3) covers handling, charging and storage of e-bikes and says changes to charging areas should be discussed with the insurer and broker (search extract) — https://static.aviva.io/content/dam/document-library/risk-solutions/electric_bicycles_lps.pdf
- UK insurer checklist: Aviva's rechargeable batteries checklist asks about standard operating procedures and battery storage areas "designed to provide at least 60 minutes fire resistance (or as stipulated in the premises Fire Risk Assessment)" (search extract) — https://static.aviva.io/content/dam/document-library/risk-solutions/rechargeable_batteries_checklist.pdf
- UK insurer guidance: Aviva LPS "Lithium-ion batteries – general considerations" (title only seen) (search extract) — https://static.aviva.io/content/dam/document-library/risk-solutions/lithium_ion_batteries_general_considerations_lps.pdf
- UK insurer research body: RISCAuthority RC61 covers selecting, storing, charging and using batteries in commercial and industrial premises, and says vehicle and mobility-scooter charging is covered by RC59. The copy found is hosted on a Shopify CDN (search extract) — https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0523/8705/files/RC61.pdf
- UK: a catalogue record lists RISCAuthority/FPA "RE2 Need to Know Guide: Lithium-ion Battery Use and Storage" (2025, 9 pp.) (search extract) — https://documentacion.fundacionmapfre.org/documentacion/publico/en/bib/187301.do
- UK cover restrictions (broker opinion): a BikeBiz piece by Bikmo says fire is the most common exclusion and that a claim involving e-bike batteries could be denied under a generic shop policy. - Counter-evidence (US): a HUB International broker blog says insurers have not changed policy wording to exclude lithium-battery losses but are "tracking rising losses closely" (search extract) — https://www.hubinternational.com/blog/2025/06/how-property-owners-can-reduce-lithium-ion-battery-fire-risk/

## 8. Buildability (components, BOM, certification)
- Off-gas sensing parts exist. Honeywell BES (battery electrolyte sensor) parts are listed at Mouser and target electrolyte vapour from initial venting, then H2 and CO during runaway (search extract) — https://www.mouser.com.gt/new/honeywell/honeywell-bes-electrolyte-sensors
- A MEMS hydrogen sensor (Posifa PGS5100) covers 0–25% H2 with a 100 ms response, and claims up to a 30-minute warning before fire (vendor claim) (search extract) — https://www.powerelectronicsnews.com/mems-hydrogen-sensor-detects-bms-thermal-runaway
- Research supports H2 as an early marker: a paper titled "Room Temperature Resistive Hydrogen Sensor for Early Safety Warning of Li-Ion Batteries" (search extract) — https://katalog.lib.cas.cz/KNAV/EdsRecord/edsdoj,edsdoj.7f482a985a3e49dbbfc694ef77e3a481
- Power budget reference: the Li-ion Tamer Gen 2 sensor draws 275 mW at 5 VDC and operates from -10 to 60 °C, so off-gas sensing is mains-powered rather than coin-cell (search extract) — https://firealarm.com/product/xtralis-lt-sen-m-l-li-ion-tamer-gen2-monitoring-sensor/

- Automatic charger cut-off means switching mains loads. That adds electrical-safety (CE/UKCA LVD) scope; no source was found on certification needs for this.

## Researcher observations
- Strong: the frequency and salience of e-mobility fires (LFB records, insurer headlines) and active insurer guidance (Aviva LPS, RISCAuthority RC61/RE2). Weak: no commercial-hub loss figures, no sighted policy conditions, and the target buyer is small and financially fragile (Zedify and Pedal Me administrations; Zedify's fleet was only ~8 vehicles per depot).
- Large operators (Royal Mail) simply ban on-site charging. That suggests the buyer with real volume is either the shared-scheme / rental operator (Voi-type warehouses with 200+ scooters) or the e-bike retailer/distributor whose cover is being restricted, rather than micro-hubs.
- The channel evidence favours charging-locker and cabinet makers (PowerShelter already bundles monitoring) and fire risk assessors. A breeder variant could be "monitoring module sold through cabinet makers to e-bike retailers and rental-scheme warehouses".
- Off-gas sensing needs mains power (~275 mW per Li-ion Tamer Gen 2 sensor), so this is a mains-powered node, not a battery sensor.
