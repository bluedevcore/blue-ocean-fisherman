# R02-A-27 — Motor trade insurers dispute theft claims when dealers can't prove keys were locked in the safe overnight

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: UK motor trade policies commonly require vehicle keys to be removed from the premises or locked in a safe / approved key cabinet outside business hours, and some wordings exclude theft where keys were in a safe or cabinet that was not locked with its key removed. After a key burglary or forecourt theft, a dealer may be unable to show the keys were locked away, and the claim may be disputed.
- Who suffers / who pays: Owner of an independent used-car dealer / motor trader; insurers and brokers handling the claim.
- Sensor concept: reed and bolt-throw switch on the key safe (optional BLE key-tag count), LTE-M; timestamped locked/unlocked log, alert if the safe is open after close of business, and a claims report for insurers.

## Researcher observations
- Strong: the insurance requirement is real and in policy wordings (NIG, Towergate, keymedia/KennCo exclusion "safe … not locked and the key removed"), and brokers say declines happen. An insurer once subsidised key cabinets with a reported 28% fall in theft claims.
- Weak: no direct evidence that disputes turn on proving the safe was locked; thieves often break the safe/key box or use relay/OBD methods, where a "locked" log helps prove compliance but does not prevent loss. Small dealers are price-sensitive and the alarm installer may already offer a door contact.
- Better buyer/channel: specialist motor-trade insurers or MGAs (NIG, Tradewise, Allianz motor trade) could bundle the sensor as a premium-discount condition, as Allianz Cornhill did with cabinets. A wider product (key-safe state + forecourt vehicle presence + RFID pouch compliance) may have a stronger pitch.
