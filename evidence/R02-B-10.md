# R02-B-10 — Continuous-flow grain dryers left running overnight at harvest: a stalled discharge or burner fault turns into a dryer and store fire

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: At harvest, on-farm and co-op continuous-flow grain dryers run for long hours, often through the night. If grain flow stops (a blocked column or a stalled metering/discharge roll) or the burner misbehaves, grain can overheat and smoulder, and the fire can destroy the dryer and sometimes the store.
- Who suffers / who pays: Arable farmers and grain-store staff (night checks, lost crop, lost drying capacity mid-harvest) and their property insurer.
- Sensor concept: Exhaust-air and plenum temperature probes, an accelerometer on the discharge metering roll or conveyor to confirm grain is moving, and a current clamp on the fan motors. LTE-M uplink. Phones the operator on a rise in temperature or a stop in flow, and keeps a night-running log.

## 3. Current workarounds
- UK fire services: Dorset & Wiltshire FRS advises farmers not to leave plant running unattended for extended periods (page dated 2017) (search extract) — https://www.dwfire.org.uk/news/grain-dryer-safety-advice-farmers/
- UK fire services: Lincolnshire FRS gives the same advice and tells farmers to check thermostats and automatic shut-off devices (search extract) — https://www.lincolnshire.gov.uk/lincolnshire-fire-rescue/farm-fire-safety
- US extension: University of Maryland says dryers "ideally should not be unattended while in operation", but that this is "not always possible" (search extract) — https://extension.umd.edu/resource/fire-hazard-safety-reminders-during-harvest
- Canada: an Alberta fire chief suggests 15–20 minutes as the most a dryer should be left unattended. This is experience, not a standard (search extract) — https://www.albertafarmexpress.ca/news/dont-let-your-grain-dryer-become-a-grain-fryer/
- US insurer: Nationwide's pre-incident planning guide says a fire watchperson should be able to tell a smoke plume from steam at night in poor light (search extract) — https://www.nationwide.com/business/agribusiness/safety-risk-management/resource-library/articles/preparing-for-grain-dryer-fires
- Built-in protection: many dryers have automatic shut-offs at critical temperature, and the extracts recommend testing any installed hazard-monitoring system before the season (search extract) — https://www.grainnet.com/article/251671/preventing-grain-dryer-fires-tips-for-startup-cleaning-shutdown
- I found no source that prices the labour cost of night checks.

- **Gap (explicit):** New US-brand dryers already ship with optional GSM/app alerts, and UK buyers can get a full control retrofit (Kentra) at £10k+. The cheap incumbents are generic GSM temperature alarms around £50, which do not detect a stalled metering roll. The gap is real but narrow: a farmer could rig a generic alarm, and the OEMs could extend their own modules.

## Researcher observations
- The pain is real and well documented as discrete incidents, and US single-fire costs run from $26k to $850k. UK evidence is thin on £ and frequency, apart from one loss adjuster's "50+ dryer fires a year".
- c7 risk is real: US-brand dryers (GSI, Sukup, Brock, Mathews) already offer GSM or app alerts. The gap is the older UK and European installed base (Opico mobile, Alvan Blanch/Svegma), which is about 3,500–4,000 Opico units alone. A clip-on, brand-agnostic "flow-stopped + over-temp" kit sold through dryer service dealers (KJ Engineering, Thorburn, LGL Agri) at their pre-harvest visit is the clearest route.
- The strongest lever may be the insurer. NFU Mutual already discounts for combine fire suppressors, so a dryer-monitor discount would be a natural extension. The loss adjuster Agrical is a good source of loss data to approach.
- The seasonality makes the subscription weak. The breeder could consider pairing it with off-season stored-grain temperature monitoring (hot spots in bins) on the same subscription, so the hardware earns all year.
