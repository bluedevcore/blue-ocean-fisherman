# R01-D-44 — Independent forecourts reconcile wetstock by hand — slow leaks, theft and short deliveries surface weeks late

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: UK petrol storage certificates (Petroleum (Consolidation) Regulations 2014) can carry conditions requiring wetstock monitoring, reconciliation or leak-detection records. Industry guidance (the APEA/EI "Blue Book") treats wetstock management as a core expectation. Some independent dealers are said to reconcile by hand, so leaks, theft and short deliveries are found late.
- Who suffers / who pays: Independent forecourt dealers and owner-operators (the payer); Petroleum Enforcement Authorities; the Environment Agency and groundwater users.
- Sensor concept: A non-ATEX data bridge on the existing in-shop ATG console (RS-232/USB) with an LTE-M uplink. It would run automated daily reconciliation, statistical leak detection and delivery verification, and keep records for the licensing authority.

## 1. Cost of incident
- ENDS Report: BP was fined £8,000 with £3,536 costs for a petrol station leak to groundwater in Hertfordshire. Clean-up "will cost £320,000" (UK) (search extract) — https://www.endsreport.com/article/1570803/bp-hit-320000-bill-petrol-station-leak
- ENDS Report: a 2009 leak at Esso's Trumpington (Cambridge) station cost Esso and its operator almost £15,500 in fines and costs. The tank was "heavily corroded and had a hole in it", and the Agency was told weeks later (UK) (search extract) — https://www.endsreport.com/article/1567225/petrol-leak-costs-esso
- Tesco was fined £8m (including £3m for the environmental offence) for a petrol leak at Haslingden, Lancashire, plus £35,434 EA costs and £22,000 county council costs. The EA blamed failure to address a known fuel-delivery problem and an inadequate alarm system (UK) (search extract) — https://forecourttrader.co.uk/news/tesco-fined-8m-for-petrol-leak-from-service-station/640178.article ; https://www.iema.net/articles/petrol-leak-costs-tesco-8m
- Shell was fined £30,000 for a 3,500-litre leak at Bishops Stortford, which it voluntarily reported in March 2005 (UK) (search extract) — https://forecourttrader.co.uk/news/shell-fined-30000-for-leak/628841.article
- Non-forecourt comparator: Taw and Torridge Coaches was fined £25,500 and ordered to pay £41,727.99 prosecution costs and £43,100.01 remediation costs for a diesel leak into the Taw estuary. Its depot fuel pumps had not been calibrated (UK; EA press release) (search extract) — https://www.gov.uk/government/news/taw-and-torridge-coaches-fined-for-diesel-leak-into-taw-estuary
- Stock loss: PRA examples put a 0.45% annual loss at about £4,455 for a 1.5m-litre site and nearly £27,000 for a 9m-litre site (UK; trade body, article date unknown) (search extract) — https://forecourttrader.co.uk/analysis/stock-loss-fightback/627821.article
- Edensure's managing director estimates a typical site selling 4m litres a year has about 12,000 litres unaccounted for, about a third through over-dispensing pumps (UK; vendor claim) (search extract) — https://forecourttrader.co.uk/news/fullstory.php/aid/2401/Taking__stock.html
- Edensure claims typical savings for retailers of around £15,000 a year (UK; vendor claim) (search extract) — https://forecourttrader.co.uk/equipment-news/edensures-hot-product/628495.article

## Researcher observations
- The compliance hook is sourced: at least one PEA's licence conditions require reconciliation or leak-detection records kept for 12 months, and the Blue Book update makes wetstock management a "fundamental expectation". Clean-up costs (BP £320k) dwarf typical fines (£8k–£30k), apart from the Tesco £8m outlier.
- Idea-record figures, checked: "£100k+ clean-up" is supported (BP £320k). "EA fines up to ~£20k" is roughly consistent with BP, Esso and Shell fines but understates the Tesco £8m case. "0.5% on 2m litres ≈ £14k" is the researcher's own arithmetic. The PRA example values 0.45% of 1.5m litres at £4,455 (about 66p a litre), so the £14k figure assumes a much higher litre cost and is not sourced.
- Main weakness: competition. Trade press says most retailers already subscribe to SIR services, and entry pricing (Edensure about £130 a month) is already low. "Independent" mostly means large groups (MFG 900+ sites) that are already contracted.
- Buildability looks feasible (documented TLS report codes over RS-232), but the six-digit security code and single serial port could block an add-on bridge.
- A better niche for the same problem may be non-retail fuel stores (coach depots, farms, plant hire yards) without ATGs or SIR contracts. See the Taw and Torridge Coaches diesel case in §1 (uncalibrated pumps; £43k remediation).
