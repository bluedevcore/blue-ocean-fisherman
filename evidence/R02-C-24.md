# R02-C-24 — Bath-water temperature not checked and recorded before every assisted bath: scalding deaths, falsified logs and six- to seven-figure fines

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: UK health-and-safety guidance says bath water for vulnerable people must not exceed 44°C. Care providers' own policies require staff to check the water before each bath, usually with a handheld thermometer and a paper record. When checks are skipped, residents have been scalded or killed. Providers have been prosecuted, and in one case records were falsified afterwards.
- Who suffers / who pays: Residents (injury or death). Care workers, who face personal prosecution. The registered provider or care group, which faces fines, CQC enforcement and civil claims.
- Sensor concept: A waterproof logging dip thermometer that records a reading tied to the bathroom and resident (e.g. by NFC tap) before each bath. Optionally, a clamp sensor on the hot feed downstream of the thermostatic mixing valve (TMV) that alerts if water above 44°C reaches the outlet. Output is a tamper-evident bath log for inspections.

## 1. Cost of incident
- Aster Healthcare (Birdsgrove Nursing Home, Bracknell, England): fined £1.04 million for corporate manslaughter, plus £184,513 prosecution costs, after the 2015 scalding death of 93-year-old Frances Norris. She suffered burns to 12% of her body and died three days later. Carers "failed to properly check the water temperature and continued to add hot water" (search extract) — https://thecarehomeenvironment.com/story/36727/aster-healthcare-fined-1m-care-staff-sentenced-after-scalding-death ; https://www.hcrlaw.com/news-and-insights/care-home-group-parent-company-convicted-of-corporate-manslaughter/ ; https://bracknellnews.co.uk/news/19634803.birdsgrove-nursing-home-death-results-1-million-fine-aster-healthcare
- In the same case two staff members received suspended sentences, including one of 16 weeks suspended for 18 months for failing to discharge a duty (UK) (search extract) — https://thecarehomeenvironment.com/story/36727/aster-healthcare-fined-1m-care-staff-sentenced-after-scalding-death
- Salutem LD Bidco IV (Birchwood Care Home, Chesham): the CQC press release title says the provider was "ordered to pay £117,681". Reports say it was fined £100,000 at Oxford Magistrates' Court after two care workers did not check the water before hoisting a 53-year-old resident into the bath. She suffered second-degree burns. This was a CQC prosecution (UK–England) (search extract) — https://cqc.org.uk/press-release/salutem-ld-bidco-iv-limited-ordered-pay-ps117681-after-failing-provide-safe-care-and ; https://caring-times.co.uk/?p=40143
- Greencroft (Aston, Deeside, Wales): directors were fined after an 88-year-old resident was lowered into a scalding bath and died of burns about a month later. HSE found nothing to stop the water exceeding 44°C, and the fine was £5,000 per the search summary (search extract) — https://www.deeside.com/aston-greencroft-nursing-home-directors-fined-resident-lowered-scalding-bath-died-burns/
- Real Life Options (Bathgate, Scotland): the provider and a support worker were both fined after a resident with learning disabilities was severely burned. The carer failed to test the water, and the immersion heater thermostat had failed (search extract) — https://www.hvpmag.co.uk/Care-home-provider-and-support-worker-fined-after-resident-severely-burned-in-bath
- Other UK fines (headline figures only): a "care charity fined £20k after scalding incident" (Edinburgh) and a "Scottish care home fined £30,000 after pensioner is scalded" (search extract) — https://www.Scotsman.Com/regions/edinburgh-fife-and-lothians/care-charity-fined-ps20k-after-scalding-incident-1491846 ; https://www.shponline.co.uk/in-court/scottish-care-home-fined-30000-pensioner-scalded/
- Civil claims: Penningtons reports a hospital scalding case (patient left unattended) that settled for £17,500 plus costs (UK) (search extract) — https://penningtonslaw.com/case-studies/scalding-injury-to-patient-left-unattended-in-hospital
- A disabled woman burned after being lowered into a scalding bath at an Essex care home received an undisclosed settlement, earmarked for equipment such as a powered wheelchair (UK) (search extract) — https://www.heart.co.uk/essex/news/local/disabled-burn-victim-awarded-damages/

## 3. Current workarounds
- Manual check and record before bathing: NHS Lothian policy requires staff to "check hot water temperature(s) before bathing/showering and record via patient/service user's care plan", and to turn the tap off if the reading is above 44°C (UK–Scotland) (search extract) — https://policyonline.nhslothian.scot/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Safe-Bathing-Showering-and-Surface-Temperature-Policy.pdf
- Prosecutions show the manual check fails in practice. In the Bathgate case, staff were supposed to check and record the temperature, but written instructions had not been provided. HSE also found no risk assessment and "inadequate" thermometers (UK–Scotland) (search extract) — https://www.hvpmag.co.uk/Care-home-provider-and-support-worker-fined-after-resident-severely-burned-in-bath
- In the 2014 HSE case of an 88-year-old scalded in a bath, staff had been told to check with a thermometer before baths, but management made no checks that this was happening. The summary did not name the URL; the Greencroft coverage fits the details (search extract) — https://www.deeside.com/aston-greencroft-nursing-home-directors-fined-resident-lowered-scalding-bath-died-burns/
- Handheld thermometers cost little. Brannan's hydro-powered digital bath and shower thermometer is marketed as "perfect for care homes or hospitals". The search summary gives £25.31 including tax (£21.09 excluding) on Brannan's site (UK) (search extract) — https://www.brannan.co.uk/?p=80870
- Engineering control: TMVs, plus HSE's expectation that outlet temperatures and TMVs are "monitored and maintained". In the Aster case the bath had the wrong type of TMV, and the one fitted had not been serviced and was not working properly (UK) (search extract) — https://www.hse.gov.uk/healthServices/scalding-burning.htm ; https://www.hcrlaw.com/news-and-insights/care-home-group-parent-company-convicted-of-corporate-manslaughter/
- No source quantified staff time spent on bath-temperature checks.

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- HSE: "where vulnerable people are at risk from scalding during whole body immersion, water temperatures must not exceed 44°C". Outlet temperatures and TMVs should be monitored and maintained. The full guidance is HSE's HSIS6, which was not seen in full (UK) (search extract) — https://www.hse.gov.uk/healthServices/scalding-burning.htm
- Falsified records are treated as aggravating. Aster provided falsified water-temperature records and a false TMV service history to the CQC and HSE (UK) (search extract) — https://thecarehomeenvironment.com/story/36727/aster-healthcare-fined-1m-care-staff-sentenced-after-scalding-death ; https://mills-reeve.com/blogs/health-and-care/october-2021/care-home-group-fined-for-corporate-manslaughter
- The CQC prosecutes directly for failing to provide safe care and treatment, as in the Salutem case (UK–England) (search extract) — https://cqc.org.uk/press-release/salutem-ld-bidco-iv-limited-ordered-pay-ps117681-after-failing-provide-safe-care-and
- Local NHS policies require recording in the care plan (Lothian) (search extract) — https://policyonline.nhslothian.scot/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Safe-Bathing-Showering-and-Surface-Temperature-Policy.pdf
- The RDaSH NHS trust has a "prevention of scalding and drowning" policy (title only) (search extract) — https://www.rdash.nhs.uk/policies/prevention-ofscalding-and-drowning-policy/

- Insurance: no insurer requirement for bath-temperature logging was found.

## Researcher observations
- Strong: the evidence on cost and pain is unusually concrete. It runs from £5k to £1.04m in fines, includes corporate manslaughter and personal sentences for staff, and Aster's falsified temperature records are the exact failure a tamper-evident log addresses. The check is per bath, so usage is daily.
- Weak: the hardware is a £15–£25 commodity, and the value is entirely in the record and workflow. That puts it in direct competition with digital care-record platforms, which could add a "bath temp" field at no cost. A connected thermometer partnership with those platforms may be the better route than a standalone product. The TMV-outlet alert overlaps with Legionella monitoring vendors (Bluemetric, Nano Sensorics, Inform).
- Possible better channel: TMV servicing and water-hygiene contractors, who already visit and are implicated when TMV service records are poor (Aster). Supported-living and learning-disability providers (Salutem, Real Life Options) appear as often as elderly-care homes and may be an equally good segment.
