# R01-C-04 — Newly planted street trees die of drought in years 1-3 while watering contractors bill for weekly visits

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Councils plant large numbers of young street/park trees, often grant-funded, and a share die of drought in the first three summers. Watering is usually done on fixed rounds by contractors or in-house bowser crews, whether or not the soil is dry, and councils have little independent proof that a scheduled watering visit took place.
- Who suffers / who pays: Council tree/arboricultural officers and parks contract managers (replacement cost, grant claims, public criticism); grant funders (Forestry Commission funds); watering/landscape contractors who are often contractually liable for replacing trees they failed to water.
- Sensor concept: Capacitive soil-moisture probe at two depths in the tree pit plus temperature, LoRaWAN, ~3-year battery; daily "needs water" route list; a moisture spike as evidence that a contractor visit happened; establishment report for the funder.

## 9. Recurring revenue
- UTCF establishment ran 3 years, with a 5-year maintenance obligation after the final payment. This fits a 3–5 year per-tree subscription (search extract) — https://www.gov.uk/guidance/urban-tree-challenge-fund
- LATF establishment claims require surveyed survival (75% minimum), so recurring evidence is needed at each claim point (search extract) — https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/local-authority-treescape-fund-claim-form/latf-beat-up-survey-guidance
- Weakness: after year 3 the tree is established and the probe can be moved to a new planting cohort. Revenue is per-cohort, not perpetual, so sensors may be redeployed rather than bought again.

## Researcher observations
- Strong: the pain is real and recurring (heatwave die-offs are national news). There is an existing contracted watering spend to optimise, and grant survival thresholds (LATF 75%) create a measurable outcome.
- Weak: the value per tree is low (£450–£1,700 replacement, ~£150/yr establishment grant). A per-tree sensor at £100+ hardware plus subscription is hard to justify on every tree, so it is more likely sampled per street or species cohort. Councils are budget-starved, and the UTCF is now closed.
- Better buyer/channel: the landscape/watering contractor who carries replacement liability (Ealing, Portland, Boston), or large developers and highways schemes with planting-warranty obligations (Falmouth, Maine). A "proof of watering" product sold to contractors to defend against replacement claims may sell better than one sold to councils.
- Competition is forming in DACH (Jena, Vienna, Climavi, Treesense, Plantobelly). The UK has pilots (Slough, Wrexham) but no dominant supplier.
