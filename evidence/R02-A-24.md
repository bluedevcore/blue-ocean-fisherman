## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: Used cooking oil (UCO) is a traded biofuel feedstock that licensed collectors buy or collect from food outlets. Thieves, sometimes posing as collectors, empty drums and tanks behind restaurants before scheduled collections, so collectors find containers part-empty and lose volume. Traceability for certified biofuel chains depends on collected volumes matching sources.
- Who suffers / who pays: Operations director of a UCO collection company (buyer); restaurant and takeaway managers; collection drivers.
- Sensor concept: Ultrasonic, radar or load-cell level sensor plus lid-open/tilt sensor in collector-supplied containers on LTE-M. A theft alert fires if the level drops with no scheduled collection, and routes are planned by fill level.

## 3. Current workarounds
- UK National Business Crime Centre advice: lockable boxes for outdoor containers ("indoor containers are obviously the most secure"), lockable fencing and gates, and an alarm system. It warns that offenders impersonate genuine oil recycling services (UK) (search extract) — https://nbcc.police.uk/images/crime-prevention/pdfs/cooking-oil-theft.pdf
- Collector-side hardware: Frontline International collection ports with an integrated anti-theft valve. Even sealed tanks are at risk if the collector's valve is left unlocked between pick-ups (US) (search extract) — https://www.qsrmagazine.com/news/frontline-protects-restaurants-waste-cooking-oil-theft
- Baker Commodities offers theft-resistant containers, indoor tanks, lock systems, documented schedules and uniformed drivers (US) (search extract) — https://bakercommodities.com/?p=2882
- Louisville collector VOCARS is replacing older tanks with anti-theft containers that cost two to three times more (US) (search extract) — https://www.wdrb.com/news/organized-crime-targets-used-cooking-oil-thefts-rise-in-louisville/article_eb497f7d-c290-476b-b4f0-80aaab18492f.html
- Darling Ingredients says it works closely with its 20,000 UCO customers in Florida to minimise thefts (US) (search extract) — https://www.fox35orlando.com/news/florida-men-accused-stealing-150-gallons-used-cooking-oil-from-restaurants

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- ISCC chain entry: oil users must sign a self-declaration before their UCO can enter the ISCC-certified supply chain. Collection is tied to terms including a Category 3 (animal by-products) agreement (UK collector page) (search extract) — https://uk.quatra.com/certification
- Transport & Environment: ISCC certification means "paper-based audits have been conducted on operators that handle the UCO", not physical testing. Points of origin provide only self-declarations (EU) (search extract) — https://www.transportenvironment.org/articles/uco ; https://transportenvironment.org/uploads/files/Used-Cooking-Oil_-The-Certified-Unknown.pdf

## Researcher observations
- The pain is well documented in UK and US news and police advice. The UK "20% stolen, £50m" estimate is widely repeated but comes from an industry source, not audited data.
- c7 is the big weakness: Sensoneo already markets an almost identical UCO antitheft and fill-level product on NB-IoT/CAT-M1. The new idea needs a clear wedge, such as a cheaper load-cell or tamper sensor, UK collector integration, or evidence-grade logs for police and insurers.
- c1 per site is small (a drum is ~€100–€135 at wholesale). The value case must be built per collector across thousands of sites plus route savings. Sensoneo's own pitch leads with route efficiency, which suggests theft alone may not justify the spend.
- A possibly better buyer for the same problem: large multi-site restaurant and pub chains that are paid for their oil (one chain cited £60 per drum) and want proof of volumes collected, or container OEMs such as Frontline-type indoor tank suppliers who could embed sensing.
