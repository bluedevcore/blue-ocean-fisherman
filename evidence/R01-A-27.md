# R01-A-27 — Church roof alarms rely on phone-line diallers the PSTN switch-off breaks, putting lead-theft cover at risk

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: UK churches with lead or copper roofs face metal theft. The main church insurer limits theft-of-metal cover for vulnerable churches unless an approved, monitored roof alarm is fitted. Existing alarms cost several thousand pounds plus annual maintenance and monitoring, and alarms that signal over analogue phone lines must be re-engineered before the UK PSTN switch-off.
- Who suffers / who pays: Parochial Church Councils (PCCs) and churchwardens pay for repairs, alarms and maintenance. Grant bodies (Benefact Trust, county church trusts) part-fund alarms. Insurers carry capped losses.
- Sensor concept: Battery wireless roof sensors (PIR plus vibration on lead sheets) with an LTE-M/LoRaWAN cellular communicator (no phone line or mains), monitored alert routing to an alarm receiving centre (ARC) and keyholders, and insurer-compliance status reports.

## 2. Frequency
- Countryside Alliance police FOI data for 2025: 3,637 crimes on church and religious premises, including "58 lead- and metal-thefts". 37 of 45 police forces provided figures (Church Times, 17 Apr 2026, UK) (search extract) — https://www.churchtimes.co.uk/articles/2026/17-april/news/uk/daily-crime-rate-is-ten-plus-in-places-of-worship-says-lobby
- The Countryside Alliance's 2022–2024 report counted 179 lead and metal thefts across three years, with 33 of 45 forces providing figures (UK) (search extract) — https://www.countryside-alliance.org/features/investigation-over-9000-crimes-reported-at-churches-across-uk-in-just-3-years
- In Norfolk, police data showed 48 incidents in the two years before the roof alarm scheme and 26 in the two years after it (UK) (search extract) — https://norfolkchurchestrust.org.uk/roof-alarm-success-campaign-to-prevent-church-lead-thefts-needs-more-funds-to-install-alarms/
- Repeat targeting happens at individual churches: Durham had three thefts at one church in under three years, and Bishops Tachbrook was "targeted twice" (search extract) — https://durhamdiocese.org/diocesan-news/metal-thefts-cause-22k-worth-of-damage-at-historic-church.php ; https://leamingtonobserver.co.uk/news/church-targeted-twice-by-lead-thieves-faces-80000-bill/
- Per church, theft is rare. Roughly 58–60 recorded thefts a year nationally is spread over many thousands of churches. No sourced monthly or weekly check duty for roof alarms was found (see Not Found). The only recurring signal found is that annual alarm maintenance is expected (search extract) — https://elydiocese.org/content/pages/documents/roof-alarms-guidance-for-churches.pdf

## 3. Current workarounds
- Purchase cost of an approved roof alarm: "Roof alarm purchases cost £3-5000 with annual maintenance" (Diocese of Oxford, UK) (search extract) — https://www.oxford.anglican.org/lead-theft-reaches-crisis-point
- Ely diocesan guidance: maintenance costs "vary between providers and the level of service chosen but are usually hundreds of pounds". A first-responder security company that attends activations is "an additional annual cost" (UK) (search extract) — https://elydiocese.org/content/pages/documents/roof-alarms-guidance-for-churches.pdf
- The OHCT/E-Bound roof alarm scheme enrolment form refers to "£600pa annual costs" after the first year (Oxfordshire, UK) (search extract) — https://ohct.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/161028enrolmentform.pdf
- Replacing lead with substitutes: one church replaced all 16 lead panels with terne-coated stainless steel. Commentary notes that like-for-like replacement is not prudent where repeat theft is likely (UK) (search extract) — https://lawandreligionuk.com/2014/11/05/church-roofs-replacement-of-lead-following-theft/ ; "Lead thefts force churches to find cheaper replacements" — https://www.sleafordstandard.co.uk/heritage-and-retro/heritage/lead-thefts-force-churches-to-find-cheaper-replacements-3962546
- Volunteer alarm response: Ely guidance suggests a churchwarden is often a poor choice to receive alerts, so a paid first responder is recommended (search extract) — https://elydiocese.org/content/pages/documents/roof-alarms-guidance-for-churches.pdf

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- Ecclesiastical (the main UK church insurer) says that "for many churches we are now able to offer unlimited cover for theft of metal claims. For some churches that are particularly vulnerable… we also require an approved roof alarm to benefit from the increased cover" (UK) (search extract) — https://ecclesiastical.com/risk-management/roof-protection-systems
- Ely diocesan guidance: "unless an approved roof alarm is fitted, churches using Ecclesiastical Insurance (EIG) will have their cover limited". The alarm "has to be active and monitored at the time of the theft" (UK) (search extract) — https://elydiocese.org/content/pages/documents/roof-alarms-guidance-for-churches.pdf
- Ely metal-theft guide (2020, revised 2022) says capped limits of "£7500… insufficient to replace even a small tower roof" (UK) (search extract) — https://elydiocese.org/content/pages/documents/metal-theft-from-church-buildings--a-guide-for-pccs--churchwardens-and-clergy.pdf
- Insurance trade press: "Church metal theft epidemic sees unlimited cover revoked". Ecclesiastical imposed a £5,000 limit (date not shown in extract, UK) (search extract) — https://www.postonline.co.uk/post/news/1252092/church-metal-theft-epidemic-unlimited-cover-revoked
- Ecclesiastical-insured church policies are adjusted to include an "Electronic Protection Condition". Claims are considered if the church can evidence compliance (Oxford DAC metal-theft guidance, UK) (search extract) — https://www.oxford.anglican.org/content/pages/documents/20210106-doc-support-services-sophie-hammond-metal-theft-prevention-v01.pdf
- Claims are reduced or refused in practice. Commentary says payments are "generally capped at quite a low level", and an insurer refused payment where scaffolding assisted access (UK) (search extract) — https://lawandreligionuk.com/2014/11/05/church-roofs-replacement-of-lead-following-theft/
- Church permission (faculty) rules: diocesan lists place roof-alarm installation under List B. Leicester notes stand-alone roof alarms fall under List A1(9) from 1 Apr 2020, with conditions (UK, Church of England) (search extract) — https://rochester.anglican.org/content/pages/documents/faculties-lists-a-and-b.pdf ; https://leicester.anglican.org/content/pages/documents/1588252098.pdf
- PSTN switch-off: BT pushed the digital switch deadline to 2027 (UK) (search extract) — https://professionalsecurity.co.uk/news/commercial-security/bt-puts-back-digital-switch-deadline-to-2027
- BT withdrew its Redcare alarm signalling service from 1 Aug 2025 (insurance broker notice, UK) (search extract) — https://www.pib-insurance.com/news/bt-withdrawing-redcare-alarm-service ; insurer notice — https://travelers.co.uk/insights/news/pstn-switch-off-bt-redcare-announcement
- Telecoms providers are migrating customers off analogue lines, and "PSTN-based alarms will fail and need to be upgraded" (CSL product news, UK, vendor context) (search extract) — https://www.ifsecglobal.com/new-look-dualcom-gradeshift-range-with-lanip-pstn-and-enhanced-roaming

## 5. Buyer & budget
- PCCs commission the work. Oxford diocesan guidance asks for a quotation showing sensor and equipment positions plus "a PCC minute" for the permission application (UK) (search extract) — https://www.oxford.anglican.org/parish-support/church-buildings/permissions-and-how-to-apply/roofs.php
- The Benefact Trust (formerly Allchurches Trust) roof protection grant requires insurer approval of the scheme and a PCC commitment to maintain it "for a minimum of five years". It funds up to 50% of cost, capped at £2,500 (UK) (search extract) — https://trurodiocese.org.uk/?p=43034
- Ely guidance is addressed to "PCCs, churchwardens and clergy" (UK) (search extract) — https://elydiocese.org/content/pages/documents/metal-theft-from-church-buildings--a-guide-for-pccs--churchwardens-and-clergy.pdf
- PCC minutes show church alarm decisions with an installation cost and an annual maintenance charge "including 24/7 monitoring, two services per year" (St Peter's Tiverton, Sept 2024; general church alarm, UK) (search extract) — https://stpeterstiverton.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/PCC-17-November-2024.pdf

## 8. Buildability (components, BOM, certification)
- A LoRaWAN PIR sensor (Milesight WS202) retails at about £52 in the UK or $44 in the US. It is IP30 (indoor only), detects at 6–8 m and runs on one ER14335 lithium battery, so an outdoor roof version would need a different enclosure (search extract) — https://store.mcci.com/products/milesight-pir-light-sensor-ws202 ; https://innon.com/products/ws202-868m
- 4G and dual-path alarm communicators are off-the-shelf (CSL DualCom range) (search extract) — https://www.ifsecglobal.com/new-look-dualcom-gradeshift-range-with-lanip-pstn-and-enhanced-roaming
- Certification and approval burden: the alarm must meet the insurer's approved-supplier minimum performance standard. Some guidance says installers should be accredited (e.g., NICEIC), and Grade I/II* churches require Historic England consultation (search extract) — https://ecclesiastical.com/risk-management/roof-protection-systems ; https://www.oxford.anglican.org/parish-support/church-buildings/permissions-and-how-to-apply/roofs.php

## Researcher observations
- Strong: insurer condition (Ecclesiastical Electronic Protection Condition, capped cover), large per-incident losses (£22k–£400k), established installer and grant channels.
- Weak: frequency per church is low (around 58 recorded lead and metal thefts a year in 2025 FOI data, across many thousands of churches). The PSTN pain is unevidenced for churches and is solved cheaply by existing 4G communicators that incumbent installers already fit. Insurer-approved status is a gatekeeping barrier for a new entrant.
- Better angle: sell a cellular communicator and compliance-status reporting through Ecclesiastical-approved installers or county trust schemes (OHCT/E-Bound, Norfolk), rather than as a new alarm.
