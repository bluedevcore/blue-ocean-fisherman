# R01-B-12 — Pallet racking uprights hit several times a week with no report until the weekly inspection

Note on method: WebFetch is blocked in this environment, so no page was fetched. Every bullet below

## Idea (neutral statement)
- Problem: In busy distribution centres, lift trucks strike adjustable pallet racking uprights, and drivers may not report the impact. A damaged upright can stay loaded until the next weekly visual check by the Person Responsible for Racking Safety (PRRS) or the annual expert (SARI) inspection.
- Who suffers / who pays: Warehouse operatives working under racking, and the PRRS. The payer would be the warehouse or operations manager.
- Sensor concept: An accelerometer/tilt tag on uprights or upright protectors (LoRaWAN, multi-year battery) logs impact severity, time and bay. It alerts the PRRS to inspect, cross-references truck shifts, and prioritises the weekly inspection route.

## 3. Current workarounds
- HSG76 names the in-house inspector the PRRS, who is expected to carry out weekly visual checks, "recording damage levels and implementing action". Expert inspections are by SEMA Approved Racking Inspectors (SARIs) using a traffic-light system (UK) (search extract) — https://warehousenews.co.uk/?p=108842 ; https://www.bureauveritas.co.uk/buildings-infrastructure/engineering-inspections/racking-inspection
- SEMA damage categories: Red, "the most serious damage and must be offloaded immediately". Amber, offload within four weeks, and "if repairs are not carried out within four weeks an Amber risk item automatically becomes a Red risk item". Green, monitor annually (UK) (search extract) — https://www.fastlineservices.co.uk/resources/sema-racking-inspection-guidance ; https://www.logisticsmatters.co.uk/?p=16157
- Racking should be inspected by a SARI "at least once a year", more often depending on damage history (UK) (search extract) — https://forkway.co.uk/why-regular-pallet-racking-inspections-matter ; https://www.protrainings.uk/blog/?p=10810
- SEMA publishes a Maintenance Management Guide (2025 edition; title only) (UK) (search extract) — https://www.ipesearch.co.uk/SEMA-Maintenance-Management-Guide-2025
- Reporting culture: "forklift drivers often don't report collisions because they don't want to get in trouble" (search extract) — https://www.forkliftcertification.com/?p=7365
- Emerging alternative: robot or drone automated racking inspection, e.g. the trade article "Automated pallet racking inspection: what changes when machines run the aisles" (Oct 2026; title only) (search extract) — https://roboticsandautomationnews.com/2026/10/21/automated-pallet-racking-inspection-what-changes-when-machines-run-the-aisles/106158/

## 4. Compliance, insurance & audit pressure
- HSE guidance HSG76 (warehousing and storage) sets out the PRRS role and weekly checks, and follows SEMA's green/amber/red classification for expert inspections (UK) (search extract) — https://warehousenews.co.uk/?p=108842 ; https://www.bureauveritas.co.uk/buildings-infrastructure/engineering-inspections/racking-inspection
- Amber items escalate to Red after four weeks without repair, which creates a time-bound duty once damage is known (UK) (search extract) — https://www.logisticsmatters.co.uk/?p=16157
- HSE prosecutes racking failures, e.g. the 2026 fatal collapse and the Corby case above (UK) (search extract) — https://press.hse.gov.uk/2026/02/11/two-firms-fined-after-racking-collapse-killed-two-workers/
- Employment cases show that failing to report an impact is a disciplinary matter. An Ontario board found dismissal excessive for a first-time failure to report, while another arbitrator upheld termination where the driver concealed the impact and falsified documents (Canada) (search extract) — https://www.hrreporter.com/chrr-plus/employment-law/forklift-driver-reinstated-after-dismissal-for-accident/318519 ; https://ohsinsider.com/spot-the-safety-violation-storage-rack-collapse-buries-worker/?print=pdf
- PUWER and insurer requirements were not searched (budget).

Battery "≥3 Years (10min reporting interval, SF10)" per the official-looking spec, while retailers claim >5 years on "2 × 2700 mAh ER14505 Li-SOCL2 Batteries". EU prices: €83 (Invibit) and €74.90 ex VAT (iot-shop.de, marked unavailable); AUD 99 ex GST (search extract) — https://invibitshop.com/products/milesight-em320-tilt-lorawan%C2%AE-tilt-sensor-eu868 ; https://iot-shop.de/en/shop/mil-em320-tilt-868m-milesight-em320-tilt-lorawan-tilt-sensor-6361 ; https://iot-store.com.au/collections/sensors/products/milesight-em320-tilt-lorawan-sensor ; https://gowifi.co.nz/specs/em320-tilt-datasheet-en.pdf
- The EM320-TILT is documented for tilt/angle change, not short shock events. Impact detection would need custom firmware or a different accelerometer configuration (search extract) — https://gowifi.co.nz/specs/em320-tilt-datasheet-en.pdf
- Scale issue: a DC has many uprights, so per-tag cost (~€75–83 retail for an off-the-shelf tilt node) multiplies quickly. Targeting only high-traffic aisle ends (where the MHI case found consistent low-level damage) may be needed (researcher inference from https://og.mhi.org/casestudies/13428).

## Researcher observations
- Strong c4 structure (HSG76 PRRS weekly checks, SEMA Red/Amber/Green with time limits, annual SARI). But the guidance asks for visual inspection, not continuous monitoring, so the sensor is a nice-to-have against the regulatory baseline.
- The frequency of unreported impacts rests on vendor claims (8–12 unreported per reported). There is no independent figure.
- Competition is the main weakness. A-SAFE RackEye has the same concept, an established channel (barrier sales) and patents, and Rack Sense and others exist. Differentiation would need to be price per upright or integration with SARI inspection software.
- Better channel idea for the same problem: partner with SARI inspection firms or racking repair firms, who could lease tags to prioritise inspections and generate repair work. Forklift OEMs and dealers (Linde's truck-side sensor) may prefer truck-mounted impact logging, which needs fewer devices per site.
